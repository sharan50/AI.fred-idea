3What phone work covers

Phone work covers non-authenticating calls and nothing else: bookings, chasing, enquiries, availability and appointment setting. No value from the vault is placed on a call in either direction, and no script asks a worker to state one (DR-021), and a party is eligible for phone work at all only where its desk asks for identity at the start of a call to find the account rather than to open it, which DR-026 fixes as a field in the party register rather than a judgement a worker makes on the call. So a step that needs a value stated or verified by voice is placed on the managed browser or the relay, where the substitution component can place it, or it bounces to the user's own device. A worker asked for a value on a call says it is not available on the phone, offers the surface that can carry it, and closes the step blocked-needs-user if the desk will not proceed. A desk that states a value unasked is an exposure: the worker closes the step on the possible-exposure control, which opens an incident with the party named.

What a worker hears on such a call is the far party using the user's first name and the details of the thing being booked. That is accepted, and it is what the need-to-know card, the vetting of section 4, the site controls of section 5 and the access ledger are for; on this surface they are the mitigation rather than the closure, and the trust story says so in the user's own words.

3.1The workflow library and its tickets

The library the architecture specifies (DR-022) is an operational asset with an operational cost. A counterparty is modelled once, per step type, and from then on the worker is given a structured task card rather than a page; a counterparty that changes its process breaks that entry, and the break surfaces as a step that cannot reach its acceptance artefact. The floor's job is to catch it rather than to work around it: the exceptions board carries the failed step, the shift lead raises a library ticket naming the party, the step type and what changed, and the step is worked by hand or bounced until the ticket lands. Two floor metrics follow, reported with the exceptions board: open library tickets by party, and the share of steps worked by hand because the library was stale. 05 prices the maintenance.

4Tiers, escalation and dual control

Three tiers, and two functions outside them. QA and escalation are standalone functions, not tiers, reporting outside the tier line so that the people who check the work do not answer to the people who route it.

4.1The three tiers

L1, the generalist pool
Reversible steps in any vertical: a call to confirm an appointment, a booking form filled from aliases, never a KYC form, a status enquiry. L1 never fires an irreversible step. Every worker starts here, and its cards are the most fully scripted.
L2, the specialist pods
Six pods, one per vertical: banking and wealth; legal and administrative; identity and security; health and medical; family affairs; travel. A pod takes the step types that name it and the irreversible human steps in its vertical, executed under dual control with the AI checking. A pod knows how its institutions behave: which desk to ask for, which forms bounce, which fees are negotiable.
L3, super-trust
The smallest tier and the most vetted. L3 executes the six identity acts of the closed list in 01 that place an identity value or credential (present a document, state an identity number, answer security questions from the context store, complete a KYC form, use a delegated login to a non-financial portal, sign or e-sign), where the policy layer permits them in a jurisdiction, always with the value placed by the substitutor and never seen, and holds the human seat in dual control whenever the AI executes an irreversible step. The seventh act, attest a fact about the user, places nothing of its own: it rides beside a committable act on any step and takes that step's routing, which is why task 12 step 3, calling a booking desk and stating no value of the client's at all, is an L1 card. L3 is drawn from L2 after a tenure and a further check, and a seat is staffed on every shift.

4.2Where a step goes

Routing is a rule, not a judgement, and it runs when the card is issued. The key is the step type, from the step-type catalogue in 02, and the rule is a routing table that operations owns, kept beside the catalogue and keyed by step type: L1 for a reversible step type with no institution-specific script; the vertical's pod for one that needs it; L3 for a step whose identity act places an identity value or credential, the six of section 4.1, and for the checking seat in dual control; attest-fact, which places nothing, routes with the step it rides on. The table's answer is stamped on the step record as the executor's tier when the card is issued, and a check card for an AI-executed step is offered only to the L3 checking pool. An irreversible step inside the envelope goes to L2 or L3, enters checking before it fires, and never goes to L1. Before any entry the table reads the scope block of the market's legal pack, as section 3.1 of 02 fixes, and for the pilot that block names L3 as the only pool for an irreversible human step, so every such step goes to L3, as 06 records; the rule is a line of the pack, not a setting of the table. A worker cannot pull a card from a pool above their own. Where a step type carries a specialist part and a reversible remainder, the plan carries them as two steps from the start, the pod's and then L1's: the split is the planner's at planning time and never a re-plan after the pod closes, and a pod that finds the split wrong closes failed-final with the reason, one of the re-planning triggers 02 fixes.

4.3Dual control on the floor

For an irreversible step inside the envelope, whichever party executes, the other checks before it fires: dual control, fixed in DR-004 and specified for the console in 02. On the floor it has two shapes. When the AI executes, an L3 worker checks: the console shows the step, the envelope lines it touches, the expected outcome and the exact act about to fire, with aliases in place of values, and the L3 confirms or refuses with a reason. When a worker executes, the AI performs the same check, and the console withholds the committing act until the result is recorded rather than trusting the worker to wait. The substitutor will not place a value for an irreversible step without the approval token the check issues; where the act places no value, the surface controller holds it. The committing actions are a closed list, the same on every page: a form submit, a committing click, a committing sentence on a call, a keypad press outside the navigation map, a relay release, a committing placement; every controller checks the envelope's validity window and version at each of them, as 02 fixes, and each surface holds the act by a default rule of its own. A committing placement is one whose alias is a payment instrument or a payment instruction, or whose catalogue entry marks it as consumed on placement: the substitutor requires the approval token for it like a submit and writes the step's fired marker before the first tone or character is delivered, so a not-confirmed receipt closes the step failed-final with the marker as its evidence, never failed-retryable, the reservation against the cap is not released, the re-plan issues a status enquiry before any repeat, and a narrowing that arrives inside the placement's window does not stop it, which the outcome report says. In the managed browser a click is a committing action unless the party directory's page map, a field beside the menu map, names the control as navigation: following a link inside the declared domains, scrolling, focusing a field and typing non-sensitive text are navigation by default; a button, a form submit or any scripted control is committing unless the page map names it; and any form submission or committing click on a party's domain is irreversible unless the catalogue entry names its undo route, the submit being fired by the browser controller on the task service's instruction, after the check. On a call there is no gate in the media path, so on a step holding no approval token the script is the bound: it carries only the lines the step needs and no committing sentence, the non-committing lexicon covers, greetings, questions and acknowledgements without agreement words; anything else the worker says reaches the far party, because there is no gate to hold it (DR-021), and the bound is the script and the check that precedes it; on a step holding a token the revealed committing sentence passes. Inside the lexicon the commitment dictionary remains the detector for what counts as agreement, agreement words, amounts, dates and account-changing verbs per language, and a commitment-form utterance that is not the revealed committing sentence for a step holding an approval token reaches the far party, which is why the check comes before the words rather than after them. On an irreversible step not yet checked, the caption shows "commitment held; send for check" and the step enters checking; a reversible step carries no committing sentence at all, so its card reads "this step commits nothing", the step does not enter checking and never holds a token, and if the party asks for an agreement the script does not carry the worker closes the step blocked-outside-envelope; agreeing the slot itself is not such an agreement, because schedule is an act the envelope admits and the script names. The committing sentence is not shown to the worker until the check is recorded; then it is released and the worker reads it as written. The step's fired marker is written at the release, as telephony in 02 specifies: a marker when the sentence reaches the worker's leg for the revealed sentence or is read out by the worker, an end marker when the sentence has been delivered. The start marker is the step's fired marker, and a step that carries one never returns to ready. A withdrawal or a narrowing that reaches the task service before the sentence is released stops it at once, the console withdrawing it from the card: on a withdrawal the step closes cancelled, cause withdrawn, and the outcome report cites the utterance record; on a narrowing it closes failed-final with the fired marker as its evidence, and the re-plan issues a status enquiry with the party before any repeat, as 02 fixes for every step with a fired marker. One that arrives after the release treats the act as fired, and the outcome report lists it with the step's fired marker as the evidence. The worker's structured result records what the party said in reply; it is never the evidence of whether the act fired. A key pressed on a party's voice menu is held by the same controller: the step's catalogue entry carries a menu map, the keys that navigate, per party, from the party register; the telephony controller sends a key the map names as navigation without a token, and any key the map names as committing, or any key the map does not name, is a committing action that needs the approval token and the window-and-version check like a submit. In the mail relay the rule is the template: the relay releases a message only if it instantiates the step's catalogue template, fixed sentences with named slots typed non-committing, or the step holds an approval token naming the hash of the message as checked; a message that matches no template is a committing action and needs the token, so a draft is not released until the check is on the record. On a reversible step, which holds no token, none of the three surfaces can commit anything: the browser controller refuses every click outside the page map and every submit, the telephony controller refuses every key outside the navigation map, and a script's line on fees is an instruction to a person, the gate being the closure. The executor is L2 or L3, never L1. Both identities live on the step record and the outcome record. The ledger records placements, views and exposures, the event types 03 fixes: the check itself writes no line of its own, and the checker's viewing of the masked stream does, as "A checker saw the masked stream for step n"; the user learns that the step was checked from the outcome report, which lists every irreversible act that fired and the party that checked it, as 01 sets out. A step in checking past its timer goes to the board.

4.4Escalation

A worker escalates by closing the step with the code that says why, and a card escalated is not a card failed. There is no escalate control: blocked-needs-user, blocked-outside-envelope, a blocked-third-party step past its window, failed-final and refused-policy are the escalations. The escalation desk is a standalone function staffed on every shift by people from L2 and L3 who are not, that shift, executing cards of their own. It does one of four things: chases a blocked third party and sets a new response window; re-routes a step that failed because its route was wrong, by adding the right route to the alternatives the failed-final step carries into re-planning, so the planner issues it again to a pod or to L3; confirms a refusal under policy, with the policy reference; or adds to the alternatives a failed-final step carries into re-planning. It never edits a card in place, because a card is a projection of the step record and the step record is the planner's. Escalations are counted per step type; a step type that keeps producing them is one QA proposes a change to, and its catalogue entry takes the change.
