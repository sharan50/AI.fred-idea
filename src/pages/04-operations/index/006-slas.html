7Service levels, stated qualitatively

We state service levels in words on purpose. Response numbers are a pilot's output, not its input, and tying them to pool size now would smuggle in a measured economics that 05 does not have, because its numbers are modelled priors. What can be fixed now is the shape of each promise and the mechanism that keeps it: every level below is carried by a timer in the state machine, so a missed level is acted on by the task service at expiry, and whatever that leaves is an exception on the board rather than a statistic discovered later.

Table 4.5 Service levels as shapes and mechanisms; any number shown is an illustrative default, not a commitment.
MomentWhat we promiseHow the floor keeps it
AcknowledgementEvery request is acknowledged on receipt, in minutes rather than hours, on the channel it arrived by.The intake gateway acknowledges as it normalises, addressing the message to an alias, {{phone}} or a WhatsApp or iMessage handle, which the channel connector at the vault's edge proxy resolves only to hand the message to the channel provider, returning nothing to any process of ours; the received timer bounds the time to planning.
Plan and envelopeA routine task gets its plan summary and envelope proposal in the same sitting; a complex one says when to expect it.The planning timer; at expiry the task service acts on the tick: it re-runs the planner once under a fresh deadline, a recorded event, and on a second expiry sets a holding timer and places the task on the exceptions board for the escalation desk, which adds what the planner lacked and has it re-planned, or has the task refused with a reason code where it falls under what we refuse to do. The task never sits without a live timer.
Step responseA ready card is picked up within the shift that issued it and closed within its step timer, unless the counterpart is closed.Routing by step type; the step timer, at whose expiry the task service acts on the tick: the card is withdrawn and the step re-queued to its pool with a fresh timer, up to the step type's cap, and past the cap the step closes failed-final, which is what re-plans; the handover rule that nothing crosses unowned.
Nudge cadenceA task waiting on the user is nudged visibly, each nudge further apart than the last, then lapses; lapsed is never withdrawn.The nudge schedule per task type, an illustrative default of three nudges then lapse, on awaiting-authority, awaiting-user and awaiting-verdict; every nudge is a recorded event.
Escalation windowA step blocked by a third party waits exactly the party's stated response window, then escalates to a re-plan or a new route.blocked-third-party carries the window; awaiting-third-party carries the timer; expiry triggers escalate-resume.
Dual-control latencyA step in checking is checked within the shift that raised it.The L3 seat on every shift; a step past its checking timer goes to the board.
Verdict promptThe outcome report and the verdict request reach the user together, the moment the last step closes.The transition to awaiting-verdict; nudges follow the cadence above.

Two levels are deliberately absent. We make no promise about how long a task takes end to end, because the longest part of most high-consequence tasks is an institution's own clock, which we can chase but not set; and none per worker, because the unit the user experiences is the task and the unit the floor manages is the step. The pilot in 06 is where these words acquire numbers.
