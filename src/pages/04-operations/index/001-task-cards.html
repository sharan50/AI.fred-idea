1Task cards and pseudonymisation

A worker never receives a task. They receive a step, on a card the task service generated from the step record, carrying the least the step needs: a client number, the aliases the system will place, the facts in clear, the script, the expected outcome and a structured result. The console that renders it is specified in 02, worker console; this section says how a card comes to exist and what it may contain.

1.1How a card is generated

A card is the projection of one step whose class is human. The planner produces the plan, the classifier marks each step ai, human or user, and the card generator inside the task service assembles a card for every human step from the step record. A need-to-know filter runs first: it admits into the facts in clear only the non-sensitive task fields the step-type catalogue entry's script template names for the step type, an institution, a department, a window, a relationship word such as nominee or daughter, and only where the script references them. It admits no person's name, with no exception of any kind: not a given name, not initials, not a name a desk reads out for confirmation, on any surface and in any channel, which DR-027 fixes as an absolute. A name, a date of birth, an identifier or a contact detail is a personal-fact alias of Table 3.1 in 03 whoever it belongs to, the user's as {{name}} and a people node's qualified by the person reference ({{name.nominee}}, {{dob.nominee}}), vaulted at the proxy and placed by the substitutor like any other alias. It drops every other fact, drops any alias the policy layer does not permit on that surface in that jurisdiction (returning the step for re-classification), and replaces the user's identity with the task's client number. The hash service's screen runs last at generation, and the task service does not compute it: it holds no digest set and matches nothing itself, but submits the card payload to the hash service's screening call, as 03 specifies, which answers clean or found; a payload that comes back found is rejected before it leaves the task service and raises an incident. The screen is not the card's last line: the step sentence, the expected outcome and the facts in clear are free text the planner wrote from the tokenised request, and a value phrased outside the four detectors' reach could ride in them, so the console renders every free-text field of a card, the step sentence, the expected outcome, the facts in clear, a question and a variance note, through the same allowlist default paint the mail relay's renderer applies to an inbound message: every token outside the cleared vocabulary, an out-of-lexicon token, a proper noun that is not a cleared term, a digit run, a class-format match, is painted before the worker reads it, and a painted token the step needs is requested as a typed field on the task's thread or closes the step blocked-needs-user. Three components, one direction of travel: the planner writes the step record, its script drawn from the step-type catalogue entry in 02; the task service generates the card, and the filter and the screen run there, at generation, before a card exists anywhere; the console renders it, with its default paint as the second line behind the screen. Cards are generated when a step becomes ready, never in advance, because a card that exists is a card that can be read; when a plan is revised, the cards of superseded steps leave the console as the steps close cancelled with the outcome code superseded.

1.2Need-to-know and Client 4471

The client number is issued per task and is stable for every step of it, so it is the same on every card of the task and different on every task: two tasks by the same user carry two different numbers, and a worker cannot join them. The task service issues it when a task is accepted, from a random space large enough that a collision is rejected rather than resolved, never in sequence. It is stored in the task record beside the task id and the user reference the request format in 02 carries, and it is derived from neither, so nothing about a number says anything about a person, a task or another number. The user reference never reaches a card, a console view or a log the floor can read; the mapping from client number to task is read by the task service alone, when it generates a card, and is exposed to no support tool. "Client 4471" is the pattern: a word and a number, nothing that hints at a name, a city or a vertical.

Need-to-know applies at the field level. A worker booking a consultation learns the hospital, the department and the acceptable window; not the diagnosis, the referral letter, or the bank account the same client opened last week. "Not the diagnosis" is a mechanism, not a courtesy. A condition, a medication, a result or a legal matter is a value of the health and legal class of Table 3.1 in 03 ({{health.condition}}, {{legal.matter}}), tokenised at the vault's edge proxy whether the user typed it in a message or entered it in the device form's health fields, an adapter returned it, or a party said it on a call, where the pipeline's pass over the recording tokenises it and an incident is opened; the detector that catches a free-text mention is the dictionary-based health and legal term detector in the detector policy the proxy and the hash service's screen both run, one artefact shipped to both, whose reach is a to-verify that 03 records and the vault evaluation in 07 measures. Free text written on our side takes the second of those two routes: a worker's variance note or failed-final line, a question's free-line answer, an onboarding answer, a fact a closed step writes or a connector pull is submitted by the task service or the console to the hash service's screening call, which runs the same four detectors the proxy runs (identifier pattern, known-value digest, the user's declaration where a field is declared sensitive, and the health and legal dictionary), tokenises any match through its tokenise-only credential and returns the text with aliases in place; the task service and the console store only what the call returns. So a diagnosis is never in clear in the context store, in the step record's facts in clear, on a card or on a caption. An amount is a value of the same kind: a balance, a transaction, a loan, a salary, an overdue sum or a quoted fee is a financial-fact alias of Table 3.1 ({{balance.hdfc}}, {{amount.fee}}), a number with a currency word or symbol in written or spoken form, caught by the amount pattern the identifier-pattern detector carries at the proxy and in the hash service's screen, and by the pipeline's pass over a recording in its spoken form, so it reaches no card, caption, frame or stored result field as a number, and the envelope's cap is no exception: the card generator renders a cap line as presence and comparison only, "spend cap: no spend" for a cap of zero and otherwise "spend cap: set", the check card shows "remaining cap: sufficient for this act: yes", computed by the task service, and the amount-pattern screen therefore holds on every card payload. Where a step needs one amount for its outcome, the catalogue entry names that field, the surface controller compares the masked amount with the envelope's cap line, and the worker sees only the comparison, as the card in section 1.4 shows. What the booking needs is the speciality or the department, a non-sensitive task field the catalogue entry names, and that is what the filter admits. Underneath sits the alias rule from 03: everything the vault classes as sensitive is only ever an alias, so a card cannot carry a real value even by mistake, and the filter governs the non-sensitive remainder. The user's name, address, phone number and email are vault-classed, and so are a nominee's, a relative's or a contact's, aliases of the same class qualified by the person reference: a desk that asks for the patient's name on a call is given the given name the card carries as a task field, which is the exception of 1.1 and an open item, because no placement happens on a call at all. That is the console's alias-only rule seen from the card's side. One-time passcodes are never ours at all; {{otp}} is not an alias, and a step that needs one bounces to the user's device. The regulator asks the same of the customer: the Reserve Bank of India cautioned the public in August 2024 not to share login details, copies of KYC documents, card details, PINs, passwords or OTPs with unidentified persons or agencies verified [1]. Our floor is built so that a worker could not receive one even if a user tried to send it.

1.3The fields

The worker console owns the card's shape: six parts, in a fixed order, and nothing else, drawn in Figure 2.5. Table 4.1 says where each part's content comes from and the rule that bounds it. The surface panel sits beside the card, not on it. The card joins the step trace by the task reference and step position its header shows, which is how QA draws its sample and how the escalation desk receives a step; the client number is a second number with no relation to the first, and neither is derived from the user reference. Nobody on the floor looks a task up by the user: the desk receives a step through its outcome code, QA samples from the step trace, and a message from the user is joined to its task by the intake gateway, upstream of the floor.

1.4The script and the result

The script is numbered, in the second person, and marks the moments at which the system places a value: the worker cues placement; the tap goes from the console to the task service and on to the step's surface controller, which requests the placement, so the console never calls the substitutor; the substitutor places the value directly into the surface and answers the controller with a receipt, never a value; the worker's stream shows dots, and a call carries no placement at all; and the access ledger records the placement in plain words.

The result is structured: one code from the fixed vocabulary in the worker console, the fields it requires, free text bounded to the variance note. On a call the worker hears the far party in the ordinary way, because no value from the vault is ever on a call (DR-021): a reference the desk reads out is typed into the result form by the worker and tokenised into an alias on arrival, the ledger reading "reference captured as {{reference.max-healthcare}}", and a value the desk states unasked closes the step on the possible-exposure control, which opens an incident with the party named. The script's line declining read-backs is now part of the closure rather than a courtesy, because the channel has no mask behind it.

The script's fee line is an instruction to a person and, on this surface, nothing stands behind it: the worker records the amount the desk quotes and the screening call tokenises it on the way into the result, so the task service holds an alias and no component compares it with the envelope's cap line. On the browser the controller makes that comparison; on a call it has no owner, which is an open item in 07 rather than a closure this page may claim. The agreement is bounded the same way, by the script rather than by a gate in the media path: a step holding no approval token carries no committing sentence, so the worker has nothing to say that could agree anything, and a desk that will not proceed without an agreement is closed blocked-outside-envelope. Behind that stands a second line: every result field, the variance note included, is submitted by the task service to the hash service's screening call, which runs the four detectors of section 1.2 and answers clean, or found with the text returned aliases in place; the task service holds no digest set and computes no match, stores only what the call returns, and raises a found answer as an incident that the shift lead holds. A question closes the step as blocked-needs-user, which the task service turns into a user step.
