1Every open item, with an owner and a trigger

An item is open when the design has stated its position and a decision, a legal answer or a piece of evidence is still needed before that position is final. Amber items are decisions with a named trigger. To-verify items are legal or factual questions to be answered from the primary source. Assumption items are working defaults the pilot will replace with measurement. Where an item was resolved in the design by taking the smallest reasonable decision, the page that took it is named so the founder can reverse it.

1.1The legal to-verifies, per jurisdiction

None of these is a design constraint; the design carries the ceiling. Each is a question for counsel whose answer sets a runtime flag.

India
Whether a third party may perform any KYC step on a customer's behalf under the RBI's KYC directions and the Commercial Banks KYC Directions 2025, given that video identification must be run by the regulated entity's own trained officials; the Aadhaar Act's constraints on authentication, e-KYC and any handling of an Aadhaar number by a private entity, and the January 2025 amendment rules for private-entity authentication; whether an assistant may act as a customer-interface partner in an Account Aggregator journey on behalf of a regulated FIU; the E-mandate framework's limits on delegated recurring payments; and the commencement timetable of the DPDP Rules.
United Kingdom
Whether an agent may hold an account-information or payment-initiation consent for a customer under the Payment Services Regulations 2017 and the FCA's approach; reliance on third parties under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 and JMLSG guidance; third-party mandates and Lasting Power of Attorney as the formal routes for someone else to act on an account; whether a delegated identity act to a bank or a government service could be characterised as impersonation under the Fraud Act 2006 even with the customer's consent; Consumer Duty obligations when a customer acts through an agent; and the transfer mechanism for Bengaluru staff processing UK personal data.
United States
Customer identification under the Bank Secrecy Act and whether a third party may act for a customer; power-of-attorney norms per state; Regulation E's treatment of a standing authorisation given to an agent; state privacy law obligations, starting with California; and California's rule that an AI acting autonomously is no defence for the business that deployed it.

1.2Naming, domain and trademark

AI.fred is a working title and is used identically everywhere so that a rename is one find-and-replace pass. The dot in the name makes ai.fred unregistrable as a domain: .fred is not a top-level domain. Candidate forms to search, in no order: aifred, ai-fred, fred.ai, aifred.co, getaifred, askfred, fredassist. A trademark search per market precedes the choice; none has been made.

1.3Netlify access

A Netlify site is public by default. The interim mitigation in place is an unguessable site name, a noindex directive on every page and an X-Robots-Tag header set in netlify.toml. That keeps the site out of search indexes; it does not control access. The site name travels in browser history and in the referrer of any outbound link a reader clicks, so the URL should be shared with nobody until the founder chooses a control: Netlify's own password protection or identity service, or a private host.
