1The alias model

A real value crosses five boundaries between the user's hands and an institution's, and no person on our side stands inside any of them. This section follows a value along that path, boundary by boundary, and states what may exist on each side. Figure 3.1 draws the zones; section 2 follows one placement; sections 3 and 4 specify the two boundaries most likely to leak. The named paths by which a person could see a value, and their closure, are gathered in the threat model. The customer's one-page version is the trust story.

1.1Alias types, classes and their policy

An alias is a typed name, never a disguised value. It is written {{type}} or {{type.qualifier}}, with an optional partial suffix, .last4 or .masked, that only the vault appends: lower-case letters, digits and hyphens in each segment; the type names the kind of value, the qualifier tells instances apart ({{card.primary}}, {{account.hdfc}}, {{address.home}}) and the suffix names a vault-issued partial ({{card.primary.last4}}, {{aadhaar.last4}}, {{doc.aadhaar.masked}}). An alias is unique to one user and stable across tasks, so the same string on two users' task cards names two values and carries no character of either. The vault maps it to the current version of the value: a reissued card becomes a new version behind the same alias, and the ledger records that as custody, not as a use. The vault is a bought tokenisation service of the Skyflow, VGS and Basis Theory class, hosted in the region the user's market requires; DR-008 fixes buy over build and the vendor is open in 07, so nothing here asserts what any provider offers to-verify. Every alias belongs to one class, and the class is its whole policy: which surfaces may receive it, which partial forms the maskers must expect and cover, and whether an echo from the third party is normal. The flag is a separate lookup: the policy layer is keyed by jurisdiction and act type and answers delegated, bounce or prohibited pending with a condition, and a missing row evaluates to bounce (section 6); the class and the surface are checked by the substitutor after the flag, and an alias whose class does not admit the surface is refused, the step closing refused-policy with nothing placed. The credential class has one boundary. A login to a bank, a payment app or any account that can move money is never an alias: it sits in the never-ours row of Table 3.1 and the step bounces. A login to a non-financial portal, a utility, a telecom, an insurer's claims desk, a records service, that the user has expressly delegated in the envelope is the credential class, admitted to the managed browser only and only where the jurisdiction's line "Use a delegated login to a non-financial portal" in Table 3.5 reads delegated; it is placed by the substitutor like any other value, never echoed and never displayed.

Partial forms are masked as strictly as full ones, because a last-four beside a card that names "Client 4471" is enough to join a value to a person. Where a step must tell two instruments apart, the vault issues a partial under its own alias, {{card.primary.last4}}, and nothing on our side ever cuts a value. Aadhaar is the strict case and has one rule everywhere on this page: {{aadhaar}} may exist in the vault as received, as a number or as a document, and is never placed in full on any surface; the only placeable form is the vault-issued partial {{aadhaar.last4}}, which is what UIDAI's requirement that an organisation processing Aadhaar display no more than its last four digits allows verified [1], and an Aadhaar document is placeable only where the vault can issue it masked to the same rule, otherwise the presentation bounces to the user's device. The never-ours class is a refusal, not a gap: the RBI cautions the public against sharing logins, KYC copies, card details, PINs, passwords or OTPs with any person or agency verified [2], which is the line the credential class stops at, and those acts bounce to the user's device as the passports section specifies. Beside the value, the vault record carries the alias's policy fields: type, class, permitted surfaces, the identity-act type its use implies, the residency region, how and when it entered, its version, and whether the user confirmed it. The policy layer and the envelope decide a use at runtime; the substitutor enforces that decision at the moment of use. The health and legal class is the one class whose values are words rather than numbers, and it follows the same rule: a condition, a medication, a result or a legal matter enters by the device form's health fields, by a health-record rail or by the dictionary detector the proxy and the hash service's screen both run (1.2, 1.3), is vaulted at the proxy, or by the hash service where the text was written on our side (1.3), and is never in clear on a card, in the context store, in a request's facts_in_clear or on a call caption; a booking carries the speciality or the department, a non-sensitive task field the catalogue entry names, never the diagnosis. The personal-fact class covers every natural person in the context store, not only the user: a people node's name, date of birth, identifiers, contact details and address are aliases of the same class qualified by the person reference ({{name.nominee}}, {{dob.nominee}}, {{phone.mother}}), their values are vaulted at the proxy like the user's, and the hash service holds their digests in both digest sets, so digest masks, the transcriber's match and the redactor catch them anywhere on any surface; the substitutor types them into a form or a card and never onto a call, the need-to-know filter in 04 admits no person's name, and the relationship word, daughter or nominee, is the non-sensitive task field the catalogue entry may name (Table 3.6, path 26).

The harness codes against one vault interface of its own, so the vendor sits behind it (DR-008). Five operations: tokenise (class, value, region, policy fields), returning an alias, held by the vault's edge proxy and, for the third door of 1.2, by the hash service; detokenise (alias, placement id), returning the current version, held by the substitutor alone; digest, returning the two keyed digest sets of 1.2 to the hash service; issue-partial (alias, form), returning .last4 or .masked under the parent alias, the masked form of a document being the vault's own rendering; and an event feed of custody events (alias, version, event kind, time, never a value), which the hash service subscribes to and turns into the ledger's custody lines. Delivering a message to the user's channel is neither a sixth operation nor a detokenise right: it is a second, address-only role, part of the proxy contract, held by the channel connector at the vault's edge proxy, which resolves the address alias ({{phone}}, a WhatsApp or iMessage handle) only to hand the message to the channel provider and returns nothing to any process of ours, so an acknowledgement or a nudge reaches a real number that no component of ours holds, and a value is detokenised into a surface by the substitutor alone. Credentials are issued per operation, per class and per region, to a workload identity and never to a person. The type of an alias is the declared schema field at the adapter door or the detector's class at the other two; the qualifier is chosen at issue from a non-sensitive attribute by a rule per class (the issuer's short name for an account, primary then secondary in order of entry for a card, home or work for an address, the document type for a document), a collision takes a numeric suffix ({{account.hdfc-2}}), and the user sees the label on their device and may rename it, which is a custody event. Nothing here asserts that a named provider offers these operations under these names to-verify.

1.2Boundary one: the three doors a real value enters by

Every real value enters by one of three doors, and at each door the vault, not a process of ours, is what receives the bytes. The first is the user's own input. The channel webhook and the device form terminate at the vault's edge proxy, a tokenising proxy the provider runs in the market's region under our detector policy, so the message body, every attachment and the audio of a voice note are the proxy's bytes and never the intake gateway's. The proxy tokenises under the four detectors 02 specifies: identifier patterns, the amount pattern of the financial-fact class among them, so a balance or a fee in a rail response or an inbound message is tokenised as it arrives, known-value digests, the user's own declaration, and a dictionary-based health and legal term detector, per market and language, that tokenises a free-text mention of a condition, a medication, a result or a legal matter in a message or a transcript into the health and legal class of Table 3.1. The same four run in the hash service's screening call (1.3), and the dictionary detector's policy is one artefact shipped to both; how completely a dictionary catches free text is to-verify, and the canary test in section 7 plants such mentions to measure it. The proxy transcribes a voice note inside the same boundary so that only the alias transcript, a document alias for the audio and the time spans of the detected values leave it (the evidence pipeline receives the transcript and the spans, never the audio), and hands the gateway the message with aliases in place of values and document aliases such as {{doc.pan-card}} in place of uploads. The gateway parses a tokenised message and holds no raw byte at any moment. That the chosen vault can run this proxy in region with our detectors loaded is a requirement in the vendor evaluation open in 07, and nothing here asserts that a named provider does to-verify. For values we know we need we do not ask in chat: the user's device shows a form whose fields post straight to the vault, and we receive the alias. Closure: no raw body reaches a log or a queue of ours, because none reaches a process of ours; the model runtime carries a second, separately deployed detector that refuses to plan on a candidate value and returns the message for re-tokenisation; the task card is generated from the tokenised request and screened again before it reaches a console; and the user's channel history stays on their device and the channel provider's servers, which are not ours.

The second door is a passport adapter. A rail returns real values by design, so the rail's response terminates at the same vault edge proxy, deployed in front of the adapter, which tokenises it against a declared schema in which sensitivity is stated per field and never inferred; an undeclared field is sensitive until it is classified. The adapter reaches the rail only through the proxy, so no response path exists that the proxy does not terminate, and the rail token the adapter presents on each call lives in the vault and is injected into the call by the proxy. What the adapter receives is the tokenised payload, aliases plus non-sensitive attributes (document type, issuer, dates, whether the name matched), and never a raw byte. Closure: no payload log, because the adapter never has a payload to log; network egress from the adapter to the proxy alone; tokenise rights at the proxy and never detokenise rights anywhere on this path; and a schema reviewed with the market's legal pack so a new rail cannot leak by omission.

The third door is a third-party echo: a confirmation page repeating a card number, a voice system reading an account number back, a reply quoting it. It opens on the surfaces and is closed on two of them, by maskers that run before any frame or message reaches a person. On a call it is not closed: there is no masker on that surface (3.2), so a reference a desk reads out is heard by the worker and typed into the result, where it is tokenised on arrival, and the exposure is an open item rather than a closure this section may claim; the masks themselves are specified in 3.1 and 3.2. A value we already hold is recognised by keyed digest and replaced with its alias; a new one, such as an account number the institution issues, is tokenised into a new alias by the hash service, which holds the tokenise credential for this door, with a ledger line. Recognition never compares against the value itself, and no masker holds anything a value could be recovered from: the vault emits, through the hash service, a keyed digest of each value in every normalised form Table 3.1 names; the key is held in the hash service alone, is never issued to a masker and rotates with the digests; a masker holds the digest set and obtains the digest of each candidate window it scans from the hash service over a batched digest call, windows in and digests out and never a match or a value, rate-limited to a frame's or a segment's volume, and matches locally. Short values such as a date of birth are weak against guessing, which is why the key stays where it is, why the maskers also mask by coordinate and by field type, why the digest set never leaves the real-value zone, and why the hash service holds no detokenise right.

1.3What exists on each side

Boundary two is the edge of the alias zone, the widest boundary in the design: the intake gateway and the passport adapters behind the vault's edge proxy, the planner and its model runtime, the classifier, the policy flags, the worker console, the task cards, the context store, the logs, the access ledger, the QA and support tools and the evidence store all live inside it and hold aliases and vault-issued partials only. The vault's client library exists in exactly two places of ours, the substitutor and the hash service; it is not installed in any of these, none holds a credential the vault would accept, and network policy denies them the vault's endpoints and the substitutor's inbound queue. A component in the alias zone that must screen text it holds, the task service over result fields and card payloads, the model runtime's second detector over its input and output, the logging pipeline over a line before it is written, never holds a digest set and never computes a match: it submits the text to the hash service's screening call, which runs the same four detectors the proxy runs (identifier pattern, known-value digest, the user's declaration where a field is declared sensitive, and the dictionary-based health and legal term detector of 1.2, from the one policy artefact shipped to both), tokenises any match through its tokenise-only credential, and answers clean, or found with the text returned with aliases in place, so the caller receives the alias and never the run. The screen's identifier-pattern detector carries the date pattern and the amount pattern beside the identifier patterns of Table 3.1, so a date of birth or a fee that has no digest yet is caught by its form. Every free-text field written on the operator side or through the device surface goes through that call before it is stored: a worker's variance note or failed-final line, a question's answer of any kind (a choice, a date or a free line), an onboarding answer, a fact a closed step writes, a connector pull, each submitted by the task service, on its own behalf or for a field the console sends it, and the task service and the console store only what the call returns, so a condition a worker types or a user answers reaches the context store, the step record and the outcome report as an alias (02, 9.3). The call is rate-limited per caller, and a found answer on text a person on our side wrote, a card payload or a log line opens an incident, so the service cannot be worked as an oracle (Table 3.6, path 24); a found answer on the user's own answer or a connector pull writes a "received" line, as the first door does. Across that boundary lies the small region where a value may exist: the vault, at rest and regionally hosted, and its edge proxy, for the bytes of one message or payload until they are tokenised; the substitutor, in memory for one placement; a surface, transiently, in a field, on a call leg or in the relay's memory-only outbound store; the evidence pipeline, in memory until redaction is verified; and the hash service, in memory while it screens a text. The accent outline in Figure 3.1 is that region's edge, and the entry boundary at the top of the figure is the vault's own proxy: between them they are everywhere a real value exists on our side. Only dots, tones, aliases and redacted evidence cross the outline outward, and one more thing: the ledger line the substitutor writes for every use, which the user reads on their own device.

The topology that carries these boundaries is two accounts per market region and one floor. The alias-zone account holds the intake gateway and the adapters behind their proxies, the planner runtime, the task service with its step runner, the policy layer, the context store, the ledger, the logs and the evidence store; the real-value-zone account holds the substitutor, the three surface controllers deployed with their surfaces (02, 4.5), the surfaces, the evidence pipeline and the hash service, with the vault's regional instance and its edge proxy beside it on the provider's side. Between the two, network policy admits four flows and no other: the step runner's instructions to the three controllers (a step's signed context, a placement tap, the worker's remote input) and the screening call to the hash service inward; the controllers' returns (the compositor's encoded stream, the panel and composer state, the receipts and the surface's signal) and the ledger writer's lines with the masked artefacts outward. No host in the real-value zone allows interactive access in production (2.3). The vault's endpoints are reachable from the real-value-zone account only; the internet, the carrier and the sandbox are reachable from the surfaces only. The floor in Bengaluru is a third network with no account of its own: a console is a client of each region's alias zone over a private link, and the stream it renders arrives from each region's stream gateway, an egress point that emits the compositor's output and nothing else, so the floor reaches three real-value zones only as a viewer of dots (section 9).

1.4What each ledger line has to be able to say

"Placed by the system; no person saw it" is not a promise about culture. It is a property of the component that placed the value, of the stream the worker watched and of the pipeline that kept the evidence, and every claim that recurs in the ledger maps to the property that makes it true. "Placed by the system" holds because exactly one non-human component can detokenise, and every placement it makes is bound to a step, a card and a policy check, so a line is never written for a placement without a step. "No person saw it" holds because a worker reaches every surface through a rendered stream that the masker clears before delivery, raw frames exist only inside the accent boundary of Figure 3.1, and the console has no channel that carries a raw frame. "No value was on the call" holds because no placement exists on that surface and no script line carries a value (DR-021), and an exposure line is written only from an incident record and never from a worker's attestation. "In our browser" and "on our phone line" hold because the site controls in 04 keep a worker's own tools out of the room. "Masked before any person saw it" holds because the store accepts an artefact only with the verifier's signature over its hash (section 4). A line the system cannot make true by construction is a line the system is not allowed to write.
