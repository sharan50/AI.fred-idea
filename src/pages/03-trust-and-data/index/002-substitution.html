2The substitution component

One component holds the right to turn an alias back into a value, and it does one thing with that right: it places the value into a surface we run, writes the line, and tells nobody, its caller included, what the value was. Boundary three is that component's edge. Aliases, target descriptors and a signed step context go in; receipts and ledger lines come out.

2.1Boundary three: the interface and the checks

The substitutor accepts one message, a placement request, over one authenticated queue that only two surface controllers can write to: the browser controller and the mail relay, each acting for a step and never for a person. The telephony controller is not among them and never places a value, because nothing from the vault goes onto a call (DR-021, 3.2); its own privilege is narrower, the navigation keys of a step’s dial map, which are no value of the user’s. The step runner inside the task service instructs a controller for an ai step; for a human step the worker's tap on "place {{pan}}" goes from the console to the task service, which hands it to the step's surface controller; the console itself never calls the substitutor, and the controllers, deployed with their surfaces in the real-value zone and instructed by the task service's step runner, are its only writers. A worker can trigger a placement and can never receive one. The request carries the task id, the step id, the alias, the surface and a target descriptor typed per surface: for the browser, the session, the frame, the field's accessibility node and declared kind (text, password, upload), and the page origin; for mail, the message id and the placeholder occurrences to rewrite. A delegated debit under the cap is a placement like any other: the alias is the payment instrument, the substitutor obtains from the passport adapter a token bound to this task, its cap and the named merchant, or a payment instruction under a mandate the user set up, and places it on the surface, so the checks below apply to it; and it is a committing placement, because the institution may execute it on receipt with no submit, click, sentence or key to follow. A placement whose alias is a payment instrument or a payment instruction, or whose catalogue entry marks it as consumed on placement, is a committing action in its own right: it needs the approval token like a submit, and the substitutor writes the step's fired marker before the first character of it is delivered, so the controller's committing-action check has nothing left to guard after it; the rail's own binding of the token to the task and its cap is a second layer, chipped in Table 3.5, never the closure (section 5). With it travels a signed step context issued by the task service when the step entered running. Its fields are fixed: the task id and step id; the step record's executor, surface, party, act type and field list, the aliases the step carries, which is what a task card renders; the envelope version with its lines, and the device-signed envelope confirmation for that version, the signature the user's device produced when it confirmed the envelope or its last extension; the policy lookup, naming the act type and both jurisdictions it used, the delegator's (the user's, the market on the request, for the user's own aliases; the one her delegation record carries for a dependant's) and, for an act toward an institution, the institution's market, with the flag and the condition's result under each, the stricter governing (section 6); for a dependant's alias her delegation record and its validity end; where the act moves money, its amount and the remaining cap, the task's cap less the committed-spend total the task service keeps, fired acts plus the amounts outstanding approval tokens reserve; for an irreversible step the dual-control approval token that the worker console page specifies, bound to the check id and the envelope version, otherwise null; issued-at and expires-at; a nonce; and the id of the signing key. The encoding is canonical JSON and the signature is Ed25519 under the task service's key for the market region, one key per region held in the alias zone's secret store; the substitutor holds the public keys, the current and the previous, in its signed build configuration, changed under the two-person rule of 2.3, so distribution needs no channel to the task service, and the key rotates on a fixed schedule (illustrative: quarterly) and on any suspicion, with a two-key overlap. The substitutor verifies the signature and checks the context itself; it has no route to the task service, the envelope store or the policy layer and needs none. The context is short-lived by construction: its validity is no longer than the placement window (illustrative: two minutes for a browser field or a message, and the same for a message; a legal pack may shorten either and never lengthen it), it names the envelope version it was issued against, for a step naming a dependant's aliases it expires no later than her delegation's validity end, and it dies with any edit to the envelope. An extension or a narrowing voids every context and every approval token the task has issued, and so does the revocation or expiry of a dependant's delegation for every step that names her aliases: the task service returns every running step to ready with its context voided, so no step stays running across an edit; a step that held a token re-enters checking and needs a fresh token before it can fire; a step already in checking is re-checked against the new version; and a fresh context is issued only against the new version, so a controller must obtain one before it can request a placement at all (dual control in 02; the console shows the same sequence). A step that names a dependant's aliases is valid for the earlier of the envelope's window and her delegation's validity, an extension of the window does not extend past her delegation, and a step left without her authority re-classifies as a user step for her (01, 1.5). The substitutor for its part refuses a context whose envelope version differs from the version named in the dual-control token and, for a reversible step, one whose version differs from the envelope version stamp, a second signed statement of the task id, the envelope version and its time under the same key, which the controller fetches from the task service at request time, dated inside the window, and sends with the request. A placement is therefore never checked against a superseded envelope.

Every check must pass, in order, and the first failure returns a receipt of refused naming the check and nothing else, with a "refused" line written; the controller closes the step and the task service records refused-policy with the check as the policy reference, whichever check failed, and a worker chooses nothing. The context signature is valid and inside its short validity. The step is running and the caller is the controller of its executor's surface. The alias is in the step record's field list carried by the context, the list a task card renders for a human step and an ai step carries with no card, so the list is the allow-list. Every delegation record the alias's owner references is live: for a dependant's alias the context names her record and its validity end, and her revocation voids the context as an envelope edit does. The alias's class admits the surface and the flag for the act, looked up under each jurisdiction the context names, reads delegated under both, the stricter result governing (section 6). The envelope admits the act, its validity window is open, an act that moves money is within the remaining cap the context carries, so two acts each under the cap cannot together exceed it, and the target belongs to a contactable party: the browser session's origin is on that party's declared domains and presents its certificate; the far leg is a number we dialled from the party's entry in the market's party register (section 3), so an inbound call never receives a placement; the recipient is on the party's mail domain in the same entry. The envelope version the context names carries the user's device signature, so a task whose envelope the user never confirmed on their own device, withdrew, or let expire has no placement at all: revocation and expiry void the context as an edit does, and no credential of ours can detokenise without it. The step is reversible or the approval token is present, current, names this envelope version and is unconsumed, so dual control is enforced at the substitutor and not only at the console: a token is single-use, the controller consumes it before it issues the committing action, a consumed token is refused, and the token reserves its act's amount against the cap from issue until the step closes without a fired marker; a committing placement is refused without the token whatever the step's classification, and for it the substitutor itself consumes the token and writes the fired marker before the first tone or character leaves it, so a step whose placement reached the surface holds the marker whatever the surface then reports. The target is plausible for the type: a card number goes only into a field whose declared kind or label matches. And the placement is the first for this step, alias and target, or carries the retry token the runner issues for any re-placement of the same step, alias and target, whatever the first receipt was, so a second format after a rejected submit and a second attempt after a failure pass the same gate. A placement window bounds the whole exchange (illustrative: two minutes), after which the request expires unplaced and the line closes as "not confirmed".

Only then does it detokenise, with a credential scoped to the alias's class and the task's market region, render the format the descriptor asks for, inject directly through the surface's privileged driver, zero its memory and return a receipt: a placement id and one of placed, not-confirmed, refused or failed, with the failed check or the reason class. The receipt returns to the controller, which closes or continues the step while the task service records the outcome, and the mapping is one way: refused closes the step refused-policy; not-confirmed and failed close it failed-retryable with a retry token, then failed-final at the step's cap, except on a committing placement, whose fired marker already stands: there not-confirmed closes the step failed-final with the marker as its evidence, no retry token is issued, the reservation is not released, and the re-plan issues a status enquiry before any repeat (Table 3.2); placed leaves the outcome to the surface's signal and the runner. Every controller writes a fired marker before it issues a committing action, and the substitutor writes it before a committing placement's first tone or character; the closed list of committing actions reads the same on every page: a form submit, a committing click, a committing sentence on a call, a keypad press outside the navigation map (3.2), a relay release, a committing placement. A step with a fired marker never closes failed-retryable: an unconfirmed fire, a submit that times out, a call that drops after the committing sentence or a committing placement the surface's signal did not confirm, closes failed-final with the marker as its evidence, and the re-plan issues a verification step, a status enquiry, before any repeat, so an irreversible act fires at most once (02). A narrowing that arrives inside a committing placement's window does not stop it, because the marker is already written, and the outcome report says so. It never returns the value, its length, a partial, a digest or a rendering of it, and it has no operation that reads a field, a leg or a message back. A caller that wants to know whether the field is filled learns it from the surface's own signal.

2.2The placement and the line are one transaction

The line is written ahead of the injection, as "placing", and confirmed as "placed" by the surface's own signal: the browser driver's input-event sequence and the field's validity state, with the driver's read-back of field values disabled; the relay's delivery record. The "no person saw it" clause of a placed line is scoped to that confirmation: it covers the frames and segments up to the surface's signal, from the compositor's mask record and the stream record, and a later unmasked exposure of the same field or leg writes its own incident line rather than rewriting this one (8.2). If confirmation does not arrive inside the window, the line closes as "not confirmed", the surface clears what it can (the field is cleared, the message is dropped from the relay's outbound store) and the step closes failed-retryable with a retry token, or, where the placement was a committing one and its first tone or character had left the substitutor, failed-final with the fired marker the substitutor wrote (2.1). The ledger is append-only: a line is never edited, only followed. A substitutor that cannot write the ledger cannot place, because the "placing" write precedes the vault call; that ordering is what makes the ledger complete rather than usually complete, and it closes the gap a crash between injection and write would otherwise leave.

2.3Isolation, and the two halves

The substitutor runs in its own account and its own hardened runtime per market region: a separate network segment, memory never swapped, no persistent volume, no core dumps, no interactive access in production. Its network position is the design: one inbound queue from the two controllers that may write to it, and outbound paths only to the vault's detokenise endpoint for its region, the browser driver's control socket, the relay's injection endpoint into its outbound store, and the ledger writer. No route exists between it and the model runtime, the console, the context store or the logging pipeline. Its logs are structured and alias-only; the value is never a log argument, and the shared logging library used elsewhere is not linked, so a careless call cannot reach a logger that does not exist. The one vault credential that permits detokenisation is issued to its workload identity, short-lived and scoped per class and region; the only other vault credentials of ours are the hash service's, a digest credential and a tokenise-only credential for the third door and never detokenise, and the gateway, the adapters, the model runtime, the console and the pipeline hold none. Deployment is by signed build under a two-person rule, one approver from outside the owning team, recorded as a change; its operators see counts and reason codes. The rule of no interactive access is the whole zone's, not the substitutor's alone: no host in the real-value zone, the proxy client, the substitutor, the surface controllers, the browser sandbox, the relay, the evidence pipeline and the hash service, offers a shell, a debugger, a packet capture or a driver socket to a person in production, and each is deployed by the same signed build under the same two-person rule. Break-glass access to any of them needs two named approvers, first drains the host of live sessions and zeroes its memory, is time-boxed, and writes an incident and a break-glass ledger event on every task that had a session on the host, so whatever was in that memory is gone before a person arrives and the user reads that someone was there (Table 3.6, path 25).

Two halves make the design fail safely. The substitutor cannot open a browser session, dial a number or address a message; it only fills a target that a surface controller created under a step, so a compromised substitutor has nowhere to send a value except where the plan already sends it. Addressing is the other role and is address-only: the channel connector at the vault's edge proxy resolves the address alias to hand an outbound message to the channel provider, returns nothing to any process of ours and can place nothing into any surface, so neither role can do the other's work (1.1). The surfaces cannot detokenise; a compromised surface holds dots, the digest set without its key, and aliases, and can match what is on its own screen and nothing beyond it (1.2). The model runtime and the console cannot reach the vault, the substitutor's queue, the managed browser's document (they receive a rendered stream), the far leg's audio during a window, or the relay's outbound body after the swap. Unauthorised disclosure of Aadhaar identity information is an offence under section 37 of the Aadhaar Act 2016 verified [3]; the isolation above is what lets us say that no employee is ever in a position to commit it.
