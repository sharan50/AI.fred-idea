8The access ledger

The ledger is the product of the boundaries above, and it is how the trust story becomes visible to the customer: one plain-words line for every use of every alias, readable by the user on their own device. It is written by the component that performed the event, from that component's own record, and never by a person. Its record form is in 02; this section fixes what it holds, how it is worded, how long it lives and what it can never contain.

8.1Fields and event types

Every line carries: the task id and step id; the time, shown to the user in their own time zone; the alias; the event type; the use, in words; the surface; the party; who placed it (the system, the user on their device, or a named component for custody events); who saw it, as a category and never a name; the outcome of the event (placing, placed, not confirmed, refused, masked, withheld); the policy reference and the envelope version; and, for evidence events, the artefact reference and, for openings, the viewer's role. The event types are fixed: received (a value entered the vault by any of the three doors); custody (a new version behind an alias, a partial issued, a value destroyed at the end of retention, a digest or signing key rotated); placed in a browser field or through the relay, never on a call (3.2), with its placing and not-confirmed forms; refused; shown-as-dots, with its checker form for the masked stream or leg a dual-control checker watched; echo-masked, with its held form for a frame the masker held rather than delivered; exposure-recorded; bounced (the user performed the act on their device), with its code-relayed form for a one-time code the driver placed from the user's device under a placement record; evidence-stored, evidence-withheld and evidence-opened; reconciled (the provider's access log matched the ledger); released (a legal request); and break-glass (a person was given emergency access to a real-value host that had handled the task, after it was drained and zeroed; 2.3). A line without a step is possible only for a custody event, which is a received line written at intake before any plan or step exists, a break-glass entry, written per task and not per step, or a key rotation, and for reconciliation and release events; each of those names the component that wrote it, and every other line carries its step. A dual-control check is not an event here, because the check itself uses nothing; the checker's viewing is, written as "A checker saw the masked stream for step 2" in the shown-as-dots form; both identities live on the step record, and the check is shown on the task's timeline and in the outcome report (verification in 02).

8.2Wording rules

Past tense, one sentence per event, and the user is "you". The alias appears as written, {{pan}}, never expanded, never as a partial, a length, a digest or a description that would narrow it ("your 10-character number"). The party is named as the user knows it, "HDFC Bank's account-opening form", not by an internal id. "Placed by the system" is written only for a substitutor placement confirmed by the surface's signal. "No person saw it" on a placed line covers the frames and segments up to the surface's confirmation, and is written only when the compositor's mask record and the stream record agree that none of them reached a person unmasked; a later unmasked exposure of the same field or leg writes its own incident line, so a line is never rewritten and never claims more than its window (2.2). There is no muted-audio line to write, because no value is ever on a call. A worker is "a worker" and a reviewer "a QA reviewer", never a name or a tier, because the ledger is the user's record of their data and not a personnel file. A refusal names what was refused and that nobody answered it. Times are the surface's, not the console's, so a placement is timed by the surface that carried it. Nothing in a line is free text a person typed; every line is assembled from fields, so the wording cannot drift and cannot carry a value by accident.

8.3Retention, reading, and what it never contains

The ledger outlives every artefact it refers to: an artefact is destroyed at the end of its retention and the ledger records that it was, so "no raw copy was kept" can still be checked after the copy is gone. Its own retention is the task's life plus the period the market's legal pack sets, and the user may export it at any time; the periods are the legal pack's to settle to-verify. The user reads it per task, alongside the outcome report, on their bound device, and may subscribe to it as it is written; a line is never shown in the chat thread itself, which receives only the notification that something is waiting. It is append-only, replicated within the market's region, and readable by the user, by the task service that assembles the outcome report, by QA for a task under review, and by nobody else without a legal request that itself writes a line. Its rendered lines never contain: a real value; a partial, a length, a digest or a rendering of one; a screenshot or a transcript; a worker's name; the user's name (the user is "you"); an internal identifier the user could not act on; or a line a person wrote by hand. The record form behind each line, which the export carries beside the rendered lines (02, 7.3), holds the identifiers an audit needs, the ledger id, the placement id, the policy reference, the envelope version and the component that wrote it, and nothing else from that list: no value, no partial or digest, no artefact, no name and no free text.
