2The context profile

The general assistants start every conversation from nothing. The user restates their preferences, their constraints and their circumstances each time, and the quality of the answer is capped by how much they can be bothered to type. A personal assistant who has worked for someone for two years does not have that problem, and neither does a butler or a consigliere: when they are told to find somewhere for dinner tonight, they already know the part of town, the diary, what the last four dinners were, who is coming and what the person will not eat.

AI.fred builds that profile deliberately, from two streams. The first is derived: mail, statements, confirmations and the residue of tasks already executed. The second is stated: what the user tells us directly, and what they confirm when we propose it. Every fact carries its provenance and a confidence (DR-016), so a derived inference is never treated as a stated preference, and the user can see and correct anything held about them through the same ledger that shows what was accessed and when.

Two limits, stated plainly because they are easy to overclaim. The profile takes months of ordinary use to become worth anything, which is the practical reason the everyday tasks cannot be deferred. And predicting how preferences change over time, through behavioural archetypes or clustering, is future product rather than a founding claim; it needs a user base large enough for stable segments, which is thousands rather than hundreds.
