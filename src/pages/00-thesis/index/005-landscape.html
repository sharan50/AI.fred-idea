5The competitive landscape

Every player in the field executes either through chat plus people who see everything, or through a model inside the user's own browser and accounts. None has an execution surface of its own, a published masking policy for its staff, or a bounded grant of authority, and the sensitive wedge is either refused or advertised with no safeguard behind it. Table 0.2 lists the players with each claim's status, and DR-013 records what "verified" means in this edition.

5.1India: Faff and its peers

The Indian field is a year or two old and already crowded, and read down Table 0.2 it has one shape: a human assistant on a chat thread, paid by the month or the hour, executing on the phone with whatever the customer has sent. The incumbents have moved too, Swiggy with Crew, Google with table bookings inside AI Mode, Jio with a call agent, NPCI with agentic UPI pilots, and all of it is low-value delegated commerce, not identity work.

Two facts define the field for us. First, the sensitive wedge is advertised without a trust architecture: Faff lists government and banking tasks, Crew lists Aadhaar and document updates, Hulp lists payments, healthcare and government documentation, and no vendor in the set publishes any limit on handling PAN, Aadhaar, bank credentials or one-time passcodes, nor any masking or need-to-know policy for its staff verified [20] [21] [22]. Second, the only published scope exclusion in the field is Faff's exclusion of business work verified [23]. The customer is being invited to hand over exactly the values the regulator tells them never to share.

5.2The US and the UK: a thinning field and a retreating one

Across the Atlantic the field is thinning rather than crowding: Table 0.2 records Panasonic closing Yohana, and Milo is reported to have shut in 2026 (to-verify in Table 0.2). Duckbill survives as the main consumer hybrid, executing with the customer's card stored in the app and a rule that it cannot impersonate the user; Fancy Hands is the one service in either field whose staff never see the card number, for capped payments the user approves one by one; Magic and Time etc work inside the client's own accounts, and Time etc's terms make the client entirely responsible for safeguarding their logins. In the UK we found no hybrid of AI and human operators launched in 2025 or 2026 to-verify: the entrants are WhatsApp assistants without human operators, human virtual-assistant firms, and the incumbents Ten Lifestyle Group and Quintessentially adding AI to human lifestyle managers.

The labs' consumer agents are a separate column and have retreated; section 3.1 and Table 0.2 record the closures. New entrants such as Instinct, Pally and Hark are raising very large rounds to act inside the user's email, screen and accounts, and Instinct's first weeks brought reports of plain-text email storage and a perpetual training licence over user data (Table 0.2). The surface is the user's own; the values pass through the vendor.

5.3What none of them can safely do

Set the two fields side by side and two postures appear. The first is refusal: the human services bar logins, account creation and medical, financial or legal work, or refer it out; the labs' agents refuse bank transfers, advise against financial, legal and medical sites, and hand logins and final purchases back to the user. The second is exposure: a card held in the app and spent up to a cap; logins handed to a named assistant through a password manager; a WhatsApp thread in which a pod of assistants reads whatever the customer sends, Aadhaar included. Only Fancy Hands keeps the payment value from its staff, and only for small purchases with per-payment approval. None describes a surface of its own, evidence redaction, a per-task grant or a ledger.

The regulators have drawn the line for them. In banking, the RBI's liability framework leaves the full loss with a customer whose own negligence, such as sharing payment credentials, caused it verified [63], and the RBI has told the public not to share logins, KYC documents, card details, PINs or OTPs with any unidentified person or agency verified [64].

In identity, UIDAI tells Aadhaar holders never to share an Aadhaar OTP, to use masked Aadhaar, and reminds organisations to display only the last four digits verified [65]; the Aadhaar Act makes unauthorised disclosure of identity information an offence punishable by imprisonment verified [66]. In the UK the business answers for its agent, and in the US a standing authorisation to an agent has no home yet in Regulation E (Table 0.1). A service whose model is "send us your details and we will sort it" is asking the customer to be negligent in the regulator's own terms, and accumulating the liability itself.
