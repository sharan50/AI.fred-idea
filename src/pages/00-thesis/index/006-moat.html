6The moat

The flat-fee players cannot follow us up the consequence axis because the property that makes their model work, a generalist who sees everything and can therefore do anything, is the property that makes the wedge unsafe. The trust architecture is not a feature they can add. It is a different company, with a different promise, floor and liability posture, visible to the user in every ledger line.

6.1Why the flat-fee model stops at the line

Unlimited tasks for a flat fee is only possible when any assistant can pick up any task with whatever the customer has sent, in a chat thread, on the assistant's own phone, using the customer's own logins. That is a leak path by design and a path to an unwanted act by design: there is no envelope because there is no boundary to draw one on. A vault bolted on afterwards changes nothing, because a person has read the value before it could be tokenised. Closing the paths means owning every execution surface, running the floor in an office under site controls, redacting every screenshot and recording before storage, and replacing "we do anything" with "we do these acts, inside this envelope, and we bounce the rest to your phone". Each cuts against the model that made them cheap, and the regulators' lines turn their exposure posture into a liability.

6.2Why the labs' agents stop there too

Models the labs have; hands they do not. Their agents act inside the user's own browser and accounts, so every sensitive value passes through the model and the vendor, and a hostile page can steer the agent; their own measurements of that are why they advise against sensitive sites (Table 0.1). They have no telephony, no counter, no human for the step that needs one, and no grant of authority beyond a confirmation prompt. Their consumer surfaces have churned within months, no foundation for a service answerable for a filing or a transfer. We buy their models and put them inside a harness they have not built.

6.3What the moat is made of

Eight structural properties, each closing a path a rival leaves open, each specified for an engineer in 02 and 03:

  1. Aliases everywhere: humans, the AI and almost all of our software see {{pan}}, never the value, which lives in a bought vault (the alias model).
  2. Substitution at the last boundary by one small isolated component, into a browser we run or a mail relay we operate, and never onto a call (DR-021) (the substitution component).
  3. Execution surfaces that are ours; a worker never uses their own phone, browser or email.
  4. Evidence redaction in the harness: no raw screenshot, recording or transcript is ever human-viewable, because the evidence system is itself a leak path (DR-009).
  5. An authority envelope per task and the point-of-no-return test on every step: irreversible and outside the envelope bounces to the user's device; irreversible and inside it runs under dual control (DR-004).
  6. A task state machine in which user abandonment is legitimate and system forgetting is forbidden as an architectural invariant, not a procedure (DR-005).
  7. An office-mandatory 24/7 floor whose site controls are part of the guarantee, with need-to-know task cards naming a pseudonymised client and only the fields the step needs (DR-006).
  8. A plain-words access ledger, per task, of every use of every alias and who saw what (the ledger specification).

Copying one is easy. Copying all eight is building us, and it cannot be done on a customer base and an operations culture assembled on the premise that the assistant sees everything.

6.4What the moat is not

It is not the model: we buy models, and the labs will keep improving them for us. It is not the channel: the front door is a per-market launch choice, amber in DR-001, above a channel-agnostic gateway. It is not price: the economics are modelled in 05 on priors the measurement protocol replaces, and this thesis carries no number of ours because the argument needs none (05; DR-015 is the deferral that was withdrawn to get there). And it is not a claim that an incumbent could never build it; Swiggy, Jio or a bank could in principle, but every incumbent move in Table 0.2 runs the other way, toward low-value commerce inside their own apps, with no human floor under site controls and no liability posture for identity acts. If one publishes a masking architecture, surfaces of its own and a per-task envelope, this thesis narrows, and we will say so in 07.
