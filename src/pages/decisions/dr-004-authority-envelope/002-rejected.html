2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

A standing mandate at account level
Set once, applied to every task. Authority stops belonging to a task: a mis-planned or hijacked task inherits everything the user ever granted, and the confirming tap becomes ceremony because it no longer changes anything.
A spend cap alone
The human-assisted assistants stop here. Duckbill executes purchases up to $2,500 on a card stored in its app verified [1]; Fancy Hands caps payments at $200 per request behind a per-payment approval button verified [2]. Money is one field of five. An application submitted, terms accepted, a call placed to a party the user never named, an identity number stated: none has a price, and a cap sees none of them.
Confirm every step
Delegation collapses into supervised execution: the user becomes the bottleneck on the tasks they delegated to escape, and fatigue makes each tap meaningless, so the safety it claims disappears with use. The envelope asks once, properly, then acts.
Reversibility judged by the executor at the moment of acting
A judgement made under time pressure by the party with the least context. The test is asked at planning, recorded on the step, and re-asked on every edit; the executor inherits the answer.
