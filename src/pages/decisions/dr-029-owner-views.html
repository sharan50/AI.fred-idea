DR-029A generated front door and one generated page per owner

A person joining reads what they own and nothing else. The publication gains a front door of at most fifteen hundred words and one page per owner, both generated by the build from the ownership file, the manifest, the dependency map and blocks marked in the canonical fragments. Ownership is set at section level, so a page can split between two owners without a word being written twice; a view states a fact only by transcluding the block that states it, never by restating it.

On this page

1Decision

The publication has a generated front door and one generated page per owner. The front door lists the decisions that matter as the ledger's own titles, the counts of where the record stands, the numbers that matter as transcluded blocks, the open items that matter with their triggers, five doors and the full contents; an owner's page lists what they own, what binds them, a reading path with its running word count and the key blocks of their sections. Ownership is set at section level in roles.json, the one source of it: a role owns pages and sections, a page's owner owns every section another role does not list, a decision follows its layer unless the file names its owner, and a decision record's page follows its decision. A fact reaches a view only one way: a block in a canonical fragment carries data-key, and the build transcludes it verbatim, its links rebased to the view's directory and its ids dropped. Nothing in a view is written by hand except one capped frame, and the harness fails a view whose transclusion differs from its source by a character.

1.1Why views, and not copies

Two reasons, one about people and one about the record. A newcomer landed on a rail of nine sections, twenty-seven records and a hundred and seventy thousand words, with no way to know which of them were theirs; now they land on a page of fifteen hundred words, then a page naming their sections, decisions and open items, then a reading path. And DR-010 rejected a second representation of any page because two representations drift; a summary or a role page written by hand is a second representation of the design, and would drift the same way. A view generated from the record cannot drift from it, because it is rebuilt from it, and the build's check and the harness's tenth section fail the moment a generated page differs from what the record would produce.

1.2What the build writes

The front door at the root, a doors page, one page per owner and the record door, from four templates under src/templates/; the sections list of every entry in manifest.json, with each section's owner and readers; and .github/CODEOWNERS, one line per owned path under the source directory. The doors page exists because the harness's reachability rule reaches an owner's page through its directory, and the record door exists so that the full review, which leaves the rail, stays one link from every page. The page that was the index keeps its text under contents/, where the "how to read" section now lives; the seven map loci and two links that pointed at its anchors point there too, the anchors themselves unchanged.

1.3What a view may not do

A view may not restate the design in its own words; it names, counts, links and transcludes. A digit on the front door sits inside a transcluded block or a generated count or the harness fails, so no number there was ever typed. A frame is at most two hundred words, and the front door's frame at most a hundred and fifty; the front door as a whole is at most fifteen hundred words with its chrome. A block whose lead carries a verified chip is not marked, because the chip's numbered source lives on its own page and cannot travel; those sections wait for a block that can.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

Hand-written role pages
A page per owner written once by a person, saying what they hold. It reads better on the day it is written and is wrong within a month, because every edit to the record then needs a matching edit to five summaries, and the rule of DR-010 is that a second statement drifts. The cost of rejecting it is a page that reads like a ledger rather than a letter; the frame is where the letter lives.
Page-level ownership
One owner per page, which is simpler to state and to enforce. It fails on 03, which splits between the engineer who owns the alias model, the substitution component, the surfaces, the redaction and the passports, and the counsel who owns the flag table, the threat model, the ledger, the residency map and the privacy-law skeleton. Splitting 03 into two pages to fit the rule moves loci and changes what a reader receives, which the brief forbids; ownership at section level costs nothing but a longer file.
Whole fragments in role pages
Including each owned section in full in the owner's page, so that the page is complete on its own. It rebuilds the largest page of the record under a new name, at twenty thousand words for the engineer, and every figure, table and source list would travel with it. A role page transcludes the block that states each section's decision or mechanism, links the section, and leaves the reading to the reading path.

3What this closes off

  • A prose edit made in a generated page; the next build overwrites it, and the harness fails until then.
  • A change of ownership made anywhere but roles.json; the role pages, the manifest's sections and CODEOWNERS are written from it and from nothing else.
  • A fact stated in a view in the view's own words; a view transcludes a marked block or links the section, and a number typed on the front door fails the harness.
  • A section with no owner or with two, a decision with no owner, or an owner cell in the open items that maps to no role.
  • A key block edited to read better in a view; the block is the page's own sentence, and the page's text does not change for the view's sake.
  • A frame over its cap, a front door over fifteen hundred words, and a component added to the stylesheet for the views' sake; the views use the existing set.

4Status

Fixed decision, taken by the founder on 2026-09-17, in the same run as DR-028, on which it rests: the views are written by the build that assembles the pages, and both are held fresh by the harness. It reopens nothing in the ledger. The ownership file starts from the assignment the brief gave and records every adjustment in VIEWS_REPORT.md, with the full ownership table, every key block with its text, and each role's reading path. The map gains this record as a node justified by DR-010 and DR-028; no locus moved except the seven that followed the index's text to contents/.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title. This publication is a design record, not an offer.
