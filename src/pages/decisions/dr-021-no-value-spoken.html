On this page

1Decision

We do not build synthesised-speech substitution, muted worker audio or a re-voiced transcript. Our people place calls in the ordinary way, and they hear the far party in the ordinary way. No value held in the vault is ever spoken on a call, in either direction. A task that cannot proceed without one either moves to an online channel where the substitution component can place it, or bounces to the user's own device.

The mechanism this replaces is specified out of 02, 4.2 and 03, 3.2, and the scope of phone work is written into 01, 2 and 04, 3.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

The audio masking pipeline
A media server we control, a per-leg server-side mute, an always-on delay line, a streaming transcriber with an allowlist default and a re-voiced rendering in the worker's headset. Its cost was a bespoke real-time media build, a rare specialist hire and a live-call latency budget nobody has shipped; what it bought was the ability to state a value on a call, which this record gives up instead.
Keeping the mute for placements only
A narrower version: no re-voicing, but a muted window while the system speaks or keys a value. Rejected because it keeps the media server, the guard interval and the vendor requirement that made the surface expensive, for the same act this record moves to a form.
Letting a worker read a value aloud under dual control
Rejected outright: it puts a value in a person's hands, which is the first fatal failure, and no procedure closes it.

3What this closes off

  • Phone work beyond non-authenticating calls: bookings, chasing, enquiries, availability and appointment setting. Any institutional interaction that requires voice verification of identity is out of scope on this channel.
  • A placement of any kind on a call, by speech or by keypad, and with it the substitution component's media function.
  • The media server, the delay line, the live transcriber and the re-voicing as components, and the media engineer as a hire.
  • The claim that nobody on our side ever sees or hears anything. A worker on a call will learn facts about the user from the far end. That is accepted, and mitigated by vetting, need-to-know task cards and the access ledger rather than by architecture; the trust story says so in the user's own words.
  • The media-path commitment gate. On a call the closure for the second fatal failure is the approval token and the release of the committing sentence after dual control, which is a narrower closure than a component that cut the words.

4Status

Fixed decision, taken by the founder on 2026-09-13, superseding the telephony provisions of the architecture as published on 2026-09-10. DR-022 is the same revision's answer on the browser surface; DR-004's third default rule is restated on its own page, and DR-006 no longer rests any part of the room's argument on a headset.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title. This publication is a design record, not an offer.
