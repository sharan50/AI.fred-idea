9Market, customer and competition

The record sizes nothing top-down and says so; its one market number is a prior in the growth model, "a reachable Indian market between twenty-five thousand and two hundred and fifty thousand users at the tenth and ninetieth percentiles" (05, 6, revised 2026-09-14). This section builds the number up from what the rivals publish, names the closest comparable that died and why, and sets the record's automation prior beside the two hybrids that ever published theirs. Every external figure here is an excerpt: the market hosts were blocked and the search budget ran out before the income-distribution and survey queries, so the rows that would anchor a top-down number are marked not found rather than filled.

9.1Bottom-up: what the field has actually sold

Faff, the record's Indian benchmark, charges Rs 1,999 a month for unlimited tasks, invite-only, reports "75k+ tasks so far", had 54 employees on 31 May 2026 and has raised $2.59m by one database and is "bootstrapped" by another to-verify [13] to-verify [99] to-verify [100]. If a paying household runs four to eight tasks a month for a year, 75,000 tasks is one to two thousand paying households in total [EST]. Hulp, funded with $2.6m in August 2026, reports 100 families, 3,000 tasks and a 50-person operations team to-verify [3]. M raised Rs 102 crore in April 2026 and has published no price or user count to-verify [101]. Swiggy Crew is Rs 999 for three months to-verify [102]. So the whole visible paid market for a human assistant in Indian cities today is low thousands of households at a quarter to a half of the record's price [EST]: AI.fred asks Rs 4,628 a month, or Rs 5,460 with tax, for 26 tasks, against Faff's Rs 1,999 for as many as the customer likes. The record's planning line needs 273 users in month 1 and about 89,000 by month 60 with marketing (models/summary_growth_viable.json) [EST]. A priced waiting list is not a nicety; it is the only way to learn whether a household pays 2.3 times Faff for a trust property it cannot see.

Abroad, the willingness-to-pay band the record needs exists and is thin. Duckbill charges from $99 a month for a single user and $149 or $169 for a household by different reviews, with about 70 employees and more than 100 copilots and no published customer count to-verify [103]; Fancy Hands sells three, five or fifteen requests a month for $35, $55 and $125 to-verify [104]; Ten Lifestyle, the listed concierge, had 436,000 active members at its half-year to 28 February 2026 and £33.7m of net revenue, about £155 a member a year, paid by banks and card issuers rather than by members to-verify [105]. The record's $105 American price sits inside Duckbill's band and its $85 British one has no comparable at all. If the first British cohort arrives by referral from India, which is the only mechanism the record has for it, London's population of Indian ethnicity was about 613,000 at the 2021 census to-verify [106] and the American population reporting Asian Indian alone was 4.4 million in 2020 to-verify [107]; at one household in a hundred that is six thousand and forty thousand, against gates of 400 and 600 [EST]. The gates are reachable; the record has no channel to reach them except word of mouth from a cohort that does not yet exist.

9.2The comparable that died, and the two that measured themselves

The closest comparable is Yohana: a monthly family-concierge membership with human specialists, a corporate balance sheet, launched in the United States in 2021 and in Japan in 2022, closed in the United States on 30 September 2025 and in Japan on 30 January 2026 after "feedback from thousands of families", with the parent saying the service and its successor "need to be paused and reconsidered from the beginning" to-verify [108]. Panasonic had more money and more patience than any seed fund, sold the record's product minus the trust boundary, and could not find enough families at its price in four years. Milo, the family-admin assistant backed by OpenAI's fund, closed in January 2026 with its founder saying the technology was "simply too early to be reliable in any useful way" to-verify [109]. Facebook M reached about 2,000 users in two and a half years with no more than about 30 per cent of its answers served by the machine, and closed on 19 January 2018 to-verify [1]. Google Duplex, on the single task of booking a table, was confirmed a year after launch to start about a quarter of calls with a person and to need one in about 15 per cent more, and the web-form variant was shut in December 2022 to-verify [2]. The scheduling assistant x.ai was acquired and sunset on 31 October 2021 to-verify [110]. The record's thesis page lists Fin, GoButler and Clara as well; this review could not check them and does not repeat them.

Two things follow that the record does not yet say. The first is that every hybrid with a published number sat at or below the record's launch prior, 42 per cent AI-only, and none reached its planning line at month 12; Figure 8.22 draws it. The second is that the concierges that died did not die of trust: Yohana barred logins and advice and still closed, Milo held nothing sensitive and still closed. They died of frequency and price, which is the record's everyday endgame argument turned against it. The trust boundary is why AI.fred can take the tasks the others refused; it is not evidence that enough households will pay for them.

9.3The field since 11 September

The Indian field added Hulp's round and three more entrants that this review saw only as names, and lost none to-verify [3]. The incumbents moved faster than the startups: Alexa+ in India on 16 September with Swiggy, MakeMyTrip and District bookings to-verify [45], Jio's call agent with self-KYC for an eSIM to-verify [46], Gemini Spark on the top Google tier to-verify [15]. Equal AI, a call-screening app, raised $30m in June with more than a million monthly users, which is the only Indian consumer number in this category that is not low thousands, and it screens calls rather than acting to-verify [111]. None of the entrants or incumbents publishes a limit on what its staff or its model may see of an Aadhaar, an OTP or a credential, which is the record's claim and it holds. One published incident is the comparable for the record's floor: in May 2025 Coinbase disclosed that overseas support agents had been bribed to hand over customers' names, addresses, partial identity numbers, masked bank details and identity images, for fewer than one per cent of its users, with a $20m ransom demand and a $20m reward verified [112]. That is the insider path the record's task cards are built against, at a company with a security budget the record will not have for a decade; the record should cite it on the threat-model page.
