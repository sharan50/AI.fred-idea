11Founder, team shape and execution risk

The team is one person and a model, and the record says so in its own front page (Contents, 5). The founder has done, in sixteen days, work that a seed-stage company normally spends its first year on: a design record of about 170,000 words, twenty-seven decision records, a dependency map with a change protocol, a sixty-month model with its own sensitivity and critique, and an executable specification with a hundred and one passing tests. We want to be exact about what that evidences and what it does not. It evidences a founder who can hold a very large design in a consistent shape, take a hostile review point by point, reverse fixed decisions when the arithmetic says to, and publish the errors found rather than the successes. It does not evidence an operated floor, a shipped security boundary, a customer, a vendor call, a counsel conversation or a hire, because the record contains none of those, and the last review's finding on each of them stands unanswered a week later (08, 10; the record's own note at the head of that page).

What the founding team must be able to do that it currently cannot, and whether each is a hire, a co-founder or a disqualification, is Table 8.22. Three of the rows are the last review's, restated because nothing has moved on them. Two are new. The first is that the record's authorship now cuts against it in a specific way: the executable specification is the answer to "could an engineer build this", and it was written by the same model that wrote the pages it tests, from the same pages, in the same session. A hundred and one passing tests that the pages' author wrote against the pages' author's reading of the pages is a consistency check, not an independent one, and the thirty-one seams that reading had to decide are exactly the places where a second engineer would have decided otherwise (spec/SEAMS.md [REC]). A founding engineer who did not write the record will find those seams in their first week, which is the point of hiring one, and the record should stop describing the specification as the harness's evidence until they have. The second is the founder's position at Faff, which the record lists as a competitor and which 07 carries as an open item on how it is disclosed in a diligence process (07). We could not verify the position from a public source in this session to-verify; if it is current employment, the questions that precede any cheque are the ones every investor asks of a founder leaving a competitor: what was assigned to the employer under the contract, when the record's ideas were formed, what the founder knows of Faff's economics that this record's priors may carry, and whether a non-compete or a non-solicit binds the first hires. None of these is a disqualification in itself and every one is a disqualification if it is discovered rather than disclosed.

On execution risk, the sequence is the finding. The roadmap builds eight gated stages before a real user, and the record now adds, inside the same seed window, a second country with an entity, a shift and a legal pack (06, 1; DR-023). The last review said the seed would be spent on the harness before a customer; this edition has kept the harness order and added a market to it. A solo founder with one co-founder can build stages 1 to 4 and open an Indian cohort on the browser and the draft mail route inside a year; the same two people cannot also incorporate abroad, execute a transfer mechanism, hire a UK carrier and a night crew, and price a foreign waiting list, and the plan as written asks them to. That is the execution risk in one sentence, and it is a plan defect rather than a team defect: the team is too small for the plan, and the cheaper fix is the plan.
