5The regulatory landscape: India

The record's Indian map is broadly accurate where it has looked and silent where it matters most. What it has looked at it has mostly got right: the DPDP Rules' three-phase commencement (the Board and definitions on 13 November 2025, consent managers on 13 November 2026, the substantive duties on 13 May 2027, with penalties to Rs 250 crore) verified [38]; the rule that video customer identification is run only by the regulated entity's own trained officials, with third parties assisting only at the customer's end to-verify [39]; the Authentication Directions' transaction-unique factor from 1 April 2026 to-verify [40]; the E-mandate Framework's thresholds of Rs 15,000 a debit and Rs 1 lakh for premiums, mutual funds and card bills to-verify [41]; the UPI Circle caps of Rs 5,000 a transaction and Rs 15,000 a month to-verify [42]; and the rule that only an entity regulated by a financial sector regulator may be a financial information user under the Account Aggregator framework verified [43]. The commercial conclusion the record draws from them is also right: in India today the assistant may fill forms, present documents and speak for the customer, but the identification act, the payment and the signature are the user's own act on their own device.

Where the record is out of date is the payment rail it has pinned its hopes on. The Reuters report of 1 September has been overtaken by Global Fintech Fest: NPCI's chairman set the boundary on 10 September that an AI agent should not be the thing that approves the payment, and the Unified Agent Protocol is described as a registry that registers, verifies and authorises agents on top of UPI Circle, with a fully delegated agent capped at Rs 15,000 a month and Rs 5,000 a transaction, aimed first at groceries and routine purchases to-verify [29] to-verify [30]. That is a rail for small merchant payments by a registered agent, not for the fees, premiums, deposits and institutional payments the wedge is built on. The record's line that a payment under the cap bounces until a rail admits a registered agent is the correct flag; the partner should read the protocol as confirming that the India payment flag stays at bounce for every high-consequence payment for the foreseeable horizon, and that registered-agent status, when it comes, will be obtained through a bank or payment-service sponsor on NPCI's terms, which the founder does not control.

Where the record is silent, the silences are consequential. First, Aadhaar: the Aadhaar (Authentication and Offline Verification) Amendment Regulations 2025, in force since 9 December 2025, require any private entity doing offline verification to register with UIDAI as an offline verification seeking entity, pay registration and per-verification fees, and never retain plain Aadhaar digits; UIDAI's Aadhaar Data Vault rules apply to any entity storing Aadhaar numbers, masked ones included to-verify [44]. The record's design of holding an Aadhaar number in a bought foreign-vendor vault as received is the wrong shape; the right shape is to hold no Aadhaar number at all. Second, liability: the RBI's 2017 circular on unauthorised electronic banking transactions gives the customer zero liability only where the customer did not share credentials; where the loss is due to customer negligence, such as sharing payment credentials, the customer bears the entire loss until the transaction is reported verified [45]. A customer who delegates to an agent is, on the bank's reading, the party at fault, and the digital mandate the record wants recognised has no status beyond ordinary agency under the Indian Contract Act 1872 and each bank's own registered power of attorney or mandate form to-verify [46]. Third, telecom: TRAI's commercial-communication regime (the second amendment of 12 February 2025 with its 1600-series numbers, seven-day consent validity and penalties from Rs 2 lakh to Rs 10 lakh, and the draft third amendment of 13 March 2026) is not mentioned anywhere in 03, although the company runs its own telephony and dials institutions in the customer's name verified [47]. Fourth, the consent-manager regime (an Indian company with Rs 2 crore net worth), verifiable parental consent for anyone under eighteen, and the fact that the Data Protection Board had not been appointed as of July 2026 are absent from Table 3.7 verified [38]; so is SEBI's rule that a regulated intermediary is solely responsible for any AI tool it uses, which is what a broker or insurer will cite when asked to let the assistant act on a client's account to-verify [48].

The floor and the phone line have a regulatory shape of their own that no page examines. The four Labour Codes came into force on 21 November 2025; section 43 of the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code lets women work at night only with consent and prescribed safeguards, and the Karnataka Shops Act filings (Form R for the IT-enabled exemption, Form S for women's night conditions) are still the practical instrument until the state notifies its own rules to-verify [49] to-verify [50]. The permit regime the record's operations page fears is gone, but the consent, transport and safeguard duties are real and cost days, not months. A Bengaluru voice floor is an Other Service Provider under the liberalised 2020 guidelines, which removed registration but kept the duty to hold call and system logs in India and left the toll-bypass bar standing, so every PSTN leg, Indian or foreign, terminates through an authorised carrier whose per-minute international rate sets the cost of the western shifts to-verify [51]. The 2026 telecom authorisation rules keep network data and logs in India and require prior permission for remote access from abroad, a constraint on any later UK or US operations centre to-verify [52]. And the Telecom Cyber Security Amendment Rules 2025 create the Telecommunication Identifier User Entity: any non-licensee that uses a mobile number to identify users, which on a strict reading is what device binding and WhatsApp intake make AI.fred, with duties to hand identifier data to the department and to suspend an identifier on order verified [53]. The record never mentions the labour codes, the OSP framework, TRAI, DLT or the identifier rules.

None of this kills the venture on its own, because the flag design already puts every reserved act on the user's device. What it does is confirm that in India the product is a form-filling, document-presenting, phone-answering assistant with the user still tapping every payment, every one-time code and every identification, and it adds compliance cost the record has not priced: OVSE registration, DLT and 1600-series registration, DPDP fiduciary duties from May 2027 (earlier if the significant-fiduciary window is compressed, as MeitY proposed in January 2026), a consent-manager entity if that route is taken, and counsel's reading of IT Act section 66D and BNS section 319, the Indian counterparts of the Fraud Act analysis the record does for the UK, both of which need cheating rather than mere personation but are exactly what a complainant pleads when a delegated act goes wrong to-verify [54]. The partner should treat the India pilot as a demonstration of the harness, not as evidence that delegation of consequence is lawful, and should expect the context-store thesis of DR-019 to run into purpose limitation under both DPDP and the Account Aggregator framework, where data fetched by a partner is for that partner's purpose and not the assistant's memory.
