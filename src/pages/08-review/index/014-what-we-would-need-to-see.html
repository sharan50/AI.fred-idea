14What we would need to see before money, and what a Series A would need after it

Before a seed. A signed technical co-founder with a shipped tokenisation or PCI boundary, treated as the condition of the round. Twenty customer interviews in one cohort, with the three task types the cohort dreads most, how often each recurs per household per year, and a priced waiting list; and the per-event cost of the dropped task from an institutional source (inoperative accounts and unclaimed deposits, repudiated claims, missed deadlines), which neither the record nor this review could obtain. Indian counsel's written answer on the identity rows of Table 3.5, on Aadhaar under the 2025 regulations, and on the customer-liability circular, before any harness stage beyond the vault. A range-based unit model labelled as estimate. A pilot definition of one cohort, one vertical, one shift, ninety days, with binding completion by age, human minutes per task and tasks per household per month as the gate metrics. And a threat model that looks outward, with the sixteen open findings closed by hand.

At a Series A, three milestones stated so they can fail. First, one Indian cohort of at least 200 households running banking, identity and legal tasks with verdict rates joined to step traces, an external red-team report on the two fatals, and median worker minutes per task below 45 across the L1 to L3 pools. Second, the signed India flag table with every identity-act row set by counsel, a consent-manager or Account Aggregator route decided, and one contracted channel (a bank's NRI desk, an insurer, a benefits platform) that pays for outcomes, because the graduation data say a consumer subscription alone will not clear US$2 to 4 million of annual recurring revenue inside 24 months verified [97]. Third, a written vault swap test passed and a domicile decision recorded, so that an acquirer or an A investor is not buying a rebuild; the domicile is cheap to decide at seed and expensive to unwind later, as the reverse flips of 2024 and 2025 showed verified [120].

The return analysis the record does not attempt. The base case, if the consumer business stalls, is a harness sale to an identity or agent-security vendor: the disclosed prices in that market were paid for enterprise security products with revenue and a threat-research team (a US$25 billion identity acquisition; press-reported estimates of US$250 to 700 million for agent-security companies) verified [121], while consumer hybrid assistants have produced acqui-hires at no disclosed price in a decade verified [86]. AI.fred's harness would sell as a team and a codebase, low tens of millions at best, unless it had enterprise customers of its own, which returns a US$4 million seed but not a fund. The upside case is DR-019's context store, and it requires the belief that dreaded tasks recur often enough (on the order of monthly, not a few times a year) for the store to fill inside the seed's runway; if the pilot shows a few tasks a year, the upside must be dropped from the memo and the company underwritten on the base case alone. The middle case, and the one we would underwrite, is a B2B2C business that sells the harness's guarantee to institutions that already hold the customer's trust and carry the liability, at institutional prices, with the consumer context store as a by-product rather than the revenue line.

Entities before revenue, since the record has none: an Indian operating company (the floor, the staff, and Rs 2 crore of net worth if the consent-manager route is taken verified [38]); a UK entity only as the registered agent of an FCA-authorised principal, when a UK cohort opens verified [60]; a US entity only when a cohort opens there; and a parent chosen once, with counsel, with a listing or sale path in view. That is three entities before a US task, each with counsel, and none of it is in the record.
