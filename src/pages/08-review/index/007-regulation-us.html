7The regulatory landscape: the United States

The record treats the US as the market where liability is firming in its favour (00, 2.2 and 00, 3.2) and where the only open questions are a Regulation E gap and a Section 1033 status check. That is out of date on both points it names and silent on the points that bite hardest. On Section 1033 the rule is not merely to check: it was enjoined on 29 October 2025, the CFPB abandoned its defence, and a replacement proposal reached the White House review office on 6 August 2026 with fees and the definition of an authorised representative on the table to-verify [69]. The court's reading, that the statute reaches consumers and fiduciary-like agents rather than commercial third parties, is double-edged: an assistant that genuinely acts as the user's agent is closer to the statute's core than an aggregator is, but the aggregator route the record plans to use is the one in doubt. On Regulation E the record is wrong in the direction that matters. The regulation does contemplate a standing authorisation to an agent: a transfer by a person to whom the consumer furnished the access device is not unauthorised, even where that person exceeds the authority given, until the consumer tells the bank verified [70], and Regulation Z applies the same test to cards. So a US user who lets AI.fred initiate a debit bears the loss for AI.fred's overreach as a matter of federal law; the record's cap and dual control are the company's promise, not the user's statutory protection, and no price or indemnity exists.

The largest US issue the record does not name is that the basis row of Table 3.5, speak to an institution as the customer, and the row for answering security questions from the context store collide with the GLBA pretexting offence: obtaining a financial institution's customer information by a false statement to its employee is a federal crime, and the listed exceptions contain no customer-consent exception to-verify [71]. Major banks' online agreements bar authorising a third party or sharing credentials except under guardianship or a power of attorney, and FinCEN treats an account operated by an attorney-in-fact as the principal's, verified as the principal. The lawful US route is therefore an agent that says it is an agent and holds an instrument the institution accepts (a state-law power of attorney, on the bank's own form where demanded), not a private authority envelope whose holder speaks as the customer. Objection A in 07 already senses this; the answer is not to bounce until counsel says otherwise but to design the US call differently: identify as the agent, present the mandate, expect refusals, and measure how much of the wedge survives.

Two design-wide exposures follow from surfaces the record is proud of. The evidence pipeline records both legs of every call (03, Table 3.6, path 9); eleven states including California, Florida, Illinois and Washington require all-party consent, and California's Penal Code section 632.7 reaches any cellular or cordless call with a private right of action to-verify [72]. Every US call would need a recording disclosure to the institution's agent, and Illinois' biometric statute adds voiceprint exposure if the transcriber ever identifies speakers, which the record does not say it does. The telephony surface also speaks values as synthesised speech; under the FCC's ruling of 8 February 2024 an AI-generated voice is an artificial voice under the TCPA, so a call to a clinic's mobile number carries US$500 to US$1,500 per-call exposure absent prior express consent, and after McLaughlin v McKesson district courts are free to read the statute either way to-verify [73]. The card-network claim in Table 3.4 is right as far as it goes, but Visa's and Mastercard's agent programmes are merchant-checkout rails for vetted, registered agents whose signatures are locked to a merchant's page verified [27]; nothing seen suggests an agentic token can be keyed into a hospital's payment line, which is the placement Table 3.2 illustrates.

The wider climate is a patchwork the record has not mapped. The CFPB is being cut from 1,723 staff to a planned 556 and is litigating its own funding, so no federal guidance on consumer agents is likely to-verify [74]; the states are filling the gap, with three more comprehensive privacy laws and California's risk-assessment and automated-decision regulations from 1 January 2026, a rule that counts the contents of a user's mailbox as sensitive personal information when the business is not the intended recipient, and Washington's health-data private right of action already in use to-verify [75]; Colorado's AI Act was rewritten and pushed to 1 January 2027 under a suit the Department of Justice joined to-verify [76]; Texas, Utah and Maine add disclosure duties; and California's AB 316, in force since 1 January 2026, removes the defence that the AI acted autonomously verified [77]. The federal pre-emption campaign (the executive order of 11 December 2025, the White House framework of 20 March 2026, the FTC's policy statement of July 2026) targets state rules on model development and on steering outputs, not on agents acting for consumers, so none of this goes away verified [78]. The Bengaluru floor faces political rather than legal obstacles for now: the HIRE Act's 25 per cent outsourcing excise sits in committee, and the FCC's onshoring proposal of April 2026 applies to carriers verified [79]. And a platform rule that DR-018 depends on: Google treats Gmail read and modify scopes as restricted, requiring an annual security assessment, so the read connector is a paid, audited dependency from day one to-verify [80].

The contradiction a partner will press is this. The horizon section says the Ninth Circuit's tool-not-person ruling and the enterprise identity drafts point to account-level delegation as the lawful US shape to-verify [32] to-verify [33]; this section says speaking to an institution as the customer is an offence. Both are true, and together they say that in the US the owned surface (moat property 3) is a liability rather than an asset: the lawful route is an agent that consumes grants institutions issue to registered agents, disclosed as an agent, holding a power of attorney where the institution demands one. The architecture should be able to consume such grants as they appear so that the owned surface is a fallback rather than the only path, and the US legal pack should be written for an agent-with-mandate, not for a delegate who speaks as the customer. What is right in the record: AB 316 is verified and correctly read; there is no CFPB guidance squarely on AI agents; the US crossing rests on contract and state law; and the card networks bind tokens to a named agent.
