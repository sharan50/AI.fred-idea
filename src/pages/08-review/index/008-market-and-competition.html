8Market, customer and competition

The thesis has read the field correctly. Every live rival either refuses consequence-bearing work (Duckbill: no impersonation, transactions to US$2,500, legal and accounting referred out to-verify [81]; Yohana's policy barred logins and financial or legal advice) or takes it on with no safeguard (Faff and Hulp list government and banking tasks on a WhatsApp thread to-verify [82]; Instinct's terms take a perpetual licence and testers found plain-text email storage verified [36]). What the record has not done is size the wedge, and when the public numbers are put beside it the wedge is small per household. India's own health survey puts hospitalisation at 2.9 per 100 persons a year, 8.1 for the over-60s to-verify [83]; India issued 13.9 million passports in 2025 against a population of about 1.4 billion to-verify [84]; the re-KYC cycle is two, eight or ten years by risk class to-verify [39]; the US issued 27.3 million passports in fiscal 2025 to-verify [85]. A household of four in an Indian metro meets one to three wedge-class events in a typical year, and DR-019's own rejected alternative concedes that a few tasks a year form no habit. Section 1 of the thesis describes the customer by task and never by count; that silence is the gap a partner presses first.

The history is unambiguous and the record under-reads it. Facebook M closed in January 2018 with 70 per cent or more of answers coming from humans to-verify [1]; Fin charged a dollar a minute and became a B2B analytics business; GoButler narrowed to flights, became Angel.ai and was acqui-hired by Amazon at no disclosed price verified [86]; Clara went to TopFunnel in 2019; Yohana charged US$249 a month, served about 4,500 families in total, closed in the US on 30 September 2025 and in Japan on 30 January 2026, and its parent told investors that scaling and monetisation were never verified to-verify [2]; Milo, at about US$40 a month, is reported closed in January 2026, with commentary blaming the cost of the human in the loop against cheaper general models, on a single unsourced note to-verify [87]. Each died because the human share did not shrink. AI.fred's design makes the human share structural rather than transitional: dual control on every irreversible step and an office-mandatory 24-hour floor. DR-015 defers the economics, but the comparables say the economics are the thesis, and 00, 4.2 records the closures without drawing the unit-cost lesson from them.

The capital is going the other way. Instinct raised US$350 million at US$2.5 billion in August 2026 and Hark US$700 million at US$6 billion in May to-verify [37]; Equal AI raised US$30 million with over a million Indian users on a call-first assistant and is moving into financial services and concierge, M raised Rs 102 crore from Peak XV, Hulp US$2.6 million to-verify [82]; Alexa+ is free with Prime, Jio's call agent is free to hundreds of millions to-verify [88], and Instinct is free in beta with a reported US$35 tier. The price of the everyday layer that DR-019 counts on for habit and context is trending to zero, and the Indian flat-fee anchor is Rs 1,999 a month for unlimited tasks (00, Table 0.2). Where consumers do pay for consequence-bearing help they pay a lot and to a named person: London personal assistants at £35,000 to £75,000, Quintessentially at £7,000 to £27,500 a year, family offices from US$1 million. Between US$169 a month (Duckbill's household plan) and £7,000 a year no consumer product has survived, and the one concierge business at scale, Ten Lifestyle Group with 436,000 active members and £33.7 million of half-year revenue, is paid by banks and card issuers at roughly £175 per member a year, not by members to-verify [89]. The claim in 00, 3.2 that willingness to pay for delegation is established in the US and UK cites a decade-old hybrid sector that has mostly closed and a listed concierge whose payer is institutional; the honest reading is that the willingness is established for institutions buying on behalf of customers, which points to a distribution route the record does not consider.

Two things the record leaves unexamined deserve a page each. The non-resident Indian and cross-border family is a sharply defined, paying, already-funded segment: 35.4 million overseas Indians, US$135 billion of remittances in fiscal 2025 to-verify [90], elder-care services priced at Rs 1,500 to 7,000 a month for the parent in India verified [91], accounts frozen pending re-KYC, and a journey the record already half-designs in 01, 1.5. It fits the wedge exactly, has higher event frequency than the user's own dreaded tasks (a parent's KYC refresh, pension life certificate, deposit renewals, hospital bookings, a lost SIM), pays in sterling or dollars, and runs on the India floor, the Indian rails and the flag table the pilot can actually sign. It gets one phrase in the thesis. And the trust bootstrap is concrete: 87 per cent of Indians in one survey believe their data is already compromised, and over half name Aadhaar or PAN to-verify [92]; KYC flows lose 40 to 60 per cent of users; and yet the Account Aggregator network has linked hundreds of millions of accounts under consent and DigiLocker had more than 52 crore sign-ups by June 2025 verified [93]. A new brand asking for a PAN through its own chat thread will bleed at onboarding; one that reaches values only through consented rails and a regulated partner may not. The record's own architecture points that way, but the thesis page does not say so.

The corpus never resolves whether privacy is a purchase driver, and neither can we. The record's moat is that no rival publishes a masking policy; the market's revealed preference in 2026 is that a rival with plain-text storage raises at US$2.5 billion. Nobody has produced willingness-to-pay-for-privacy evidence, and a partner should not assume it. The stronger form of the argument is not that customers pay for masking but that institutions and regulators require it: a bank's NRI desk, an insurer or an employer that puts its name beside the product will demand exactly the posture the record designs, and that is the payer the numbers point to. Read that way, the masking architecture is a condition of distribution rather than a consumer feature, and the consumer brand is a cost the company may not need to carry.

The head-to-head the record never runs is the three markets on the pilot's own rules. Under 06, 2.3, only India can exercise an identity act in the pilot, and only if counsel signs; the UK and the US bounce every identity act and every payment. India's price anchor is Faff's flat fee; the UK's is Samwise at £25 with no human operators; the US's is Duckbill at US$99 to 169. Cost to serve is the same Bengaluru floor whichever market is first. The UK needs the transfer mechanism before the first task; the US needs an agent-with-mandate design. The conclusion is not a build convenience: India-first, defined as Indian institutions first, is the only way the thesis can be tested at all, with the NRI household as the paying cohort once the UK mechanism is executed, and with the UK and the US as counsel questions, a cheap demand test and a Series A narrative rather than pilot markets. Two facts would reverse it: an Indian counsel answer that a private party may not state an Aadhaar-linked number or complete a KYC form, which would set India's identity rows to bounce too and collapse the wedge to non-identity admin everywhere; or measured willingness to pay that shows Indian urban households will not pay above the flat-fee anchor for an episodic task while NRI households will pay a professional fee per event.
