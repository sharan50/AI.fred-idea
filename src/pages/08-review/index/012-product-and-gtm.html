12Product, go-to-market and retention

The product is designed backwards from the failure modes, not forwards from a customer. That is defensible for the harness, but it leaves the path to customers thin in exactly the places a seed partner looks first. Three things stand out. First, the pilot as scoped runs the signed flag table with every identity act in the UK and the US set to bounce and every payment set to bounce in all three markets (06, 2.3). In India the pilot will state an account number to a bank's deposit line with the worker muted, which is the promise; in the UK or the US it will prepare a form and hand the tap back, which is a checklist product that ChatGPT offers for nothing. The thesis can therefore be proven only in India in the first year, whatever the thesis says about commercial ground, and the founder's open item on the first cohort has in practice one honest answer. Second, the onboarding stack in 01, 3 and 6 (identity-rail device binding on the rail's own screen, a structured interview with vaulted answers, connector consents, then an envelope confirmation before anything runs) is heavier than a bank account opening, and the only quantified benchmark reachable says 68 per cent of European consumers abandoned a financial onboarding in the year surveyed, 21 per cent because too much personal information was asked verified [112]; a challenger bank took its US sign-up completion up about four-fold by deferring one check to-verify [113]. The record has no funnel, no pilot size and no step-completion target. Third, the endgame asks the assistant to become the first port of call for everyday tasks against a habit that already exists: ChatGPT reached about a tenth of the world's adults by mid-2025 with non-work use at 73 per cent of messages verified [114], a third of US adults had used it to-verify [115], and Apple, Google and Meta all announced personal agents inside the surfaces the user already lives in during 2026.

Where the record is wrong or silent. The trust story promises that no person who works for us, at any level, sees your numbers, while 07 records sixteen unresolved findings from the seventh adversarial pass, two of them paths on which a person on the operator side could see a value (trust story against 07, Table 7.1). A customer-facing page that asserts an absolute while the engineering record lists open exceptions is a sales document a diligent buyer will catch; the sentence should distinguish proven from designed before it is shown to anyone. The front-door amber of DR-001 is correctly held for the US, where no chat surface dominates, but for the UK Ofcom's figure of 90 per cent of adults on WhatsApp makes it less open than the record implies to-verify [116]. In all three markets, though, the design makes the front door secondary: every authority tap lands on a bound device with a device key, so the real adoption question is the app install and the identity-rail binding, and the record never says how the key is held (no mention of passkeys, a platform authenticator or a native app), which is an under-built seam.

The sharper wedge is the one journey 1.5 already half-designs: an adult child in the UK acting for a parent in India. Dread is high, frequency is higher than the user's own dreaded tasks, the user pays in sterling, and execution runs on the India floor, the Indian rails and the flag table the pilot can sign. The record's two-jurisdiction lookup, delegator record and rule that an act India reserves to the person bounces to her device fit it exactly. Its cost is the hardest onboarding of all, and the record does not know it: the parent must bind a device through an identity rail on her own screen, sign every tap after a lock-screen factor, and operate a signed prompt. A 72-year-old in Pune must therefore own a smartphone, pass an Aadhaar-class factor (worn fingerprints are the documented reason face authentication was added to-verify [117]), set a lock-screen factor and read the envelope; a step without her authority re-classifies as a user step for her, which lands on the same device she could not bind. Nothing in 01, 03, 06 or 07 mentions age, capacity, literacy, disability or a proxy binder. This review's low-confidence estimate is that 15 to 30 per cent of the Indian over-70 cohort could complete the binding unaided, 45 to 60 per cent in the UK and 50 to 65 per cent in the US [EST]; for the cross-border family the parent is the Indian figure, so the recommended cohort starts with roughly three in four parents unable to take the first step the record requires of them.

The elder cohort also walks into the rules institutions use to stop elder fraud. In the US, FinCEN's red flags for elder financial exploitation include a new third party speaking for an older customer, a caregiver's involvement, unexplained contact-detail changes and requests for information by an intermediary, with holds of fifteen business days available to brokers to-verify [118]; in the UK the vulnerability guidance, the Banking Protocol and the reimbursement regime push a bank to refuse rather than to admit a third-party caller to-verify [68]; in India the RBI already requires doorstep banking for customers above 70 and allows self-declaration re-KYC where nothing has changed, which removes part of the dreaded banking list for exactly this cohort to-verify [119]. The product's default call script, in which the far party that asks who is calling is answered with the user's own name in aliases, is the pattern those rules describe as a red flag. The design changes are concrete and mostly cheap: an assisted binding ceremony with a family witness and a rail-side face or one-time-code factor, or a doorstep agent; a capacity attestation on the dependant's delegation record with a shorter validity; disclosed agency as the default script for regulated institutions; registering the institution's own instrument (a bank mandate, a trusted contact, a power of attorney or an LPA) as the first task type for any dependant profile; and an accessibility floor for the device surface with an alternative to the lock-screen factor. The sixth, a voice-first channel for a parent without a smartphone, contradicts the roadmap's gate that no confirmation is accepted from a channel that authenticates only a phone number, so it is an architecture decision rather than a feature.

Product surface is over-built for a pilot in the width of channels, surfaces and roles (SMS and iMessage normalisation, a mail relay with send scope, a 24-hour shift wheel, L3 dual control, per-task card tokens, a temporal knowledge graph) and under-built where a customer meets it: no onboarding funnel or completion targets, no chosen device implementation, no demand evidence from interviews, no price hypothesis, no make-good policy, and no definition of the size, duration or success bar of the pilot beyond verdict rates per task type. A credible ninety-day pilot would fix those first: one cohort (the NRI household acting for a parent, or an Indian urban household in a care episode), three task types the cohort names in interviews, a price per event, a sponsor who vouches for the product to the first fifty users (a bank's NRI desk, an employer caregiving benefit, a professional association), a binding completion rate by age as the first metric, human minutes per task and tasks per household per month as the gate metrics, and the everyday endgame treated as a hypothesis with a kill criterion rather than a fixed decision.
