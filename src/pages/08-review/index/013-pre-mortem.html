13Pre-mortem: what kills it, ranked

Five different dimensions of this review each call a different thing fatal: household frequency, human minutes per task, seed exhaustion before a paying task, no co-founder, and the model vendors lifting their advice against financial sites. A pre-mortem has to rank them, date them, give each a leading indicator and a kill criterion, and say which decision record would have to be reversed to defuse it. The ranking below is by the product of likelihood and impact inside a 24-month seed runway, and every kill criterion is written so that it can fail. The comparables that could be reached are named; several (Facebook M's internal numbers, Duckbill's retention claim, Milo's cause of death) rest on secondary notes and are marked so in the sources.

Read together, the pilot as scoped proves little in any market: the product critique says the promise can be exercised only in India; the India critique says that in India every payment, one-time code and identification bounces, so the product is a form-filler with a caller attached; the security critique says the pilot will be dominated by those bounces. The ranking reflects that: the first three kills are about whether anyone wants the thing often enough, at a human cost low enough, in a market where it can be done at all, and the harness only matters once those three have been survived.
