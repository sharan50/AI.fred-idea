5The state machine

A task cannot be forgotten, because forgetting has no transition. The task service holds every task in exactly one named state, gives every non-terminal state a live timer, records every change of state as an explicit event and hands anything that has lost its timer to the watchdog. The invariant below is the contract; it appears identically on the worker console page, where a person is most tempted to leave something for later.

A task never leaves the system by omission. Every task is in exactly one named state. Every non-terminal state carries a live timer. Every change of state is an explicit, recorded transition, and no task reaches a terminal state without one. Silence from the user is a legitimate way for a task to end: it moves through visible nudges into lapsed, and lapsed is not withdrawn. Silence from the system is forbidden: a task with no live timer is an exception, and the watchdog places it on the exceptions board until a person gives it one.

Expiry is acted on by the system, on the tick, never by a person; a person acts only on the entries the watchdog raises on the exceptions board. At a step deadline the task service reads the step type's on_timeout. requeue revokes the claimed card as a recorded step-state event, releases the surface, returns the step to ready with a fresh deadline and counts the attempt, and closes it failed-final with the reason "deadline" at the retry cap. replan closes it failed-final with that reason at once. escalate closes it blocked-third-party with a holding window, so it enters the board's no-route column for the escalation desk. In every case the card tells the worker so and no worker is waited for. Nudges are recorded events on the timer of awaiting-authority, awaiting-user and awaiting-verdict, visible to the user and on the task's timeline; each goes to the device surface, with a notification and nothing more on the conversation channel; a message from the user on the thread that does not resolve the wait resets the schedule to its start as a recorded nudge-reset; the awaited action itself is the transition out; and when the waiting step is a dependant's own act, the nudge goes to the dependant's bound device and the user is told. After the last nudge the task service writes the lapse event itself, recording the state lapsed from and cancelling every step not yet fired; lapsed covers both a task abandoned before authorisation and one completed but never judged, and the state lapsed from is what keeps the two apart in measurement assumption. For awaiting-verdict the schedule runs from the recorded delivery of the outcome report, the bound device's signed receipt, which the task service checks; a report the device has not yet received keeps the task on the same schedule counted from the send time, each nudge re-sending it, and when the receipt arrives the schedule restarts from that delivery, so an unreachable device never holds a task without a timer, and no person confirms anything before a lapse. When a response window expires the task service re-plans: a new route applies escalate-resume; no route sets a holding window, a recorded timer event named extend-window, and places the task on the exceptions board for a person to chase the party. A lapsed task is terminal and has no timer; revive is the only way out, into planning rather than the state it left, because the world may have moved.

The response window is chosen when the step closes: the closing party, the worker on the result form or the ai executor, picks from the step type's allowed windows, never a free number. Chasing a party is the escalation desk's, with three tools and no others: the board card's actions, which are extend-window with the date the party gave, resume when the party answers, escalate-resume when a re-plan prompted by what the desk learned finds a route, and add-alternative for a route the planner should try; a task card of the market's chase-the-party step type, issued by the planner on request, so the chase itself runs on our telephony or relay with the same masks; and the incident queue for a party that has stopped answering.

The watchdog runs on a fixed cycle (illustrative: one minute) and asks four questions of every record: does every live task have a future-dated timer; does every step in checking have a checker and a check deadline not yet passed; has any response window or planning deadline expired without resume, escalate-resume, extend-window or extend-planning following within one cycle; and has any nudge schedule been exhausted without a lapse event within one cycle. The last two are defects in the task service, not routine work. Any failure places the task on the exceptions board until a person gives it a timer, a checker or a transition. Every board action is an operation of the task service, recorded as a board-action event whose actor is a person with a staff id and a role, and authorised by role: the shift lead and the escalation desk may set a timer of the right kind for the state, reassign a check, extend a window, add an alternative or, with an incident, close the nudge schedule early, after which the task service writes the lapse event on the next tick with the incident recorded as its cause; no person writes a lapse event, and nobody may dismiss a card. The watchdog's own heartbeat is an alarm in ordinary monitoring, and monitoring is three alarms and one pager: the heartbeat, the hash service's rate-limit breaches and the pipeline's incident counts, each paged to the shift lead on shift with the on-call engineer as the second line, so a silent watchdog is itself an exception. The board is specified on the exceptions board on the worker console page and staffed per QA and escalation in 04.

5.4Step states and the event log

Steps have their own small machine: pending (in the plan, not ready), ready, running, checking (dual control; the other party is checking), bounced (a user step, with the user), closed (with an outcome code) and cancelled (withdrawn, lapsed or superseded before it fired; lapsed covers every end of the task's authority, the nudge schedule exhausted, the validity window ended, the binding suspended; a dependant's delegation ending cancels nothing and re-classifies the steps that name her aliases instead, Table 2.8). A cancelled step never ran and carries no outcome code, except that a step a re-plan removes carries superseded with the superseding step, so the plan's history shows what replaced it. A surface acts only for a step in running; a substitution request for any other state is refused and logged. The task's state follows its steps by a fixed rule the task service applies as transitions from Table 2.2 and never as a second mechanism: any step ready, running or checking keeps the task active, timed by the earliest step deadline; otherwise any step bounced, or an open question or extension request, is awaiting-user on the nudge schedule; otherwise any step closed blocked-third-party is awaiting-third-party on that step's response window; otherwise, while a renewal of the authority is open with the user after expire-authority, the task is awaiting-authority on the nudge schedule; and when every step is closed or cancelled and no renewal is open, report moves the task to awaiting-verdict. Each step event names the transition it causes, if any, by that rule; the rule is recomputed on every event as a check, and a task whose state disagrees with it is an exception the watchdog raises, so the task's state and its steps' states cannot drift apart.

The event log is the audit trail, the input to the improvement loop and the source for the user's task timeline. It is append-only, replicated within the market's region, and retained as long as the ledger of section 7.3. It carries aliases and pseudonymous references only; the logging pipeline has no vault SDK and no digest set, and every line passes through the hash service's screen (section 8.3) before it is committed, so a line that matches a known value is dropped and raises an incident rather than being written. Withdrawal is an ordinary event: steps in running are cancelled with the surface released, steps in checking are cancelled before they fire and every approval token is voided, and an irreversible step that has already fired is listed in the outcome report, the only honest response to a withdrawal that came too late; on a call, which side of that line the act fell is decided by the step's fired marker, written when the committing sentence was released to the worker (section 4.2), a withdrawal before the release stopping the utterance at once, and the outcome report cites that record, never the worker's structured result, as the evidence that it fired. Expiry of the validity window follows DR-004 and is the task service's act on the tick, by the validity-window row of Table 2.8: it cancels every step not yet fired, cause lapsed, and moves the task by expire-authority to awaiting-authority with a one-tap request to renew the window, on the nudge schedule, so nothing starts and no step in ready begins, because the step runner claims a step only inside the window and the version the signed context names (section 4.5); no call is dialled, because the dialler checks the window and the version before it resolves a number (section 4.2); no placement is made, because every signed step context carries the window's end as its own expiry and has died with it (section 4.6); an irreversible step cannot fire at all, because an approval token is valid only while the window is open; and a reversible step already running completes what needs no further placement and no committing action, and otherwise stops where it stands and is cancelled, because every surface controller checks the window and the version at every committing action, a form submit, a committing click, a committing sentence on a call, a keypad press outside the navigation map, a relay release, a committing placement, as at every placement, and after expiry refuses it and closes the step blocked-outside-envelope naming the window (section 4.5). The renewal the user taps is an envelope extension, and its re-plan re-issues the cancelled steps (section 2.2). The decision behind the machine is DR-005.
