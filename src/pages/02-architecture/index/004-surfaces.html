4Execution surfaces

We own every surface, and a worker never touches a task with their own tools. Because the browser, the phone line and the mail relay are ours, the substitution component (isolated as specified in the substitution component in 03) sits at the edge of each, what a worker sees of a value is masked, no value is placed on a call at all (DR-021), and the evidence is redacted before it leaves. A real value exists on a surface only between placement and the third party's consumption of it. Each surface has a controller, and only two of the three controllers may write a placement request, the browser controller and the relay, a call carrying no value to place (4.2): the console never calls the substitution component, and the worker's tap on Place goes from the console to the task service, which hands it to the step's surface controller, which requests the placement (section 4.5). The site controls that keep a worker's own devices out of the room are the site controls in 04; the human side of each surface, the masks and the failure modes, are the surfaces in 03.

4.1The managed browser

For a workflow the library of section 4.4 has modelled, the worker never sees the page. The browser controller derives a structured task card from it, the fields the step needs with their labels, their validity state and their aliases, and the worker works the card; the substitution component places the real values at submit, as it does on any surface. Masking is then a property of the data the card carries, which is deterministic, rather than of the pixels a masker paints, which is probabilistic (DR-022). The streamed page with the mask, the delay line and the compositor hooks specified in the rest of this section is the fallback for a counterparty the library has not modelled; it is deferred until volume justifies building it, and until then an unmodelled counterparty is an ai step where section 3.1's conditions hold, a card the library is extended to cover, or a bounce.

The managed browser runs in our environment, in the market's region, on a browser sandbox we buy; the worker sees a video stream of it and drives it by remote input through the browser controller. Navigation is allow-listed to the declared domains of the envelope's contactable parties in the party directory; anything else is blocked and logged. When a step reaches a field that needs a real value, the card offers a Place control for the alias. Pressing it sends the task service the step and the alias; the task service hands the tap to the browser controller, which writes the placement request to the substitution component's queue with the target descriptor, the page origin and the accessibility node of the field, and the signed step context. The substitution component runs the checks 03 specifies, refuses with a receipt naming the failed check and a ledger line any placement whose origin does not resolve to a contactable party, and otherwise types the value into the field with its own privileged driver. The receipt returns to the controller; the console renders "placed", "not confirmed", "refused" or "failed", never the value. For a dual-control check on a filled form the browser controller also runs a field-match check, asking the hash service whether a field's digest equals the digest of the alias the step names, and reports the answer as a check-panel line (section 7.2).

What we require of the bought sandbox is fixed by the design: a privileged driver channel the worker's client does not have, on which the substitutor's driver focuses and types; access to the document's text and accessibility tree for the masker and the executor, with read-back of field values disabled on every channel but the driver's own input events; compositor hooks so that masking happens before a frame is encoded; disabled autofill, form history, extensions, clipboard and downloads; and one session per task, meaning one profile and cookie jar created at the task's first browser step and destroyed at its close, one open site at a time, session state kept across the task's steps. Redirects are followed only within the party's declared domains and to a payment page the directory names for that party; a third-party origin a page fetches (fonts, scripts, content networks) is loaded but never navigated to; a download is written only into the vault as a document alias, never to the sandbox's disk. The per-site mask map is the party directory's entry: a list of page patterns and the image or canvas regions cleared on each, signed with the legal pack.

Masking runs on the DOM text and the accessibility tree, never on pixels, behind the delay line the surface settings set per market, and applies the five stream masks in the order the managed browser in 03 fixes: coordinate masks at every placement record, field-type masks, digest masks through the hash service, format masks, and region masks for image and canvas unless the mask map clears them, with the shape rule after any submit that used a placement. The format mask matches every text run on every frame against the class formats of Table 3.1 in 03: identifier patterns, dates, card and account shapes, currency amounts in written form, the financial-fact class, a name in a party's identity field or a people node's name in any field, and the alphanumeric reference shapes the party register records for that party; a run that matches is painted. The masker delivers no text run it has not accounted for: a run it can neither match nor clear against the step's mask map is painted by default and the frame is held for re-masking, so "plain text below the threshold" is never a delivered residual. An authenticated portal, which shows balances, history, other account numbers, names and addresses that were never vaulted, is masked by default on every page the party register marks as one: every text run and every figure is painted except the fields the step's mask map whitelists as needed for the step's outcome, the capture at submit and at response is the same masked frame and a document snapshot with the same whitelist applied, the verifier of section 8.1 checks the whitelist as well as known values and formats, and the worker, a checker and QA see only the whitelisted fields; a step whose mask map whitelists nothing has no readable stream, so it is never human: it is ai where the conditions of section 3.1 hold, the party machine-navigable for the step type, a verifier the harness can run and a surface the scope block opens, and otherwise it bounces to the user's own device as a user step. The DOM capture the evidence pipeline receives is masked the same way. The threat model in 03 names each path through the screen and closes it with this mechanism; the camera and the two-person zone in 04 are the record of a failure of it, never the closure, and no frame reaches a person as a residual.

The masking fails closed, in the way 03 fixes: a frame the masker cannot clear, because the hash service is unreachable, the frame missed the delay or a region can be neither named nor covered, is not delivered; the stream shows the hold screen with a reason, the frame is re-masked with wider shape masks and re-checked by the verifier before it can leave, the ai executor may continue on the driver alone, and a human step waits with the surface held and its timer live, until the task service, if the hold outlasts the bounded wait 03 sets, closes it on the tick: failed-retryable, re-queued to another worker, where no fired marker exists, and failed-final with the marker as its evidence where one does, the re-plan issuing a status enquiry before any repeat (section 7.1); the possible-exposure control on the console closes a step by the same rule. A worker never sees an unmasked frame. Copy, download, print and screenshot are disabled in the console, and the stream is the only representation of the page that leaves the sandbox.

4.2Telephony

Calls run on our numbers and our call platform, one leg for the worker and one for the far party. The dialler checks the envelope's validity window and version, and the validity of any dependant's delegation the step's aliases reference, before it resolves the contactable party named in the envelope to a number in the party directory for the desk the step names, so no call is dialled after the window has closed; a worker never types a number, and a party outside the envelope cannot be dialled. The worker then speaks to the far party in the ordinary way and hears them in the ordinary way. No value from the vault is placed on a call, in either direction: there is no media function on this surface, no synthesised or keyed placement of a vaulted value, no delay line, no re-voicing and no muting of the worker's leg (DR-021). A step that needs a value stated or verified by voice is not run here at all: it moves to the managed browser or the relay, where the substitution component can place it, or the classifier makes it a user step and it bounces to the user's own device.

Two consequences are written down rather than left implied. The first is what a worker hears. Phone work now covers only non-authenticating calls, bookings, chasing, enquiries, availability and appointment setting, so a desk has no reason to state a value and the script never asks for one; but the worker hears the far party directly, which means they hear the user's name, the thing being booked and whatever else that desk volunteers. Facts about the user reaching a worker's ear is accepted on this surface and bounded by the need-to-know card, the vetting and site controls of 04 and the ledger, not by architecture. A value the far party states unasked is not a designed path: the worker closes the step and the possible-exposure control opens an incident, and the pipeline's second pass over the recording tokenises the span and records it, as it does for any exposure.

The second is the commitment gate. With no media server there is nothing in the path to cut a committing utterance before it is delivered, so on a call the closure for the second fatal failure is the approval token and dual control ahead of the words: an irreversible step inside the envelope is checked before the worker is given the committing sentence to read, the step's fired marker is written when the sentence is released to them (section 7.2), and a reversible step's script carries no committing sentence, so there is nothing to say. A worker asked to agree something the script does not carry closes the step blocked-outside-envelope; a worker asked for a value says it is not available on a call and offers the surface that can carry it, or closes the step blocked-needs-user.

What we require of the bought call platform is correspondingly smaller: numbers and caller identity per market, provisioned as the shift line of the playbook in 06 lists; per-leg dialling under the telephony controller; keypad navigation of a voice menu by the worker or the ai executor through the controller, where a key the step's menu map names as navigation needs no token and a committing key, or a key the map does not name, needs the approval token like a submit; and both legs delivered to the evidence pipeline in region, where redaction and the transcriber's tokenisation happen as section 8 fixes. A menu that demands an identity number is a step the classifier bounces. An inbound callback from an institution is answered on the task's own number, bound to the task by that number, and is a blocked-third-party step resuming, never a placement.

4.3The mail relay

Outbound mail is composed with aliases, by a worker in the console or, where the scope block permits, by the ai executor, and sent from the user's own address on one of the two routes DR-018 fixes: where the user has granted their mailbox's send scope beside its read scope, the finished message is submitted through the user's own mail provider by the vault's edge proxy's channel connector, which injects the send-scope token as it injects every connector's token, so the relay holds no token and the provider keeps the sent copy in the user's own mailbox; where the user has not, the step is a user step in the "do this yourself" form, the device shows the draft with the user's labels where a value belongs, and the user pastes it into their own mail app, fills their own values and sends it. No message to a third party leaves from an address of ours. At send time the relay, as the surface controller, sends the substitution component a placement request naming the message and the placeholder occurrences, with the signed step context; the substitution component checks, rewrites the occurrences in the relay's memory-only outbound store, writes the ledger entry, and the relay dispatches, holds the message only until the receiving server accepts it and keeps no sent copy. An attachment is a document alias in the draft that the substitutor resolves with its upload kind into the outbound store the same way. The relay releases a message only if it instantiates the step's catalogue mail_template, fixed sentences with named slots typed non-committing, or the step holds an approval token naming the hash of the message as checked; a message that matches no template is a committing action and needs the token, so on a reversible step, which holds none, the relay cannot commit anything, and the relay writes the step's fired marker before a committing release (section 7.2). The task's relay address, a random local part at the market's receiving domain, created at the task's first mail step, alive for the task's life plus the ledger's retention so a late forward still binds, and never reused, is inbound only and never a sender, and the receiving domain carries the market's authentication records for what it receives. On the relay route a provider that has not accepted the submission inside the placement window, or a receiving server that defers past the placement window gets nothing further: the body is dropped, the line closes "not confirmed", the step closes failed-retryable with a retry token, and the next attempt is a fresh composition and a fresh placement, except that a step whose fired marker exists closes failed-final with the marker as its evidence and the re-plan verifies before any repeat (section 7.1). The evidence copy is the alias draft plus the delivery receipt, and the draft a worker sees never contains a value. A reply lands in the user's own inbox on either route and reaches us bound to the task by the thread of the relay's message id: by the mailbox's read connector where the user holds one, or by the user's forward to the task's relay address; either way it terminates at the vault's edge proxy like any other channel, attachments included, and reaches the intake gateway tokenised. A late reply still binds, because the thread's ids are kept for the ledger's retention. What a worker asks a party to send a masked span on is the task's thread, the reply address being the user's own. What a worker reads of it is the relay's rendering, and the renderer's default is the same allowlist as the browser masker's default paint and the transcriber's: after the proxy's four detectors, every token outside the cleared vocabulary, the language's common lexicon, the party register's terms for that party and the catalogue entry's expected phrases, is painted, an out-of-lexicon token, a proper noun that is not a cleared term, a digit run, a class-format match, and the worker reads the painted rendering; a painted token the worker needs is asked for as a typed field sent on the task's thread, where the proxy tokenises it on arrival, or the step closes blocked-needs-user. The planner receives the tokenised message, screened again by the model runtime's detector, never the rendering.

All three surfaces emit into the evidence pipeline (section 8), and all three refuse to operate for a step not in running: the state machine, not the surface, decides whether anything happens at all.

4.4The workflow library

The library is the record of how each counterparty's process actually works: per party and per step type, the pages or the desk to reach, the fields and their labels, the acceptance artefact that proves completion, the undo route where one exists, and the mask map and menu map the surfaces need. It is what makes a structured task card possible (DR-022), so it is load-bearing rather than convenient: a counterparty the library has not modelled cannot be worked as a card, and coverage is an engineering backlog rather than a configuration.

Two costs follow and both are named rather than absorbed. Identity and institutional acts are engineered per counterparty, so the unit of work is a party and a step type, not a feature; and a counterparty that changes its process breaks the entry, which the harness finds as a failed acceptance artefact and which raises a ticket against the library with the party's name on it. 04 holds the operational side of that ticket and 05 prices the maintenance, because the rate at which the tenth institution costs less to add than the second is one of the five measurements the economics rest on.

4.5The surface controllers and the step runner

Each surface has one controller, a process deployed with the surface inside the real-value zone, and the browser controller and the relay are the only writers to the substitution component's queue, the telephony controller never placing a value (4.2). A controller holds no detokenise right and no vault SDK: it creates and destroys the surface session under a step, forwards the worker's remote input from the console and returns the masked stream, the call panel state or the composer state, requests placements with the target descriptor and the signed step context and receives the receipts, returns the surface's own signal (the field's validity state, the call platform's own call record, the delivery record) to the task service, and emits every capture, with the placement registry, into the evidence pipeline. The browser controller also carries the ai executor's actions to the sandbox and the one-shot channel for a one-time code; the telephony controller dials, answers callbacks, sends navigation keys and holds a line while a step is in checking; the mail relay is its own controller. Every controller checks the envelope's validity window and version at every placement and at every committing action, whose closed list reads: a form submit, a committing click, a committing sentence on a call, a keypad press outside the navigation map, a relay release, a committing placement: after expiry it refuses the action and closes the step blocked-outside-envelope naming the window, and an approval token is valid only while the window is open. On the managed browser a click is a committing action unless the party directory's page map (section 3.3) names the control as navigation: following a link inside the declared domains, scrolling, focusing a field and typing non-sensitive text are navigation by default, and a button, a form submit or any scripted control is committing unless the page map names it. The browser controller treats any form submission or committing click on a party's domain as irreversible unless the step's catalogue entry names the undo route for it, so without a named undo route the action needs the approval token whatever the step's classification, and on a reversible step, which holds none, the controller refuses every click outside the navigation map and every submit, so the step cannot commit anything. The mail relay carries the same default rule in its own form, releasing only a message that instantiates the step's template or whose hash a token names as checked (section 4.3), so on a reversible step neither the browser nor the relay can commit anything. On telephony the rule is a person's, not a component's: the controller sends no key outside the step's navigation map without a token, and the committing sentence is released to the worker only after dual control (section 4.2), which is the narrowing DR-021 accepts. Before any committing action, a submit, a release, a committing key, the committing utterance's start, the controller consumes the approval token, which is single-use, and writes the step's fired marker (section 7.2). A committing placement is the sixth: a placement whose alias is a payment instrument or a payment instruction, or whose catalogue entry marks it consumed_on_placement (Table 2.6), is itself the act the party executes, so the substitutor requires the approval token for it like a submit, which a reversible step never holds, and writes the step's fired marker, its own ledger line, before the first character is delivered, the controller carrying the marker to the task service with the receipt; a not-confirmed receipt on it closes the step failed-final with the marker, never failed-retryable, the reservation is not released and the re-plan issues a status enquiry before any repeat (section 7.1); and a narrowing that arrives inside its placement window does not stop it, because the marker is already written, which the outcome report says.

The step runner sits inside the task service and drives every step's lifecycle whatever its class: it claims a step for a worker or the ai executor only inside the envelope's validity window and only against the version the signed context names, so no step in ready begins after expiry; it moves the claimed step to running, obtains the signed step context, opens the surface through its controller, starts the ai executor or offers the card, hands a worker's tap on Place to the controller, issues the retry token for any re-placement of the same step, alias and target, records the outcome the controller or the console returns, and releases the surface. Its interfaces are three: to the console (cards, taps, results), to the controllers (open, place, signal, close), and to the substitution component only indirectly, through a controller, so that no path exists from the task service to a value.

4.6The signed step context

A placement is authorised by a signed step context, not by the caller's word. The task service signs one with its private key when a step enters running: the step record with its executor and its card's alias list, the envelope version with its lines, the policy lookup with both jurisdictions it was keyed on, the delegator's, the user's or the dependant's, and the institution's market, and the act type, and, for an irreversible step, the dual-control approval token of section 7.2. The substitution component verifies the signature with the task service's public key and checks the context itself; it has no route to the task service, the envelope store or the policy layer and needs none. The context is short-lived by construction: valid no longer than the placement window the catalogue sets per market (illustrative: two minutes), it names the envelope version it was issued against and the flag its policy lookup returned, it carries as its own expiry the earlier of the envelope's validity window end and the end of any dependant's delegation the step's aliases reference, so it dies at that end as it dies on an edit, and it dies with any edit to the envelope or any flip of that flag. Every delegation record a step's aliases reference is a checked authority, not a switch: the substitutor's ordered checks include that every delegation record the alias's owner references is live, and revocation or expiry of a dependant's delegation voids every context and token of every step that names her aliases, exactly as an envelope edit does, after which those steps re-classify and a step now without authority becomes a user step for her; the validity of a step naming her aliases is the earlier of the envelope's window and her delegation's validity, and an extension of the window never extends past her delegation. An extension, a narrowing or a policy flag flip voids every context and every approval token the task has issued: the task service returns each running step to ready, so no step stays running through an edit, re-checks each checking step against the new version before it can fire, re-classifies each step a flip touched before it can run again, and issues a fresh context only against the new version; a step whose placement request is already inside its placement window completes that placement under the version its context names, and nothing more; and a bounced user step in progress on the device is not re-classified by any new version, task-wide or step-scoped, nor by a flip, until its bounce record closes, done, declined or expired, so the user's own act is never doubled by ours. A step returned to ready this way keeps its claimed card and its surface session, marked "envelope changed; re-issuing", with the surface paused, no input forwarded and no placement possible, until the fresh context exists, at which point it returns to running, or to checking first if it is irreversible. For a reversible step the controller also fetches from the task service an envelope version stamp, signed and dated inside the window, and sends it with the request, so a placement is never checked against a superseded envelope; the full check list is the substitutor's interface in 03.

4.7The device surface

The user's own device is the fourth surface and the only one we do not run. It is the user's app or logged-in web session, bound to the user record as the device_binding of section 10.2, and it is where a user step lands in one of the four bounce forms, where the plan summary and the envelope are confirmed with one tap, where the envelope is extended or narrowed, where a question is answered, where the ledger and the outcome report are read, and where the verdict is given. It is authenticated whatever channel the conversation runs in: a chat-only front door carries the prompt and a link, and the tap happens in the app or the web view, as DR-001 fixes, so a thread bound only to a phone number never approves anything. Every response it returns is signed by the bound device and validated by the task service against the binding before it becomes an event, and the device's key signs only after the device's own unlock factor, a biometric or the device passcode, for every tap, so possession of the phone alone signs nothing. A suspension of the binding is available from the web login and from any channel, and needs only the login, because it can only stop acts and never authorise one: it suspends the binding at once, voids every outstanding prompt, context and token, and moves every task that holds authority, one in active, awaiting-user or awaiting-third-party, to awaiting-authority on the nudge schedule by expire-authority (section 5.2), while a task in received or planning, which holds no authority yet, and a task in awaiting-verdict, whose verdict tap is deferred to the replacement binding, wait in their state; a suspended device's late responses are refused against the binding, and a replacement binding needs the same identity rail proof as the first. The device form, by which we ask for a value we know we need rather than asking in chat, is rendered here too: its fields post straight to the vault's edge proxy, and the alias comes back to the task service as a received event and a ledger line. Binding a device, a replacement included, is an account act performed on this surface through an identity rail the market's adapter accepts, as 01 fixes, and not a step of any task: a task that needs it waits in awaiting-authority on the nudge schedule until the bound device exists to give the authorising tap, and nothing executes on a surface of ours meanwhile.
