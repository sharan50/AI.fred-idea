8The evidence pipeline

No raw artefact is ever human-viewable, and the pipeline that guarantees it is part of the harness, not a later clean-up. Every surface emits screenshots, DOM captures, recordings, transcripts and call metadata into four stages: capture, redact, verify, store. Raw artefacts exist only in the pipeline's memory; a person may view an artefact only after verify has passed it, only for audit, dispute or a dual-control check (section 7.2), with the viewing logged. The evidence system is a leak path in its own right, and the threat model in 03 treats it as one; the decision is DR-009, the redaction contract is artefact redaction in 03.

8.1The four stages

Capture takes the raw artefact from the surface together with the placement registry the substitution component's receipts built for the step: which alias went into which field rectangle, which audio window, which message occurrences; an artefact without its registry is not accepted. Redact applies the five artefact masks in the fixed order 03 specifies: coordinate masks from every registry entry, with a margin, from the frame of placement to the frame of navigation; known-value masks by keyed digest over text layers, document captures and transcripts, with spoken digits normalised; audio windows, every known value and every class-format match replaced with silence, the allowlist run over the pipeline's own batch transcript and every span a worker flagged with the possible-exposure control masked with its incident reference, there being no live transcriber and no placement on a call to mask (DR-021), with a transcript marker naming the alias for a match and "span masked" for a withheld span; format masks on every frame and transcript, digit runs beyond the threshold and any run matching a class format of Table 3.1 in 03 or the party's reference shapes; and mask-map masks, the image and canvas regions the party's mask map names and, on an authenticated portal's frame, every run outside the step's whitelist. Aliases replace matches in transcripts and document captures as the by-product, so a transcript reads {{dob}} where the value was. Verify is a second, independent pass by a separately built service with its own recognisers and its own digest set under its own key, which scans the redacted artefact for any known value, any run in a class format, on a portal's frame any run outside the whitelist and, on a recording or a transcript, any audible or readable span the allowlist would not clear, as it checks the mask map, and fails closed: a detection quarantines the artefact in memory, opens an incident naming only the alias and the rule that missed, re-runs redaction with wider masks, and after a small fixed number of attempts drops the raw material and records "evidence withheld". Store writes the masked artefact with its step reference, its redaction manifest and the verifier's signature over its hash, after which it is viewable for audit and dispute by a person whose viewing is itself a ledger event.

Redact and verify share no code, so a defect in one does not hide in the other, and the pipeline holds no vault SDK: it holds the maskers' digest set and queries the hash service, never the values, so a compromise of the pipeline yields masks and digests, not data. Retention periods are per market and belong to the legal pack.

8.2The data contract

The pipeline is built against a contract, not stage names. Capture points are fixed per surface by the catalogue's evidence_expected: the browser controller captures a frame and a document snapshot before each submit and after each response; the telephony controller delivers both raw legs from the call platform and the call metadata; the relay delivers the alias draft and the delivery receipt; the device surface delivers its signed response. An artefact is identified as ev_<task>_<step>_<kind> and carries the step reference, the capture point, the surface's clock, the placement registry (placement id, alias, rectangle or window or occurrence, frame or time span) and, once redacted, the manifest: one entry per mask applied, in aliases, naming the rule (coordinate, digest, audio window, format, mask map), the region and the registry entry it came from. The store encrypts each artefact under a key held per task in the market's region, released to the viewer of 03's viewer table only, and keeps it for the retention the market's legal pack sets; artefacts attach to outcome records by identifier and the outcome record never embeds one. The viewer renders from the store, never from the pipeline, and writes an evidence-opened ledger line with the viewer's role. Audio has two sources and one rule: a voice note is transcribed inside the vault's edge proxy and its audio enters the vault as a document alias, so it is never an evidence artefact and the pipeline receives only the alias transcript and the time spans of detected values; a call's audio is captured from the call platform, in the market's region, and exists raw only in the pipeline's memory until the verifier passes the redacted recording, the transcriber writing an alias for any value a far party stated unasked and the exposure opening an incident.

8.3The hash service

The hash service is the second of the two places the vault SDK exists, and it exists so that no component in the alias zone ever computes a known-value match itself. It lives in the real-value zone, in region, with two vault credentials and no detokenise right: digest-only, for the digest sets below, and tokenise-only for the third door of 03, by which a value a surface echoes that matches no alias, an account number the institution issues, becomes a new alias with a "received" ledger line. It holds the keys: for every value in the vault it computes a keyed digest of each normalised form Table 3.1 in 03 names per class (spaced and unspaced, hyphenated, spoken digit by digit, case-folded, every partial form), and it issues two digest sets under two keys, one to the maskers on the surfaces and in the redactor and one to the verifier, rotating both with the digests on a cadence the legal pack sets (illustrative: daily, and at once on any incident). Neither key leaves it. A masker holds its digest set and obtains the digest of each candidate window it scans from the hash service over a batched call rate-limited to a frame's or a segment's volume, so a masker compromised end to end can match what is on its own screen and nothing beyond it; a breach of the rate limit is one of the three monitored alarms of section 5.3. For the alias zone it offers one further operation, the screen: a component sends a candidate text, a card payload at generation, a result field or a free line at entry, a fact before it is written, a log line before commit, and the hash service runs over it the same four detectors the vault's edge proxy runs (section 10.1), identifier pattern, known-value digest, the user's declaration where the field is declared sensitive, and the dictionary-based health and legal term detector, whose policy is one artefact shipped to the proxy and to the hash service alike; the screen's identifier-pattern detector carries the date pattern, dates in every numeric order and with month names, and the amount pattern beside the identifier patterns of section 10.1, so a date of birth or a sum that reaches a text written on our side has a pattern to match even before it has a digest; the text is held in memory for the call and never stored, and no digest leaves. For text written on our side, a worker's variance note or failed-final line, a question's answer of any kind, an onboarding answer, a fact a closed step writes, a connector pull, the screen tokenises every match through its tokenise-only credential, with a "received" ledger line per alias, and returns the text with aliases in place, and the caller stores only what the call returned. For a payload that should already be alias-only, a card payload or a log line, a match is a defect: the screen returns reject naming the class matched together with the screened text, and the caller keeps at most the screened text and opens an incident. The hash service holds a real value only in memory while it screens a text, which is why section 1 counts it in the real-value zone; it holds no detokenise right, so the worst it can yield is digests and new aliases.
