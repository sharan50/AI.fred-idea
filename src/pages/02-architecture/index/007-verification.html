7Verification

Verification is the control loop, and it runs before "done" is ever shown to the user: every step closes with exactly one outcome code from a fixed vocabulary and the structured fields that code requires. The test is the expected outcome written on the step record before execution, so the executor never decides after the fact what success meant. Artefacts attach for audit and dispute only. Outcome codes are not ground truth of quality; the user's verdict is, the improvement loop in the verdict system in 01, split from the control loop by DR-002. The vocabulary is the table on the worker console page; this section specifies the mechanics around it.

7.1Closing a step

An ai step is closed by the harness, which runs the verifier the catalogue entry names over the surface's state after execution, returned by the controller as the masked text and accessibility layer of the page, the masked DOM capture, the delivery receipt or a calendar the harness can read: a reference in the expected form, a confirmation phrase the template names, an entry that now exists. The reference form and the expected pattern come from the template's expected-outcome text, filled by the planner with the step's particulars, so the verifier compares against a statement written before the step ran. A human step is closed by the worker's structured result on the task card, which the console will not submit until the required fields are present. A user step is closed by the device's signed response (section 3.2). The outcome record names the closing party and, for a dual-controlled step, the checking party.

A variance is never an envelope line: anything that would breach one, a fee against this task's spend cap of zero included, closes blocked-outside-envelope with the line it would breach, regardless of how small. The code chosen has consequences the task service applies and a worker never chooses. blocked-needs-user creates a user step. blocked-outside-envelope always creates an extension request naming the line, one per task per envelope version for a given line: a closure after the validity window has ended names the window as the line, and the request it creates is the task's renewal request, which the task service already raised on the tick (section 5.4), so the closure attaches to the request that stands rather than raising a second; the request's prompt on the device offers "do this yourself" where the catalogue names a user-act form for the step, and accepting that turns the step into a user step, which is the one way the code produces one. blocked-third-party sets a response window and, when nothing else is open, awaiting-third-party. failed-retryable re-queues the step on the same route: it returns to ready at once with a fresh deadline, to the pool rather than the worker who closed it, and the catalogue's retry cap (illustrative: two) bounds the closures, after which the task service closes it failed-final; a placement retried inside a step carries the runner's retry token up to the same cap. A step with a fired marker (section 7.2) never closes failed-retryable, so an irreversible act is at most once: an unconfirmed fire, a submit that times out, a call that drops after the committing sentence began, a release the receiving server never confirmed, a committing placement whose receipt is not-confirmed (section 4.5), closes failed-final with the fired marker as its evidence, and the re-plan issues a verification step, a status enquiry with the party, before any repeat. failed-final re-plans. refused-policy carries the policy reference and re-plans, which routes around the act or bounces it.

7.2Dual control

Nothing irreversible inside the envelope fires until the other party has checked it. The executing party prepares the act and the step moves to checking with a check deadline from Table 2.8. The checker receives the act in one sentence, the envelope lines it touches, rendered as presence and comparison like a card's, with the line "remaining cap: sufficient for this act: yes" computed by the task service from the cap less the task's committed-spend total and never the amount, the expected outcome and, as the evidence, the live masked stream of the surface for the step, where the surface has one, or an artefact the evidence store holds after verify has passed it, never material from the pipeline before verify; the checker's viewing of the stream is a ledger event, "a checker saw the masked stream for step n", though the check itself is not one; and the checker approves or declines, and cannot edit. Where the prepared act submits a form that carries an alias, a party-issued reference or the user's details, the checker sees those fields as dots and any attachment by its document-alias name and count, and checks the non-sensitive fields, a deadline or a contravention code, in clear; the alias fields are checked by the browser controller's field-match check, which asks the hash service whether the form field's digest equals the digest of the alias the step names, the notice's {{reference.council}} in the legal journey of 01, and the check panel shows the answer as a line, "reference matches the notice: yes", never the value. On approval the step returns to running with an approval token: a record signed by the task service's key that carries the check id, the step id, the envelope version the check was made against, the checker's identity, a hash of the act as checked and an expiry equal to the step deadline or the envelope's validity window, whichever is sooner. The task service keeps a committed-spend total per task, fired acts plus reserved acts: a token reserves its act's amount when it is issued, the substitutor and the checker test each act against the remaining cap and never against the cap alone, so two steps each under the cap cannot together exceed it, and a reservation is released only when the step closes without a fired marker. A token is single-use: the controller consumes it before the committing action is issued and writes the step's fired marker, a submit issued, a release issued, a committing key sent, the committing utterance's start marker, before the action leaves the surface, and for a committing placement the substitutor writes the marker before the first character is delivered (section 4.5); the substitutor refuses a consumed token. A surface refuses to fire an irreversible act without a token naming the current envelope version, and the substitutor refuses a placement for an irreversible step without one, so an approval never outlives its check, fires at most once, and dual control is enforced at the boundary and not only at the console. An envelope edit or a policy flag flip voids every outstanding token and context: a step holding a token returns to ready and must pass checking again, and a step already in checking is re-checked against the new version (section 4.6); a withdrawal voids the tokens and cancels the step. The outcome record carries both identities. On decline the step returns to ready with the checker's reason, or re-plans if the reason says the route is wrong.

Who checks whom follows the tiers in 04. When the AI executes an irreversible step, an L3 worker checks, from a check card routed to the L3 checking seat staffed on every shift. When a worker executes one, the worker is L2 or L3 (L3 alone under the pilot's scope block; never L1), and the AI checks in a separate model call that receives the envelope, the act, the expected outcome and the masked evidence and no conversation history, returns approve or decline with a reason in a schema the harness validates, and is stored with the token; a model call that errors, times out or fails validation is a decline with the reason "checker unavailable", and the step returns to ready. The checker is never the executor and never a worker who executed or prepared any step of the same task; a check that passes its deadline goes to the exceptions board and the act does not fire. On a call the prepared act is the committing sentence: the step enters checking with the sentence not yet shown to the worker, the telephony controller holds the line, which is the one thing a surface does for a step not in running, and when the token arrives the step returns to running and the sentence is released for the worker to read as written; no placement and no committing act happens in between, and on this surface the closure is the token and the release rather than a gate in the media path (DR-021). The worker's view is on the worker console page; the decision is DR-004.

7.3The envelope record and the ledger entry

The envelope is a versioned record; the user's view of it is the envelope in 01. The proposal is version 1, the planner's unsigned draft, and it carries no authority; authorisation and every later edit produce a new version signed by the user's bound device, no other device signs one, and a version is authority only once signed. Steps carry the version they were classified against, so a step whose version is older than the current envelope is re-classified before it runs. Every line, a party, an act, an identity act, a sub-cap, carries a scope: task, or one step's id. The scope has three sources: a one-tap approval of a step's request produces a line scoped to the step the request named; a one-tap approval of a task-level request the task service raises, the renewal of the validity window (section 5.4), produces a task-wide line; and the user's own edit produces a task-wide line. Classification re-runs for every step on any new version, while a step-scoped line admits only its step. The two act lists use the identifiers of Table 2.5 and validate against them; the identity acts are the seven 01 names, and an act the jurisdiction flags bounce is never delegated even if the user ticks it.

The ledger is an append-only store in the market's region, replicated within it, retained for the task's life plus the period the market's legal pack sets, and read by the device surface per task, as a subscription while a task is open, and as an export the user may take at any time, which carries the record form and the rendered lines together. Rendering is by locale from fields, never from free text, so "the country's plain words" is a rendering table per market and not a rewrite. A custody or reconciled line is produced by a process the hash service's owner runs against the provider's exported access log: every detokenisation in the provider's log is matched to a placement id, and a detokenisation with no placement, or a placement with no log entry, opens an incident and writes a line the user can read, per DR-008.

7.4The verdict identifiers

The verdict is the user's one tap and closes every task; its words and their order are the verdict system in 01. In the event log and the outcome record it is one of five identifiers: done-satisfied, done-rough, wrong-outcome, could-not-complete and withdrawn. lapsed is a state the system assigns and never a verdict identifier, so the five-option ceiling holds in the data as in the interface. The verdict joined to the step trace is the improvement measurement, and every product change is judged against verdict rates per task type.
