3The classifier

Every step gets exactly one class, one reversibility and one route, by a rule that is the same in every document of this publication. The classes are ai (executed by the harness on a surface, without a person), human (executed by a worker from a task card) and user (bounced to the user's own device). The class says who executes; the reversibility and the envelope say how. An undo route the catalogue names counts under the test below only where the envelope admits the undo's act, because the undo is an act of ours like any other and nothing of ours runs outside the envelope: where the envelope does not admit it, the step is irreversible for that task, whatever the catalogue's default. Task 12's envelope keeps cancel beside schedule, so its booking step stays reversible (sections 3.2 and 7.3). The mail relay is open to a step only where the user has granted their mailbox's send scope, which the classifier reads as it reads the scope block; a mail step for a user without it is the user's, with a draft the harness wrote (DR-018).

TestThe point-of-no-return test: "Can we return the world to its prior state by our own action, at no cost to the user and without a third party's consent? If not, the step is irreversible."

RuleIrreversible and outside the envelope: bounce to the user's device. Irreversible and inside it: dual control. Reversible and inside it: execute (ai or human per classifier). Anything outside the envelope that is reversible still cannot be executed by us; it becomes an extension request.

As a lookup, two questions give four routes, and none has us executing outside the envelope:

Reversible and inside: execute
ai or human, per the classifier.
Reversible and outside: extension-request
Nobody, until the user extends the envelope from their device.
Irreversible and inside: dual-control
ai or human executes; the other party checks before it fires.
Irreversible and outside: bounce
The user, on their own device, in one of the four forms in section 3.1.

3.1Choosing between the three classes

A step is user in three cases, checked in this order. First, the act needs a factor only the user holds: a one-time code, a biometric, a UPI or card PIN, a bank-app approval; these are never ours by design, {{otp}} is never an alias, and no flag table is consulted. Second, the policy layer returns bounce or prohibited pending for the step type. The lookup has one interface, keyed by jurisdiction and the step's act type (Table 2.5), and for an act toward an institution it is made twice: once under the delegator's jurisdiction, for the user's own aliases the user's jurisdiction, the market on the request, and for a dependant's aliases the jurisdiction her delegation record carries, and once under the institution's market, which its party directory entry records (section 3.3); the stricter result governs, prohibited pending over bounce over delegated, a missing row in either evaluates to bounce, and the signed step context names both jurisdictions used (section 4.6). Bounce wins even when the user ticked the identity act, and prohibited pending closes the step refused-policy before anyone attempts it; a signature is decided here, as an identity act whose flag in the policy flag table in 03 reads bounce today. Third, the step is irreversible and outside the envelope. The alias class and the surface are checked after the flag, by the substitutor at placement and by the classifier when it stamps the surface.

Between ai and human the rule is computable. A step is ai when its catalogue entry lists ai among the executor classes allowed, its surface is one the legal pack's scope block opens to ai steps, the party directory marks that party's site or line as machine-navigable for that step type (a form or an interface the ai executor of section 3.4 has passed conformance on, recorded per party and step type), and the entry names a verifier the harness can run on the surface's state after execution. Otherwise it is human: a conversation with a person on the far end, a form the executor has not passed on, a queue that needs a voice. The pilot's restrictions are configuration, not a second classifier: the scope block names the surfaces open to ai steps (the managed browser alone in the pilot), whether the harness may compose mail (off in the pilot), the pool that may execute an irreversible human step (L3 alone in the pilot) and the verticals open; the classifier and the routing rule read it before the catalogue entry, and a step that would be ai on a closed surface becomes human. The judgement stays in the plan; the worker gets a script.

A user step bounces to the device surface in one of four forms, and the catalogue entry records which; the device's signed response closes it, and the device bounce-back in 03 specifies the device side. In the approval form the user's one tap confirms an envelope, an extension, or an irreversible act outside the envelope; that third case is a device-signed envelope extension whose new line carries the scope of that step's id, which produces a new envelope version; classification re-runs for every step on the new version, the step-scoped line admits only its step, so that step is re-classified as inside and fires under dual control, and the ledger line names the version it fired under. Nothing we perform runs on the tap alone. In the user-act form the user performs the act on their own device, in the institution's app or session, with our script on the device and a callback to the task, and we record only the device's signed outcome. In the one-time-code form the user enters a code on their own device; where the code must land on a surface of ours it travels over a one-shot channel into the surface's driver under a placement record, painted as dots, and is never vaulted, as 03 specifies. In the do-this-yourself form the user is handed a precise instruction because a jurisdiction reserves the act to them, shown at plan time as "you will do this yourself".

The classifier is deterministic wherever it can be. The step-type catalogue's schema is core; its entries per market ship in the legal pack (section 6.2) and carry what section 3.3 specifies. The model proposes a step type; the catalogue and the policy layer decide. An unknown step type, one the planner proposes and the catalogue does not carry, is classified irreversible and outside the envelope, so it bounces in the do-this-yourself form with the planner's purpose sentence as the instruction, and the plan continues when the device reports it done. That bounce is also how the catalogue grows: the task service writes a catalogue-request event carrying the proposed step type and the market, the legal pack's owner drafts the entry, and it ships in the next signed legal pack. Nothing is hot-patched into a running catalogue.

3.4The ai executor

An ai step is executed by the ai executor, the planner's model runtime acting only through a surface controller: it never holds a session, a call or a message itself, and it has no route to the substitutor, the vault or the console. The step runner starts the step, obtains the signed step context and opens the surface through its controller; the executor then works in a loop of observe and act. It observes the masked text and accessibility layer of the page, the delayed masked transcript of a call or the alias draft of a message, never pixels and never a raw layer. It acts through a fixed tool set per surface: for the browser, navigate within the party's declared domains, focus a field by accessibility node, type non-sensitive text, scroll, click a control the party's page map names as navigation, request a placement of an alias into the focused field, and submit or click a committing control; for telephony, in the pilot none, since ai steps run on the browser alone; for the relay, compose an alias draft when the scope block permits. Every action carries an allow-list check against the step type's entry and the step context: the origin must be on the party's domains, the field kind must match the alias class for a placement, a click is a committing action unless the party directory's page map names the control as navigation, a submit or a committing click on a party's domain is treated as irreversible unless the entry names the undo route for it and so needs the approval token whatever the step's classification, which a reversible step never holds, so on a reversible step the controller refuses every click outside the navigation map and every submit, the controller checks the envelope's validity window and version at every such action and not only at a placement, and an action outside the list is refused and logged. The target descriptor the executor passes to the controller for a placement is the origin and the accessibility node of the field it has focused, which the controller completes with the session and frame. Prompt injection from the page is bounded the same way: the page's text is data, the tool set is closed, the allow-list is enforced outside the model, a committing act on a page reached inside a reversible step cannot fire because no token exists for it and a hostile page cannot mint one, and the model runtime's separately deployed detector refuses to act on a candidate real value in its input. A step the executor cannot progress in its step deadline closes failed-retryable or, with the alternatives it tried, failed-final; a step whose controller has written the fired marker of section 7.2 closes failed-final only, with the marker as its evidence, never failed-retryable (section 7.1); and the executor never falls back to a person mid-step: a route that needs a person is a human step the planner issues.
