2The planner

A plan and an envelope proposal are the planner's output, and its contract is that neither contains a real value. Its inputs are the normalised request, the context-store facts that bear on it (each with confidence and validity), the step templates, which are the catalogue entries of section 3.3 for the step types the task type in the taxonomy in 01 names (the planner's template library that 04 refers to is that part of the catalogue), the flag table and scope block of the user's market and, when re-planning, the task's envelope and closed steps. The planner is a model with a schema-validated output, and the harness rejects a plan that fails validation: each rejection is a recorded plan-rejected event, the planner is re-run with the validator's errors, up to three attempts (illustrative) inside the planning deadline, and at the cap, or when the deadline expires first, the task service acts as section 5.3 specifies for a planning deadline. The model runtime has no vault SDK and the request reaches it already tokenised, so the planner cannot leak what it never held.

2.1Input and output

A plan is an ordered list of steps, each carrying a one-sentence purpose, a proposed step type from the catalogue, a proposed class, the surface, the party, the act type, the fields it needs as aliases, the non-sensitive facts it needs in clear, the script drawn from the step template, an expected outcome written as a testable statement, a deadline, a reversibility hint and an after list. The planner proposes; the classifier stamps the authoritative class, reversibility and route. Ordering is the after list, not the position in the plan: a step is ready when every step in its after list is closed, so steps with no dependency between them may run at once, limited to one running step per surface per task (one browser session, one call, one message in flight), and the list order is what the user reads and the tiebreak for which ready step is offered first. Where a step type carries a specialist part and a reversible remainder, as the routing rule in 04 describes, the plan holds both from the start with the remainder after the specialist step; that is ordinary progression, not a re-plan.

The envelope proposal is computed, not guessed, and it is the planner's unsigned draft, which carries no authority until the user's bound device signs it (section 7.3). Each step type's catalogue entry names the act it commits, the alias classes it places and the role of the party it addresses; the proposal is the union of those acts, the parties the plan names, resolved in the party directory of section 3.3, the smallest spend cap that covers the fees the catalogue entries and the context store predict (zero when none), a per-step sub-cap on each step that carries a fee, the identity acts the steps imply, and a validity window from the task type's row in the timer table of section 5.3. Anything above the user's standing preferences is marked. The cap is one currency, the user's market's; a step that would pay in another currency is outside the envelope until an extension names that currency and amount. The plain-words plan summary goes to the user's device with the envelope, and no step executes before the user authorises them.

  "summary": "Book the earliest cardiology appointment next week

Every plan records the facts it relied on and their confidence at the time, so when a fact later changes the task service can find every open plan that depended on it. The illustrative task on this page and the worker console page is task 12, six steps, whose third step is a booking call that places nothing, because no value from the vault reaches a call (4.2).

2.2Re-planning triggers

Re-planning is a recorded event, never a silent edit, and it never touches a step that has already fired. A new plan version carries supersedes naming the old one. A step the new plan no longer needs ends in step state cancelled with the outcome code superseded and the superseding step named; a step in running is allowed to close first; a step in checking that the new plan keeps continues its check under the new plan version; a step in checking that the new plan removes is cancelled with its approval token voided; and the claimed cards of cancelled steps leave the console with the cancellation shown. The triggers are fixed, listed here once and referenced everywhere else:

  • A step closes failed-final: the route is exhausted and the planner finds another, or reports that none exists.
  • A step closes refused-policy: the planner routes around the act, bounces it where a bounce is lawful, or reports what was and was not done.
  • A checker declines in dual control with a reason that says the route is wrong (a decline on the act itself returns the step to ready without a re-plan).
  • The user approves an envelope extension: classification re-runs for every step on the new version, because a step that was outside may now be inside; a line a one-tap approval of a step's request produced carries the scope of that step and admits only it, while a line the user's own edit produced is task-wide (section 7.3), so an approval scoped to one step and a re-run for every step are the same event. A renewal of the validity window after expire-authority (section 5.4) is such an extension: the task service raised the request for the task and no step raised it, so its line is task-wide, and the re-plan re-issues the steps the expiry cancelled. A bounced user step in progress on the device is not re-classified by any new version, task-wide or step-scoped, until its bounce record closes, done, declined or expired, so an extension never turns an act the user is performing into one we perform beside it.
  • A policy flag flips during a task, or the user narrows the envelope, which applies the same way (DR-003): the flip voids the context and the token of every step not yet fired, exactly as an envelope edit does (section 4.6), every such step is re-classified against the new flag before it can run again, a step in checking is re-checked, and a step now outside or now flagged bounce becomes an extension request or a user step. A step whose placement request is already inside its placement window completes that placement under the version its context names, and nothing more; and a bounced user step in progress on the device is not re-classified by a flip, any more than by a new envelope version, until its bounce record closes, done, declined or expired, so the user's own act on the device is never doubled by ours.
  • A blocked-third-party response window expires: the task service re-plans on the tick; a new route applies escalate-resume, and no route sets a holding window and puts the task on the exceptions board (section 5.3).
  • A lapsed task is revived by a user message: it re-enters planning so that stale steps are re-checked against the world as it now is. The envelope chain continues as a new version, re-proposed unsigned by the planner for one-tap confirmation and carrying no authority until the user's bound device signs it, because no authority survives lapsed.
  • A fact the plan relied on is corrected, contradicted or loses validity or confidence mid-task (section 9.2): the affected steps are re-planned before they run, and a blocked-needs-user step that resolves with a new fact is the common case.

Nothing else re-plans. The verdict wrong-outcome is not a re-plan of the parent: the parent closes with that verdict and a linked child task opens in planning, inheriting the parent's request record, its closed steps and outcome records as read-only trace, its envelope as the proposal template, and a link in both directions; the child's envelope is confirmed afresh. A worker cannot change a plan from the console: a worker who finds the script does not fit closes the step failed-final with the reason "script does not fit" and the alternatives-tried checklist, or blocked-needs-user where the gap is a fact only the user holds, and the triggers above do the rest.
