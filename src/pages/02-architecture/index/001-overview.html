1Overview

The harness is a control system wrapped around a planner, not a chat model with tools attached. A request enters through one gateway, becomes a plan of classified steps, and each step executes on a surface we own, closes with a code from a fixed vocabulary and leaves behind a redacted artefact. Every component of ours outside the real-value zone handles aliases only, and the zone holds four of ours: the substitution component, the three surface controllers with their surfaces, the evidence pipeline, and the hash service while it screens a text. A real value exists on our side only inside the real-value zone of Figure 2.1: in the vault and its edge proxy, which are bought and hold the bytes of one message or one rail response until they are tokenised; in the substitution component, in memory for one placement; on the surface field, call leg or outbound store it has just placed into, for as long as the third party takes to consume it; in the evidence pipeline, in memory until redaction is verified; and in the hash service, in memory while it screens a text written on our side (section 8.3). Every task sits in one named state with a live timer until a recorded transition ends it. Those sentences are the architecture; the rest of this page is their consequences.

Vocabulary: a task is one user request from intake to a terminal state; a step is the unit of execution and classification; a worker is a person on our side who executes a step from a task card; the user is the customer who delegated it. The worker console specifies what a worker sees; the alias model in 03 and the threat model in 03 specify and close the trust mechanics this page enforces.

Each component has one responsibility, and the boundaries between them are where the guarantees live. The components are these, and sections 2 to 10 specify them: the vault's edge proxy, the provider's (section 10.1); the intake gateway (section 10); the planner (section 2) and the ai executor that runs its steps (section 3.4); the classifier (section 3) and the step-type catalogue it reads (section 3.3); the context store (section 9); the envelope and the policy layer (sections 7.3 and 3); the task service, in the alias zone, which holds the state machine (section 5) and the step runner (section 4.5) and issues the signed step context (section 4.6); the passport adapters (section 6); the worker console (its own page); the three execution surfaces, each with its controller, in the real-value zone (sections 4 and 4.5); the substitution component (section 4, specified in full in 03); the evidence pipeline (section 8) and the hash service (section 8.3); the access ledger (section 7.3); and the device surface (section 4.7), the user's own app or logged-in web session, which receives bounces, one-tap authorisations, envelope edits and the verdict, authenticated whatever channel the conversation runs in. The policy layer's mechanism is core but its table is the policy flag table in 03; the user's device is where the access ledger in 03 and the verdict live.

Three boundaries carry the guarantees. The entry boundary is the vault's edge proxy: the channel webhook, the device form and every rail response terminate there, so the intake gateway and the passport adapters receive aliases, hold no raw byte, hold no vault SDK and have no network route to the vault. The edge of the alias zone encloses every component that handles aliases; the vault SDK exists in exactly two places of ours, the substitution component and the hash service, so nothing in the alias zone can detokenise or compute a known-value match. The user's device is where authority originates and where irreversible acts outside the envelope are performed by the user, never by us. The vault is bought (DR-008), outside our software and inside the real-value zone: its regional hosting is part of the residency answer, and a boundary we do not operate is one our staff cannot cross. No host in the real-value zone allows interactive access in production, and deployment to it is by signed build under a two-person rule; break-glass access needs two named approvers, first drains the host of live sessions and zeroes its memory, is time-boxed, and writes an incident and a ledger-level event on every task that had a session on the host, as the threat model in 03 fixes for the whole zone. The build order is the build sequence in 06; the build-versus-buy line is the build-versus-buy table in 06.
