4Dual control from the worker's side

For an irreversible step inside the envelope, whichever party executes, the other checks before it fires, and a worker meets this in two roles. Only an irreversible step enters checking: a reversible step never holds a token, and there is no gate to cut a commitment on one (DR-021), so the card carries no committing sentence and the caption says what the step may not agree, "this step commits nothing", and never brings it here (section 1). As executor, and only as an L2 or L3 worker (L3 alone in the pilot; L1 never fires an irreversible step, per the tiers in 04), the worker prepares the act from the card as usual, but the control that would fire it reads "Send for check" rather than "Fire"; pressing it moves the step to checking, freezes the prepared act, starts the check deadline and shows the worker a waiting state with the timer. On a call the prepared act is the committing sentence: it stays hidden on the card while the telephony controller holds the line, and it is released to the worker only when the check is on the record. The checker for a worker-executed step is the AI, in a separate model call that receives the act in one sentence, the envelope lines it touches, the expected outcome and, as the evidence, the masked text layer of the live surface for the step or an artefact the store holds after verify, and no conversation history. It answers approve or decline with a reason in a schema the harness validates; a call that errors, times out or fails validation is a decline with the reason "checker unavailable". On approve the step returns to running with an approval token, a record signed by the task service that names the check, the envelope version it was checked against, the checker and its expiry, the step deadline or the envelope's validity window, whichever is sooner; the Fire control is enabled only while a valid token exists and the window is open, the surface and the substitutor refuse an irreversible act without one, the token is single-use and reserves the act's amount against the task's remaining cap, the controller consumes it and writes the step's fired marker before the act leaves the surface, and where the act is a submission the surface fires it for the worker; on a call the card releases the sentence and the worker reads it as written, and the step's fired marker is written at the release, so a withdrawal or a narrowing that arrives before it stops the sentence at once, and one that arrives after it means the act fired, which the outcome report states from the fired marker and never from the worker's result. The outcome record carries both identities. On decline the card shows the reason and the step returns to ready, or re-plans if the reason is that the route is wrong.

As checker, an L3 worker receives a check card for an AI-executed step at the checking seat staffed on every shift. It is smaller than a task card: the act in one sentence, the envelope lines it touches, as presence and comparison like a card's, with the relevant values as aliases and the line "remaining cap: sufficient for this act: yes", computed by the task service from the cap less the task's committed spend and never shown as a number, the expected outcome, the evidence, which is the live masked stream or masked leg of the surface for the step being checked, or an artefact the store holds after verify has passed it, never material from the pipeline before verify, the field-match line the browser controller supplies where the act submits a form carrying an alias, "reference matches the notice: yes", in place of the value the checker sees as dots, as dual control in 02 fixes, and two controls, approve and decline with a reason. The checker's viewing of the stream writes the ledger line "a checker saw the masked stream for step n"; the check itself writes none, because nothing was used. A checker cannot edit anything; a check is a yes or a no against the envelope and the expected outcome, and that is deliberate, because a checker who can adjust the act becomes a second executor with nobody checking them. A check card, which only ever concerns an AI-executed step, never goes to a worker who executed or prepared any step of the same task. It counts as the worker's one live card: a worker holding a task card is not offered a check card until that card closes. A check card has its own deadline; a check that passes it goes to the exceptions board as a pending dual-control check, and the act does not fire in the meantime.

Two edge cases are handled by the task service, and the console shows their effect. If the user edits the envelope, or a policy flag the step's act depends on flips, while a step is in checking, the step is re-checked against the new version: the checker sees a fresh check card marked as a re-check and the executor's waiting state says why. If either happens while a step holds an approval token in running, the token and the signed step context are voided and the step returns to ready under the same worker's claim, marked "envelope changed; re-issuing", with the surface paused; a flip also re-classifies the step before it can run again, and a step now flagged bounce leaves the console as a user step. When the fresh context exists the worker sends it for check again, and Fire stays disabled until a new token exists. A placement request already inside its placement window when the edit or the flip lands completes under the version its context names, and nothing more. If the user withdraws the task while a step is in checking or holds a token, the step is cancelled before it fires and both cards close with the cancellation shown. The rule that a worker is checked by the AI and the AI is checked by a worker is recorded in DR-004; the mechanics on the system side are in dual control in 02.
