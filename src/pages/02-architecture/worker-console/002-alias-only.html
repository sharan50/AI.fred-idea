2The alias-only rule

Aliases are all a worker ever handles, and the console enforces this by what it is built from rather than by what workers are told. The rule has three layers: what a worker can see, what a worker cannot see, and what the console technically cannot render even if someone tried.

2.1What a worker can see

  • The pseudonymous client reference, the task and step position, the pool and the timer.
  • The step, the script and the expected outcome, in plain words, generated from the step record.
  • Each field the step needs, as a typed alias such as {{name}} or {{dob}}, and the state of its placement: not yet placed, placed, refused.
  • The non-sensitive facts the step needs, as plain statements ("prefers morning appointments"), drawn from the context store by the planner, written into the step record and projected into the step sentence and the script; they are not a seventh part of the card. A speciality or department is such a fact; a diagnosis is a vaulted class and never one.
  • The surface panel, masked: a browser stream in which every substitution point, every echoed value and every run in a class format renders as dots, a call heard directly, because nothing from the vault reaches a call and there is nothing on it to mask (DR-021), a mail draft that carries aliases and an inbound message rendered with every token outside the cleared vocabulary painted.
  • The result form, the outcome codes and their required fields.

2.2What a worker cannot see

  • The user's name, address, phone number or email in the clear, or any other person's: a nominee's or a relative's name and date of birth are aliases of the same class qualified by the person, {{name.nominee}}, {{dob.nominee}}, and the relationship word is the only fact in clear. The user's are vault-held aliases, {{name}}, {{address.home}}, {{phone}} and {{email}}, placed like any other; the user is Client 4471 on every card of this task and a different number on their next task, because the pseudonym is issued per task and never derived from the user id, as 04 fixes, and a party we call is dialled from the envelope, never from a number a worker reads.
  • Any real value, on any surface, at any time. Not in a field, not in a frame, not on the call, not in a draft, not in an artefact.
  • Any other step of the task, the plan, the envelope beyond the lines the step touches, the task's history or the user's verdict. A card carries the one line of context its step needs, and a worker who needs more closes the step failed-final with the reason "script does not fit" and the alternatives tried, or blocked-needs-user where the gap is a fact only the user holds.
  • The context store. A worker never queries it; the planner draws from it into the step record.
  • The evidence store. Redacted artefacts are for audit, dispute and a dual-control check, and viewing them is a logged act by a person in a QA or escalation function or by a checker, never a worker executing a step.

2.3What the console technically cannot render

The console has no vault SDK and no route to the substitution component's output, because that component never returns a value to any caller. Every record the console receives, the step record and the card payload, is screened before it leaves the task service by the hash service's screening call in 02, which runs the four detectors the vault's edge proxy runs and never hands the task service a digest, because no component in the alias zone computes a known-value match itself; a payload the screen had to tokenise was a defect, and the task service keeps only the screened text and raises an incident; the card's free-text fields are then rendered through the allowlist default paint of section 1, the same as the relay's below, so a term no detector could name is painted rather than read. The browser stream is masked before it is encoded, inside the sandbox, so the unmasked frame never exists on the console's side of the wire. The audio the worker hears is the far party, in the ordinary way, because nothing from the vault is ever on a call: the console has no placement control on the call panel and no script line carries a value, as telephony in 02 fixes. On a call there is no placement and no mask, because no value from the vault is ever on a call: the call panel carries the line state and the script, the worker hears the far party directly, and a value the far party states unasked closes the step on the possible-exposure control. The mail composer holds aliases, and replacement happens at the relay after the worker is done; while a mail step is live the composer shows the alias draft as sent, the only copy that exists on the worker's side. An inbound message reaches the console as the relay's rendering, tokenised at the vault's edge proxy and then painted by default: every token outside the cleared vocabulary is painted, an out-of-lexicon token, a proper noun that is not a cleared term, a digit run, a class-format match, and the worker reads the painted rendering; a painted token the worker needs is asked for as a typed field sent on the task's thread, where it is tokenised on arrival, or the step closes blocked-needs-user. Copy, paste out, download, print and screenshot are disabled in the console, and the console runs on a locked station under the site controls in 04; the console is designed so that the site controls are the second line, not the first.

Two worker actions exist for the residual case, and the task service, never the worker, applies the code, as the failure modes in 03 record in the same words. A third party who asks a worker to read out or confirm a real value is answered from the script: we do not hold it, and the system will place it; a worker who is pressed records the demand in the structured result, and the task service closes the step blocked-needs-user where the user can perform the act on their own device, which is where the step type's catalogue entry names a user-act bounce form, and failed-final otherwise, with the ledger saying the demand was refused. A worker who asks to see a value, anywhere, is refused and the step is reassigned, closed refused-policy by the task service. And a worker who sees anything that looks like a real value, a form echo the masker missed or a document rendered in the clear, presses one control: it blanks the surface panel, holds the surface, opens an incident on the queue QA and escalation own, and closes the step: failed-retryable with the error class "possible exposure", so that it is reassigned to another worker, where no fired marker exists, and failed-final with the marker as its evidence where one does, the re-plan issuing a status enquiry before any repeat (section 3); the incident, not the code, carries the report. The path is named and closed in the threat model in 03; the console's job is to make the report one press away.
