1The task card

A task card is the whole of a worker's world for the duration of one step, and it is generated, not written. When a human step reaches ready, the task service's card generator projects the step record from the step record in 02 into a card, with the script the planner wrote from the step template, and offers it to the pool the step type's catalogue entry names in its pool field: L1 generalists, an L2 specialist pod for the vertical, or L3 super-trust, per the tiers in 04. Cards are offered in order of earliest step deadline, then plan order, to every signed-in worker whose tier and pod on the shift roster match the pool; a worker's identity is the console sign-in bound to their badge, and their id, w_..., is on every event they cause. A worker claims a card, the step moves to running, and the card is live until the step closes. A claim survives a dropped connection for a grace interval (illustrative: two minutes), after which the card returns to the pool as a recorded step-state event with the surface released; at shift end a live card is handed over by name under the handover rule in 04 or returned the same way, and a call in progress is finished by the worker on it first. Then it is gone: the console keeps no history a worker can open once a step closes, and a worker never holds two live cards at once.

The card has six parts, in this order, and nothing else. A header naming the pseudonymous client ("Client 4471"), the task and step position, the pool, and the live step timer. The step, in one sentence. The script: what to say or do, in order, with the moments at which a value is to be placed marked as actions rather than as text. The fields for this step, as aliases, each with a Place control and a masked rendering slot. The expected outcome, which is the test the result is checked against. And the result form, which closes the step with one outcome code and the fields that code requires. Every free-text part of the card, the step sentence, the expected outcome, the facts in clear projected into them, a question and a variance note, is rendered through the same allowlist default paint as an inbound message (section 2.3), after the hash service's screen: a token outside the cleared vocabulary is painted, and a painted token the step needs is requested as a typed field on the user's device or closes the step blocked-needs-user. The Place control is the only way a worker cues a value onto a surface, and the value never passes through the console, which never calls the substitution component. Pressing it sends the task service the step and the alias; the task service hands the tap to the step's surface controller, the browser controller, the telephony controller or the mail relay, of which the browser controller and the relay alone may write a placement request, naming the task, the step, the alias, the surface and the target (the field's accessibility node and origin in the managed browser, the frame and the field in the browser, the placeholder occurrences in the mail relay) with the signed step context, as the surface controllers in 02 and the substitutor's interface in 03 specify. The substitution component runs its checks, performs the placement directly into the surface, writes the ledger entry, and returns a receipt to the controller: placed, not-confirmed, refused naming the check that failed, or failed with a reason class. The controller closes or continues the step and the task service records the outcome; the console renders the receipt in the masked slot and has no route by which the value could arrive. On placed the script continues. On not-confirmed or failed the slot says so and offers Place again, a retry under a token the step runner issues, up to the step's cap, after which the task service closes the step failed-retryable and re-queues it; a committing placement is the exception, a payment instrument or instruction or an alias the catalogue marks consumed on placement, which needs the approval token like a submit, carries the fired marker from its first tone or character and on not-confirmed closes failed-final with the marker and no retry, as 02 fixes. On refused the worker chooses nothing: the task service closes the step refused-policy with the check named as the policy reference, the card shows the closure, and the planner re-plans or bounces the act. A worker chooses blocked-outside-envelope whenever the party asks for something the envelope's lines on the card do not cover, a fee the caption reports outside the sub-cap included, before or after a placement; a placement already made stands in the ledger and is not undone by the code. The envelope lines a card carries are presence and comparison only, never a number: a cap of zero reads "spend cap: no spend", any other cap "spend cap: set", and a fee is reported by the caption's comparison line, so no card, caption or check card carries an amount and the hash service's amount-pattern screen holds on every card payload.

Beside the card sits the surface panel: the structured task card of a modelled workflow or, for an unmodelled counterparty, the masked video stream of the managed browser; the call panel, which carries the line state and the script and no placement control at all, because no value is ever on a call (DR-021); the comparison line where the step's catalogue entry names an amount field, and on a call the amount the desk quotes, which the worker records in the result and which no component compares with the envelope's cap line, an open item in 07, and the non-committing lexicon: "held; off script" when it holds anything else, on which the worker re-phrases, and two the card carries before the worker speaks at all, nothing on this surface being able to cut what is said (DR-021): "commitment held; send for check" on an irreversible step not yet checked, which moves the step to checking, and "this step commits nothing" on a reversible step, where the step enters no check and holds no token, and a party who will not proceed without a commitment is answered by closing the step blocked-outside-envelope; or the mail composer with aliases in the text and, for an inbound message, the relay's painted rendering (section 2.3). The card's timer is the step deadline from the state machine, and it is live in the sense the invariant requires: when it expires, the step does not wait for the worker; the task service acts on the tick by the step type's on_timeout in the timers in 02, which re-queues the step with the card revoked, re-plans it or escalates it, and the card tells the worker so. How cards are pseudonymised, and what the floor around a worker looks like, is operational and specified in task cards in 04.
