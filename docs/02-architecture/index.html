1Overview

The harness is a control system wrapped around a planner, not a chat model with tools attached. A request enters through one gateway, becomes a plan of classified steps, and each step executes on a surface we own, closes with a code from a fixed vocabulary and leaves behind a redacted artefact. A real value exists on our side only inside the real-value zone of Figure 2.1: in the vault and its edge proxy, which are bought and hold the bytes of one message or one rail response until they are tokenised; in the substitution component, in memory for one placement; on the surface field, call leg or outbound store it has just placed into, for as long as the third party takes to consume it; and in the evidence pipeline, in memory until redaction is verified. Every task sits in one named state with a live timer until a recorded transition ends it. Those sentences are the architecture; the rest of this page is their consequences.

Vocabulary: a task is one user request from intake to a terminal state; a step is the unit of execution and classification; a worker is a person on our side who executes a step from a task card; the user is the customer who delegated it. The worker console specifies what a worker sees; the alias model in 03 and the threat model in 03 specify and close the trust mechanics this page enforces.

Each component has one responsibility, and the boundaries between them are where the guarantees live. The components are these, and sections 2 to 10 specify them: the vault's edge proxy, the provider's (section 10.1); the intake gateway (section 10); the planner (section 2) and the ai executor that runs its steps (section 3.4); the classifier (section 3) and the step-type catalogue it reads (section 3.3); the context store (section 9); the envelope and the policy layer (sections 7.3 and 3); the task service, in the alias zone, which holds the state machine (section 5) and the step runner (section 4.4) and issues the signed step context (section 4.5); the passport adapters (section 6); the worker console (its own page); the three execution surfaces, each with its controller, in the real-value zone (sections 4 and 4.4); the substitution component (section 4, specified in full in 03); the evidence pipeline (section 8) and the hash service (section 8.3); the access ledger (section 7.3); and the device surface (section 4.6), the user's own app or logged-in web session, which receives bounces, one-tap authorisations, envelope edits and the verdict, authenticated whatever channel the conversation runs in. The policy layer's mechanism is core but its table is the policy flag table in 03; the user's device is where the access ledger in 03 and the verdict live.

Three boundaries carry the guarantees. The entry boundary is the vault's edge proxy: the channel webhook, the device form and every rail response terminate there, so the intake gateway and the passport adapters receive aliases, hold no raw byte, hold no vault SDK and have no network route to the vault. The edge of the alias zone encloses every component that handles aliases; the vault SDK exists in exactly two places of ours, the substitution component and the hash service, so nothing in the alias zone can detokenise or compute a known-value match. The user's device is where authority originates and where irreversible acts outside the envelope are performed by the user, never by us. The vault is bought (DR-008), outside our software and inside the real-value zone: its regional hosting is part of the residency answer, and a boundary we do not operate is one our staff cannot cross. No host in the real-value zone allows interactive access in production, and deployment to it is by signed build under a two-person rule; break-glass access needs two named approvers, first drains the host of live sessions and zeroes its memory, is time-boxed, and writes an incident and a ledger-level event on every task that had a session on the host, as the threat model in 03 fixes for the whole zone. The build order is the build sequence in 06; the build-versus-buy line is the build-versus-buy table in 06.

2The planner

A plan and an envelope proposal are the planner's output, and its contract is that neither contains a real value. Its inputs are the normalised request, the context-store facts that bear on it (each with confidence and validity), the step templates, which are the catalogue entries of section 3.3 for the step types the task type in the taxonomy in 01 names (the planner's template library that 04 refers to is that part of the catalogue), the flag table and scope block of the user's market and, when re-planning, the task's envelope and closed steps. The planner is a model with a schema-validated output, and the harness rejects a plan that fails validation: each rejection is a recorded plan-rejected event, the planner is re-run with the validator's errors, up to three attempts (illustrative) inside the planning deadline, and at the cap, or when the deadline expires first, the task service acts as section 5.3 specifies for a planning deadline. The model runtime has no vault SDK and the request reaches it already tokenised, so the planner cannot leak what it never held.

2.1Input and output

A plan is an ordered list of steps, each carrying a one-sentence purpose, a proposed step type from the catalogue, a proposed class, the surface, the party, the act type, the fields it needs as aliases, the non-sensitive facts it needs in clear, the script drawn from the step template, an expected outcome written as a testable statement, a deadline, a reversibility hint and an after list. The planner proposes; the classifier stamps the authoritative class, reversibility and route. Ordering is the after list, not the position in the plan: a step is ready when every step in its after list is closed, so steps with no dependency between them may run at once, limited to one running step per surface per task (one browser session, one call, one message in flight), and the list order is what the user reads and the tiebreak for which ready step is offered first. Where a step type carries a specialist part and a reversible remainder, as the routing rule in 04 describes, the plan holds both from the start with the remainder after the specialist step; that is ordinary progression, not a re-plan.

The envelope proposal is computed, not guessed. Each step type's catalogue entry names the act it commits, the alias classes it places and the role of the party it addresses; the proposal is the union of those acts, the parties the plan names, resolved in the party directory of section 3.3, the smallest spend cap that covers the fees the catalogue entries and the context store predict (zero when none), a per-step sub-cap on each step that carries a fee, the identity acts the steps imply, and a validity window from the task type's row in the timer table of section 5.3. Anything above the user's standing preferences is marked. The cap is one currency, the user's market's; a step that would pay in another currency is outside the envelope until an extension names that currency and amount. The plain-words plan summary goes to the user's device with the envelope, and no step executes before the user authorises them.

2.2Re-planning triggers

Re-planning is a recorded event, never a silent edit, and it never touches a step that has already fired. A new plan version carries supersedes naming the old one. A step the new plan no longer needs ends in step state cancelled with the outcome code superseded and the superseding step named; a step in running is allowed to close first; a step in checking that the new plan keeps continues its check under the new plan version; a step in checking that the new plan removes is cancelled with its approval token voided; and the claimed cards of cancelled steps leave the console with the cancellation shown. The triggers are fixed, listed here once and referenced everywhere else:

  • A step closes failed-final: the route is exhausted and the planner finds another, or reports that none exists.
  • A step closes refused-policy: the planner routes around the act, bounces it where a bounce is lawful, or reports what was and was not done.
  • A checker declines in dual control with a reason that says the route is wrong (a decline on the act itself returns the step to ready without a re-plan).
  • The user approves an envelope extension: classification re-runs for every affected step, because a step that was outside may now be inside.
  • A policy flag flips during a task, or the user narrows the envelope, which applies the same way (DR-003): the flip voids the context and the token of every step not yet fired, exactly as an envelope edit does (section 4.5), every such step is re-classified against the new flag before it can run again, a step in checking is re-checked, and a step now outside or now flagged bounce becomes an extension request or a user step. A step whose placement request is already inside its placement window completes that placement under the version its context names, and nothing more.
  • A blocked-third-party response window expires: the task service re-plans on the tick; a new route applies escalate-resume, and no route sets a holding window and puts the task on the exceptions board (section 5.3).
  • A lapsed task is revived by a user message: it re-enters planning so that stale steps are re-checked against the world as it now is. The envelope chain continues as a new version, re-proposed for one-tap confirmation, because no authority survives lapsed.
  • A fact the plan relied on is corrected, contradicted or loses validity or confidence mid-task (section 9.2): the affected steps are re-planned before they run, and a blocked-needs-user step that resolves with a new fact is the common case.

Nothing else re-plans. The verdict wrong-outcome is not a re-plan of the parent: the parent closes with that verdict and a linked child task opens in planning, inheriting the parent's request record, its closed steps and outcome records as read-only trace, its envelope as the proposal template, and a link in both directions; the child's envelope is confirmed afresh. A worker cannot change a plan from the console: a worker who finds the script does not fit closes the step failed-final with the reason "script does not fit" and the alternatives-tried checklist, or blocked-needs-user where the gap is a fact only the user holds, and the triggers above do the rest.

3The classifier

Every step gets exactly one class, one reversibility and one route, by a rule that is the same in every document of this publication. The classes are ai (executed by the harness on a surface, without a person), human (executed by a worker from a task card) and user (bounced to the user's own device). The class says who executes; the reversibility and the envelope say how.

TestThe point-of-no-return test: "Can we return the world to its prior state by our own action, at no cost to the user and without a third party's consent? If not, the step is irreversible."

RuleIrreversible and outside the envelope: bounce to the user's device. Irreversible and inside it: dual control. Reversible and inside it: execute (ai or human per classifier). Anything outside the envelope that is reversible still cannot be executed by us; it becomes an extension request.

As a lookup, two questions give four routes, and none has us executing outside the envelope:

Reversible and inside: execute
ai or human, per the classifier.
Reversible and outside: extension-request
Nobody, until the user extends the envelope from their device.
Irreversible and inside: dual-control
ai or human executes; the other party checks before it fires.
Irreversible and outside: bounce
The user, on their own device, in one of the four forms in section 3.1.

3.1Choosing between the three classes

A step is user in three cases, checked in this order. First, the act needs a factor only the user holds: a one-time code, a biometric, a UPI or card PIN, a bank-app approval; these are never ours by design, {{otp}} is never an alias, and no flag table is consulted. Second, the policy layer, keyed by the user's jurisdiction and the step's act type (Table 2.5), returns bounce or prohibited pending for the step type: bounce wins even when the user ticked the identity act, and prohibited pending closes the step refused-policy before anyone attempts it; a signature is decided here, as an identity act whose flag in the policy flag table in 03 reads bounce today. A missing row evaluates to bounce. Third, the step is irreversible and outside the envelope. The alias class and the surface are checked after the flag, by the substitutor at placement and by the classifier when it stamps the surface.

Between ai and human the rule is computable. A step is ai when its catalogue entry lists ai among the executor classes allowed, its surface is one the legal pack's scope block opens to ai steps, the party directory marks that party's site or line as machine-navigable for that step type (a form or an interface the ai executor of section 3.4 has passed conformance on, recorded per party and step type), and the entry names a verifier the harness can run on the surface's state after execution. Otherwise it is human: a conversation with a person on the far end, a form the executor has not passed on, a queue that needs a voice. The pilot's restrictions are configuration, not a second classifier: the scope block names the surfaces open to ai steps (the managed browser alone in the pilot), whether the harness may compose mail (off in the pilot), the pool that may execute an irreversible human step (L3 alone in the pilot) and the verticals open; the classifier and the routing rule read it before the catalogue entry, and a step that would be ai on a closed surface becomes human. The judgement stays in the plan; the worker gets a script.

A user step bounces to the device surface in one of four forms, and the catalogue entry records which; the device's signed response closes it, and the device bounce-back in 03 specifies the device side. In the approval form the user's one tap confirms an envelope, an extension, or an irreversible act outside the envelope; that third case is a device-signed envelope extension scoped to that step, which produces a new envelope version, after which the step is re-classified as inside and fires under dual control, and the ledger line names the version it fired under. Nothing we perform runs on the tap alone. In the user-act form the user performs the act on their own device, in the institution's app or session, with our script on the device and a callback to the task, and we record only the device's signed outcome. In the one-time-code form the user enters a code on their own device; where the code must land on a surface of ours it travels over a one-shot channel into the surface's driver under a placement record, painted as dots, and is never vaulted, as 03 specifies. In the do-this-yourself form the user is handed a precise instruction because a jurisdiction reserves the act to them, shown at plan time as "you will do this yourself".

The classifier is deterministic wherever it can be. The step-type catalogue's schema is core; its entries per market ship in the legal pack (section 6.2) and carry what section 3.3 specifies. The model proposes a step type; the catalogue and the policy layer decide. An unknown step type, one the planner proposes and the catalogue does not carry, is classified irreversible and outside the envelope, so it bounces in the do-this-yourself form with the planner's purpose sentence as the instruction, and the plan continues when the device reports it done. That bounce is also how the catalogue grows: the task service writes a catalogue-request event carrying the proposed step type and the market, the legal pack's owner drafts the entry, and it ships in the next signed legal pack. Nothing is hot-patched into a running catalogue.

3.4The ai executor

An ai step is executed by the ai executor, the planner's model runtime acting only through a surface controller: it never holds a session, a call or a message itself, and it has no route to the substitutor, the vault or the console. The step runner starts the step, obtains the signed step context and opens the surface through its controller; the executor then works in a loop of observe and act. It observes the masked text and accessibility layer of the page, the delayed masked transcript of a call or the alias draft of a message, never pixels and never a raw layer. It acts through a fixed tool set per surface: for the browser, navigate within the party's declared domains, focus a field by accessibility node, type non-sensitive text, click, scroll, request a placement of an alias into the focused field, and submit; for telephony, in the pilot none, since ai steps run on the browser alone; for the relay, compose an alias draft when the scope block permits. Every action carries an allow-list check against the step type's entry and the step context: the origin must be on the party's domains, the field kind must match the alias class for a placement, a submit or a committing click on a party's domain is treated as irreversible unless the entry names the undo route for it and so needs the approval token whatever the step's classification, which a reversible step never holds, the controller checks the envelope's validity window and version at every such action and not only at a placement, and an action outside the list is refused and logged. The target descriptor the executor passes to the controller for a placement is the origin and the accessibility node of the field it has focused, which the controller completes with the session and frame. Prompt injection from the page is bounded the same way: the page's text is data, the tool set is closed, the allow-list is enforced outside the model, a committing act on a page reached inside a reversible step cannot fire because no token exists for it and a hostile page cannot mint one, and the model runtime's separately deployed detector refuses to act on a candidate real value in its input. A step the executor cannot progress in its step deadline closes failed-retryable or, with the alternatives it tried, failed-final, and never falls back to a person mid-step: a route that needs a person is a human step the planner issues.

4Execution surfaces

We own every surface, and a worker never touches a task with their own tools. Because the browser, the phone line and the mail relay are ours, the substitution component (isolated as specified in the substitution component in 03) sits at the edge of each, what a worker sees is masked, what a worker hears is muted, and the evidence is redacted before it leaves. A real value exists on a surface only between placement and the third party's consumption of it. Each surface has a controller, and only the three controllers may write a placement request: the console never calls the substitution component, and the worker's tap on Place goes from the console to the task service, which hands it to the step's surface controller, which requests the placement (section 4.4). The site controls that keep a worker's own devices out of the room are the site controls in 04; the human side of each surface, the masks and the failure modes, are the surfaces in 03.

4.1The managed browser

The managed browser runs in our environment, in the market's region, on a browser sandbox we buy; the worker sees a video stream of it and drives it by remote input through the browser controller. Navigation is allow-listed to the declared domains of the envelope's contactable parties in the party directory; anything else is blocked and logged. When a step reaches a field that needs a real value, the card offers a Place control for the alias. Pressing it sends the task service the step and the alias; the task service hands the tap to the browser controller, which writes the placement request to the substitution component's queue with the target descriptor, the page origin and the accessibility node of the field, and the signed step context. The substitution component runs the checks 03 specifies, refuses with a receipt naming the failed check and a ledger line any placement whose origin does not resolve to a contactable party, and otherwise types the value into the field with its own privileged driver. The receipt returns to the controller; the console renders "placed", "not confirmed", "refused" or "failed", never the value.

What we require of the bought sandbox is fixed by the design: a privileged driver channel the worker's client does not have, on which the substitutor's driver focuses and types; access to the document's text and accessibility tree for the masker and the executor, with read-back of field values disabled on every channel but the driver's own input events; compositor hooks so that masking happens before a frame is encoded; disabled autofill, form history, extensions, clipboard and downloads; and one session per task, meaning one profile and cookie jar created at the task's first browser step and destroyed at its close, one open site at a time, session state kept across the task's steps. Redirects are followed only within the party's declared domains and to a payment page the directory names for that party; a third-party origin a page fetches (fonts, scripts, content networks) is loaded but never navigated to; a download is written only into the vault as a document alias, never to the sandbox's disk. The per-site mask map is the party directory's entry: a list of page patterns and the image or canvas regions cleared on each, signed with the legal pack.

Masking runs on the DOM text and the accessibility tree, never on pixels, behind the fixed delay line the catalogue sets per market, and applies the four masks in the order the managed browser in 03 fixes: coordinate masks at every placement record, field-type masks, digest masks through the hash service, and region masks for image and canvas unless the mask map clears them, with the shape rule after any submit that used a placement. Beyond those, every text run on every frame is matched against the class formats of Table 3.1 in 03: identifier patterns, dates, card and account shapes, a name in a party's identity field, and the alphanumeric reference shapes the party register records for that party; a run that matches is painted. The masker delivers no text run it has not accounted for: a run it can neither match nor clear against the step's mask map is painted by default and the frame is held for re-masking, so "plain text below the threshold" is never a delivered residual. An authenticated portal, which shows balances, history, other account numbers, names and addresses that were never vaulted, is masked by default on every page the party register marks as one: every text run and every figure is painted except the fields the step's mask map whitelists as needed for the step's outcome, the capture at submit and at response is the same masked frame and a document snapshot with the same whitelist applied, the verifier of section 8.1 checks the whitelist as well as known values and formats, and the worker, a checker and QA see only the whitelisted fields; a step whose mask map whitelists nothing has no readable stream and is classified ai. The DOM capture the evidence pipeline receives is masked the same way. The threat model in 03 names each path through the screen and closes it with this mechanism; the camera and the two-person zone in 04 are the record of a failure of it, never the closure, and no frame reaches a person as a residual.

The masking fails closed, in the way 03 fixes: a frame the masker cannot clear, because the hash service is unreachable, the frame missed the delay or a region can be neither named nor covered, is not delivered; the stream shows the hold screen with a reason, the frame is re-masked with wider shape masks and re-checked by the verifier before it can leave, the ai executor may continue on the driver alone, and a human step waits with the surface held and its timer live, until the task service, if the hold outlasts the bounded wait 03 sets, closes it failed-retryable on the tick and re-queues it to another worker. A worker never sees an unmasked frame. Copy, download, print and screenshot are disabled in the console, and the stream is the only representation of the page that leaves the sandbox.

4.2Telephony

Calls run on our numbers and our call platform, with two legs, the worker's and the far party's, mixed by a media server we control. The dialler resolves the contactable party named in the envelope to a number in the party directory for the desk the step names; a worker never types a number, and a party outside the envelope cannot be dialled. When the script reaches a value the worker presses Place; the tap goes to the task service, then to the telephony controller, which requests the placement with the call, the far leg, the mode (synthesised speech or keypad tones), the spoken format and the cue, and the substitution component's media function puts the value onto the far leg. For the placement window, from the tap until the last tone or syllable is delivered plus a guard interval, the worker's leg is muted in both directions by the media server, from its own record and never from the worker's client, and the panel shows "value placed" with a timer, then "line open". The inbound leg runs behind a delay line at all times. The window is recorded as a mute event; the evidence pipeline silences the same span in the recording, and the transcriber writes the alias where the value was. The media server also records the start and end of every committing utterance on a call, the sentence a worker reads as written after dual control or the system speaks: a withdrawal or a narrowing received before the end marker stops the utterance at once, and one received after it treats the act as fired, the outcome report listing it with the media server's utterance record as the evidence; the worker's structured result is never the evidence of whether it fired.

A third party can also say a value: a clerk reading a name or a date of birth back, a bank agent reading an account number aloud, a policy number issued on the call. The delay line and the streaming transcriber therefore run on the inbound leg at all times, before, during and after any placement, in the delay buffer the catalogue sets per market: every transcribed span is matched through the hash service against the known digests and against the class formats of Table 3.1 in 03, spoken digit runs, spelled sequences, dates in every spoken order and the reference shapes the party register records for the party, and a span that matches either is replaced by a tone before it reaches the worker's ear, the panel reading "value read back, masked" for a known value and the caption "reference masked" for a format match. A free-text sensitive value in no known form that matches no class format, a medical condition spoken aloud, is closed by the dictionary-based health and legal term detector of section 10.1, which the pipeline's transcriber runs over the same delayed transcript and which mutes the span the same way. The far party hears nothing different, and each mute is a recorded ledger event. The inbound leg fails closed: when the matcher cannot keep pace with the buffer or cannot reach the hash service, the worker's inbound audio mutes, the panel shows the reason until the matcher catches up, and the mute is recorded. The script still tells a worker to ask a party issuing a new identifier to send it by the mail relay or to the user directly, but that is an instruction to a person and closes nothing: the format-match mute is the closure, the pipeline's transcriber flags any run the live buffer missed, tokenises it and opens an incident, and the threat model in 03 carries the path with that closure.

What we require of the bought call platform follows: per-leg media control with a server-side mute the client cannot lift; keypad tones and synthesised speech in each language of the market, with the spoken formats of Table 3.1 in 03 as the cue vocabulary (digit by digit, letters by name, dates in the party's expected order); the guard interval and the delay buffer set from the catalogue; a number pool and caller identity per market, provisioned as the shift line of the playbook in 06 lists; and both raw legs delivered to the evidence pipeline in region. A voice menu is navigated by the worker or the ai executor pressing keys through the controller, never with a placement, so a menu that demands an identity number is a step the script routes to a human desk or the classifier bounces. An inbound callback from an institution is answered on the task's own number, bound to the task by that number, and is a blocked-third-party step resuming, never a placement: an inbound call never receives one, per the substitutor's checks in 03.

4.3The mail relay

Outbound mail is composed with aliases, by a worker in the console or, where the scope block permits, by the ai executor, and sent from a per-task address we own: a random local part at the market's sending domain, created at the task's first mail step, alive for the task's life plus the ledger's retention so a late reply still binds, and never reused. At send time the relay, as the surface controller, sends the substitution component a placement request naming the message and the placeholder occurrences, with the signed step context; the substitution component checks, rewrites the occurrences in the relay's memory-only outbound store, writes the ledger entry, and the relay dispatches, holds the message only until the receiving server accepts it and keeps no sent copy. An attachment is a document alias in the draft that the substitutor resolves with its upload kind into the outbound store the same way. The sending domain carries the market's authentication records, and a receiving server that defers past the placement window gets nothing further: the body is dropped, the line closes "not confirmed", the step closes failed-retryable with a retry token, and the next attempt is a fresh composition and a fresh placement. The evidence copy is the alias draft plus the delivery receipt, and the draft a worker sees never contains a value. Inbound mail to a task address, attachments included, terminates at the vault's edge proxy like any other channel and reaches the intake gateway tokenised, bound to the task by the address; a reply is threaded to the message it answers by the relay's message id.

All three surfaces emit into the evidence pipeline (section 8), and all three refuse to operate for a step not in running: the state machine, not the surface, decides whether anything happens at all.

4.4The surface controllers and the step runner

Each surface has one controller, a process deployed with the surface inside the real-value zone, and the three controllers are the only writers to the substitution component's queue. A controller holds no detokenise right and no vault SDK: it creates and destroys the surface session under a step, forwards the worker's remote input from the console and returns the masked stream, the call panel state or the composer state, requests placements with the target descriptor and the signed step context and receives the receipts, returns the surface's own signal (the field's validity state, the media server's playback record, the delivery record) to the task service, and emits every capture, with the placement registry, into the evidence pipeline. The browser controller also carries the ai executor's actions to the sandbox and the one-shot channel for a one-time code; the telephony controller dials, answers callbacks and holds a line while a step is in checking; the mail relay is its own controller. Every controller checks the envelope's validity window and version at every committing action, a form submit, a committing click, a committing sentence on a call, a relay release, and not only at a placement: after expiry it refuses the action and closes the step blocked-outside-envelope naming the window, and an approval token is valid only while the window is open. The browser controller treats any form submission or committing click on a party's domain as irreversible unless the step's catalogue entry names the undo route for it, so without a named undo route the action needs the approval token whatever the step's classification, and a reversible step, which holds none, cannot commit anything.

The step runner sits inside the task service and drives every step's lifecycle whatever its class: it moves a step to running, obtains the signed step context, opens the surface through its controller, starts the ai executor or offers the card, hands a worker's tap on Place to the controller, issues the retry token for any re-placement of the same step, alias and target, records the outcome the controller or the console returns, and releases the surface. Its interfaces are three: to the console (cards, taps, results), to the controllers (open, place, signal, close), and to the substitution component only indirectly, through a controller, so that no path exists from the task service to a value.

4.5The signed step context

A placement is authorised by a signed step context, not by the caller's word. The task service signs one with its private key when a step enters running: the step record with its executor and its card's alias list, the envelope version with its lines, the policy lookup for the jurisdiction and act type, and, for an irreversible step, the dual-control approval token of section 7.2. The substitution component verifies the signature with the task service's public key and checks the context itself; it has no route to the task service, the envelope store or the policy layer and needs none. The context is short-lived by construction: valid no longer than the placement window the catalogue sets per market (illustrative: two minutes), it names the envelope version it was issued against and the flag its policy lookup returned, and it dies with any edit to the envelope or any flip of that flag. An extension, a narrowing or a policy flag flip voids every context and every approval token the task has issued: the task service returns each running step to ready, so no step stays running through an edit, re-checks each checking step against the new version before it can fire, re-classifies each step a flip touched before it can run again, and issues a fresh context only against the new version; a step whose placement request is already inside its placement window completes that placement under the version its context names, and nothing more. A step returned to ready this way keeps its claimed card and its surface session, marked "envelope changed; re-issuing", with the surface paused, no input forwarded and no placement possible, until the fresh context exists, at which point it returns to running, or to checking first if it is irreversible. For a reversible step the controller also fetches from the task service an envelope version stamp, signed and dated inside the window, and sends it with the request, so a placement is never checked against a superseded envelope; the full check list is the substitutor's interface in 03.

4.6The device surface

The user's own device is the fourth surface and the only one we do not run. It is the user's app or logged-in web session, bound to the user record as the device_binding of section 10.2, and it is where a user step lands in one of the four bounce forms, where the plan summary and the envelope are confirmed with one tap, where the envelope is extended or narrowed, where a question is answered, where the ledger and the outcome report are read, and where the verdict is given. It is authenticated whatever channel the conversation runs in: a chat-only front door carries the prompt and a link, and the tap happens in the app or the web view, as DR-001 fixes, so a thread bound only to a phone number never approves anything. Every response it returns is signed by the bound device and validated by the task service against the binding before it becomes an event. The device form, by which we ask for a value we know we need rather than asking in chat, is rendered here too: its fields post straight to the vault's edge proxy, and the alias comes back to the task service as a received event and a ledger line. Binding a device, a replacement included, is an account act performed on this surface through an identity rail the market's adapter accepts, as 01 fixes, and not a step of any task: a task that needs it waits in awaiting-authority on the nudge schedule until the bound device exists to give the authorising tap, and nothing executes on a surface of ours meanwhile.

5The state machine

A task cannot be forgotten, because forgetting has no transition. The task service holds every task in exactly one named state, gives every non-terminal state a live timer, records every change of state as an explicit event and hands anything that has lost its timer to the watchdog. The invariant below is the contract; it appears identically on the worker console page, where a person is most tempted to leave something for later.

A task never leaves the system by omission. Every task is in exactly one named state. Every non-terminal state carries a live timer. Every change of state is an explicit, recorded transition, and no task reaches a terminal state without one. Silence from the user is a legitimate way for a task to end: it moves through visible nudges into lapsed, and lapsed is not withdrawn. Silence from the system is forbidden: a task with no live timer is an exception, and the watchdog places it on the exceptions board until a person gives it one.

The watchdog runs on a fixed cycle (illustrative: one minute) and asks four questions of every record: does every live task have a future-dated timer; does every step in checking have a checker and a check deadline not yet passed; has any response window or planning deadline expired without resume, escalate-resume, extend-window or extend-planning following within one cycle; and has any nudge schedule been exhausted without a lapse event within one cycle. The watchdog's own heartbeat is an alarm in ordinary monitoring, and monitoring is three alarms and one pager: the heartbeat, the hash service's rate-limit breaches and the pipeline's incident counts, each paged to the shift lead on shift with the on-call engineer as the second line, so a silent watchdog is itself an exception. The board is specified on the exceptions board on the worker console page and staffed per QA and escalation in 04.

5.4Step states and the event log

Steps have their own small machine: pending (in the plan, not ready), ready, running, checking (dual control; the other party is checking), bounced (a user step, with the user), closed (with an outcome code) and cancelled (withdrawn, lapsed or superseded before it fired). A cancelled step never ran and carries no outcome code, except that a step a re-plan removes carries superseded with the superseding step, so the plan's history shows what replaced it. A surface acts only for a step in running; a substitution request for any other state is refused and logged. The task's state follows its steps by a fixed rule the task service applies as transitions from Table 2.2 and never as a second mechanism: any step ready, running or checking keeps the task active, timed by the earliest step deadline; otherwise any step bounced, or an open question or extension request, is awaiting-user on the nudge schedule; otherwise any step closed blocked-third-party is awaiting-third-party on that step's response window; and when every step is closed or cancelled, report moves the task to awaiting-verdict. Each step event names the transition it causes, if any, by that rule; the rule is recomputed on every event as a check, and a task whose state disagrees with it is an exception the watchdog raises, so the task's state and its steps' states cannot drift apart.

6The market adapter model

One core is built once and knows no country; each market is added as a repeatable unit of three parts. The core is the planner, the classifier, the task cards, the alias vault and substitution, the authority envelopes, the state machine, verification, the worker console, the access ledger, the intake gateway and the policy layer's mechanism; the flag table that mechanism reads is the per-market half. The unit per country is a passport adapter, a legal pack and a shift. The harness that keeps a worker in Bengaluru from seeing a value is the same harness everywhere, so a new market never re-argues the trust design. The decision is DR-007; the playbook is the expansion playbook in 06.

6.1The adapter interface

An adapter implements one interface, the same for every market, and never holds a raw byte: it reaches its rail only through the vault's edge proxy, deployed in front of it, which terminates every rail response and tokenises it against a declared per-field schema before the adapter receives the payload, as the three doors in 03 specify; the adapter holds no vault SDK and has no network route to the vault, and the rail token it presents on each call lives in the vault and is injected by the proxy. Four operations: fetch a document (returns an alias and non-sensitive metadata such as type and issue date); fetch financial data under the user's consent (returns aliases for account identifiers and non-sensitive facts for the context store); manage a consent or mandate (create, inspect, revoke, always initiated by the user on their device); and present a document or identity attribute to a third party on a surface, through the substitution component like any other placement. The device bounce-back is not an adapter; it is universal. The rails each adapter wraps are the brief's fixed list, drawn in Figure 2.3; what each permits a delegate to do is researched in the passports in 03. On UPI the adapter wraps the mandate rail for consents the user creates on their own device; a delegated debit under the cap is a bounce in India today, performed by the user's own device on UPI Circle or Reserve Pay, per the flag table, and the ai dual-control form is held in reserve for a rail that admits a registered agent.

6.2The legal pack and the shift

The legal pack holds four things the core reads at runtime and never hard-codes: the policy flag table, which the policy layer consults for every step, together with the scope block of section 3.1; the data-residency map, which says which component's data lives where and under what safeguard, the vault's regional hosting included; the refusal list, which the planner consults before any step executes, carrying the reason codes of what we refuse to do in 01 plus the market's own lines, a vertical not yet open among them, refused from received with the reason code refused-not-offered; and the step-type catalogue's entries for the market (section 3.3), both tables, whose schema is core and whose values per step type and per party are the market's. In form the pack is a directory of tables in a plain structured format with a manifest that names each table's schema version and row count, versioned as a whole, signed by two holders (the market's legal owner and the platform owner) before a pilot opens, and verified against the core's schema at load: a required row missing (a flag for every act type in Table 2.5, a residency entry for every store, a timer row for every task type) fails the load, and the previous version stays in force. A new version is loaded on a signed reload without a restart and applies exactly as a flag flip does (section 2.2): it voids the context and the token of every step not yet fired, which is re-classified against the new version before it can run again, and a step whose placement request is already inside its placement window completes that placement under the version its context names, and nothing more. The shift is the operational third: a market is not live until a shift covers its waking hours from the office, per the shift design in 04. The residency map itself is the data-residency map in 03.

7Verification

Verification is the control loop, and it runs before "done" is ever shown to the user: every step closes with exactly one outcome code from a fixed vocabulary and the structured fields that code requires. The test is the expected outcome written on the step record before execution, so the executor never decides after the fact what success meant. Artefacts attach for audit and dispute only. Outcome codes are not ground truth of quality; the user's verdict is, the improvement loop in the verdict system in 01, split from the control loop by DR-002. The vocabulary is the table on the worker console page; this section specifies the mechanics around it.

7.1Closing a step

An ai step is closed by the harness, which runs the verifier the catalogue entry names over the surface's state after execution, returned by the controller as the masked text and accessibility layer of the page, the masked DOM capture, the delivery receipt or a calendar the harness can read: a reference in the expected form, a confirmation phrase the template names, an entry that now exists. The reference form and the expected pattern come from the template's expected-outcome text, filled by the planner with the step's particulars, so the verifier compares against a statement written before the step ran. A human step is closed by the worker's structured result on the task card, which the console will not submit until the required fields are present. A user step is closed by the device's signed response (section 3.2). The outcome record names the closing party and, for a dual-controlled step, the checking party.

7.2Dual control

Nothing irreversible inside the envelope fires until the other party has checked it. The executing party prepares the act and the step moves to checking with a check deadline from Table 2.8. The checker receives the act in one sentence, the envelope lines it touches, the expected outcome and, as the evidence, the live masked stream or masked leg of the surface for the step, or an artefact the evidence store holds after verify has passed it, never material from the pipeline before verify; the checker's viewing of the stream is a ledger event, "a checker saw the masked stream for step n", though the check itself is not one; and the checker approves or declines, and cannot edit. On approval the step returns to running with an approval token: a record signed by the task service's key that carries the check id, the step id, the envelope version the check was made against, the checker's identity, a hash of the act as checked and an expiry equal to the step deadline or the envelope's validity window, whichever is sooner. A surface refuses to fire an irreversible act without a token naming the current envelope version, and the substitutor refuses a placement for an irreversible step without one, so an approval never outlives its check and dual control is enforced at the boundary and not only at the console. An envelope edit or a policy flag flip voids every outstanding token and context: a step holding a token returns to ready and must pass checking again, and a step already in checking is re-checked against the new version (section 4.5); a withdrawal voids the tokens and cancels the step. The outcome record carries both identities. On decline the step returns to ready with the checker's reason, or re-plans if the reason says the route is wrong.

Who checks whom follows the tiers in 04. When the AI executes an irreversible step, an L3 worker checks, from a check card routed to the L3 checking seat staffed on every shift. When a worker executes one, the worker is L2 or L3 (L3 alone under the pilot's scope block; never L1), and the AI checks in a separate model call that receives the envelope, the act, the expected outcome and the masked evidence and no conversation history, returns approve or decline with a reason in a schema the harness validates, and is stored with the token; a model call that errors, times out or fails validation is a decline with the reason "checker unavailable", and the step returns to ready. The checker is never the executor and never a worker who executed or prepared any step of the same task; a check that passes its deadline goes to the exceptions board and the act does not fire. On a call the prepared act is the committing sentence: the step enters checking with the sentence hidden from the worker and the worker's outbound leg muted, the telephony controller holds the line, which is the one thing a surface does for a step not in running, and when the token arrives the step returns to running and the worker reads the sentence as written or the system speaks it; no placement and no committing act happens in between. The worker's view is on the worker console page; the decision is DR-004.

8The evidence pipeline

No raw artefact is ever human-viewable, and the pipeline that guarantees it is part of the harness, not a later clean-up. Every surface emits screenshots, DOM captures, recordings, transcripts and call metadata into four stages: capture, redact, verify, store. Raw artefacts exist only in the pipeline's memory; a person may view an artefact only after verify has passed it, only for audit, dispute or a dual-control check (section 7.2), with the viewing logged. The evidence system is a leak path in its own right, and the threat model in 03 treats it as one; the decision is DR-009, the redaction contract is artefact redaction in 03.

8.1The four stages

Capture takes the raw artefact from the surface together with the placement registry the substitution component's receipts built for the step: which alias went into which field rectangle, which audio window, which message occurrences; an artefact without its registry is not accepted. Redact applies the four masks in the fixed order 03 specifies: coordinate masks from every registry entry, with a margin, from the frame of placement to the frame of navigation; known-value masks by keyed digest over text layers, document captures and transcripts, with spoken digits normalised; audio windows, every placement and every matched read-back replaced with silence and a transcript marker naming the alias; and class-format masks on every frame and transcript, digit runs beyond the threshold, any run matching a class format of Table 3.1 in 03 or the party's reference shapes, and, on an authenticated portal's frame, every run outside the step's whitelist. Aliases replace matches in transcripts and document captures as the by-product, so a transcript reads {{dob}} where the value was. Verify is a second, independent pass by a separately built service with its own recognisers and its own digest set under its own key, which scans the redacted artefact for any known value, any run in a class format and, on a portal's frame, any run outside the whitelist, and fails closed: a detection quarantines the artefact in memory, opens an incident naming only the alias and the rule that missed, re-runs redaction with wider masks, and after a small fixed number of attempts drops the raw material and records "evidence withheld". Store writes the masked artefact with its step reference, its redaction manifest and the verifier's signature over its hash, after which it is viewable for audit and dispute by a person whose viewing is itself a ledger event.

Redact and verify share no code, so a defect in one does not hide in the other, and the pipeline holds no vault SDK: it holds the maskers' digest set and queries the hash service, never the values, so a compromise of the pipeline yields masks and digests, not data. Retention periods are per market and belong to the legal pack.

8.2The data contract

The pipeline is built against a contract, not stage names. Capture points are fixed per surface by the catalogue's evidence_expected: the browser controller captures a frame and a document snapshot before each submit and after each response; the telephony controller delivers both raw legs from the media server, the delayed transcript and the call metadata; the relay delivers the alias draft and the delivery receipt; the device surface delivers its signed response. An artefact is identified as ev_<task>_<step>_<kind> and carries the step reference, the capture point, the surface's clock, the placement registry (placement id, alias, rectangle or window or occurrence, frame or time span) and, once redacted, the manifest: one entry per mask applied, in aliases, naming the rule (coordinate, digest, audio window, class format), the region and the registry entry it came from. The store encrypts each artefact under a key held per task in the market's region, released to the viewer of 03's viewer table only, and keeps it for the retention the market's legal pack sets; artefacts attach to outcome records by identifier and the outcome record never embeds one. The viewer renders from the store, never from the pipeline, and writes an evidence-opened ledger line with the viewer's role. Audio has two sources and one rule: a voice note is transcribed inside the vault's edge proxy and its audio enters the vault as a document alias, so it is never an evidence artefact and the pipeline receives only the alias transcript and the time spans of detected values; a call's audio is captured from the media server, in the market's region, and exists raw only in the pipeline's memory until the verifier passes the silenced recording.

8.3The hash service

The hash service is the second of the two places the vault SDK exists, and it exists so that no component in the alias zone ever computes a known-value match itself. It lives in the real-value zone, in region, with two vault credentials and no detokenise right: digest-only, for the digest sets below, and tokenise-only for the third door of 03, by which a value a surface echoes that matches no alias, an account number the institution issues, becomes a new alias with a "received" ledger line. It holds the keys: for every value in the vault it computes a keyed digest of each normalised form Table 3.1 in 03 names per class (spaced and unspaced, hyphenated, spoken digit by digit, case-folded, every partial form), and it issues two digest sets under two keys, one to the maskers on the surfaces and in the redactor and one to the verifier, rotating both with the digests on a cadence the legal pack sets (illustrative: daily, and at once on any incident). Neither key leaves it. A masker holds its digest set and obtains the digest of each candidate window it scans from the hash service over a batched call rate-limited to a frame's or a segment's volume, so a masker compromised end to end can match what is on its own screen and nothing beyond it; a breach of the rate limit is one of the three monitored alarms of section 5.3. For the alias zone it offers one further operation, the screen: a component sends a candidate payload, a card payload at generation, a result field at entry, a log line before commit, and receives accept or reject with the class matched and nothing else; the payload is held in memory for the call and never stored, no digest leaves, and a reject sends the payload to the vault's edge proxy for tokenisation and opens an incident. The hash service holds no detokenise right, so the worst it can yield is digests and new aliases.

9The context store

The context store lets the planner act without asking the user things they have already told us, and its design rule is that every fact says where it came from and how much to trust it. It is a temporal knowledge graph: nodes are the user, institutions, documents, people and places; edges are facts with validity intervals. The brief fixes a Graphiti-style temporal graph; the library choice is recorded in DR-016. Sensitive fields live in the vault: the store holds the alias, never the value, and a fact about a PAN is a fact about {{pan}}.

This record is the canonical fact schema; Table 1.2 in 01 is the user's view of the same fields, and its four source values map onto the five kinds here: interview is onboarding, connector is connector, task step is task-outcome, user statement is user-statement, and passport-adapter is the fifth, for a fact a rail returned; its "sensitivity" is the sensitive flag, true when the value lives in the vault and value_alias carries the alias while object names only the class or the institution. User-confirmed is a separate flag, because a fact can be high-confidence and unconfirmed: a fact used in an irreversible step must be confirmed or come from a passport adapter; a fact used to fill a form the user will see before submission may be merely high; each step type's entry names the threshold it needs, and a fact below it becomes a question (section 9.4) rather than a guess.

9.2The temporal graph

Every fact has a validity interval, and a query is always made at a point in time: the planner asks what is true now, the audit asks what we believed when a step ran. A new fact that contradicts an existing one does not overwrite it; it opens with its own interval, the old fact's valid_to is set when the contradiction is resolved, and until then both carry a quality_flag. A flagged fact cannot be used by a step, and the flag propagates: the task service finds every open plan whose facts_used names the fact, re-plans it before the affected steps run, and turns the conflict into a question to the user at the next natural point, or into a blocked-needs-user step if a running task depends on it. A fact past its valid_to is never used. Facts are learned mostly by doing tasks: every ok outcome carries the facts the step established, written with the step as their source; the onboarding interview and read-only connectors top the store up, per onboarding in 01.

9.3Who reads and writes it

The planner reads the store through one query: subject, an optional predicate filter, a point in time, and a minimum confidence, answered with the facts valid at that time and their provenance; there is no free-text search and no query across users. The write path is one too: the task service takes the facts an ok outcome, an onboarding answer, a connector pull or a rail response established, runs each through the identifier-pattern detector and the hash service's screen, tokenises any match through the vault's edge proxy so that a sensitive fact is written as an alias, and appends the fact with its source; the store itself rejects any value that matches a pattern. The user reads their own store as a profile in which every fact shows its source and date and can be confirmed, corrected or deleted from their device; a correction is a new fact with the user as its source, and it re-plans any open task that relied on the old one. Nothing else reads it, and its non-sensitive contents sit in the region the residency map assigns.

9.4Questions to the user

A question is a task-level record, not a step: question_id, the task, the step it blocks if any, the text in plain words, the predicate it would establish, the answer kind (a choice, a date, a free line screened at entry like a result field) and an expiry. The planner raises it when a fact is missing, flagged or below the step's threshold; the task service records question-asked, moves the task to awaiting-user by ask, and shows it on the device surface, never in the chat thread. The answer is written as a fact with source kind user-statement, confidence confirmed and the question as its reference, the task service records question-answered and resume, and the steps that depended on the gap are re-classified.

10The intake gateway

Every channel arrives at one door and leaves it as one format, already tokenised. The gateway accepts WhatsApp, the native app, SMS and iMessage, and the web, in text, voice and attachments, and produces the internal request record every other component understands; inbound mail to a task address enters by the same door as a reply and is never a front door. Which channel is the front door in a market is a launch choice, held amber with its trigger in intake in 01 and recorded in DR-001; the gateway is the fixed decision underneath, and nothing downstream knows which channel a request came from.

10.1Tokenisation at the edge

The gateway is behind the first of the three doors a real value enters by, and it never holds a raw byte. The channel webhook, the device form and, in front of the adapters, every rail response terminate at the vault's edge proxy: a tokenising proxy the provider runs in the market's region under our detector policy, so the message body, every attachment and the audio of a voice note are the proxy's bytes and never the gateway's. Whether the chosen vault can run this proxy in region with our detectors loaded is a requirement of the vendor evaluation in 07; the proxy is a provider product configured with our detector policy, not a component we build, and the policy is three detectors we ship to it per market: identifier-pattern detection for the value types each market knows (a PAN's form, an Aadhaar number's form, a card number's check digit, a National Insurance number's form), known-value matching against the digest set the hash service issues to the proxy, and user declaration, where the user marks a value as sensitive or answers the device form. Each detected value is tokenised inside the proxy, and the alias takes its place; the proxy adds an aliases_issued header to the tokenised message, from which the gateway fills the field of the same name. Attachments are tokenised whole: the document image goes into the vault, and the request carries a document alias, the document type and the metadata the planner needs. A voice note is transcribed inside the proxy's boundary and the transcript tokenised the same way; the audio enters the vault as a document alias, and the request carries the alias transcript and the time span of every detected value, which is all the evidence pipeline ever receives of it. The gateway parses a tokenised message, holds no vault SDK and has no network route to the vault; if the proxy is unavailable the channel receives a retry response and no message is accepted, because fail closed here means no byte of a message reaches a process of ours.

Typing at detection is the proxy's, by the detector policy: a pattern detector types a value by its format and check digit, a known-value match types it by the alias it matched, and a declared value takes the type the user gave. A format two classes share, a sixteen-digit number that may be a card or an account, an unspaced ten-digit number that may be a phone number or an NHS number, is tokenised under the broader class, and the request carries the alias marked ambiguous, which the gateway resolves by asking the user to confirm the type in the device form before any step uses it. A value the detectors miss is this component's residual risk; the model runtime's separately deployed detector refuses to plan on a candidate value and returns the message for re-tokenisation, the known-value matching on every surface and in the evidence pipeline is the second line, and the threat model in 03 names the path.

Two rules from DR-001 are stated here as the gateway applies them behind the proxy. The pass fails closed: a message carrying a suspected value the detectors cannot type never becomes a task. The proxy holds the message in its own memory, in region, for the hold window of Table 2.8, the gateway asks in the channel for the value in a typed field, which is the device form, and nothing opens in received until the pass is clean; if no re-entry arrives before the window expires, the proxy discards the message unseen and the gateway tells the user that nothing was started. And the sender is bound before anything else: the proxy tokenises before the sender is known, so a message from an unknown number or address reaches the gateway tokenised, the gateway finds no user binding, replies with the onboarding route of onboarding in 01 and nothing else, discards the message, and instructs the proxy to destroy the aliases it issued; the message is never stored and never queued for a person.
