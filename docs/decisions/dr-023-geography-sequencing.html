1Decision

The United Kingdom opens between months 8 and 14 rather than as a later market, with acquisition focus genuinely moved there rather than treated as secondary. amber India is the delivery base, the workflow laboratory and a secondary revenue market.

Three assumptions carry the case and are worth naming in the decision rather than only in the instrument: the model has word of mouth abroad compounding at 1.6 times the Indian rate, from a launch cohort 3.4 times the size of the late sequence's, and allows foreign churn to run below the Indian figure. assumption Nearly all of the foreign arrivals in this scenario are that multiplier rather than anything bought, which is why the trigger below is a measured waiting list and not a plan.

Two gates govern any market: 400 users in the United Kingdom and 600 in the United States to carry the local cost base. assumption No market is opened that cannot plausibly reach 500 users within twelve months of entry, and no second market is opened until the first has cleared its gate. The playbook that a market runs through is 06, 4; the arithmetic is 05, 8 and the model page behind it.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

The published sequence: the UK around month 20, the US around month 36, both secondary
Modelling says that version is worse than not expanding at all, because the standing cost of a foreign entity arrives long before the users do: on the published line it roughly doubles the conservative cash requirement, $3.98m against $2.04m, and lowers the odds of being profitable by month 36 from 93 per cent to 78. assumption The earlier run, which had no media in it, made the same version nearly triple the requirement, so the promotion narrows the margin without moving the ranking. Two qualifications belong with that, and both are in 05, 8: at matched percentiles of what a path actually needs the gap is 1.3 to 1.7 times rather than roughly double, and compared like for like without the commerce layer this decision's own sequence costs almost as much cash as the one it rejects, $3.85m against $3.98m. What survives is a contribution argument, not a cash one.
India only, no foreign entity
Coherent and cheaper, and it gives up the purchasing-power arbitrage that a US user at $105 and an Indian user at $52 cost the same to serve. assumption It remains the fallback if the gate is not met.
Opening both foreign markets at once
Rejected by the second gate: two standing cost bases before either has users.

3What this closes off

  • It commits capital to foreign acquisition at a cost per user 7.4 times the Indian figure in the United Kingdom and 9.7 times in the United States, against a price roughly twice as high. assumption That works on lifetime value and is expensive on cash. The same unmeasured ratio prices the media of DR-025 abroad and the referral incentive abroad, so a single wrong ratio is wrong twice. What the run actually pays is higher than the ratio suggests, because the channels saturate: about $2,938 a paid arrival in the United Kingdom and $1,839 in the United States by month 36 in this sequence, which is at or above the only outside benchmark the record holds. 05, 8 carries the figures.
  • A market opened on optimism rather than on its gate, and a second market opened before the first has cleared.
  • The description of India as the first of three equal markets. It is the delivery base and the laboratory, and its revenue is secondary.
  • A foreign entity stood up before the gate that pays for it, with the counsel, the registration and the standing cost that follow.

4Status

Amber decision, taken by the founder on 2026-09-13, with the trigger in section 5. DR-006 is staged against the same event, because the first foreign market live is one of the two things that calls the attested site into being.

Putting the four sequencing scenarios on the published line surfaced a finding that cuts against this sequence rather than for it. An even split of the media budget across live markets thins the foreign share the sequence exists for, because the same rupee buys about seven times as many Indian arrivals, and revenue per user in the foreign-led case falls from $107 to $92 at month 36. assumption The allocation rule is not decided here; it is an open item in 07, and the arithmetic is 05, 8.

5Trigger

A priced United Kingdom waiting list aimed at non-resident households, measured for conversion, and a net promoter score of 60 or above on the Indian paying cohort. The second half is the condition DR-025 puts on the organic mechanism: without it the word of mouth this sequence compounds abroad is not established, and a second market is being opened on borrowed growth. 06, 4.1 states the trigger in both halves and this record now matches it. It is amber because the case rests on an untested assumption, that we can acquire abroad from a standing start with no brand and no local team, and because a well-funded prior attempt at a comparable service in the United States closed in 2025 after reaching a few thousand households. The waiting list is an open item with an owner in 07; until it answers, the dates in 06, 4.1 are a plan and not a commitment.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title.
