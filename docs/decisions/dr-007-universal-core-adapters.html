DR-007A universal core with per-country adapters, legal pack and shift

One harness, built once and country-agnostic, carries every trust property. Each market adds passport adapters to its rails, a legal pack and a data-residency map, plus a shift. Expansion is that playbook repeated, and it never touches the core.

On this page

1Decision

We build one universal core and add a thin, named layer per country. The core is country-agnostic: the planner, the classifier, task cards, the alias vault and the substitution component, authority envelopes, the state machine, verification and the evidence pipeline, the worker console, the access ledger and the intake gateway. Per country we add passport adapters to that market's identity and financial rails, a legal pack and a data-residency map; operationally we add a shift. India is the build base, the US and the UK are the commercial ground, and we build for all three from the first line.

1.1What lives in the core

Everything that makes the two fatal failures structurally impossible, so that each is proven once. The alias type system is universal: {{pan}}, {{ni-number}} and {{ssn}} are three values of one type, so the planner treats an identity number the same way in three countries and the substitutor places it through the same boundary. The policy layer's mechanism is core; the content of its flag table is per country and travels with the legal pack. The rule of classification, the state-machine invariant and the outcome-code vocabulary have one text each, and a country never edits them.

1.2What lives in the adapter

A passport adapter is the per-country integration to a rail, plus the universal device bounce-back that every adapter carries. For India the rails are India Stack: DigiLocker, Account Aggregator and UPI Autopay. For the UK, Open Banking plus the current identity rails. For the US, bank-data aggregation and card tokenisation. Each is researched at write time in 03, and each returns values tokenised at the adapter's own boundary, so the planner sees aliases whichever rail produced them. An adapter holds tokenise-only credentials to the vault; the substitutor alone can detokenise. A raw rail response exists only in the adapter's memory and is never persisted, logged or human-viewable, and the adapter's own logs carry aliases only. The region key has two parts: the user's market fixes the vault instance that holds their values, and the adapter's market fixes the rail. A task that crosses markets, a UK resident acting on an Indian institution, follows the rule in the residency map in 03 for where the substitutor runs.

The rails also show why the adapter is a country's and not the core's. DigiLocker's partner API requires the user's own authorisation through an OAuth flow before a partner can list or pull a document verified [1], and the RBI's Account Aggregator direction, as at its 2017 text, reserves the information-user role to entities regulated by a financial sector regulator and forbids an aggregator from supporting transactions verified [2]. Whether a rail is reached directly, through a regulated partner, or by a bounce to the user's device is therefore a per-country answer, held as a flag with a legal item behind it, exactly as DR-003 requires.

1.3The legal pack and the residency map

The legal pack holds the filled-in flag table, the KYC last-mile items to verify for that jurisdiction, the privacy-law skeleton mapping, and the contractual safeguards the market needs. One safeguard is fixed in the ledger: Bengaluru staff processing UK or EU data is a restricted cross-border transfer needing contractual safeguards, and the vault provider's regional hosting is part of why that layer is bought rather than built, which DR-008 records. The residency map says where each class of data sits for each market; sensitive values sit in the vault's regional instance, everything else with us. Both are drawn in 03.

1.4The playbook

A new country is an adapter, a legal pack and a shift, in that order, as 06 sequences it. If a new country would need a change to the core, the core was not universal, and that is a defect to fix for every market rather than a customisation for one.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

Build for India first, fork for the west later
The fork carries rupee, Aadhaar and UPI assumptions into the planner and the envelope, and it multiplies: three harnesses, each re-proving the two fatals, with the invariant and the rule of classification drifting between them.
Build for one market only
The ledger places commercial viability in the US and the UK. A one-market build defers the markets that carry the business, and the later port is the fork above under another name.
Adapters that hand real values to the planner
A second and third entry point for real values into the model runtime. The alias model would be true of the intake gateway and false of every rail, and the threat model would gain a path per adapter per country.
One global deployment with no residency map
The cross-border transfer question goes unanswered: Bengaluru staff processing UK data would be a restricted cross-border transfer with no contractual safeguard in place, the legal pack's to-verify items would stand unanswered, and the vault's regional hosting would go unused.

3What this closes off

  • A component in the core that knows which country it is in. Country is data: in the adapter, the flag table and the residency map.
  • A rail integration that talks to the planner, a worker or the context store in real values.
  • An expansion that requires a change to the core.
  • Sensitive values hosted outside the market's region, or workers processing a market's data before its legal pack is in place.
  • An adapter that bypasses the device bounce-back for an act the flag table says bounces.
  • A second alias type system, or a second text for any rule the core owns.
  • An adapter with detokenise rights.

4Status

Fixed decision, taken by the founder and recorded in the brief's ledger on 2026-09-02. The seam between core and adapter is drawn in 02, the rails and the residency map are specified in 03, and the expansion playbook is sequenced in 06.
