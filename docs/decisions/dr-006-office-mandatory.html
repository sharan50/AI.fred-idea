1Decision

Operations are office-mandatory and run 24 hours a day, seven days a week. A day shift in Bengaluru covers India's business hours and two night shifts cover the UK and the US as their business days run, so a task in any of the three markets meets a live worker in its own hours. Site controls, the device policy, a clean floor, monitored substitution stations and an attestable room, are part of the trust guarantee we make to the user, not a workplace preference. Remote-safe operation is a designed-later programme, parked in 07 with an owner and a trigger.

1.1Why the room is a trust control

The last surface a human can see is a screen and a headset. When the substitutor places a real value, the worker's stream renders the field as dots, and on a call the worker's leg is muted in both directions from the tap until the last tone or syllable plus a guard interval while the value goes out on the far leg, with the inbound leg behind a delay line at all times; when a third party echoes a value, the masker on the stream and the delay buffer on the inbound leg catch it, by known-value digest, by class format and by shape, and both fail closed: a frame carrying a run the masker can neither match nor clear is painted by default and held for re-masking, and a span the buffer cannot clear is replaced by a tone, so a value the vault has never seen is covered by its form and no residual is delivered to a screen or a headset. Those are software controls, and they hold for as long as the device is ours and untouched. What runs past them runs through the room. A station can be tampered with: developer tools on the console, a capture device on the video path before the mask, a recorder on the headset line before the mute. Task detail that never enters the vault, the institution, the appointment facts, the script, can be photographed by a personal phone. And a masker or a mute that fails is a failure the threat model in 03 names, to be recorded and deterred as well as closed. The threat model is a list of paths by which a human could see a real value, each named and closed, and the site controls are entries in it: the device policy closes tampering, because every station is ours, locked down and signed into by badge; the locker at the door and the clean floor close the photograph and the note; and the monitored stations are the record and the deterrent for a masking failure, never its closure: the closure is the masker's hold and repaint, and a held frame or a station-integrity event is an incident that names the station and the person at it, under camera, badge log and console recording, rather than a secret. The L3 seats and the escalation desk sit in a two-person zone. Table 4.4 in 04 maps each control to its path, and its fourth control, the attestable room, lets us show that mapping to an auditor rather than assert it.

1.2Shifts and tiers

Every shift draws on the same three tiers and the same need-to-know task cards, specified in 04: a night-shift worker sees a client number, aliases, a script and an expected outcome, and nothing more. Handover at shift change is the moment the shift wheel in 04 marks, because an awaiting-third-party step with a callback window in London hours must have a live desk when the callback comes. No cost model is attached; economics is deferred by 05.

1.3Remote-safe, later

A remote-safe programme would have to close, off-site, every room path the threat model names, with controls we can verify rather than trust, which is what the attestable room gives the office and a home does not. That is a design job, scheduled after the office model has run and the threat model has been exercised against real tasks. It is not a maybe and it is not now; its owner and trigger are in 07.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

A remote workforce
The norm among the human-assisted assistants: Magic's assistants are based in the Philippines and Latin America verified [1], and Time etc has clients share logins with a named assistant through password managers verified [2]. Its cost is a human-visible surface in a room we do not control: the threat model would carry paths we cannot name and close, and "no person saw it" would rest on trust in a household rather than on a control.
A hybrid floor: sensitive steps in the office, the rest remote
The classifier gains a "where" dimension and the console splits in two; every step's sensitivity must be judged before it runs, a task whose alias set changes mid-plan moves rooms, and two rule sets drift, the harder one under least use.
A day shift only, with western tasks queued to the next Indian morning
The markets that carry the business would meet a dead desk in their own hours, and a promised callback from a London institution would ring an empty floor. The 24/7 floor makes the three-market shape real.
Three offices, one per market
Three sites to control before any market has earned one. DR-007's playbook is adapter plus legal pack plus shift, and the shift is added in the existing building.

3What this closes off

  • A worker executing any step from a personal device, a home or a shared workspace.
  • A night shift staffed remotely because it is night. The western hours are the sensitive ones.
  • A substitution station that is not monitored, or a floor that is not clean.
  • Site controls described anywhere as a workplace policy rather than a trust control.
  • Remote-safe operation as "never" or as "now". It is later, with a trigger.
  • A cost model attached to the shift design.
