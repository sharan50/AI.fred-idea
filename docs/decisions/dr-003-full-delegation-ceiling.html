On this page

1Decision

Full delegation, identity acts included, is the designed capability ceiling of the harness. What a given country permits is a runtime matter: a policy layer holds a per-jurisdiction flag table that decides, step type by step type, whether a step is delegated, bounced to the user's own device as a one-tap or user-performed act, or prohibited pending a regulatory answer. Detracting from the ceiling is a flag; adding to it would be a rebuild, so we build the ceiling now and set the flags conservatively. This record supersedes the earlier A/B/C capability-tier framing.

1.1The ceiling

The harness can plan, classify and execute every step type a high-consequence task contains, including the identity acts: presenting a document, stating an identity number, answering security questions from the context store, completing a KYC form. Delegating an act never means seeing a value; the act is performed through the alias model and the substitution component, and the worker or the model handles {{pan}} while the substitutor places the digits. One thing sits above the ceiling in every jurisdiction: an OTP is never ours. {{otp}} is not an alias, and the step that needs one always bounces.

1.2The policy layer

Each flag takes one of three values: delegated, bounce, prohibited. A flag filters the envelope, so an identity act the jurisdiction flags as bounce is never delegated even if the user ticks it; a step type flagged prohibited closes with refused-policy and the user is told why. The flag table lives in 03 and doubles as the lobbying agenda: line by line, what we would ask a regulator to permit and the benefit to the end user of permitting it. Country-specific KYC constraints are recorded as legal items to verify in 07, never as design constraints. Design carries the ceiling; the table carries today's answer.

India shows why the table exists. The RBI's KYC directions let a regulated entity rely on due diligence performed by a third party only where that party is itself regulated and supervised, with ultimate responsibility staying with the entity verified [1]; video customer identification must be run only by the entity's own trained officials verified [1]; and UIDAI tells users never to share an Aadhaar-linked OTP with anyone verified [2]. So in India the form-filling step is delegated, the identification act itself bounces, and the flag row records both, with the legal item behind it and the request we would put to the regulator beside it.

1.3What this supersedes

The earlier framing, carried from the founder's prior work and not reproduced in this repository, described capability as three tiers, A, B and C, with identity acts at the top. Tiers made scope a product ladder. This record replaces the ladder with one ceiling and one flag table: the difference between markets is data, and the difference between launch and maturity is which flags have flipped.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

The A/B/C capability tiers (superseded)
Each tier boundary hardens into architecture: the classifier, the envelope and the console are built for the tier in hand and rebuilt for the next. The top tier, identity acts, where the trust design matters most, would be designed last and therefore worst. And it leaves three products to explain to a customer and to a regulator.
Design to the most restrictive jurisdiction
Bakes today's constraints into the harness as if they were physics. A permission won later by lobbying would need a rebuild to use, and there would be no lobbying agenda at all, because a design that never states what it wants cannot ask for it.
No policy layer; a fork of the core per country
Three harnesses with three drifting rule sets, and the rule of classification and the state-machine invariant proven three times over. The universal core in DR-007 exists to prevent exactly this.
The full ceiling as launch scope, with no flags
Acts a regulator has reserved to the customer would be attempted, which is a refused-policy outcome at best and a liability at worst. The lobbying agenda would become a defence instead of a request.

3What this closes off

  • A capability the runtime cannot express. If a step type cannot be flagged, it does not exist in the harness.
  • Adding a delegation type later as a rebuild. The ceiling is designed now, whether or not any market permits all of it at launch.
  • A jurisdiction's constraint written as a design constraint anywhere in 01 to 03. It is a flag, with a legal to-verify behind it.
  • An identity act delegated where the flag says bounce, whatever the user ticked in the envelope.
  • An OTP as an alias, a delegated act or a stored value, in any market.
  • Capability tiers as the unit of scope. A, B and C appear nowhere else in this publication.

4Status
