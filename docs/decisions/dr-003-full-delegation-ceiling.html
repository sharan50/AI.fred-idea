A per-jurisdiction policy layer decides at runtime which acts are delegated, which bounce to the user's device and which are prohibited pending a regulatory answer. This record supersedes the earlier A/B/C capability-tier framing.

1Decision

Full delegation, identity acts included, is the designed capability ceiling of the harness. What a country permits is a runtime matter: a policy layer holds a per-jurisdiction flag table that decides, act type by act type, whether a step is delegated, bounced to the user's own device, or prohibited pending a regulatory answer. Detracting from the ceiling is a flag; adding to it would be a rebuild, so we build the ceiling now and set the flags conservatively. Conservative has one meaning: a lookup that finds no row evaluates to bounce, never to delegated, an unknown step type counts as irreversible and outside the envelope and bounces too, and every such bounce is a request to add a row, which is the catalogue rule in 02. This record supersedes the earlier A/B/C capability-tier framing.

1.1The ceiling

The harness can plan, classify and execute every step type a high-consequence task contains, the identity acts included, which are the closed list in 01: present a document; state an identity number; answer security questions from the context store; complete a KYC form; use a delegated login to a non-financial portal; sign or e-sign; attest a fact about the user. Delegating an act never means seeing a value: the worker or the model handles {{pan}} while the substitutor places the digits. One thing sits above the ceiling in every jurisdiction: an OTP is never ours, {{otp}} is not an alias, and the step that needs one always bounces.

1.2The policy layer

Each flag takes one of three values, delegated, bounce or prohibited pending, and the lookup is keyed by the delegator's jurisdiction and the act type the step-type catalogue names for the step, exactly as the classifier in 02 reads it: for the user's own aliases the user's jurisdiction, the market on the request; for a dependant's aliases the jurisdiction her delegation record carries, so a parent resident in India and a user in the UK are flagged under two different keys, and the signed step context names the jurisdiction the lookup used; a line that carries a condition returns its predicate too, evaluated from the envelope and the adapter's facts at classification time, and a delegated whose condition does not hold reads bounce. The alias class and the surface are not in the key: the substitutor checks them after the flag. A missing row evaluates to bounce. The filled table for each market is Table 3.5 in 03, which records the designed flag per cell, the ceiling, each a legal item to verify; the setting a pilot runs under may sit below it, and for identity acts outside India it is bounce until counsel's answer sets the flag, as 03 and 07 state. For an act toward an institution the lookup keeps its one interface and is made twice, once under the delegator's jurisdiction and once under the institution's market; the stricter result governs, prohibited pending over bounce over delegated, a missing row in either evaluates to bounce, and the signed step context names both jurisdictions used. A user abroad is the same case: the delegator's jurisdiction is the market on the request and the institution they deal with carries its own, so travel adds rows to read and never a build, and a country with no legal pack has no rows and every act there evaluates to bounce. The catalogue carries no flag of its own; the flag lives in the policy layer. A flag filters the envelope, so an identity act flagged bounce is never delegated even if the user ticks it. A step whose act is flagged prohibited pending closes refused-policy and the user is told why; the planner re-plans by another route, bounces the act where a bounce is lawful, or, where neither exists, reports what was and was not done and moves the task to awaiting-verdict, where "we couldn't complete it" is on offer. Refusing a whole request is different: that is the refused state, reached before any step executes. A flag flipped during a task is a re-planning trigger, one of the fixed list under the planner in 02: the flip voids the context and token of every step not yet fired, exactly as an envelope edit does, those steps are re-classified against the new flag before they can run again, a step in checking is re-checked before it fires, and a bounced user step in progress on the device is not re-classified until its bounce record closes, done, declined or expired. A dependant's delegation record is an authority of the same standing, and the substitutor's ordered checks in 03 test that every delegation record an alias's owner references is live: her revocation, from her device like a connector, or her delegation's expiry voids every context and token of every step that names her aliases, exactly as a flag flip does, and those steps re-classify, a step now without authority becoming a user step for her. The table doubles as the lobbying agenda: line by line, what we would ask a regulator to permit and what the end user gains. Country-specific KYC constraints are 07 legal items, never design constraints.

India shows the row. The RBI's KYC directions reserve video customer identification to the regulated entity's own trained officials verified [1], so the form-filling step is delegated and the identification act bounces; the row records both, the legal item behind it and the request to the regulator beside it.

1.3What this supersedes

The earlier framing, not reproduced in this repository, described capability as three tiers, A, B and C; the brief records the framing and nothing of its content, and this record replaces it whole. A tier ladder makes scope a product ladder. One ceiling and one flag table make the difference between markets data, and the difference between launch and maturity which flags have flipped.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

The A/B/C capability tiers (superseded)
Each tier boundary hardens into architecture: the classifier, the envelope and the console are built for the tier in hand and rebuilt for the next. Whatever sits on the top rung is designed last, after the harness has set in the shape of the rungs below. And a ladder leaves three products to explain to a customer and a regulator.
Design to the most restrictive jurisdiction
Bakes today's constraints into the harness as if they were physics. A permission won later would need a rebuild to use, and there would be no lobbying agenda, because a design that never states what it wants cannot ask for it.
No policy layer; a fork of the core per country
Three harnesses with three drifting rule sets, the rule of classification and the state-machine invariant proven three times over. The universal core in DR-007 exists to prevent this.
The full ceiling as launch scope, with no flags
Acts a regulator has reserved to the customer would be attempted, a refused-policy outcome at best and a liability at worst, and the lobbying agenda would become a defence instead of a request.

3What this closes off

  • A capability the runtime cannot express. If an act cannot be flagged, it does not exist in the harness.
  • A lookup that finds no row and returns anything but bounce.
  • Adding a delegation type later as a rebuild. The ceiling is designed now, whether or not any market permits all of it.
  • A jurisdiction's constraint written as a design constraint anywhere in 01 to 03. It is a flag, with a legal to-verify behind it.
  • An identity act delegated where the flag says bounce, whatever the user ticked in the envelope.
  • An OTP as an alias, a delegated act or a stored value, in any market.
  • Capability tiers as the unit of scope. A, B and C appear nowhere else in this publication.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title.
