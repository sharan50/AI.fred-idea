DR-004The authority envelope and the point-of-no-return test

Every task carries one authority envelope: spend cap, contactable parties, committable acts, delegated identity acts and a validity window. Delegation is total inside it and impossible outside it, and every step is sorted by a point-of-no-return test before anyone acts.

On this page

1Decision

Every task carries exactly one authority envelope, confirmed by the user with one tap before any step executes, and every step is classified against a point-of-no-return test. The planner proposes the envelope with the plan, least-authority by construction from the task type and the context store; the user confirms it, which takes the task from awaiting-authority to active, or revises it, which returns the task to planning. Inside the envelope we act without asking again; outside it we cannot act at all, and the test in 1.2 sorts every step into execute, dual control, bounce or extension request.

1.1The five fields

Spend cap
Currency and amount for the task, with a per-step sub-cap where the plan proposes one. The cap is tested as a running total, never act by act: the task service keeps a committed-spend total per task, fired acts plus reserved acts; an approval token reserves its act's amount when it is issued, the substitutor and the checker test each act against the remaining cap, and a reservation is released only when the step closes without a fired marker, so two acts each under the cap cannot together exceed it.
Contactable parties
The named institutions, businesses and people we may contact as the user. Anyone else is outside, and a call or message to them is an act outside the envelope.
Committable acts
The act types we may commit the user to: book, cancel, submit an application, accept terms, make a payment under the cap, file a document, schedule.
Delegated identity acts
The identity act types the user delegates in this jurisdiction, filtered by the policy layer of DR-003: an act the jurisdiction flags as bounce is never delegated even if the user ticks it.
Validity window
Start and expiry. Past expiry nothing fires, reversible or not, and five components make that so rather than assert it. The window is a row of the timer table in 02, Table 2.8, per task and set to the envelope's own end: on the tick the task service cancels every step not yet fired, step state cancelled with lapsed as its cause, and moves the task from active to awaiting-authority with a one-tap request to renew the window, on the nudge schedule, where it nudges and lapses like any other wait. The step runner claims a step in ready for a worker or the ai executor only inside the window and under the version the signed step context names, so no step begins after expiry. The dialler checks the window and the version before it resolves a party to a number, so no call is dialled. The signed step context carries the window's end as its own expiry, so it dies at expiry as it dies on an edit, and no substitution is placed. And every surface controller checks the window and the version at every committing action, a form submit, a committing click, a committing sentence on a call, a relay release, and not only at a placement; once the window has closed it refuses the action, and a dual-control approval token is valid only while the window is open, so an act that places no value is gated exactly as one that places a value is. A reversible step already running completes what needs no further placement and no committing action, and otherwise stops where it stands, as the step-state rules in 02 fix; a step the controller refuses after the window has closed closes blocked-outside-envelope, naming the window as the line it would breach, and raises no request of its own, because the task is already in awaiting-authority on the tick's renewal request; an act that fired inside the window is reported in the outcome report. Where a step names a dependant's aliases, its validity is the earlier of this window and her delegation's validity, an extension of the window never extends past her delegation, and the Table 2.8 row covers her delegation's end as it covers the envelope's.

1.2The test and the rule

The point-of-no-return test is one question, asked of every step at planning and again on every edit: can we return the world to its prior state by our own action, at no cost to the user and without a third party's consent? If not, the step is irreversible. A booking with free cancellation passes; a submitted application, a payment, an identity number stated to an institution, or a cancellation that forfeits a deposit does not. The rule of classification is then mechanical and identical everywhere: irreversible and outside the envelope, bounce; irreversible and inside, dual control; reversible and inside, execute by ai or human as the classifier says; reversible and outside, an extension request, because even a harmless act outside the grant is not ours to take. Under dual control, whichever party executes, the other checks the step against the envelope and the expected outcome before it fires, and both identities are recorded.

Three default rules make the test bite on the surfaces, whatever a step's classification says, and each is a component's gate, not an instruction to a person. The browser controller treats any form submission or committing click on a party's domain as irreversible unless the step's catalogue entry names the undo route for it, so without one the action needs the approval token. Telephony mirrors it: the worker's outbound leg runs behind the same delay line as the inbound leg, and the media server's outbound gate matches the worker's speech against a commitment-form detector per language, agreement words, amounts, dates and account-changing verbs from the catalogue's commitment dictionary; a commitment-form utterance that is not the revealed committing sentence for a step holding an approval token is cut before delivery, the caption reads "commitment held; send for check", and the step enters checking; the far party never hears the cut utterance. The mail relay releases a message only if it instantiates the step's catalogue template, fixed sentences with named slots typed non-committing, or the step holds an approval token naming the hash of the message as checked; a message that matches no template is a committing action and needs the token. A reversible step holds no token, so on none of the three surfaces can it commit anything; the script's line telling a worker not to agree a fee stays as an instruction, and the gate is the closure.

The approval token is single-use and the act it approves fires at most once. The controller consumes the token before it issues the committing action, and the substitutor refuses a consumed token. Every controller writes a fired marker before it issues a committing action, a submit, a release, the committing utterance's start marker, and a step with a fired marker never closes failed-retryable: an unconfirmed fire, a submit that times out, a call that drops after the committing sentence, closes failed-final with the fired marker as its evidence, and the re-plan issues a verification step, a status enquiry, before any repeat.

1.3Edits

The user can extend or narrow the envelope at any time from their own bound device, and nobody else can. A proposal, version 1 with the plan or a re-proposal after revival, is the planner's unsigned draft and carries no authority; a version is authority only once the user's bound device signs it, and no other device signs one. A dependant onboarded as a delegator, with a bound device and vault entries of their own, is a second delegator and never a second editor, as 01 draws: her one tap, on her own device, signs a delegation record of her own, naming the act types and aliases she delegates and a validity no longer than the envelope's, and the envelope's identity-act line for her references that record and is inert until it exists; the envelope stays the user's, and the user's tap confirms it. Her record is a checked authority, not a note on the line: the substitutor's ordered checks in 03 include that every delegation record the alias's owner references is live, and her revocation, from her device like a connector, or her delegation's expiry voids every context and token of every step that names her aliases, exactly as an envelope edit does, and those steps re-classify, a step now without authority becoming a user step for her. An extension applies to steps not yet fired and re-runs classification for them. Narrowing applies immediately to every step not yet fired: a step in checking is re-checked against the narrowed envelope before it fires, and an irreversible step that has already fired is reported in the access ledger and in the outcome report, because a narrowed envelope cannot undo the world. The remaining cases, and the user's view of all this, are in 01.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

A standing mandate at account level
Set once, applied to every task. Authority stops belonging to a task: a mis-planned or hijacked task inherits everything the user ever granted, and the confirming tap becomes ceremony because it no longer changes anything.
A spend cap alone
The human-assisted assistants stop here. Duckbill executes purchases up to $2,500 on a card stored in its app verified [1]; Fancy Hands caps payments at $200 per request behind a per-payment approval button verified [2]. Money is one field of five. An application submitted, terms accepted, a call placed to a party the user never named, an identity number stated: none has a price and all are irreversible.
Confirm every step
Delegation collapses into supervised execution: the user becomes the bottleneck on the tasks they delegated to escape, and fatigue makes each tap meaningless, so the safety it claims disappears with use. The envelope asks once, properly, then acts.
Reversibility judged by the executor at the moment of acting
A judgement made under time pressure by the party with the least context. The test is asked at planning, recorded on the step, and re-asked on every edit; the executor inherits the answer.

3What this closes off

  • Any step executing before the envelope is confirmed. The state machine has no path from awaiting-authority to active except the user's authorisation.
  • An irreversible act outside the envelope by the AI, a worker, a surface controller, which checks the window and the version at every committing action, or the substitutor, which checks the envelope before every placement.
  • An irreversible act inside the envelope fired by one party alone.
  • An envelope that spans tasks, or a version of it carrying authority without the user's own bound device's signature; a proposal is unsigned and authorises nothing, and a dependant's tap signs a delegation record of her own, never an envelope version.
  • Any step firing after the validity window, or after a dependant's delegation has been revoked or has expired, without re-authorisation, reversible or not, at a placement or at a committing act that places no value.
  • A commitment reaching a party past the surface's gate: a spoken utterance of commitment form that is not the revealed sentence, or a relay message outside the catalogue template, on a step holding no approval token.
  • A cap exceeded by acts each under it, an approval token used twice, or a step with a fired marker re-queued.
  • Contact with a party the envelope does not name, and a delegated identity act the policy layer flags as bounce.
