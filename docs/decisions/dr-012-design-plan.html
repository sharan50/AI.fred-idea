DR-012The design plan: an engineering dossier

The publication is set as an engineering dossier on a dark ground: light ink on a near-black paper, Inter for prose, one accent spent on a stamp and one stroke per figure, a fixed status vocabulary, and diagrams as the memorable element, inverted with the ground. This record fixed the system after an independent three-lens critique and was revised on 2026-09-10 at the founder's direction.

On this page

1Decision

Every page of the publication is built from one token file, one component set and one diagram language, in the direction the founder agreed: an engineering dossier. The system is fixed as follows. Revised 2026-09-10: the founder judged the paper look too generic and unengaging and directed a dark ground with Inter as the prose face; the tokens, the components and the diagram language are unchanged in structure, so the revision is new values in the token file and new faces in the assets directory, exactly the route 1.1 reserves for a change, and print keeps the paper values so a PDF is still ink on white.

1.1Palette

Six colours in the roles the brief gives them, with the dark values the founder directed on 2026-09-10, plus two derived neutrals, defined once in tokens.css and nowhere else. The accent never fills an area and appears at most twice on any screen: the stamp and one stroke per figure. Links are ink with an ink underline. Amber and verified are dot and stroke colours only, never text. Line colour never carries meaning inside a figure.

1.2Type

Inter for prose, one variable face carrying every weight; IBM Plex Mono for the data voice: chips, control-block values, figure labels, table cells holding codes or values, the ledger, aliases and code. Never body text. Both faces are self-hosted as woff2 under the SIL Open Font Licence, with system fallbacks and no synthesised weights. Hierarchy comes from number, weight and space, not size: body 17px at 1.55; standfirst 1.1875rem; h1 1.625rem Medium; h2 1.25rem Medium with two and a half lines above; h3 at body size, Medium. Meta, chips and figure labels are 13px Mono. The measure is 60ch, about 72 characters. Text is left aligned with manual hyphenation and tabular numerals wherever numbers align.

1.3The numbering voice

One numbering voice runs through the publication. Documents are 00 to 08 and DR-001 onward, shown as a numeral hanging beside the title. Headings are numbered in the text as hanging numerals, 1 and 1.1, mirrored in the rail. Figures are Figure S.n and tables Table S.n, where S is the section digit (0 for the thesis, i for the index, D for decision records) and n runs across every page of the section. Cross-references are links whose text is the identifier or the document's name.

1.4Layout and page kinds

Every page opens with the same masthead, which names the venture in one sentence at every URL, and the same title block: the stamp top right, the numeral hanging in the rail column, the title, the standfirst that states the conclusion, and a document control block (Document, Revised, Status, Reader) closed by an ink rule. The rail carries two lists, the publication map and the page's own headings, so a cold reader always knows what this is, where it sits, how settled it is and where to go next. There are four page kinds, all from one component set and with no per-page CSS: the report (rail plus document column), the ledger (a data table listing rows that link to their pages, never cards), the single sheet (no rail, one column, prints on one page), and the decision record. Figure D.1 shows the anatomy.

1.5Components

Defined once in base.css and composed everywhere: masthead; page header with stamp and control block; rail; status chip; reference link and sources list; stamp; decision record; figure with a focusable scroll region and numbered caption; data table with a numbered caption above, an ink rule under the header, hairlines between rows and no fills; note with hairline rules and a run-in mono label; the invariant, the one place a heavy ink left rule is permitted; the ledger, a mono block with a timestamp column; inline code without a box; block code on the second ground; footer with the colophon and three fixed links. A new layout on a future page means extending tokens and components first.

1.6Status vocabulary at two levels

Five words, identical everywhere: decision, assumption, amber, to-verify and verified. Each chip is an outline in ink with a small dot, filled or hollow, so the status survives print and colour-blindness. A verified chip is always followed by a bracketed reference to a numbered source on the same page, with its date and address in the sources list; a hover title is never the only carrier. At page level the same words mean: amber, the page states an amber decision of its own with a trigger; to-verify, the page carries external claims not yet verified; assumption, the page rests on stated assumptions; decision, the page rests only on fixed decisions. Verified is never a page status. When several apply, the precedence is amber, then to-verify, then assumption, then decision, so a page's status is a function of its own inline chips and the harness checks it. The stamp in the title block carries the same word as the status chip.

1.7Diagram language

One canvas, 720 units wide on a 40-unit module, no frame and no background. One shape vocabulary: a component is an ink box; a human actor a dotted box; the user's own device a box with rounded corners; a zone or trust boundary a dashed rectangle labelled inside; a terminal state an ink-filled box; a lifeline the only use of line colour. Every distinction is a stroke pattern and a label, never a colour alone: the accent is a 2-unit stroke used exactly once per figure and named in the figure's description; verified is solid with a square end; amber is dashed. Labels are 13-unit mono in ink, 11 on edges. All colour and type come from classes that resolve to tokens; no colour or font attribute appears inside any SVG. Each figure has a title, a description, a focusable scroll region so labels never fall below about 11px on a phone, and a caption stating the takeaway.

1.8Print

Page size follows the printer (the three markets split between A4 and Letter) with 18mm and 16mm margins, a running head and a page counter where the engine supports page margin boxes. Navigation does not print; the wordmark, title block, control block, page contents and sources do. Figures, table rows, records and notes stay whole; headings keep their next paragraph; external links print their address; the page's own address is never printed, because the unguessable address is an interim mitigation, not access control (07). Chip dots are drawn as borders, so they print without colour adjustment.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

The first draft: a documentation-site skeleton
A masthead with a sections menu, a sticky rail of this page's headings only, a previous-and-next footer, a tinted callout with an accent rule, a tinted table header, an oversized heading ladder with a larger index title, and sources hidden in a hover title. It passed the letter of the brief's banned list and failed its spirit: three independent critics found it would read as another generated docs site, would leave the index, the two ledgers and the trust story to default to a hero and a card grid, and would print verified claims bare. Cost of rejecting it: a week of apparatus (numbering, control block, sources list, table rules, page kinds) that the first draft did not need.
A framework, a UI kit or a CSS library
Rejected in DR-011; restated here because the critique made the reason concrete: every kit ships the defaults the brief bans.
Heading numbers by CSS counters
Counters keep the markup clean but vanish when text is copied, are read unevenly by assistive technology and cannot be checked by the harness. Numerals in the text cost a small authoring discipline and are checked on every pass.
Source and date in a hover title only
Invisible in print, on touch and to keyboard users; it would have met the brief's "verified with source and date" for mouse users alone. The numbered reference and sources list cost a few lines per page.
A dark theme
A paper document has one look. A second palette would double the contrast work and the diagram classes for no reader the brief names. Reversed on 2026-09-10 by the founder, who found the paper look too generic and unengaging: the publication has one look again, dark on screen, with the print stylesheet keeping paper for the page that is printed. The cost named here was paid once, as one set of token values and one contrast pass, and the diagram classes needed no change because every colour in them is a token.
The one permitted moment of motion
The brief allows one deliberate non-user-triggered moment. We spend none; a dossier that moves is less trusted, not more.

3What this closes off

  • Per-page styles, inline styles and new colours anywhere but tokens.css. The harness rejects them.
  • The accent as a link colour, a callout rule, a fill or a text colour anywhere but the stamp.
  • Cards, card grids, heroes, shadows, gradients, all-caps labels, tracked-out type, middle-dot meta strings, arrow characters, sticky mastheads, hamburger menus and previous-and-next footers.
  • Any figure outside the 720-unit canvas, any colour or font attribute inside an SVG, any status carried by colour alone.
  • Any verified claim without a numbered, dated, addressed source on the same page.
  • A page whose stamp, status chip and manifest entry disagree.

4Status

Fixed decision, taken during the build on 2026-09-02 after the critique. Revising it means extending tokens and components first and re-running the harness; it does not mean styling a page. Revised 2026-09-10 by the founder: dark ground, Inter for prose, the diagrams inverted with the ground, recorded as batch 0004 of the dependency map; DR-011 and the brief's visual section were reopened by the change and the brief is left as the instruction set that produced the first edition.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title. This publication is a design record, not an offer.
