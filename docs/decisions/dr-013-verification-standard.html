In this edition a verified chip means the claim was checked against a search-engine excerpt of the named source, with the source's name, date and address recorded on the page, because the build environment blocked full page reads and capped web searches at 200 per session. No page circulates outside the founder's circle before its load-bearing sources are re-read from the page itself.

On this page

1Decision

A build decision, taken on 2026-09-02. The brief's research rule is that every external claim carries a chip, verified with source and date or to-verify, that nothing external is asserted from memory, and that where web access is unavailable everything external becomes to-verify and the loop notes the downgrade. Access in this build was partial rather than absent. Web search worked, capped at 200 calls per session, and the cap was reached during the research sprint. Page fetches were refused by the build environment's egress proxy for almost every host tried, regulators, vendors and press included; a handful of code-hosting and developer domains answered. The standard for this edition is therefore fixed as follows.

1.1What each chip means here

Verified: the claim's text was found in a search-engine excerpt attributed to the named page, and the page's sources list carries the source's name, title, publisher, date and address, with the words "read as a search excerpt" and the date of the check. Where a research note read an official text in full from a faithful mirror, an RBI direction, a press release or an API specification hosted on a code-hosting site, the sources entry says "read from the mirror" and that is the stronger case, not the norm. To-verify: everything else external, including a claim resting on a third party quoting a document, vendor or press commentary, and every sub-question the search budget never reached. The sprint covered the Indian concierge field, the US and UK assistant field, UK rails and part of India rails; US rails, the vault vendor class, the privacy-law mapping and the why-now evidence were not reached and carry to-verify chips wherever a page touches them.

1.2What the standard establishes and what it does not

An excerpt establishes that the source exists, is dated, and contains the sentence the page attributes to it. It does not establish the sentence's context, a correction the page may carry further down, detail behind a paywall, or a later revision of the same address. A verified chip in this edition is therefore a claim about a sentence, not about a page, and a reader who acts on one should treat it as a pointer to a source worth opening rather than as the source itself.

1.3Re-verification before circulation

The consequence is an open item in 07, owned by the next editor, with the trigger "before any page is shown outside the founder's circle": re-run the research sprint with page reads enabled, open every address a verified chip points at, confirm or correct the claim, and change the sources entry to say the page was read. A page whose load-bearing claims fail that re-read is corrected before it circulates, not after.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

Mark everything external to-verify
The brief's literal downgrade for no web access. Its cost is that the distinction the research did establish is lost: a claim found word for word in the named source's excerpt would carry the same chip as a claim nobody could find at all, chips become noise, and the next editor cannot tell which of several hundred claims to re-check first. The downgrade is written for a session with no access, and this one had search.
Assert from memory
Forbidden by the brief: it is September 2026, the field and the regulatory picture move fast, and a stale or invented claim under a chip the reader trusts is the worst outcome the research rules exist to prevent.
Wait for page reads to work
The loop is capped at forty turns and the missing capability has a stated downgrade, so the brief says re-aim, not exit. Waiting would have traded a documented limitation for no publication.
Call the excerpt verified without saying so
A reader would assume full reads. That is silence about what could not be verified, which the brief's forbidden moves name outright.

3What this closes off

  • Circulating any page externally before its load-bearing sources are re-read from the page itself; the item in 07 carries that trigger.
  • A verified chip whose sources entry does not say what was read and when.
  • Upgrading a to-verify chip on the strength of another excerpt or a secondary source; only a read of the named page upgrades it.
  • A verified chip on a claim whose source was not reached in the sprint, however familiar the claim feels.
  • A later edition reusing this edition's chips without re-running the sprint.

4Status

Fixed for this edition, taken during the build on 2026-09-02. It expires when the re-verification item in 07 closes: at that point every sources entry says the page was read, this record is marked superseded, and the chip vocabulary carries its full meaning again.
