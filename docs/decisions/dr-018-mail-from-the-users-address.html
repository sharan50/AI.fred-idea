DR-018Mail sent from the user's own address

Every email a third party receives for a user comes from the user's own address. Where the user has granted their mailbox's send scope, the relay we run sends it; where they have not, the harness writes the draft and the user sends it from their own mail app. Replies land in the user's inbox and reach us by the read connector or by the user's forward. No message to a third party leaves from an address of ours, and nobody migrates their mail to us.

1Decision

Every email a third party receives for a user comes from the user's own address. The relay we run composes it with aliases, the substitutor swaps the values in, and the message leaves either through the user's own mail provider, under a send scope the user granted on their own device, or by the user's own hand, from a draft the harness wrote. Which route a task takes is decided by what the user has connected, and the harness helps to the extent the user lets it: with a send scope we send, with a read scope we see the reply, with neither the user pastes the draft and forwards the reply. No message to a third party leaves from an address of ours; the per-task address at our domain that the previous edition sent from becomes inbound-only, an address the user forwards to. Nobody migrates their mail to us, and the recipient never sees a stranger's domain. The relay stays ours, as the ledger's line that every execution surface is ours requires: what changes is whose address the message carries, not who composes, checks and places.

1.1The two routes

On the relay route the user has granted the mailbox's send scope beside its read scope, on the consent screen 01 specifies for connectors. The relay composes with aliases, by a worker in the console or, where the scope block permits, by the ai executor; the substitutor swaps the values into the relay's memory-only outbound store under its ordered checks; the relay releases only a template instance or a message whose hash an approval token names, and writes the fired marker before a committing release; and the vault's edge proxy's channel connector submits the finished message through the user's provider, injecting the send-scope token as it injects every connector's token, so the relay holds no token and the provider keeps the sent copy in the user's own mailbox, which is the user's store and the point. The relay keeps no sent copy. On the draft route there is no send scope, so the mail step is a user step in the "do this yourself" form 03 fixes: the device shows the draft, written with the user's labels where a value belongs, because nothing of ours places a value anywhere but on a surface we run (DR-008); the user pastes it into their own mail app, fills their own values, sends it and taps done, and the ledger writes "you sent this yourself, from your own address". A reply on either route lands in the user's inbox. It reaches us by the read connector where the user has one, terminating at the vault's edge proxy with the digest set added and bound to its task by the thread of the relay's message id, kept for the ledger's retention so a late reply still binds; or, where the user has no connector, by the user's forward to the task's relay address, the inbound-only address 01 names at intake. What a worker asks a party to send a masked span on is the task's thread, never an address of ours.

1.2The send scope is not a connector

A connector is read-only by construction, and DR-016 closes off a connector that writes, pays or acts. The send scope is not a connector: it is the mail relay's route. It is granted separately, on the same consent screen and with the same switch; its token lives in the vault and is injected by the edge proxy; it is used by no component but the relay, for nothing but a release the relay makes under a step, with the step's template or approval token and the fired marker as for any committing action; and revoking the read scope revokes it, voiding the token in the vault and closing every open mail step blocked-needs-user, as 01 fixes for a connector. The classifier reads whether the scope is held as it reads the scope block: a mail step for a user without it is the user's, with a draft, and no special case is needed.

1.3The task's relay address, inbound only

The per-task address at our domain remains, inbound only: a random local part at the market's receiving domain, created at the task's first mail step, alive for the task's life plus the ledger's retention, never reused, and never a sender. It exists so that a user without a connector can forward a reply, and so that any user can hand us a notice, the maturity notice of 01's first journey, without connecting anything. Mail to it terminates at the vault's edge proxy like any other channel and is never a front door. The receiving domain carries the market's authentication records for what it receives; it sends nothing.

1.4The basis for a message in the user's name

A message in the user's name rests on the basis the last row of Table 3.5 in 03 records for a call: speaking to an institution as the customer under a recorded, revocable digital mandate with the ledger as its audit trail. Disclosure where a jurisdiction requires it is that row's flag, read for a message as for a call, and the refusal in 01 of impersonation without permitted delegation applies unchanged: we write as the user only within the acts they delegated, and never as a person who has not delegated to us.

1.5Where it is specified

The relay's mechanics and the two routes are in 02, 4.3 and 03, 3.3; the connectors and the send scope in 01, 6.2; the reply path in Table 3.6 of 03, path 3; the stage 5 gate in 06; the providers' send scopes as an open item in 07.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

A per-task address at our domain
The previous edition's design: a random local part at our sending domain per task, replies threaded to it. Its cost is the one the founder named: the recipient sees a stranger's domain and may not answer, the reply lands with us so the user has no view of the exchange except through us, and a late reply lives on an address the user does not own. It survives here only as an inbound address for forwards.
An AI.fred mailbox per user
One address of ours per user, the user moving their correspondence to it. The migration is the headache the product exists to remove, and every institution that already holds the user's address would keep writing to the old one.
Forwarding rules in the user's mailbox
A rule the user sets that copies mail to us. Fragile and provider-specific, a copy of every message crosses to us whether a task needs it or not, and the user cannot see, per task, what we read; the read connector, with its scope, its consent date and its switch, is the honest form of the same access.
The user's mail credentials held by us
Sending through the user's own login or app password stored on our side. A credential of the user's held outside the substitutor, which the vault model forbids; a send scope under the provider's consent is a revocable grant, not a credential, and the vault's proxy injects it.
A bounce with no draft
For a user without a send scope, a plain user step: write and send it yourself. It keeps our hands clean and gives the user nothing; the draft route keeps the composition, the template rule and the ledger line, and leaves the user only the paste.

3What this closes off

  • A message to a third party from any address but the user's own: no sending domain of ours, and no display name of the user's over an address of ours.
  • A user asked to move, forward by rule, or migrate their mail to an address of ours.
  • The send scope used by any component but the relay, for anything but a release under a step, or surviving the revocation of the read scope.
  • A value of the user's placed by us into a draft on the device: the draft carries the user's labels, and the user fills their own values.
  • A raw byte of a reply held by a process of ours, whichever route it arrives by: the connector's fetch and the forward both terminate at the vault's edge proxy.
  • A sent copy kept by the relay, or a history of sent mail a worker can open.
  • A worker asking a party to write to an address of ours: a masked span is asked for on the task's thread.

4Status

Fixed decision, taken by the founder on 2026-09-10 in the publication's first deliberate revision and recorded as batch 0002 of the dependency map (DR-017). The relay is specified in 02 and 03, the connectors and the send scope in 01, and the providers' send scopes are an open item in 07 with an owner and a trigger. The gateway of DR-001 and the read-only connectors of DR-016 are confirmed unchanged by this record.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title. This publication is a design record, not an offer.
