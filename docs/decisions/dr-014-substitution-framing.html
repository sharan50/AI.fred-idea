1Decision

Framing A, boundary-sequence led, is the organisation of sections 1 to 4 of 03 Trust and data. It follows one real value across five boundaries, from the user's hands to an institution's; Figure 3.1 draws the zones and Figure 3.2 is a substitution sequence in the canon's lifeline language. Both judges who returned picked A: the engineer lens, building the substitutor and the evidence pipeline from the text alone, and the investor-and-customer lens.

In the judges' terms A won on the two things the brief calls expensive to get wrong. Closure: A names five crossings and, where they leak in practice, gives a mechanism rather than an assertion, a fixed delay line on the worker's browser stream and on inbound call audio so the masker and the digest matcher finish before a frame or a segment reaches a person, with a late read-back replaced by a tone; neither judge found an open path through evidence, logs, console, telephony audio or the ledger. Figures: A keeps the vault inside the accent outline of Figure 3.1, and Figure 3.2 carries the value into the surface on one accent message with nothing returning that carries it. Canon: A's tables, codes and figure grammar agree with the shared specification. Buildability: a tie. Legibility: B led for the engineer, so the graft is large.

What A now carries from B, its spine and figures unchanged: the argument that "placed by the system; no person saw it" is not a promise about culture but a property of the component that placed the value, of the stream the worker watched and of the pipeline that kept the evidence, each recurring ledger claim mapped to the property that makes it true; the rule that the placement and the line are one transaction, "placing" written before the vault call and "placed" confirmed from the surface's own signal, so a substitutor that cannot write the ledger cannot place; every ledger line written from a component's own record, never from a worker's attestation; the alias grammar and versioning; the partial-forms and echo-expected columns of the class table; class-format over-masking after a submit; the dual-control token, the placement window and the inbound-call rule checked at the substitutor; the placement registry, the redaction manifest, the verifier's signature as the only key to the store and the viewer table; a ledger-line column in the failure table; and in Figure 3.1 an arrow from the substitutor to the ledger and a "reads" arrow to the user's device.

Two defects the judges found in A were repaired in the same pass: the substitutor checked step state, envelope and policy while its egress reached none of them, so every request now carries a signed step context; and the mail relay now acts as surface controller, which settles the direction of its endpoint. A's "blocks an unchecked ok" gave way to the canon's rule that artefacts attach for audit and dispute only.

2Rejected alternative(s) and their cost

Framing B, ledger-led: what it did better
B opened with the access ledger as the specification: five events in plain words, and a component earning the right to perform one only by being built so that its line is true. For a customer that is the most legible statement in either text, and its honesty argument for each line answered the investor's question directly. Its substitutor and evidence contracts were the more complete.
The cost: a read-back path left open
The founder's bar allows no path by which a person could see a real value. B let a far-end agent's read-back after the mute tail reach the worker live, cleaning only the transcript and the stored audio, and asserted browser masking before the frame left the boundary with no timing mechanism behind it. As the spine, B would have shipped the fatal path open.
The cost: its figures
B's Figure 3.1 drew the vault, labelled "real values at rest", outside the accent line its caption called the last boundary, and left the worker actor unconnected. Its Figure 3.2 was a ledger-row matrix, not the mandated substitution sequence: the caller, the checks, the vault call and the "no value returned" reply were lost, and the header "receives the value" sat over "Worker stream" and "Store", where a sceptic stops. That matrix is the rejected form.
The cost: drift from the canon
B declared five ledger events as the only ways a value is used, drew six, and wrote other lines elsewhere. Its class table admitted payment instruments to browser and telephony only while its events table, figure and example ledger sent them through the relay, a contradiction an engineer coding the class policy meets on day one. Its verifier derived its own hash set from the vault, a second component with value-derived rights. Against the bar that no two statements may contradict, B contradicted itself inside one page.

3What this closes off

  • A second competing organisation of sections 1 to 4 of 03: later edits extend the boundary sequence rather than reframe it.
  • Re-litigating Figures 3.1 and 3.2: the zone map with the vault inside the accent and the eight-lifeline sequence are fixed; corrections touch labels and messages, not the form.
  • A ledger-first rewrite: the access ledger is specified in section 8 of 03 as what the boundaries produce, and it does not become the spine.

4Status

Fixed on 2026-09-02, taken during the build rather than in the founder's ledger: the brief calls for two genuinely different framings of the substitution and redaction specification, picked against the bars in its sections 8.1 and 8.2, with the loser recorded. Three judges were briefed, one per lens; the engineer and the investor-and-customer lenses returned, both for A, and the adversary lens did not return in time, so its hunt for an open path falls to the build's adversarial review and to the threat model in 03. DR-009 fixes the redaction the specification contains and DR-012 the diagram language it draws in.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title. This publication is a design record, not an offer.
