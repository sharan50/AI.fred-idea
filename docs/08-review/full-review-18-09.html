1Executive verdict

VerdictNot yet. The record now knows exactly what it has to measure and has measured none of it, and the round it asks for is sized to a line its own geography decision has already left.

DecisionNot yet, and the offer is closer than it was on 11 September. We would fund the India-only company that review described, at a seed sized net of tax to the record's own conservative line, on four conditions met before the cheque rather than after it: a signed technical co-founder who has shipped a tokenisation or payments boundary; the thirty-task run of the record's own measurement protocol, with its numbers; two hundred refundable deposits against the $52 price; and Indian counsel's signature on the identity rows of Table 3.5.

What would prove this wrongIf by 31 December 2026 the measured run returns an AI-only share above 40 per cent and a median assisted time under twelve minutes on the record's own rubric, and two hundred households have paid a deposit, the verdict is wrong and we would say so and fund. 2The idea in one page, at its strongest

At its strongest the idea is this. Every household has a short list of tasks it dreads, and they share a shape: they touch a bank, a government office, an insurer, a hospital or a court; they need the person's own identity numbers, documents and money; they take hours of waiting, calling and re-submitting; and a mistake costs something real. Today those tasks are done badly by the person, or handed to a human assistant who is trusted with everything, or refused outright by the software agents the labs shipped in 2025 and withdrew in 2026. The record's thesis is that the dreaded tasks are the door, that a company which can be trusted with them earns the right to the ordinary ones, and that the context built task by task is what makes the ordinary ones cheap (DR-019, revised 2026-09-13).

The mechanism is what no rival has. The user's real values, an Aadhaar or National Insurance number, a card, a balance, a diagnosis, enter a bought vault at the edge and are replaced by typed aliases before anything on the company's side reads them (03, 1, revised 2026-09-13). The AI plans; a worker on a floor in Bengaluru executes one bounded step at a time from a task card that names the client as a number and every value as an alias (Worker console, revised 2026-09-13); a single component, the substitutor, places a real value into a browser field or a mail body under a signed step context, and writes a ledger line the user can read (03, 3). Every task carries an authority envelope; every step meets a point-of-no-return test; anything irreversible either bounces to the user's own device or runs under dual control (DR-004). OTPs, PINs, bank logins and biometrics never enter the system at all: they are not aliases, they are bounces. The claim the record makes for this design is exact and defensible: "no worker ever sees a real value" is a property of a component boundary, not of a policy, and the specification now tests it.

The economics are stated as one ratio, and the record is right that it is the one. "Delivery cost scales with usage, not with users. The governing number is therefore human minutes per user per month" (05, 2, revised 2026-09-14). Four levers move it: a task allowance of 26 a month, a price of $52 in India, an automation ceiling of 80 per cent and an assisted floor of 1.7 minutes a task; with those set the planning line falls from 246 minutes a user in month 1 to 104 at month 18, 61 at month 36 and 42 at month 60, and the company crosses into contribution around month 25 [EST]. The record does not claim those numbers are true. It claims they are the right numbers to measure, and it built a thirty-task protocol to measure them (Measurement protocol). Figure 8.19 draws the ratio over the plan's own months; the seed window ends where the line is still at 104.

Why now is argued in three parts and two of them hold. The labs shipped consumer agents and withdrew them: Operator closed on 31 August 2025, the agent mode of ChatGPT was removed in August 2026, Atlas closed on 9 August 2026, Google's Mariner ended on 4 May 2026 to-verify [14] to-verify [15], and the survivors hand the keyboard back at a login or a purchase and tell users to keep them off financial, legal and medical sites to-verify [16]. The rails for delegated consent are being built by the state and the networks rather than by the assistants: DigiLocker's requester model already puts the consent screen on the citizen's own device with a stated purpose and an expiry verified [17]; UPI Circle admits software profiles under a cap to-verify [18]; the Treasury is consulting on agentic payments to-verify [11]. The third part, that people are being taught to delegate by the assistants themselves, is a hope about habit, and the record's own endgame page admits the previous cadence of "three or four hard tasks a year" could not form one (DR-019).

The founder's advantage is the record itself. Nothing in the Indian field publishes what its assistants may see; nothing in the American field masks values from its own staff; the labs will not touch the wedge. Twelve pages, twenty-seven decisions with rejected alternatives, a dependency map, a model with its critique attached, a threat model with twenty-six insider paths, and an executable specification that fails when a page changes a word of an invariant: that is more design discipline than most seed-stage companies show at their A, and it was produced by one person in about six weeks. The bet at its strongest is that the same discipline, applied to a cohort of two hundred Indian households and a floor of six, turns the ratio page from a prior into a measurement before the money runs out, and that the measurement lands near the planning line. If it does, the company that results is the only one in three markets that can be trusted with the tasks that matter, at a price its rivals cannot match because their human share never fell.

3.1What the revision proves about the founder

Eleven findings, six answered in substance, two in wording, three standing. The three that stand are the three that are not documents: a person, a cohort and a lawyer's signature. That pattern is the review's central observation about the founder and it cuts both ways. Given a finding that can be answered by writing, the founder answers it, quickly and well, and the record is now unusually honest about its own limits: the model page lists its artefacts, the critique sits beside the rationale, the specification records its seams. Given a finding that can only be answered by leaving the record, nothing has moved in a week. A seed partner reads that as a founder who is stronger at building the argument than at exposing it to a customer, and prices the round accordingly.

3.2The geography decision moved the cash and the round did not

The largest substantive change is DR-023, and it moved the wrong way for the raise. The review of 11 September argued for India only inside the seed; the revision promoted the United Kingdom to months 8 to 14 on the ground that late expansion "is worse than not expanding" because a foreign entity's standing cost arrives before its users (05, 8, revised 2026-09-14). The contribution argument is sound on the model's own terms. What the record then does is leave Table 5.4 at $2.5m to $3m while its own paragraph above the table says the sequence of record asks for 22.3 crore twice, that the seed is "27 per cent larger than stated and a Series A two and a half times it", and that the band's "floor is exactly equal to" the arithmetic. Figure 8.20 sets every cash figure the record and its runs contain against the band.

The arithmetic a partner would put in the memo is short. Take the record's rule, window burn on the conservative line times 1.3, and apply it to the 22.3 crore the record states for the sequence of record: the seed is about 29 crore, or $3.3m [EST]. Take the tax out, which lifts the conservative window burn by about nine per cent on the published line [EST], and it is nearer 32. Take a percentile of the paths instead of the conservative average, which the model page itself says is the better statistic, and the foreign-led run's eightieth percentile is 33.0 crore before any buffer (models/geo_growth_percentiles.csv). Either the sequence goes back to India only inside the seed, in which case the model's India-only run needs 12.8 crore on its planning line and 18.3 on its conservative one [EST] and the band holds with room, or the round is $3.5m and the pitch says why. The record cannot commit to DR-023 and Table 5.4 at once, and it does.

4Technology today: what exists, what is plausible, what is research

The record lists twenty components, four bought and sixteen built (06, Table 6.1, revised 2026-09-14). This section asks of each layer the only question that matters at seed: does it exist as something the company can buy or copy, is it plausible to build in the window, or is it research. The review of 11 September answered for the model layer; this one goes to the vendors and the code, because the revision moved the risk from the models to the boundary.

4.1The bought boundary does not exist in the shape the record buys it

DR-008 buys a tokenisation vault whose edge proxy tokenises inbound traffic in region, and 02 has that proxy run four detectors, for identity numbers, payment instruments, financial facts and the health and legal dictionary, before anything reaches the intake gateway (02, 9, revised 2026-09-13). No vendor sells that. What the market sells is a structured inbound proxy that tokenises named fields of a webhook or form: Skyflow's inbound Connections, with a Mumbai vault since December 2021 to-verify [7]; VGS inbound routes in Virginia, Frankfurt and Singapore to-verify [19]; Basis Theory's proxy, whose documentation names India but whose SDKs map only United States and European hosts verified [8]. Custom detection inside the proxy is regex lists in Skyflow's Detect API verified [9] or a sandboxed Starlark script in VGS verified [20]; none runs the customer's dictionary, an OCR model or a Hindi named-entity model over free text, images or audio inline. The nearest buildable shape is the one the record should draw: a thin ingress the venture owns, in Mumbai and in a British or European region, running Presidio with its Indian recognisers for Aadhaar, PAN, GSTIN, passport and voter number verified [21] plus the venture's own dictionary, calling the vault's tokenise API; the vault becomes a key-value store with policy. That is a build, it is on the promise path, and the "second provider swap" of DR-008 collapses to swapping a client library, which is where it should be.

Three consequences follow. The detectors, the most consequential code in the company, are the venture's to write and to prove, which is what the review of 11 September said the co-founder must have shipped before. A Skyflow-class contract is quote-only and reported near $195,000 a year to-verify [7]; Basis Theory starts at $995 a month for 20,000 active tokens, in a United States environment to-verify [22]; VGS from $1,000 a month to-verify [19]. And the redaction of screenshots and transcripts in Indian languages, which the record treats as a detector configuration, is research: Amazon Comprehend's PII detection is English only verified [23], Presidio publishes no accuracy for Hindi or Hinglish, and no vendor reached publishes one either.

4.2The hash service is a second copy of the vault

The specification records, as seam 10, that the hash service must hold every vault value in memory each time it computes its digest sets, or the vault must compute them (spec/SEAMS.md, seam 10). No vendor reached offers a keyed digest computed inside the vault against arbitrary inputs; the nearest primitive is Basis Theory's fingerprint, made at tokenise time from an expression to-verify [22], or Skyflow's deterministic token, which equals itself but is not a keyed hash over free text verified [24]. So the component that screens every worker-bound frame for an echo of a real value is, in the design as written, a service that periodically loads every real value. Section 12 treats the consequence; here the point is classification: this is plausible to build, and it is not what "bought, not built" means.

4.3Computer use, injection and the managed browser

Short tasks on a browser are a solved problem at the benchmark level and long ones are not, and both facts changed since 11 September. On OSWorld-Verified, the benchmark whose maintainers replaced the original set in July 2025 verified [25], GPT-5.5 is reported at 78.7 per cent and Claude Opus 4.8 at 82.3, above the 72.4 human baseline to-verify [26]. On OSWorld 2.0, 108 workflows with a median human time of 1.6 hours, the best result reported is 20.6 per cent complete and 54.8 partial to-verify [27]. The record's journeys are the second kind: multi-step, multi-site, days long, with a wait in the middle. That is why its AI steps are single bounded actions and its human steps carry the workflow, and it is the correct reading of the evidence.

Injection is the reason the human is there, and the numbers are mixed. AgentDojo's published table has the best model of early 2025 at a 7.31 per cent targeted attack success rate with 77 per cent utility under attack, and weaker models at 20 to 26 verified [28]; an independent 2026 study of two open-source browser agents reports 41.67 to 68.16 per cent indirect success over 3,168 runs to-verify [29]; Anthropic reports 0 to 0.3 per cent on a new red-team set for Claude in Chrome at general availability on 26 August 2026, which its own post says replaced a saturated set, so the 23.6 the record quotes and the 0.3 are not like for like to-verify [16]. The record's controls, no credential in the agent, bounce at the point of no return, a substitutor that never reads a page, are the ones Meta's "Agents Rule of Two" says survive an adaptive attacker: an agent may have at most two of untrustworthy inputs, sensitive data and the power to change state verified [30]. AI.fred's executor has the first and the third and never the second, which is the right pair.

The isolated browser with a human live view and no recording is off the shelf. Browserbase exposes a live debugger URL and records sessions by default unless recordSession is set false verified [31]; Hyperbrowser's SDK lists an asia-south region and opt-in recording flags verified [32]; Cloudflare's Browser Run prices at $0.09 a browser-hour verified [33]. None names a British region and only Hyperbrowser's label suggests Mumbai; a contractual "recording disabled" was not seen anywhere. The record's residency map should say which of these it will accept, because the vault's region is now the only one it controls.

4.4Calls, mail, memory and the rails

The call is a person on a leg from the company's number, and after DR-021 nothing from the vault goes on it (03, 4.2, revised 2026-09-13). That exists in India without registration under the 2020 and 2021 guidelines for other service providers to-verify [34]; it exists in the United Kingdom only if the call originates on a British carrier, since Ofcom's condition C6 has blocked calls from abroad presenting a British number since 15 May 2023 to-verify [35]; and in the United States only if the call originates on domestic facilities with the Bengaluru worker joined over SIP, since a foreign-originated call bearing an American number carries the lowest attestation and is labelled or blocked to-verify [36]. Mail from the user's address under a send scope exists: Microsoft Graph's Mail.Send needs no administrator consent verified [37]; Google's send scope is classed sensitive and needs brand verification, not the restricted-scope security assessment to-verify [38]. The temporal context graph is a shipped open-source library: Graphiti under Apache-2.0 verified [39], with the community edition of the product built on it no longer supported verified [40]. The rails are the same as on 11 September: DigiLocker's requester model with its consent page, purpose values and expiry is specified verified [17]; Account Aggregator data flows only to regulated information users verified [41]; the software profiles of UPI Circle are a closed-group pilot to-verify [18].

4.5The executable specification

The revision compiled the record into code: schemas for every record, reference implementations of the state machine, the envelope engine, the classifier, the hash service, the substitutor's ordered checks and the evidence pipeline, with fault-injection and property tests and a canary, 101 tests passing (spec/README.md, read in full; the suite run for this review). It is the most useful artefact the record added, and it should be read for what it is. It proves that the pages are consistent with each other and with the map's vocabulary. It does not prove that the boundary holds: the vault, the proxy and the browser are stubs, the detectors are not implemented, and every number is illustrative. The thirty-one seams it records are, read as a list, the specification of the second engineer's first quarter.

6Regulatory position: India

India is the market where the promise can be exercised, and the review of 11 September said the whole case turns on whether Indian counsel signs the identity rows of Table 3.5 as delegated. The record has not asked counsel; this section reads the texts instead, through dated mirrors where the regulators' own hosts were blocked, and classes each rule as build, licence, contract or refusal. The short answer: nothing read forbids a private company holding a customer's identity numbers and filling a form for them; three rules decide what it can never do (be on a video KYC, hold a card number if it is "in the chain", initiate a payment with a stored credential); and the statute that governs everything, the DPDP Act, lands in full in May 2027, inside the seed window and before the first British user.

6.1Data protection: a fiduciary from May 2027, never a consent manager

The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025 and rule 1 phases them: the Board and appeal machinery on publication, consent-manager registration one year on, and everything a fiduciary must do, notice, security, breach, retention, children, transfers, significant-fiduciary duties, "eighteen months after the date of publication" verified [53]. That is May 2027. AI.fred is a data fiduciary on the Act's definition, the person who "determines the purpose and means of processing" verified [54]; it cannot be a consent manager, because the First Schedule requires that a consent manager's sharing be "such that the contents thereof are not readable by it" and AI.fred reads everything verified [53]. The record's residency map should stop treating the consent-manager route as a rail. Two duties bite the design directly. A hospital booking for a minor needs verifiable parental consent under rule 10 from May 2027; and rule 7 requires notice to every affected principal "without delay" and a report to the Board within seventy-two hours, which the access ledger can generate and the record should say so verified [53]. Significant-fiduciary status, with its India-based officer, annual audit, algorithmic due diligence and localisation of specified categories, is by government notification on "volume and sensitivity" with no numeric threshold verified [54]; a company holding PAN, Aadhaar, card and account numbers, dates of birth and call recordings for households is a plausible candidate, and the record should carry it as an amber with a trigger rather than not at all. Cross-border transfer is default-permitted, subject to orders not yet made verified [53]; that is what keeps a foreign-hosted vault possible and what could close it with one notification. Penalties run to Rs 250 crore for a security failure verified [54]. Class: build, with one amber.

6.2Payments: the dynamic factor and the card chain

The Authentication Directions were issued on 25 September 2025 and comply-by is 1 April 2026 verified [55]. Their operative clause, quoted identically by three secondaries and not read at source, requires for every transaction other than card-present that "at least one of the factors of authentication is dynamically created or proven", and makes the issuer compensate in full for a loss on a non-compliant transaction to-verify [56]. A stored, replayable credential held by AI.fred cannot be that factor. The record's design, "user taps UPI on own device", is the compliant shape and the only one; the "make a payment under the cap" row of Table 3.5 is bounce not because of policy caution but because of this rule. The card rule is the one the record has not faced. The circular of 7 September 2021 says that "no entity in the card transaction / payment chain, other than the card issuers and / or card networks, shall store the actual card data" from 1 January 2022, and makes the networks responsible for "all entities involved" verified [57]. AI.fred is not an issuer, a network, an aggregator or a merchant, and a vault that fills a merchant's form on the cardholder's instruction looks like a password manager, which the circular does not address. But the networks are made answerable for every entity in the chain, and an entity that programmatically injects card numbers into checkouts can be treated as in it. No text either way was found. This is the first question for Indian counsel and the record's {{card.primary}} alias depends on the answer. Class: build for UPI; refusal or contract for stored cards pending counsel.

6.3Identity: Aadhaar may be held, KYC video may not be joined

The Aadhaar Act binds requesting entities and offline verification-seeking entities: identity information they hold may not be used or disclosed beyond the stated purpose, and no Aadhaar number or photograph may be "published, displayed or posted publicly" verified [58]. The offence in section 37 attaches to information "collected in the course of enrolment or authentication", which AI.fred does not perform; nothing read forbids a private company holding a number its customer gave it. What bites is downstream: the KYC Master Direction requires regulated entities to redact the Aadhaar number, so a bank will not accept a full number from a third party anyway verified [59], and the design should hold a virtual ID or a masked form and verify offline by QR. Video KYC is closed to the company outright: the process "shall be operated only by officials of the RE", "any prompting observed at end of customer shall lead to rejection", and assistance is permitted only from a bank's own business correspondent at the customer's end verified [59]. The record's bounce on "undergo identification" is therefore not a to-verify; it is the rule. The rows "present a document", "state an identity number" and "complete a KYC form" have no text against them and one practical barrier: reliance. A bank performs its own due diligence on data a delegate enters, so the record's framing, "data entry on the customer's behalf is not reliance on a third party", is the right question and the one for counsel. DigiLocker is a requester model: the consent screen is the citizen's, with a purpose value and a consent_valid_till verified [17], and requesters are onboarded by MeitY through API Setu, which is a contract and a wait, not a licence. Account Aggregator data flows only to information users regulated by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA; an unregulated assistant receives nothing except through a regulated partner verified [41] to-verify [34]. Class: build for documents and numbers, refusal for the video, contract for the two rails.

6.4The floor: calls, personation, recording and the night shift

A domestic voice floor needs no registration since the 2020 and 2021 guidelines for other service providers to-verify [34], and calls placed to an institution on a customer's instruction are not commercial communications under the telemarketing regulations, though calls from ordinary SIMs at volume get flagged to-verify [34]. Personation is a crime only with cheating: section 319 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita and section 66D of the IT Act both require deception with dishonest inducement verified [60] verified [61]. A worker who says they call on behalf of the customer commits nothing; a script that has the worker answer an identity challenge "as" the customer is where the exposure begins, and the record's "basis" row in Table 3.5, "speak to an institution as the customer", is the row that needs rewording before it needs a lawyer. 8Regulatory position: the United States

The record says "the research sprint did not reach the US rails, so this section is design intent" (03, 5.4, revised 2026-09-13). 8.1Regulation E does have a rule, and it runs against the user

The record states that "Regulation E has no rule for a standing authorisation given to an agent". It has one. Section 1005.2(m) excludes from "unauthorized electronic fund transfer" a transfer "by a person who was furnished the access device to the consumer's account by the consumer, unless the consumer has notified the financial institution that transfers by that person are no longer authorized", and the official comment 2(m)-2 adds that where such a person "exceeds the authority given, the consumer is fully liable for the transfers" until the bank is told verified [4]. So if a user furnishes AI.fred with a card, a credential or any other means of access and an unwanted transfer follows, it is not unauthorised; the $50 and $500 tiers never engage; the user bears the whole loss until they notify the bank; the user's remedy against AI.fred is contract. Three gaps remain and they are what the record should say instead: a per-task network token requested from a token service under the user's consent is untested against "furnished by the consumer"; a scoped mandate does not limit liability, because exceeding the authority is covered; and revocation is a notice to the bank, not to AI.fred. No CFPB statement on AI agents was found, and the personal financial data rule under Section 1033 is enjoined since 29 October 2025 with a replacement sent to review on 6 August 2026, so aggregator access runs on bilateral contract and bank fees are a live possibility verified [79] to-verify [80]. Class: build (the user furnishes nothing that is an access device) plus contract (who pays, in the terms).

8.2Liability and state AI law: one defence removed, no duty added

California's AB 316, chapter 672 of 13 October 2025, provides that in an action against a defendant who "developed, modified, or used" artificial intelligence, "it shall not be a defense ... that the artificial intelligence autonomously caused the harm", while preserving every other defence including comparative fault verified [52]. It removes one defence and creates no duty; the access ledger naming who placed every value is the answer to it, and the user's instruction is comparative-fault evidence. Colorado's act was repealed and re-enacted on 14 May 2026 as an automated-decision law applying from 1 January 2027 to consequential decisions about a consumer; an assistant that files for its user is not the decision-maker to-verify [81]. Texas's act of 1 January 2026 leaves private actors with intent-based prohibitions only to-verify [82]. The December 2025 executive order and the FTC's proposed policy statement of July 2026 assert pre-emption and enact none; state law applies to-verify [83]. Class: build.

8.3Privacy: nearly everything AI.fred holds is sensitive

The CCPA reaches a business over $25m in revenue as adjusted, or one handling 100,000 consumers' data verified [84]; a seed-stage entry falls under it only at scale, but its definition of sensitive personal information already covers what the vault holds: account and card numbers with credentials, precise location, "citizenship or immigration status", "contents of a consumer's mail, email, and text messages", health and sex-life data verified [84]. The same section lets a consumer "authorize another person to opt out ... on the consumer's behalf", the one place American privacy law contemplates the agent. Washington's health-data act makes a recorded insurer call naming a diagnosis "consumer health data", permits its collection for a requested service, and gives a private right of action through the consumer-protection act to-verify [85]. Illinois treats a voiceprint as a biometric identifier with a written-release requirement and statutory damages; a raw recording is not a voiceprint, a speaker embedding used to identify is to-verify [86]. Class: build (a health-data policy, no voiceprints, retention bounded to the task).

8.4Calls, pretexting and the acts the company may not do

Section 227(b) of the Communications Act bars artificial or prerecorded voices to cellular and residential lines without consent verified [87], and the FCC ruled on 8 February 2024 that AI-generated voices are such voices to-verify [36]; a person dialling a business line is outside it. Recording is one-party under federal law verified [87] and all-party in about eleven states; California's section 632 attaches to a "confidential communication" and the cellular section does not need confidentiality to-verify [88], so the build is an audible announcement on every call. A call from Bengaluru presenting an American number carries the lowest attestation under the gateway rules and is labelled or blocked unless it originates on domestic facilities to-verify [36]. Three acts are closed. Obtaining a customer's financial information "by making a false, fictitious, or fraudulent statement" to a bank is the federal pretexting offence and it has no customer-consent exception verified [89]: speaking to a bank "as the customer" is a refusal in America as it is a rewording in India. Representation before the immigration service is confined to attorneys, accredited representatives and unpaid "reputable individuals", and "preparation, constituting practice" includes selecting forms and assessing eligibility for pay, with an exception only for filling blank spaces for nominal reward verified [90]. Practice before the revenue service "comprehends all matters connected with a presentation" including "corresponding and communicating" with it, and limited practice is confined to family and employees verified [91]. Public-adjuster licensing covers property claims for compensation in California and nearly everywhere; health claims are outside it and need a HIPAA authorisation verified [92]. Account opening requires the bank to take the identifying information "from the customer" verified [93]. India is not a country of concern under the data-security rule of 8 April 2025, so no federal statute restricts the transfer verified [94]. Class: refusal for bank impersonation, immigration and tax representation, property claims and account opening; build for clerical assembly; contract with an attorney, an enrolled agent or a licensed adjuster where the task needs one.

The record sizes nothing top-down and says so; its one market number is a prior in the growth model, "a reachable Indian market between twenty-five thousand and two hundred and fifty thousand users at the tenth and ninetieth percentiles" (05, 6, revised 2026-09-14). 9.1Bottom-up: what the field has actually sold

Faff, the record's Indian benchmark, charges Rs 1,999 a month for unlimited tasks, invite-only, reports "75k+ tasks so far", had 54 employees on 31 May 2026 and has raised $2.59m by one database and is "bootstrapped" by another to-verify [13] to-verify [99] to-verify [100]. If a paying household runs four to eight tasks a month for a year, 75,000 tasks is one to two thousand paying households in total [EST]. Hulp, funded with $2.6m in August 2026, reports 100 families, 3,000 tasks and a 50-person operations team to-verify [3]. M raised Rs 102 crore in April 2026 and has published no price or user count to-verify [101]. Swiggy Crew is Rs 999 for three months to-verify [102]. So the whole visible paid market for a human assistant in Indian cities today is low thousands of households at a quarter to a half of the record's price [EST]: AI.fred asks Rs 4,628 a month, or Rs 5,460 with tax, for 26 tasks, against Faff's Rs 1,999 for as many as the customer likes. The record's planning line needs 273 users in month 1 and about 89,000 by month 60 with marketing (models/summary_growth_viable.json) [EST]. A priced waiting list is not a nicety; it is the only way to learn whether a household pays 2.3 times Faff for a trust property it cannot see.

Abroad, the willingness-to-pay band the record needs exists and is thin. The gates are reachable; the record has no channel to reach them except word of mouth from a cohort that does not yet exist.

10Business model and unit economics

The deferral is dead and the model is real: twenty thousand paths, sixty months, every driver drawn from a stated prior, reproducible to the character, and interrogated by its own author with a sensitivity, a critique and three rounds of fresh-context passes that found and disclosed its errors (05, revised 2026-09-14; the model page). That is more than most seed decks carry and we say so. It is also, on its own admission, a structured way of being wrong until a customer exists, and the interrogation the brief asks for lands in the places the record has already half-named and in three it has not. We take them in the brief's order.

10.1Gross or net of consumption tax

Net, until counsel says otherwise, and the record now says the same. Neither model carries a tax term on either side, and the page states that dividing the Indian price by 1.18 and the British by 1.20 moves the planning trough from 14.1 crore to 18.7, the conservative line from 20.5 to 28.5, revenue per user at month 36 from $70 to $59 and the share of paths profitable from month 36 onward from 84 to 71 per cent (05, 4). Two things the record leaves out. First, the direction of the answer is not open: a consumer subscription sold in India carries goods and services tax at the standard rate the record itself assumes, and the priced waiting list of DR-023 will quote a price the customer pays, so the safe reading is the only reading until an invoice exists [EST]. Second, the export half: a subscription sold from an Indian entity to a UK or US resident is a cross-border supply whose zero-rating and place-of-supply treatment depends on how the service is characterised and where the entity that bills sits, which is a question the record's own open item names for counsel and which the foreign-led sequence of DR-023 makes urgent rather than later (07, the gross-or-net item). Our estimate of the swing is the record's: a seventh of Indian revenue and a sixth of British revenue in every figure, and every figure on 05 read net of tax until the invoice is designed [EST].

10.2Which costs are steps modelled as slopes

Three, and the record has found two of them itself. The attested floor is a step: badge-controlled zones, camera coverage, a two-person glass-walled zone and locked-down workstations across three shifts are carried at about Rs 5.2 lakh a month at the cash trough, 1.3 per cent of cost, which the page calls a co-working desk and not an attested floor, and the step lands in the months the seed is sized against because DR-006 triggers it on the first foreign market (05, 4; DR-006). The foreign entity is a step: the published line opens the United Kingdom around month 20 and the United States around month 36 without charging a foreign entity's standing cost or a night premium, while the geography model charges Rs 0.95 crore a month by month 36 and Rs 2.60 by month 60, Rs 55.5 crore cumulatively, that the published line never pays (05, 6.5). The third is ours: the night shift. The model prices night and language cover as a wage uplift on general agents scaled by the foreign share of users, so a market with five per cent of the users buys five per cent of a night shift, with no night term on supervisors or quality assurance at all, and the record concedes that a shift cannot be staffed in fractions (the model page, the night-cover limit). A rostered night crew with an L3 seat, a shift lead and escalation cover is, on the tier design of 04, at least six people before the first UK user, and at the model's own generalist cost of Rs 5.2 lakh a year with a supervision multiple and a night premium that is roughly Rs 45 to 60 lakh a year of standing cost per shift, or about Rs 4 to 5 lakh a month, on top of the entity (04, 2) [EST]. None of the three is in the line the seed is sized on, and all three arrive inside the seed window if DR-023 holds.

10.3What the first customer in the second market costs

The record prices the arrival and not the customer. Under DR-023 the first paying UK household needs, before it can be served at all: a UK controller or a UK representative under Article 27, a transfer mechanism and a transfer risk assessment for Bengaluru processing, a UK carrier to originate calls that present a UK number, counsel's answer on the identity rows, and a legal pack with a signed flag table, a residency map and a refusal list (06, 4; section 7 of this review). The geography model's own entity cost is Rs 0.9 to 3.2 million a month for the United Kingdom before a single user (the model page). Add the record's own legal-pack estimate from the last review, US$150,000 to 400,000 a market, and the first UK customer costs on the order of Rs 2 to 5 crore before their first task, which is a tenth to a fifth of the seed the record asks for [EST]. That is the number a partner reads against the gate of 400 users, and it is why the gate arithmetic that does not reconcile (07, the entity gates item) matters: at both gates met, the standing cost is about $122,000 a month against $97,000 of foreign subscription revenue on the model's own medians, so a market at its gate does not cover its own entity, let alone the shift (05, 8).

10.4What acquisition costs at volume rather than at the anchor

The record has answered this better than most plans do, and the answer is unfavourable. The input anchor is $120 a paid arrival for short video at low volume; the run itself pays about $122 in month 1, $140 by month 12, $471 by month 36 and $1,368 by month 60 at home, and in the sequence of record about $2,938 in the United Kingdom and $1,839 in the United States by month 36, and $8,423 and $5,352 by month 60, because a budget held at a share of revenue meets channels that saturate and nothing caps it (05, 6.3; 05, 8). Figure 8.23 puts the home series beside what a user is worth. The record's own conclusion is that the marginal bought user stops paying for itself before month 60 and that nothing in the plan caps the budget; ours is stronger. The break-even the plan quotes, about $1,124 an arrival at fifteen per cent of revenue, is struck on the anchor, so it tests a number the run never pays, and the only outside benchmark the record holds, the consumer fintech band of $1,340 to $2,140 carried over from the last review, is a US figure at a US price to-verify [113]. In India nobody has priced a reel for this product, and the two-week instrument the record proposes is the right first spend, but a partner should assume the anchor is wrong by a factor rather than a percentage until it runs [EST].

10.5Is the lifetime value a gross margin presented as a net one

It was, and the record corrected it on 2026-09-14: the $1,291 is a delivery margin only, revenue less labour and tokens at eighty per cent, and the all-in figure after engineering, compliance, overhead, one-time costs and payment fees is $637 to $865, with month 36 contributing $27 a user after everything (05, 6.2). Two things remain wrong with the net figure as stated. It is taken at month 36, where the ratio has fallen to 61 minutes; a user acquired in month 12 is served at 137 minutes and contributes less than nothing on the planning line, since the line does not cross until month 23 (05, 5). And it is gross of tax. On the net-of-tax run the month-36 revenue per user is $59 rather than $70, which on the same arithmetic takes the all-in worth of a user to roughly $450 to $650, below the month-36 cost of a bought arrival at home and a fifth of the UK cost [EST]. The honest lifetime-value sentence for the seed memo is: a user is worth nothing on a net basis until the ratio holds, and roughly $500 afterwards, net of tax.

10.6Three things the interrogation adds

First, cost per task on the planning line, which the record never states because it prices a user. Dividing the month's total cost by users times the allowance of 26 gives about Rs 590 a task at month 6, Rs 360 at month 12, Rs 265 at month 18 and Rs 145 at month 36, so a task costs about $7 when the pilot cohort is a few hundred households and about $1.60 at thirty thousand, and the seed window is spent entirely on the left of that curve (the model page; models/sim_growth.csv) [EST]. Second, the burn at the start: the planning line carries twelve floor heads, eight engineering heads and Rs 19 lakh a month of general and administrative cost in month 1, for 273 users and Rs 13 lakh of revenue, a burn of about Rs 70 lakh a month before the first quarter is over, so the seed is spent at roughly the same rate whether or not users arrive, which is what a step cost means [EST]. Third, the general and administrative prior is a single lognormal with no mechanism and owns twenty-eight per cent of the variance of the cash need, more than any other driver on the published line (05, 6); the founder's own overhead in the first quarter is the cheapest measurement in the whole plan and the record has not made it.

12Security, trust and safety: a red-team read

The trust design is the best thing in the record and the last review said so; a week on it is narrower, more honest and, in three places, differently wrong. Narrower, because DR-021, DR-026 and DR-027 have removed the two mechanisms that were research-grade, the re-voiced call and the compositor mask, and replaced them with scope: no value on a call, a call only to a desk that maps rather than authenticates, no name to a worker, and a structured card in place of a masked stream (DR-021; DR-026; DR-027; DR-022). More honest, because the threat model now says which of its twenty-six paths are closed by a component and which by a procedure, the trust story names its two open exceptions in the customer's own words, and the console says a worker hears a booking reference and types it (03, 7; the trust story; the worker console). We attack the design as it now stands, not the pitch, and we ask where it fails unsafely rather than safely and what the failure costs the user.

12.1The hash service is a second vault

The alias model rests on one sentence: the vault's SDK exists in exactly two places of ours, the substitutor and the hash service, and the hash service holds no detokenise right, so the worst it can yield is digests and new aliases (02, 8.3). The executable specification found what the pages do not say: to compute the two keyed digest sets it issues, in every normalised form of every value, the hash service must hold every value in the vault in memory on every refresh and every rotation, daily on the illustrative cadence and at once on any incident (spec/SEAMS.md, seam 10 [REC]). A component that holds every real value of every user in memory once a day, under a credential the pages call digest-only, is a second vault with a different name, and it sits in the real-value zone with the surfaces and the evidence pipeline rather than behind the provider's attestation. It fails unsafely: a compromise of the hash service during a refresh is a compromise of every value, not of one placement, and no ledger line records it, because the ledger records uses and a refresh is not a use. The fix is the one the seam names, moving the digest computation into the vault under keys the vault never keeps, and it is a vendor requirement to add to DR-008's tests rather than a page edit; until a vendor can do it, the real-value zone's crown jewel is not the substitutor but this.

12.2The call is now the only surface with a person in the loop and no mechanism behind them

Dropping the media path was the right trade for the pilot and the record says what it costs: on a call the closure for the second fatal failure is the approval token and the release of the committing sentence, which is a procedure a person follows, and the record's own rule is that a closed path never rests on a procedure (DR-021, closes off; batch 0008 in the map). Three consequences fail unsafely. A reversible call step never receives a fired marker, so a worker who agreed something the script did not carry leaves no record and the step can be re-queued and attempted twice (07, the at-most-once item). A fee quoted on a call has no comparator, because the amount is tokenised on the way into the result and no component compares it with the cap, so the worker's instruction not to agree a fee above the sub-cap is an instruction about a number the worker is not allowed to know (07, the fee item). And a worker hears the far party in the ordinary way, so the exposure the design accepts on this surface is bounded by vetting, the card and the room, which the record calls a mitigation and not a mechanism (03, 3.2). The user pays for each of these in the same coin: a wrong outcome they cannot dispute from the record, because the record on a call is the worker's structured result and a redacted recording. DR-026 and DR-027 narrow the surface to the calls where that matters least, which is the right direction; the residual is that the pilot's health vertical and most Indian institutional desks live on exactly this surface.

12.3The attacker is still on the wrong side of the diagram

The last review's largest security finding was that the threat model lists paths by which an insider could see a value and none by which an outsider could steer an act, and nothing in the revision has changed that: the twenty-six paths of Table 3.6 are visibility paths, the prompt-injection closure is one paragraph in 02, 3.4, and the executable specification's canary plants values to find and never plants an instruction to follow (03, 7; spec/README.md [REC]). The evidence that this is the live risk has strengthened since 11 September. The independent 2026 measurements of indirect prompt injection against open browser agents sit between about 42 and 68 per cent attack success, against a vendor figure of 0 to 0.3 per cent on the vendor's own new red-team set to-verify [29] to-verify [16]; the record quotes the vendor. Meta's rule that an agent should hold at most two of untrusted input, private data and the power to change state or communicate is the plainest statement of the pattern, and the ai executor holds all three on every reversible step verified [30]. The record's out-of-band controls, the allow-list, the envelope and the token enforced outside the model, are the right pattern and remain the best in the field; what is missing is the gate that measures the residual, per party page and per mail template, before an ai step is allowed on a portal that holds money or identity. Without it the design fails unsafely in one specific way: a reversible step on a hostile or compromised party page can request placement of any alias in the step's field list into any field whose accessibility label the page controls, and the placed value is readable by the page's own scripts, which the design requires so that validation runs (02, 3.4; 03, 3.1). The user's PAN goes to the attacker's field, the ledger records a placement into a contactable party's domain, and the line reads "no person saw it", which is true.

12.4Two paths the record reopened while closing others

The revision added a mechanism and a promise that each open a path. The mechanism is the mailbox send scope of DR-018: a message in the user's name leaves through the user's own provider under a token the vault's proxy injects, and a reply lands in the user's inbox and reaches the planner through the read connector (DR-018). That is the right answer to the stranger's-domain problem and it makes the user's mailbox a route into the planner for anyone who can send the user an email; the proxy's detectors screen a reply for values, not for instructions, so the residual is a steered re-plan from a crafted reply, which the last review named from the EchoLeak precedent and which the record has not answered (08, 11). The promise is the commerce layer of DR-019 and DR-024: the assistant routes purchases through channels that pay a take rate, with the one rule that a merchant may never pay for placement (DR-024). The rule is right and it is not a mechanism: nothing in the planner's design records which channel a purchase was routed through or why, and a ledger that records placements of values does not record placements of orders, so the user cannot audit the one thing the take rate creates an incentive to bend. A commerce line on the ledger, in the same plain words, is a page edit; without it the promise is a policy.

12.5What the specification proves and what it cannot

The executable specification is the most useful thing added since the last review and we have run it: a hundred and one tests pass on Node 22 in under a second, the state-machine invariant holds under fault injection and random walks, the substitutor refuses in order and names the check, and the canary finds a planted value of every class on the surface where it was placed and nowhere else [REC]. It is also, by its own account, in memory, single process, no persistence, no network, no model, with a stub planner and the streamed masks unimplemented, and it pins every illustrative number (spec/README.md). Read as evidence it proves that the pages are consistent enough to be coded from, which is the engineer's bar of the brief met in the only way it can be met without an engineer; it does not prove that the design is safe, because the properties that fail unsafely, the hash service's refresh, the injected field label, the crafted reply, the fee on a call, are outside what it models. A seed diligence should treat it as a well-written test harness for the second engineer to inherit, and should ask for the four missing tests by name.

13Product, go-to-market and retention

Why does anyone come back? The record's answer changed on 13 September and the change is the right one to have made: the everyday tasks are in scope from the first day, because a profile built from three dreaded tasks a year never compounds and because the assistant that knows where you eat is the one you trust with where you bank (00, 2; DR-019; DR-020). It is also the answer that puts the product beside the free platform agents on the layer where they are strongest, which the thesis concedes in its own words (00, 1.1). Between 8 and 17 September the platforms shipped on that layer again: a consumer agent from Meta that books, buys and now calls, distributed where the record's Indian front door lives, and a cloud browser from OpenAI that stays logged into the user's accounts with an auto-approve option to-verify [42] to-verify [14]. The retention case therefore rests on one claim: that the same household will pay $52 a month for an assistant that does the ordinary things a free agent does, because the same assistant can also do the dreaded things a free agent cannot, and because it holds a profile the user can audit. That claim has no evidence in the record and the honest test of it is the priced waiting list the record already plans, provided the price on it is the one the customer pays.

13.1What the pilot can actually show

Figure 8.24 counts the steps of the six journeys in 01 by who performs them, as the page writes them and under the pilot's signed table, where identity acts bounce in the UK and the US and a payment bounces everywhere (06, 2.3). As written, the user performs eight of twenty-eight steps themselves; under the pilot table it is eleven, and in the two foreign journeys it is a majority [EST]. That is not a criticism of the flag table, which is conservative for good reasons; it is a description of the product a foreign cohort would meet in the seed window: a form-filler that hands back the tap, which is the last review's phrase and which nothing since has changed. In India the pilot can show the promise on the browser and the relay, and cannot show it on the phone, where DR-021, DR-026 and DR-027 now exclude every call that authenticates, every desk that needs a name after mapping, and every value in either direction. The product the pilot demonstrates is narrower than the product the thesis sells, and the roadmap should say so in the pilot's scope rather than leave a reader to count.

13.2Onboarding, the bound device and the cohort the numbers point to

Nothing in the revision touched the onboarding stack, and it is still the heaviest in the field: an identity-rail binding on the rail's own screen, a structured interview whose sensitive answers post to the vault, connector consents, a mailbox send scope, and an envelope confirmation before any step runs (01, 3; 01, 6). The record has no funnel, no completion target and no chosen device implementation, and the last review's finding that the recommended cohort, an adult child abroad acting for a parent in India, requires the parent to bind a device through an Aadhaar-class factor stands (08, 12). DR-023 has now made that cohort the plan: the UK waiting list is aimed at non-resident households (DR-023, trigger). So the binding of a parent in India is no longer an edge case in a journey; it is the first step of the second market's first customer, and the record still does not mention age, capacity, an assisted binding or a proxy. The one addition since the last review cuts the other way: DR-027 excludes from the phone surface any task whose desk needs a relative's details after mapping, which is a great many of the parent's tasks, so the cohort chosen for its frequency has lost part of the channel that frequency lives on (DR-027, 1.2).

13.3Go-to-market, as the record now states it

DR-025 gives growth two mechanisms and names three channels: reels bought on performance, AI-influencer placements and earned coverage in the technology and business papers, with word of mouth compounding on the base at a rate conditional on a net promoter score of 60 or above on the paying cohort (DR-025). Three things are worth saying against it. The condition is the right kind of condition and it is set at a level few consumer services reach; the record should say what happens to the plan at 40, because the plan's own words are that media is not the remedy. The channels are the channels of a consumer app and the product is sold on dread, which the last review argued is bought like an accountant's fee and reached through the institutions that already hold the customer, a bank's non-resident desk, an insurer, an employer; the record has not answered that argument and the flag table's own lobbying agenda is addressed to exactly those institutions (03, 6). And the first hundred users have no named source at all: the record's trigger for DR-025 is the first media spend of the pilot, which means the cohort that measures everything is recruited by the mechanism whose cost is the largest unknown (07, the paid-arrival item). A seed memo needs the first hundred by name or by channel, and the founder's own network, a professional association and a sponsor desk are the three answers a partner would accept.
