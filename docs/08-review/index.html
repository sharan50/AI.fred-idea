1Executive verdict

VerdictNot yet. The trust design is ahead of the field and the wedge is real, but the plan spends a seed on a harness before a customer, the promise can be tested in one market only, and the economics that decide the company are deferred past the round they must justify.

DecisionNot yet, and we would fund a narrower company: India only for three years, a browser-and-draft pilot on one vertical with a day-shift floor of six, a technical co-founder who has shipped a security boundary, a range-based unit model published now, and the everyday endgame held as a hypothesis with a dated kill criterion.

This is a review of the AI.fred record as it stood on 11 September 2026, written as a seed partner would write an investment memo after a week with the documents and the public record: what the technology can do today, what is arriving that threatens the idea, where the law stands in India, the UK and the US, what the market and the comparables say, what the economics the record defers would look like, who has to be on the team, how the trust design reads from the attacker's side, how the product reaches a customer, and what would kill it. It was produced with the same tools and the same limits as the record itself [REC]: most primary hosts were refused from this session, so a large share of the external claims below carry a to-verify chip and should be read as leads for diligence, not as diligence done. Where a number is this review's own estimate it says so and carries an assumption chip [EST].

The short version. The idea is a personal AI that takes on the high-consequence, low-frequency tasks people dread, does them through surfaces the company controls so that nobody, including its own staff, sees the user's real values, and earns from those tasks the trust and the context to become the user's first port of call for everything personal. The thesis is coherent and the trust architecture is the best we have seen at this stage from any consumer assistant. The problems are not in the design. They are in the order of work, the size of the fixed cost, the absence of a customer, the absence of a team, and the fact that the record's own rules mean the promise can be demonstrated in one country only.

Three findings shape the verdict more than any other. The pilot as scoped in 06, 2.3 loads bounce for every identity act outside India and for every payment everywhere, so a UK or US cohort would experience a form-filler that hands the tap back to the user, which ChatGPT does for nothing; only an Indian cohort can exercise the promise, and only if Indian counsel signs the India column as delegated. The wedge is, by the record's own words in DR-019, a few tasks a year, and every prior human-in-the-loop assistant closed or pivoted because the human share never shrank to-verify [1] to-verify [2]. And the largest fixed cost in the plan, a 24-hour attested office floor, is required by neither law nor, in the record's own words, by safety; it is priced by nobody and challenged by nobody.

What we would do. We would not fund the plan as written. We would fund a narrower company on conditions, and the conditions are the substance of this review: a co-founder before the cheque, a cohort and a price hypothesis before the harness, counsel's answers before the build, a unit model now, a browser-only India pilot with a day shift, and a threat model that looks outward. Each is set out in the sections that follow, with the evidence, the numbers where they exist, and the questions we would put to the founder.

The reasons, in the order a partner would give them: First, the wedge is real and the design is unusually serious: no live rival masks values from its own staff, and the record's out-of-band controls are the pattern the 2026 injection literature says survives an adaptive attacker. Second, the promise can be exercised in the pilot only in India, because the record's own flag table bounces every identity act in the UK and the US and every payment in every market, so a western cohort would test a checklist product. Third, frequency is the first kill: the record itself says the wedge is a few tasks a year, and no hybrid assistant has survived a human share that did not shrink. Fourth, the cost structure is fixed before it is measured: a 24-hour office floor of roughly 24 to 43 heads, a vault per region and three legal packs precede the first paying task, and DR-015 defers the only number that decides the round. Fifth, the team does not yet exist: a solo founder at design stage, with a record written by an AI in nine days, needs a co-founder who has shipped a security boundary and a real-time media engineer nobody has yet found in Bengaluru. Sixth, the platforms now hold the hands the thesis said they lacked, and the everyday endgame of DR-019 runs into them at a price of zero.

The conditions before money: A signed technical co-founder with a shipped tokenisation or PCI boundary, treated as the condition of the round rather than a hire after it. Twenty customer interviews and a priced waiting list in one cohort, with the per-event cost of the dropped task named from an institutional source. Indian counsel's written answer on the identity rows of Table 3.5 and on Aadhaar, before any harness stage beyond the vault; if those rows come back as bounce, the wedge is admin and the round is a different round. A range-based unit model now, labelled as estimate: floor, vault, legal pack, minutes per task and price per event, with DR-015 reopened to that extent only. A narrower pilot: the managed browser and the draft mail route only, India only, one vertical, one day shift, opened as soon as the browser canary passes, with telephony funded by what it shows. An attacker-centric threat model and an injection gate beside the canary test, with ai steps on authenticated financial and identity portals excluded until the residual rate is near zero.

2The idea in one page

The record describes a service for the tasks that carry real consequence and arrive a few times a year: a bank's KYC refresh, a passport renewal, a tax notice, a hospital admission for a parent, an insurance claim, an estate or property act. The customer is described by task rather than by count in 00, 1, and the argument against the general assistants in 00, 2.1 and 00, 5 is that a generalist that sees everything cannot be trusted with the wedge, while a flat-fee errand service that refuses consequence cannot serve it. AI.fred's answer is a harness: one component that ever holds a real value (a bought tokenisation vault and its substitutor), aliases everywhere else, a device-signed authority envelope that names parties, act types and caps, a classifier that routes each step to the AI, to a worker on a Bengaluru floor or back to the user's device, dual control on every irreversible act, and a ledger written from component records that tells the user what their screen could show [REC].

Three surfaces carry the work: a managed browser the company hosts with a masked stream to the worker, a telephony platform where the worker hears a delayed, re-voiced, allow-listed transcript and never raw audio, and mail sent from the user's own address under a send scope or drafted for the user to send (DR-018). Nineteen decision records fix the shape: full delegation as the ceiling with per-jurisdiction flags (DR-003), an office-mandatory 24-hour floor (DR-006), a bought vault (DR-008), economics deferred to after a Series A or B (DR-015), a context store that compounds (DR-016) and an endgame in which the wedge is the proof and the everyday tasks follow on the same harness once a market's legal pack opens them (DR-019).

The build order is harness first: eight gated stages, from the vault and substitutor through the envelope, classifier and planner, the state machine, the managed browser, telephony and mail, the console, the ledger, and finally the gateway and device surface, before a pilot in one of three markets (06, 1). The demo order, surfaces first and vault later, is rejected by decision, on the argument that a harness built after the surfaces would carry real values in every log. Three target markets share one core with per-market adapters and a legal pack per market (DR-007); India is the build base, the UK and the US the commercial ground (00, 3).

What the record does not contain is as important as what it does. It carries no price, no cost per task, no headcount, no burn, no pilot size, no market size and no date, all by decision. It evidences no customer interview, no institution, no vendor call and no counsel conversation. It was written, by its own account, with an AI as writer, designer, researcher and verifier in nine days, under a verification standard in which verified means a search-engine excerpt because page fetches were refused (DR-013), and its closing report marks the founder's bar on the two fatal failures as not met on the last pass, with sixteen findings open [REC]. A partner reading it sees a specification of unusual quality and a company that does not yet exist.

3Technology today: what exists, what is plausible, what is research

The harness is a control system around a rented planner, so the question is whether each load-bearing mechanism exists in September 2026. Split three ways, the answer is: the deterministic parts (the substitutor, the envelope, the hash service, the ledger, the classifier as a policy layer) are proven engineering or a bought product; the browser parts are plausible but sit on a foundation the best-resourced teams have just retreated from; and the telephony masking is research-grade product design that nobody has shipped. On the browser, the record's own hedge in 02, 3.1 that an ai step runs only where a party's site has passed conformance per step type is the right one, because the public numbers do not support general reliability on institutional sites. Short-horizon computer use is strong: the OSWorld-Verified leaderboard tops out at 83.4 per cent verified [3]. Long-horizon computer use, which is the shape of a bank's KYC refresh or a council portal, is not: the independently verified OSWorld 2.0 leaderboard tops out at about 20.6 per cent binary completion to-verify [4], and live-website scores span roughly 40 to 84 per cent depending on scaffold and judge to-verify [5].

On prompt injection the record's design is ahead of most of the field, and the numbers it quotes in Table 0.1 hold: 23.6 per cent attack success before mitigation in August 2025, 11.2 per cent after verified [6], 1.4 per cent by November 2025 and 0.4 per cent by May 2026 against an adaptive attacker in the vendor's own reporting to-verify [7], and 0 to 0.3 per cent claimed at the browser product's general availability to-verify [8]. The stronger point is architectural. The record enforces the allow-list, the envelope and the approval token outside the model (02, 3.4), which is the out-of-band pattern (CaMeL and its kin) that the 2026 literature says survives an attacker who knows the defence, whereas twelve in-band defences that reported near-zero attack success were broken above 90 per cent once the attacker adapted to-verify [9]; on AgentDojo the targeted attack success of an undefended model fell from 56 per cent in 2024 to about 7 per cent in 2025 verified [10]. The residual the record does not state is utility: CaMeL solves 77 per cent of AgentDojo tasks against 84 per cent undefended to-verify [11], and a rule that a reversible step can never click outside the page map or submit anything turns most institutional forms into human steps or bounces, which moves cost onto the floor and the user. The record's separately deployed detector is exactly the classifier class the adaptive-attack paper breaks; it should be described as a tripwire, not a closure, which is a wording fix rather than a design fault because the deterministic paints are already the closure.

Telephony is where the record is furthest from what exists. The per-leg controls it needs are shipped products: Telnyx exposes send_dtmf, speak, play and mute scoped to named conference participants verified [12]; Twilio's participant resource has muted and an announce URL, with recording off by default verified [13]; Indian platforms carry bidirectional streamed audio. Speaking or keying a value to the far leg while the worker's leg is server-muted is therefore proven engineering. What is not proven is the decision in 02, 4.2 and 03, 3.2 that the worker never hears raw far-leg audio and instead hears a re-voiced, delayed, allow-listed transcript in which every out-of-lexicon token, proper noun and digit run is a tone. Production speech recognition misses about one named entity in six on representative audio, mixed alphanumerics sit near 85 per cent even with vendor uplift, and a stitched pipeline already costs 600 milliseconds to 1.7 seconds before the record's delay buffer is added to-verify [14]. A fail-closed allow-list turns every recogniser error into a tone in the worker's ear, so the mechanism is safe by construction and very possibly unusable in a live conversation with a hospital booking desk in Kannada-inflected English. Nobody has shipped this. It is research, and the pilot's health vertical depends on it.

The vault and the sandbox are half-products. Skyflow, VGS and Basis Theory sell detokenising proxies for outbound requests and inbound tokenising routes, and all three claim an India footprint; Basis Theory's published starter tier is US$995 a month for 20,000 active tokens, US-only, with regional hosting and PII handling on a custom-quoted enterprise tier verified [15], and Skyflow's SDK documents role-scoped service accounts and redaction policies verified [16]. None detokenises into a browser field or a phone leg, which is fine because the substitutor is a normal API principal with detokenise rights, but the vault's edge proxy of 02, 10.1, terminating channel webhooks and transcribing voice notes under a custom four-detector policy, is not a product any of the three describes; it is a bespoke build the record has assigned to the provider. The managed-browser stream with a compositor-level mask, a delay line and read-back disabled on every channel but the driver's is likewise not a feature of Browserbase, Kernel or Anchor, which sell a live-view iframe and CDP access and record a screencast by default to-verify [17]. Enterprise remote-browser-isolation vendors show that pixel-mirrored streaming with field-level loss prevention is a real category, so the masker is plausible engineering on a Chromium the company builds and hosts, at the price of owning a browser.

Read as a partner, the technology dimension says three things. The company will own a browser, a media server and a tokenising proxy whether or not the record calls them bought, so Table 6.1 understates the build. The ai share of a wedge task will be small at first, because institutional forms are long-horizon and the reversible-step rule is strict, so the floor carries the load the AI was meant to take, and the economics must be modelled on that assumption. And the health vertical should not open until a throwaway prototype of the re-voiced transcript has been run against recorded Indian booking-line audio and a worker has completed a call.

4Technologies on the horizon, and how each threatens the idea

The record's competitive argument rests on two claims about the labs and the platforms: that they have models but no hands (no telephony, no counter, no human for the step that needs one, 00, 5.2) and that 2026 was the year they found the ceiling and withdrew consumer agents (00, 2.1). Read against the 2025 to 2026 sources, the first claim is out of date and the second misreads consolidation as retreat. Google now runs the browser (Gemini in Chrome with auto browse for all US Android users from 18 August 2026), the phone line (agentic calling on Duplex, rolling to everyone in the US this summer and identifying itself as AI from Google), the mailbox (Gmail) and a 24-hour background agent with its own email address (Gemini Spark, announced 19 May 2026) to-verify [22]. Apple ships Siri AI with iOS 27 this month, with App Intents as the route into third-party apps, personal context indexed from messages and email, and a privacy story (on-device models and Private Cloud Compute) stronger than a vault run by a start-up verified [23]. Microsoft's Scout autopilot runs with its own identity in the background to-verify [24]. OpenAI's agent did not die; it became a workspace agent that runs for hours, and Anthropic's browser agent went generally available on 26 August 2026 acting without per-action approval to-verify [8]. The vendors' advice against financial, legal and medical sites that the record leans on in Table 0.1 is a policy line in a release note, not a structural ceiling, and it can be reversed in one release by the companies AI.fred buys its models from.

The rails are moving faster than the record allows, and in a direction that commoditises the envelope. Google's Agent Payments Protocol launched on 16 September 2025 with more than sixty partners verified [25]; OpenAI and Stripe published the Agentic Commerce Protocol two weeks later and were still releasing versions in April 2026 verified [26]; Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol signs an agent's requests to a merchant's page under a registry Visa vets verified [27]; Visa put tokenised credentials with per-transaction, daily and weekly caps, merchant whitelists and approval thresholds inside ChatGPT on 10 June 2026 to-verify [28]; and NPCI's Unified Agent Protocol, unveiled at Global Fintech Fest this week, is precisely the rail that admits a registered agent under the user's mandate which 01, 7 names as its trigger, but capped at Rs 15,000 a month and Rs 5,000 a transaction, aimed first at groceries, with NPCI's chairman saying on 10 September that an agent should not be the thing that approves the payment to-verify [29] to-verify [30]. Worse for the moat, the vault vendors the record plans to buy from now sell the agentic credential layer to anyone: Crossmint launched an agentic cards API on Visa Intelligent Commerce and Basis Theory on 2 June 2026 to-verify [31]. The claim in 00, 2.4 that the trust layer can be bought is true, and it is true for every rival too.

Two moves cut in opposite directions and the record is silent on both. On 4 August 2026 the Ninth Circuit vacated Amazon's injunction against Perplexity's Comet, holding that an AI agent is a tool, not a person, and that it is the user who accesses the site to-verify [32]; that removes the computer-misuse barrier to agents acting inside a user's own accounts in the US and undercuts the record's premise that acting inside the user's accounts is the weak column. Against that, Cloudflare's default block of the Agent category on ad-bearing pages for new domains from 15 September 2026, Web Bot Auth, and banks' passkeys, device binding and terms that forbid automation let institutions admit only agents they recognise verified [20] verified [21]. AI.fred's managed browser, moat property 3 in 00, 5.3, is from the institution's side an unsigned bot unless it registers and is allow-listed, and the allow-list will favour the labs. Enterprise identity is moving the same way: Okta's agent identity products are generally available and the IETF drafts on acting on behalf of a user are advancing, but all of it is enterprise-first and no consumer bank or government implementation exists to-verify [33].

The deepest threat is to DR-019 rather than to the wedge. The endgame says the context store is the asset and the first port of call is a result of holding it. Apple, Google and Microsoft now hold that context at zero marginal cost, on the user's own device or in their own mailbox, with platform-grade privacy claims; and the institutions are removing the dread from the dreaded tasks by making them first-party: the NHS App's AI triage lets more than a million patients book directly and is due for every NHS App user by April 2028 to-verify [34], GOV.UK Chat launched in the app on 14 May 2026 with agentic task completion stated as the next step to-verify [35], UIDAI's new Aadhaar app with selective sharing passed 21 million downloads in three months, and the banks are piloting their own agents. Meanwhile capital is flowing to rivals whose posture is exposure: Instinct raised US$350 million at US$2.5 billion two days after a report that it held users' email in plain text under a perpetual training licence verified [36] to-verify [37], and Hark raised US$700 million before a product to-verify [37]. The record's observation that no rival publishes a masking policy is correct; the market's revealed preference in 2026 is that it does not yet care.

The wedge remains defensible for the acts no platform will touch: identity acts, institutional filings, the counter, the parent's hospital, anything that spans several institutions or a border. Those are also the smallest and slowest to compound, which is exactly the ceiling DR-019 rejected when it declined to stay a specialist. The honest reframing is that the everyday endgame is a hypothesis with a kill criterion, and that the company must clear the return bar on the wedge alone.

5The regulatory landscape: India

The record's Indian map is broadly accurate where it has looked and silent where it matters most. What it has looked at it has mostly got right: the DPDP Rules' three-phase commencement (the Board and definitions on 13 November 2025, consent managers on 13 November 2026, the substantive duties on 13 May 2027, with penalties to Rs 250 crore) verified [38]; the rule that video customer identification is run only by the regulated entity's own trained officials, with third parties assisting only at the customer's end to-verify [39]; the Authentication Directions' transaction-unique factor from 1 April 2026 to-verify [40]; the E-mandate Framework's thresholds of Rs 15,000 a debit and Rs 1 lakh for premiums, mutual funds and card bills to-verify [41]; the UPI Circle caps of Rs 5,000 a transaction and Rs 15,000 a month to-verify [42]; and the rule that only an entity regulated by a financial sector regulator may be a financial information user under the Account Aggregator framework verified [43]. The commercial conclusion the record draws from them is also right: in India today the assistant may fill forms, present documents and speak for the customer, but the identification act, the payment and the signature are the user's own act on their own device.

Where the record is out of date is the payment rail it has pinned its hopes on. The Reuters report of 1 September has been overtaken by Global Fintech Fest: NPCI's chairman set the boundary on 10 September that an AI agent should not be the thing that approves the payment, and the Unified Agent Protocol is described as a registry that registers, verifies and authorises agents on top of UPI Circle, with a fully delegated agent capped at Rs 15,000 a month and Rs 5,000 a transaction, aimed first at groceries and routine purchases to-verify [29] to-verify [30]. That is a rail for small merchant payments by a registered agent, not for the fees, premiums, deposits and institutional payments the wedge is built on. The record's line that a payment under the cap bounces until a rail admits a registered agent is the correct flag; the partner should read the protocol as confirming that the India payment flag stays at bounce for every high-consequence payment for the foreseeable horizon, and that registered-agent status, when it comes, will be obtained through a bank or payment-service sponsor on NPCI's terms, which the founder does not control.

Where the record is silent, the silences are consequential. First, Aadhaar: the Aadhaar (Authentication and Offline Verification) Amendment Regulations 2025, in force since 9 December 2025, require any private entity doing offline verification to register with UIDAI as an offline verification seeking entity, pay registration and per-verification fees, and never retain plain Aadhaar digits; UIDAI's Aadhaar Data Vault rules apply to any entity storing Aadhaar numbers, masked ones included to-verify [44]. The record's design of holding an Aadhaar number in a bought foreign-vendor vault as received is the wrong shape; the right shape is to hold no Aadhaar number at all. Second, liability: the RBI's 2017 circular on unauthorised electronic banking transactions gives the customer zero liability only where the customer did not share credentials; where the loss is due to customer negligence, such as sharing payment credentials, the customer bears the entire loss until the transaction is reported verified [45]. A customer who delegates to an agent is, on the bank's reading, the party at fault, and the digital mandate the record wants recognised has no status beyond ordinary agency under the Indian Contract Act 1872 and each bank's own registered power of attorney or mandate form to-verify [46]. Third, telecom: TRAI's commercial-communication regime (the second amendment of 12 February 2025 with its 1600-series numbers, seven-day consent validity and penalties from Rs 2 lakh to Rs 10 lakh, and the draft third amendment of 13 March 2026) is not mentioned anywhere in 03, although the company runs its own telephony and dials institutions in the customer's name verified [47]. Fourth, the consent-manager regime (an Indian company with Rs 2 crore net worth), verifiable parental consent for anyone under eighteen, and the fact that the Data Protection Board had not been appointed as of July 2026 are absent from Table 3.7 verified [38]; so is SEBI's rule that a regulated intermediary is solely responsible for any AI tool it uses, which is what a broker or insurer will cite when asked to let the assistant act on a client's account to-verify [48].

The floor and the phone line have a regulatory shape of their own that no page examines. The four Labour Codes came into force on 21 November 2025; section 43 of the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code lets women work at night only with consent and prescribed safeguards, and the Karnataka Shops Act filings (Form R for the IT-enabled exemption, Form S for women's night conditions) are still the practical instrument until the state notifies its own rules to-verify [49] to-verify [50]. The permit regime the record's operations page fears is gone, but the consent, transport and safeguard duties are real and cost days, not months. A Bengaluru voice floor is an Other Service Provider under the liberalised 2020 guidelines, which removed registration but kept the duty to hold call and system logs in India and left the toll-bypass bar standing, so every PSTN leg, Indian or foreign, terminates through an authorised carrier whose per-minute international rate sets the cost of the western shifts to-verify [51]. The 2026 telecom authorisation rules keep network data and logs in India and require prior permission for remote access from abroad, a constraint on any later UK or US operations centre to-verify [52]. And the Telecom Cyber Security Amendment Rules 2025 create the Telecommunication Identifier User Entity: any non-licensee that uses a mobile number to identify users, which on a strict reading is what device binding and WhatsApp intake make AI.fred, with duties to hand identifier data to the department and to suspend an identifier on order verified [53]. The record never mentions the labour codes, the OSP framework, TRAI, DLT or the identifier rules.

7The regulatory landscape: the United States

The record treats the US as the market where liability is firming in its favour (00, 2.2 and 00, 3.2) and where the only open questions are a Regulation E gap and a Section 1033 status check. That is out of date on both points it names and silent on the points that bite hardest. On Section 1033 the rule is not merely to check: it was enjoined on 29 October 2025, the CFPB abandoned its defence, and a replacement proposal reached the White House review office on 6 August 2026 with fees and the definition of an authorised representative on the table to-verify [69]. The court's reading, that the statute reaches consumers and fiduciary-like agents rather than commercial third parties, is double-edged: an assistant that genuinely acts as the user's agent is closer to the statute's core than an aggregator is, but the aggregator route the record plans to use is the one in doubt. On Regulation E the record is wrong in the direction that matters. The regulation does contemplate a standing authorisation to an agent: a transfer by a person to whom the consumer furnished the access device is not unauthorised, even where that person exceeds the authority given, until the consumer tells the bank verified [70], and Regulation Z applies the same test to cards. So a US user who lets AI.fred initiate a debit bears the loss for AI.fred's overreach as a matter of federal law; the record's cap and dual control are the company's promise, not the user's statutory protection, and no price or indemnity exists.

The largest US issue the record does not name is that the basis row of Table 3.5, speak to an institution as the customer, and the row for answering security questions from the context store collide with the GLBA pretexting offence: obtaining a financial institution's customer information by a false statement to its employee is a federal crime, and the listed exceptions contain no customer-consent exception to-verify [71]. Major banks' online agreements bar authorising a third party or sharing credentials except under guardianship or a power of attorney, and FinCEN treats an account operated by an attorney-in-fact as the principal's, verified as the principal. The lawful US route is therefore an agent that says it is an agent and holds an instrument the institution accepts (a state-law power of attorney, on the bank's own form where demanded), not a private authority envelope whose holder speaks as the customer. Objection A in 07 already senses this; the answer is not to bounce until counsel says otherwise but to design the US call differently: identify as the agent, present the mandate, expect refusals, and measure how much of the wedge survives.

Two design-wide exposures follow from surfaces the record is proud of. The evidence pipeline records both legs of every call (03, Table 3.6, path 9); eleven states including California, Florida, Illinois and Washington require all-party consent, and California's Penal Code section 632.7 reaches any cellular or cordless call with a private right of action to-verify [72]. Every US call would need a recording disclosure to the institution's agent, and Illinois' biometric statute adds voiceprint exposure if the transcriber ever identifies speakers, which the record does not say it does. The telephony surface also speaks values as synthesised speech; under the FCC's ruling of 8 February 2024 an AI-generated voice is an artificial voice under the TCPA, so a call to a clinic's mobile number carries US$500 to US$1,500 per-call exposure absent prior express consent, and after McLaughlin v McKesson district courts are free to read the statute either way to-verify [73]. The card-network claim in Table 3.4 is right as far as it goes, but Visa's and Mastercard's agent programmes are merchant-checkout rails for vetted, registered agents whose signatures are locked to a merchant's page verified [27]; nothing seen suggests an agentic token can be keyed into a hospital's payment line, which is the placement Table 3.2 illustrates.

The wider climate is a patchwork the record has not mapped. The CFPB is being cut from 1,723 staff to a planned 556 and is litigating its own funding, so no federal guidance on consumer agents is likely to-verify [74]; the states are filling the gap, with three more comprehensive privacy laws and California's risk-assessment and automated-decision regulations from 1 January 2026, a rule that counts the contents of a user's mailbox as sensitive personal information when the business is not the intended recipient, and Washington's health-data private right of action already in use to-verify [75]; Colorado's AI Act was rewritten and pushed to 1 January 2027 under a suit the Department of Justice joined to-verify [76]; Texas, Utah and Maine add disclosure duties; and California's AB 316, in force since 1 January 2026, removes the defence that the AI acted autonomously verified [77]. The federal pre-emption campaign (the executive order of 11 December 2025, the White House framework of 20 March 2026, the FTC's policy statement of July 2026) targets state rules on model development and on steering outputs, not on agents acting for consumers, so none of this goes away verified [78]. The Bengaluru floor faces political rather than legal obstacles for now: the HIRE Act's 25 per cent outsourcing excise sits in committee, and the FCC's onshoring proposal of April 2026 applies to carriers verified [79]. And a platform rule that DR-018 depends on: Google treats Gmail read and modify scopes as restricted, requiring an annual security assessment, so the read connector is a paid, audited dependency from day one to-verify [80].

8Market, customer and competition

The thesis has read the field correctly. Every live rival either refuses consequence-bearing work (Duckbill: no impersonation, transactions to US$2,500, legal and accounting referred out to-verify [81]; Yohana's policy barred logins and financial or legal advice) or takes it on with no safeguard (Faff and Hulp list government and banking tasks on a WhatsApp thread to-verify [82]; Instinct's terms take a perpetual licence and testers found plain-text email storage verified [36]). What the record has not done is size the wedge, and when the public numbers are put beside it the wedge is small per household. India's own health survey puts hospitalisation at 2.9 per 100 persons a year, 8.1 for the over-60s to-verify [83]; India issued 13.9 million passports in 2025 against a population of about 1.4 billion to-verify [84]; the re-KYC cycle is two, eight or ten years by risk class to-verify [39]; the US issued 27.3 million passports in fiscal 2025 to-verify [85]. A household of four in an Indian metro meets one to three wedge-class events in a typical year, and DR-019's own rejected alternative concedes that a few tasks a year form no habit. Section 1 of the thesis describes the customer by task and never by count; that silence is the gap a partner presses first.

The history is unambiguous and the record under-reads it. Facebook M closed in January 2018 with 70 per cent or more of answers coming from humans to-verify [1]; Fin charged a dollar a minute and became a B2B analytics business; GoButler narrowed to flights, became Angel.ai and was acqui-hired by Amazon at no disclosed price verified [86]; Clara went to TopFunnel in 2019; Yohana charged US$249 a month, served about 4,500 families in total, closed in the US on 30 September 2025 and in Japan on 30 January 2026, and its parent told investors that scaling and monetisation were never verified to-verify [2]; Milo, at about US$40 a month, is reported closed in January 2026, with commentary blaming the cost of the human in the loop against cheaper general models, on a single unsourced note to-verify [87]. DR-015 defers the economics, but the comparables say the economics are the thesis, and 00, 4.2 records the closures without drawing the unit-cost lesson from them.

The capital is going the other way. Instinct raised US$350 million at US$2.5 billion in August 2026 and Hark US$700 million at US$6 billion in May to-verify [37]; Equal AI raised US$30 million with over a million Indian users on a call-first assistant and is moving into financial services and concierge, M raised Rs 102 crore from Peak XV, Hulp US$2.6 million to-verify [82]; Alexa+ is free with Prime, Jio's call agent is free to hundreds of millions to-verify [88], and Instinct is free in beta with a reported US$35 tier. 9Business model and unit economics: the numbers the record defers

The record defers economics by decision (05, DR-015) and the deferral is internally coherent: every number would be a guess before a pilot, and the harness is built so that minutes per step and pool utilisation fall out of the step trace. A partner cannot accept it, because the design pages already fix the cost structure, and they fix it in the most expensive shape available. DR-006 and 04, 2 require one office running three shifts on a 24-hour wheel, each shift carrying an L1 pool, L2 pods for the markets held, at least one L3 seat inside a two-person zone, a shift lead and escalation cover. Rostered seven days a week with leave cover, that is a floor of roughly 24 heads in the leanest reading and about 43 in the reading the record describes, before the first customer [EST]. Built up from Bengaluru pay excerpts, statutory on-costs of 18 to 22 per cent, night-shift transport and a seat at the city's warm-shell rents, that is about Rs 2.9 to 6.8 crore a year (US$330,000 to 780,000) of fixed labour and site cost [EST] to-verify [50]. At a pilot of, say, 200 households doing four tasks a month with 45 human-minutes each, the floor is 11 to 19 per cent utilised and labour alone is US$40 to 75 per task [EST]. Nothing in the record acknowledges that the trust architecture has a minimum size, or that the pilot will run at a utilisation that makes its cost per task meaningless as a pricing input.

The field has already run this experiment at consumer price points and lost. Milo, at about US$40 a month, is reported closed with the human-in-the-loop model blamed to-verify [87]; Panasonic closed Yohana at US$249 to-verify [2]; Duckbill, the survivor, moved from an unlimited plan to a credit meter in which a task consumes about five of the eight credits US$99 buys to-verify [81]. AI.fred's labour is Indian rather than American, which is the one structural advantage it holds: an L1 productive hour at US$4 to 6 and an L3 hour at US$9 to 13 against a US copilot [EST], with outsourced Indian voice capacity priced at US$6 to 14 an hour fully loaded to-verify [94]. The sketch in this review says that at 2,000 households paying US$149 a month, 45 human-minutes a task and 70 per cent floor utilisation, gross margin lands near 55 to 60 per cent; at 90 minutes it falls below 30 per cent; at 15 minutes it exceeds 75 per cent [EST]. Minutes of human time per task is therefore the single variable the pilot must measure. The record is right that only a pilot can measure it; the record is wrong that the number can wait for a Series A, because the Series A will be raised on it.

Technology cost is not the problem. With Sonnet 5 at US$2 and US$10 per million tokens verified [18], browser sandboxes at about US$0.10 to 0.12 an hour to-verify [17] and carrier minutes at a cent or two, the model, sandbox and telephony cost of a multi-step task is a few dollars at most [EST]. 10Founder, team shape and execution risk

The record describes a company that needs, before its first customer, the disciplines of a payments-security vendor, a browser-automation lab, a real-time media company, a three-jurisdiction compliance function and an operations floor, and it describes them well. What it does not describe is who builds them. The founder is alone, at the design stage, with no code, no customer and no pilot (index, 5), and the roadmap forbids the one thing a small team normally does to survive that position: ship something a customer can touch early. Eight gated stages, three execution surfaces and a bought vault sit between today and the pilot, and the demo order is rejected by decision. Read against the sequence, the founding team the plan needs is a technical co-founder who has shipped a tokenisation or PCI boundary in production and can own the substitutor and hash service (stage 1), a second senior engineer for the planner, classifier and evaluation sets (stage 2), a real-time media engineer for the browser masker and the telephony delay line (stages 4 and 5), a separate owner for the evidence verifier, which 03 says must be owned by a different team and share no code with the redactor, an operations lead who has run an attested 24-hour floor in India, and retained counsel per market to sign the flag table. That is five to six senior engineers plus an operations lead plus counsel plus the founder, a Series A shape asked of a seed round.

The hardest hire is not the operations lead, who exists in Bengaluru in quantity, nor counsel, who can be bought by the hour. It is the engineer who can build masking at the media layer on both surfaces: compositor-hook masking of a streamed browser before a frame is encoded, and a server-side delay line with a streaming transcriber and re-voiced audio on the inbound call leg, with a security engineer's instinct for what fails closed. The carrier layer is not the problem, since per-participant mute and coaching exist on a bought platform verified [13]; the bespoke media server and the masker are, and the people who have built both are rare. Bengaluru pays senior engineers at tier-1 product companies Rs 35 to 70 lakh, with a security premium of 20 to 40 per cent, so a vault-boundary engineer is roughly Rs 42 to 98 lakh verified [100]; no market data exists for a real-time media masking engineer; and Anthropic has been hiring in Bengaluru since February 2026 verified [101]. Founder-market fit, from public sources only. The founder's reachable footprint is a GitHub account with ten public repositories, all created between March and September 2026: Indian income-tax and GST tooling, a specification-first end-to-end-encrypted messaging app whose server is a blind ciphertext relay, two agent-built event sites, and small experiments verified [102]. 11Security and trust: a red-team read of the design

The trust design is unusually well thought through for a pre-product venture, and its central mechanism holds up: one detokenising component, a device-signed envelope, aliases everywhere else, and a ledger written from component records rather than from people. Read as an attacker, though, the record has a scoping flaw that shapes everything after it. Table 3.6 in 03 is, by its own title, the list of paths by which a human could see a real value, meaning a human on the operator's side. There is no adversary-centric threat model at all: no compromised party website, no third-party script on the bank's page reading the field the substitutor just typed into, no attacker who is the user (a scam victim tapping the envelope, or a synthetic identity using the harness to run KYC at scale), no lawful-access request to the vault vendor, no supply-chain compromise of the signed build. Twenty-six paths, seven adversarial passes and 123 findings were spent almost entirely on insider visibility, which is the one problem the architecture already solves by construction, while the problems the market has actually been losing on since 2025 (indirect prompt injection into browser agents, mail-borne zero-click injection, bribed offshore support staff, agents that fire irreversible acts against explicit instruction) are bounded in a single paragraph or absent.

The prompt-injection paragraph in 02, 3.4 is the record's weakest load-bearing claim. It is correct that a hostile page cannot mint an approval token, so an irreversible act cannot be fired by injection alone. But on a reversible step inside the envelope the ai executor can navigate within the party's domains, type, and request placement of an alias into any field whose declared kind or label matches, and labels are attacker-controlled DOM. The field the substitutor fills is readable by the page's own scripts, which the record requires so the site's validation runs, and third-party scripts on the party's page are explicitly loaded. Meta's Rule of Two says an agent that processes untrusted input, touches private data and changes state must be supervised verified [107]; the executor has all three and is supervised only when the step is irreversible. The best-funded browser agent still fell to 11.2 per cent of adaptive attacks after mitigation in August 2025 and blocks financial-services sites by default verified [6]; its maker's rival called the problem frontier and unsolved and later said it is unlikely ever to be fully solved. A serious reviewer will not let ai steps run on bank or government portals in a pilot without a per-action human check or an isolation design the record does not describe. Inbound mail is the second door: every reply fetched by the connector or forwarded to the relay reaches the planner through the edge proxy, screened by a detector for values, not for instructions; the planner never holds a real value and cannot mint a token, so the residual is a poisoned plan or a steered re-plan rather than exfiltration, and EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711, a single crafted email executing hidden instructions once pulled into an assistant's context) is the precedent verified [108].

Three further matters change the plan rather than kill it. First, the legal basis for speaking to an institution as the customer is delegated to-verify in every market and is what the India pilot runs on; the record has not read a single bank's terms in any market, the RBI's 2017 circular puts the loss on a customer who shared credentials verified [45], and the illustrative health card in 04 already uses disclosed agency (I am calling on behalf of a patient), which should be the rule rather than the exception. Second, the tokenisation vendor is a single point of trust whose closures are contractual; a production order served on the vendor, a request under the CLOUD Act to a US-headquartered provider, or a provider insider is a detokenisation the substitutor never made and the ledger will not show, and the record's line that nobody sees your values is honestly nobody on our side. Third, the real crown jewel is not the substitutor but the alias-zone task service that writes step records and signs step contexts: an attacker inside that account can direct any alias in any step to any field on a contactable party's domain, because the envelope names act types and parties, not fields, and the step record is both the allow-list and the thing the task service writes; the penetration-test scope should centre there. Economics being deferred means there is no make-good policy, no insurance and no reserve for the wrong irreversible act, in a market where AB 316 removes the defence that the AI did it verified [77].

The floor and the stream leak in ways the canary test would not catch. Bribed offshore support staff are a proven, priced attack: two support agents at a vendor in India photographed screens for Coinbase customers in 2025, exposing tens of thousands of customers at an estimated cost of US$180 to 400 million, without a single password leaking verified [109] to-verify [110]; a worker on the Bengaluru floor holds enough non-sensitive task detail (institution, desk, timing, relationship words, a pseudonym per task) to be worth bribing even though no value is visible, and the record's own words are that the room is the record and the deterrent, never the closure. Run-sized masks on the browser stream encode string length and partial glyph width, which is an established attack class on redacted text verified [111]; the value-placed timer and the tone-for-span replacement on telephony give digit and span counts; and a fee-inside-sub-cap comparison beside a spend-cap card leaks a bound on an amount. The device binding models the user only as a legitimate principal: a new device binds and unbinds the old with no cooling-off period, no out-of-band notice, no velocity limit on identity acts after a re-bind, and no fraud-typology screening, so a SIM-swap attacker's afternoon with the product runs from re-bind to an envelope that adds a new bank. Finally the specification's own adversarial loop never converged: 29, 22, 13, 14, 16, 13 and 16 findings across seven passes, with sixteen open, two of them paths on which a person could see a value [REC].

12Product, go-to-market and retention

The product is designed backwards from the failure modes, not forwards from a customer. That is defensible for the harness, but it leaves the path to customers thin in exactly the places a seed partner looks first. Three things stand out. First, the pilot as scoped runs the signed flag table with every identity act in the UK and the US set to bounce and every payment set to bounce in all three markets (06, 2.3). In India the pilot will state an account number to a bank's deposit line with the worker muted, which is the promise; in the UK or the US it will prepare a form and hand the tap back, which is a checklist product that ChatGPT offers for nothing. The thesis can therefore be proven only in India in the first year, whatever the thesis says about commercial ground, and the founder's open item on the first cohort has in practice one honest answer. Second, the onboarding stack in 01, 3 and 6 (identity-rail device binding on the rail's own screen, a structured interview with vaulted answers, connector consents, then an envelope confirmation before anything runs) is heavier than a bank account opening, and the only quantified benchmark reachable says 68 per cent of European consumers abandoned a financial onboarding in the year surveyed, 21 per cent because too much personal information was asked verified [112]; a challenger bank took its US sign-up completion up about four-fold by deferring one check to-verify [113]. The record has no funnel, no pilot size and no step-completion target. Third, the endgame asks the assistant to become the first port of call for everyday tasks against a habit that already exists: ChatGPT reached about a tenth of the world's adults by mid-2025 with non-work use at 73 per cent of messages verified [114], a third of US adults had used it to-verify [115], and Apple, Google and Meta all announced personal agents inside the surfaces the user already lives in during 2026.

Where the record is wrong or silent. The trust story promises that no person who works for us, at any level, sees your numbers, while 07 records sixteen unresolved findings from the seventh adversarial pass, two of them paths on which a person on the operator side could see a value (trust story against 07, Table 7.1). A customer-facing page that asserts an absolute while the engineering record lists open exceptions is a sales document a diligent buyer will catch; the sentence should distinguish proven from designed before it is shown to anyone. The front-door amber of DR-001 is correctly held for the US, where no chat surface dominates, but for the UK Ofcom's figure of 90 per cent of adults on WhatsApp makes it less open than the record implies to-verify [116]. In all three markets, though, the design makes the front door secondary: every authority tap lands on a bound device with a device key, so the real adoption question is the app install and the identity-rail binding, and the record never says how the key is held (no mention of passkeys, a platform authenticator or a native app), which is an under-built seam.

The sharper wedge is the one journey 1.5 already half-designs: an adult child in the UK acting for a parent in India. Dread is high, frequency is higher than the user's own dreaded tasks, the user pays in sterling, and execution runs on the India floor, the Indian rails and the flag table the pilot can sign. The record's two-jurisdiction lookup, delegator record and rule that an act India reserves to the person bounces to her device fit it exactly. Its cost is the hardest onboarding of all, and the record does not know it: the parent must bind a device through an identity rail on her own screen, sign every tap after a lock-screen factor, and operate a signed prompt. A 72-year-old in Pune must therefore own a smartphone, pass an Aadhaar-class factor (worn fingerprints are the documented reason face authentication was added to-verify [117]), set a lock-screen factor and read the envelope; a step without her authority re-classifies as a user step for her, which lands on the same device she could not bind. Nothing in 01, 03, 06 or 07 mentions age, capacity, literacy, disability or a proxy binder. This review's low-confidence estimate is that 15 to 30 per cent of the Indian over-70 cohort could complete the binding unaided, 45 to 60 per cent in the UK and 50 to 65 per cent in the US [EST]; for the cross-border family the parent is the Indian figure, so the recommended cohort starts with roughly three in four parents unable to take the first step the record requires of them.

The elder cohort also walks into the rules institutions use to stop elder fraud. In the US, FinCEN's red flags for elder financial exploitation include a new third party speaking for an older customer, a caregiver's involvement, unexplained contact-detail changes and requests for information by an intermediary, with holds of fifteen business days available to brokers to-verify [118]; in the UK the vulnerability guidance, the Banking Protocol and the reimbursement regime push a bank to refuse rather than to admit a third-party caller to-verify [68]; in India the RBI already requires doorstep banking for customers above 70 and allows self-declaration re-KYC where nothing has changed, which removes part of the dreaded banking list for exactly this cohort to-verify [119]. The product's default call script, in which the far party that asks who is calling is answered with the user's own name in aliases, is the pattern those rules describe as a red flag. The design changes are concrete and mostly cheap: an assisted binding ceremony with a family witness and a rail-side face or one-time-code factor, or a doorstep agent; a capacity attestation on the dependant's delegation record with a shorter validity; disclosed agency as the default script for regulated institutions; registering the institution's own instrument (a bank mandate, a trusted contact, a power of attorney or an LPA) as the first task type for any dependant profile; and an accessibility floor for the device surface with an alternative to the lock-screen factor. The sixth, a voice-first channel for a parent without a smartphone, contradicts the roadmap's gate that no confirmation is accepted from a channel that authenticates only a phone number, so it is an architecture decision rather than a feature.

And a threat model that looks outward, with the sixteen open findings closed by hand.

At a Series A, three milestones stated so they can fail. First, one Indian cohort of at least 200 households running banking, identity and legal tasks with verdict rates joined to step traces, an external red-team report on the two fatals, and median worker minutes per task below 45 across the L1 to L3 pools. Third, a written vault swap test passed and a domicile decision recorded, so that an acquirer or an A investor is not buying a rebuild; the domicile is cheap to decide at seed and expensive to unwind later, as the reverse flips of 2024 and 2025 showed verified [120].

The return analysis the record does not attempt. The base case, if the consumer business stalls, is a harness sale to an identity or agent-security vendor: the disclosed prices in that market were paid for enterprise security products with revenue and a threat-research team (a US$25 billion identity acquisition; press-reported estimates of US$250 to 700 million for agent-security companies) verified [121], while consumer hybrid assistants have produced acqui-hires at no disclosed price in a decade verified [86]. AI.fred's harness would sell as a team and a codebase, low tens of millions at best, unless it had enterprise customers of its own, which returns a US$4 million seed but not a fund.
