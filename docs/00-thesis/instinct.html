00Instinct, read against AI.fred

Instinct is the first consumer agent to reach a $10 billion valuation: fourteen people, a free product, and a cloud computer per user that holds the user's passwords and card and acts inside the user's own accounts. Head to head on the everyday tasks it wins, and it is not close. On the consequential tasks its own terms and its own incidents show why it cannot enter safely, and that is the only race we can win: not by being earlier, but by being different in the three places Instinct cannot cheaply copy.

1What Instinct is

Instinct is a personal AI agent you text or call, built by Spear Street Technology Inc. of San Francisco. It went into private beta in February 2026, opened an invite-only beta in August, and on 28 September 2026 announced a $1 billion Series C at a $10 billion valuation from Sequoia Capital, Benchmark and Coatue, four times the $2.5 billion it was valued at thirty-three days earlier verified [1] [5] [6]. The four rounds, a $25 million seed from Conviction and Greenoaks, a $75 million Series A led by Kleiner Perkins, a $250 million Series B and the Series C, sum to about $1.35 billion raised in under a year verified [2] [8]. It is free, and its founder has said he does not want to charge users verified [1].

The founder is Noah Shinn, 23. Fortune describes him as a college dropout whose assistant has become a challenger to Meta, whose AI chief Alexandr Wang has taken to mocking it on X since Meta launched its own agent, Muse verified [4]. At 20 he co-wrote Reflexion, one of the early papers on agents that learn from their own mistakes, and in 2023 he left Northeastern to be one of the first employees at Sierra, the customer-service agent company, where he spent two and a half years before founding Spear Street in 2025 to-verify [9]. That history matters for what follows: Instinct is built by someone whose whole career is agents that act, not by a concierge business adding a model.

The numbers that describe it are in Table 0.3. Three carry the argument of this page. The Information reported on 15 September that the user base had passed 100,000 and that compute capacity was already running short, so most of the new money goes to compute, which the founder spends two-fifths of his time acquiring verified [7]. Transaction volume through the agent is approaching $1 billion a year, half of it travel verified [1] [6]. And the company is fourteen people, growing roughly ten per cent a day on no marketing spend, by invites that users and investors hand out to-verify [10] [23].

2How it works

Instinct has three layers, and Figure 0.2 draws them. The first is the conversation: the user texts it on iMessage or WhatsApp or calls it, and it texts or calls the user first when something in their life needs attention. The second is a persistent cloud computer, one per user, that keeps running between messages: a model, a browser logged into the user's accounts, from 16 September a phone line of its own for outbound calls, and from 9 September an email address of its own at mail.instinct.com so that it can open accounts, write to restaurants and take forwarded threads without touching the user's inbox verified [11] [12]. The third is the set of connections the user grants: Google Workspace through its official interfaces (Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Tasks), the messaging thread, a Mac app for the screen, audio and location, and a Vault into which the user puts logins and a card so that the agent can sign in and pay as them to-verify [18] [19]. There is no iOS or Android app; the product is the thread, the web workspace at app.instinct.co and the Mac app to-verify [19].

The execution model is the point. Instinct does not call an airline's interface or a restaurant's booking rail; it drives a browser as the user, with the user's own password, from a machine it owns. That is why it can do anything a person at a laptop can do, and why the sites it visits cannot tell it from a person until it behaves like a machine. Where a site needs a password it does not hold, it has been reported to reset the password itself and carry on, including to complete a purchase to-verify [3] [19]. Because the machine keeps state, a task can run for hours: research, an email to a vendor, a wait for the reply, a confirmation, all without the user. On 4 September the founder said Instinct would broker credentials through 1Password rather than only through its own Vault to-verify [22].

Two things about the model of control are published, and both are terms rather than mechanisms. The terms of service appoint Instinct as the user's agent, authorised to enter into agreements, commitments and transactions on the user's behalf, and say that the company may implement confirmation requirements on certain actions but makes no representation that such safeguards will prevent unintended or erroneous ones to-verify [17] [19]. No published page says it asks before it buys; where most agents stop to confirm, Instinct continues, and early testers reported an email it sent that nobody had approved to-verify [19] [27]. On data, TechCrunch reported on 24 August that the first terms granted a perpetual training licence over user materials and that email was being stored in plain text verified [3]; the terms were rewritten two days later and the perpetual licence was dropped, but training stays on by default, the opt-out applies only from the day it is set, Google Workspace data is exempt from training while screen, audio, location and messaging data are not, and the Vault is excluded to-verify [20] [21]. In our vocabulary: the model sees every real value, the authority is unbounded, and the surface is borrowed.

3The user experience

Onboarding is three steps and under a minute: a phone number, permission for Google Workspace, and iMessage or WhatsApp; the workspace at app.instinct.co is where the user adds the Mac app, the Vault and a card later to-verify [18]. Because it has read the inbox and the calendar before the first message, it acts with context from the first exchange, and reviewers describe the first day as the product's best: it flags a meeting someone declined, notices a clash, follows up a thread the user dropped, and arranges the ride to the airport for the flight it found in email to-verify [18] [19]. The reported task range is the life admin our product also names: reservations, appointments, cancelling subscriptions, bill negotiation, vendor coordination across time zones, medical paperwork, inbox triage, groceries, flights and the ride to them; the founder's examples are a cross-country road trip, the weekly groceries and forgotten subscriptions verified [5].

The shape of a task is a thread that goes quiet and comes back. The user sends a line; the agent replies with what it will do and starts; the thread goes silent while the machine works, for minutes or for hours when a vendor has to answer; then one message arrives with the outcome. Where a thing can only be done by phone, a restaurant without online booking, a dentist's cancellation list, a dispute with a cable company, the agent calls, in early access from 16 September verified [12]. The user can forward it an email to handle a return, add it to a group thread, or call it. What users report back has two registers. The first is delight: an early investor called it as close to the personal assistant everyone has been waiting for, with a jaw-dropping experience, and users describe bills negotiated, vendors coordinated and medical forms filled without being asked twice to-verify [18] [19]. The second is the ick. On 30 September TechCrunch reported users put off by product suggestions the agent had started making from what it saw in their email and their upcoming trips verified [13]. In the week before, responses slowed and email alerts lagged, the agent stopped answering many users on 21 and 22 September with no status page to check verified [16], and Resy deactivated a venture investor's account after his agent made about two hundred reservation requests an hour; Resy said it does not permit third-party agents on its platform, and Amex, Amazon and Uber were reported to have flagged or blocked the agent too verified [14] [15]. Both registers come from the same design choice: a machine that acts as you, everywhere, without asking.

4Where it strains

Read against the two fatal failures the thesis is built around, Instinct has chosen to accept both. The first, a person or a model on the vendor's side seeing a real value, is the architecture: the agent holds the user's passwords and card and reads the whole inbox, and until 26 August the terms claimed a perpetual licence over all of it. The second, an irreversible act the user did not want, is the terms: the company is appointed the user's agent for binding transactions and promises no confirmation. Three strains follow, and Figure 0.4 and Table 0.4 date them.

4.1Authority without an envelope

There is no spend cap, no list of parties it may contact, no list of acts it may commit the user to, and no validity window, because there is no object in the product that holds them. The agent's authority is whatever the user's credentials can do. A reset password to finish a purchase and an email nobody approved are not bugs in that model; they are the model working. The product's answer so far is speed and memory: ask it to stop and it stops, and it learns what you dislike. That is a learned preference, not a bound, and the terms say so.

4.2Exposure by design

The Vault is excluded from training and Google Workspace data is exempt, which is more than the first terms offered. Everything else, the screen, audio, location and messaging, trains the model by default, the opt-out works only from the day it is set, and revoking a connection does not delete what was already taken to-verify [20] [21]. More important for our comparison, the model itself sees every value: there is no label, no substitution component and no machine without a screen, because the whole machine is the executor. A compromise of one user's cloud computer is a compromise of that user's accounts; a prompt injected into a web page the browser visits speaks with the user's own authority. Reviewers raised both and the company has not published a threat model to-verify [17] [19].

4.3A borrowed surface and a scarce one

Instinct executes inside other people's platforms as the user, and those platforms can refuse it. Resy did, and said it does not permit agents at all verified [15]. The thesis names this as the trait every rival shares, "no surface of its own", and Instinct is the strongest case of it: its surface is a browser pretending to be a person, and every site that detects the pretence can close the door. The second scarcity is compute. A persistent cloud computer per user is a cost of goods that scales with users, not with tasks, and the company's own account is that compute, not product, is what the Series C buys and what the founder spends two-fifths of his time on verified [7]. A free product with that cost line has to find revenue on the transactions; the founder has said he wants a fee on purchases and not advertising, and that an agent smarter than its user, used to sway purchases, would be dangerous to-verify [24]. The recommendations users found unwelcome on 30 September are the first visible pull in the other direction.

5What we share

More than the thesis's landscape table allows for. Instinct is not a chat-plus-people concierge and not a model inside the user's own browser; it is a machine of its own that acts, and that is the shape we chose too. Table 0.5 sets the nine points of agreement against the record that fixes each on our side. Two of them are worth saying in prose. Instinct's scope is the whole of everyday life with the hard tasks as the draw, which is exactly what DR-019 and DR-020 record for us; its founder calls it "everyday intelligence for everyone". And its founder's stated rule, that an agent must never use its intelligence to sway what its user buys, is DR-024's no-paid-placement rule in other words, said by someone who has a billion dollars of transactions to be tempted by.

6Where we differ

The differences are not in what the product does but in who holds what while it does it. Figure 0.5 draws the two paths side by side and Table 0.6 lists ten differences with the record that fixes each. Three are structural and the rest follow from them. Instinct's model holds every real value and ours holds labels, with one isolated component placing the value once (03). Instinct's authority is whatever the user's credentials allow, under terms that promise no confirmation; ours is one envelope the user confirmed, with a point-of-no-return test on every step and dual control inside it (DR-004). And Instinct has no people at all, fourteen employees and a machine per user, where we put a person in the gap on a task card that names nobody and shows no value (04). Everything else, the refusals, the ledger, the verdict, the subscription, the markets, is downstream of those three.

7Who wins, and why

Instinct, on the race it is running, and it is not close. It has a working product, a hundred thousand users compounding at a rate no marketing budget buys, $1.35 billion against our nothing, fourteen people led by someone who wrote one of the agent papers, and a year's head start on the everyday tasks that are the bulk of any assistant's volume. Its rival there is Meta's Muse, which ships inside WhatsApp with a free tier and an approval on every purchase, not us. If the question is who the general consumer agent in the US will be in two years, we are not on the list, and the thesis never claimed we were.

On the race we chose, the consequential tasks with money, identity and institutions in them, Instinct as built cannot enter safely, and its own record says why. A product whose terms disclaim confirmation, whose machine holds the user's passwords in the clear and has reset one to finish a purchase, and whose surface is a browser that platforms are already banning, is exactly the posture the thesis's section 5.3 describes as exposure: under the RBI's liability framework a customer who shares payment credentials bears the loss, and the UIDAI rules make a model that reads Aadhaar from a screen a liability the vendor cannot insure. That is why Instinct is a travel-and-dining product today, half its volume in travel, and why it is in one country. The gap is real.

But the gap is a choice, not a capability, and that cuts both ways. Everything structural that Instinct lacks on authority is a design decision it could take in a quarter: a spend cap and a party list, a confirmation on irreversible steps, credentials brokered through 1Password and swapped in at the network edge, which Meta has already shipped in Muse. Meanwhile, Instinct's own users tell us something the thesis assumes the other way. Four in ten hand it a card within three weeks, and the ones who share the most sensitive data are the ones who stay to-verify [23]. The backlash came from reporters and a minority of users, the company moved its terms in two days, and growth did not pause. The demand side is not waiting for a trust architecture; it is trading trust for speed, at least in the US, at least on tasks where the loss is a dinner. Our moat is proven on the supply side, in what regulators and institutions will allow an agent to do, and in the loss cases that have not happened yet. It is unproven on the demand side, and Instinct is the first evidence against it.

So the honest verdict is in two parts, and both are assumptions until the pilot measures them assumption. First, head to head on the same tasks in the same market, Instinct wins, today and for as long as it keeps its compute; the only thing that changes that is a loss event large enough to make a bounded authority the price of entry, and the regulator decides when. Second, we can win only by not being head to head: the consequential tasks first, in India first, where the law already makes Instinct's posture untenable and the rails (DigiLocker, UPI, the account aggregator) reward a surface of our own, and with an everyday product fast enough that the context compounds, because the thesis is explicit that the wedge alone does not. The three things Instinct cannot cheaply copy are the three we should build first: people in the gap, a published masking policy with a ledger behind it, and the passport adapters to the institutions. Those are where we are different, not merely earlier.

What this page should change on our side is small and specific, and recording it is the founder's call. The assumption that customers pay for trust needs a question in the measurement protocol, since Instinct's figures are the first data against it. The channel amber in 07 now has a hundred thousand users' worth of evidence that the thread wins in the US. And the cost model in 05 should carry a line for a persistent execution surface per user, because Instinct's compute crunch is the first public measure of what that costs to-verify [28].
