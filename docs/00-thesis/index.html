00Thesis

AI.fred wins where the flat-fee errand players cannot follow: the tasks that touch a customer's money, identity, institutions and health, where a leak or an unwanted act costs something real. Every rival either refuses the consequential task or does it by letting a person or a model see what the customer sends; we are built so that nobody on our side ever sees a real value and nothing irreversible fires outside an envelope the user confirmed, and that architecture, not a price or a channel, is the moat.

1Customer and wedge

The customer is a person with institutions to deal with and no time to deal with them. The wedge is the small share of their tasks that carry real consequence, because that is where no existing service can safely go, and where a customer pays close attention to who is holding their affairs.

1.1The customer

Picture the tasks rather than the demographic: a bank account to open in a second city; a know-your-customer refresh the bank keeps chasing; an insurer to pursue for a claim; a parent's hospital admission with three forms and a payment; a tax notice to answer. Each needs someone to act as the customer: present a document, state an identity number, answer a security question, pay, commit. Today the customer does it in stolen hours, hands it to a relative or a trusted employee, or drops it: a PAN card or a one-time passcode given to a driver, a clerk or a cousin is a leak with a friendly face, and a dropped task returns months later as a lapsed policy or a frozen account. Our customer is the urban professional or family, in India's large cities and in the US and the UK, whose affairs increasingly span more than one country. What they cannot yet delegate safely is the part that matters, and we serve that part across six verticals carried in from the founder's prior work: banking and wealth; legal and administrative; identity and security; health and medical; family affairs; travel.

1.2What consequence means

A task carries consequence when one of two things could go wrong and could not be put right. The first is a person on the operator side seeing a value they should not: an identity number, a card, a password, a diagnosis. Once seen it cannot be unseen. The second is an act the user did not want that cannot be undone: money moved, an application submitted, terms accepted. We test every step against one question, the point-of-no-return test: can we return the world to its prior state by our own action, at no cost to the user and without a third party's consent? If not, the step is irreversible. These are the two fatal failures the whole design is built around; the index states them and how each is made structurally impossible.

Below the line those two failures draw sit errands and bookings, where a wrong table is an annoyance; above it sit the verticals, where a wrong transfer, a leaked Aadhaar number or a missed filing deadline is a harm. Figure 0.1 maps the field onto that line.

1.3How we cross it

The AI is the brain and the humans are hands. Every request becomes an ordered plan, and a classifier marks each step as one the AI can run, one a worker can run from a task card, or one only the user can do on their own device. Workers, the AI and almost all of our software handle aliases such as {{pan}}; the real value lives in a bought vault and is placed into a form, a phone line or an email by one small isolated component at the last boundary, so the worker sees a field of dots and hears a muted line. Every task carries an authority envelope: a spend cap, the parties we may contact, the acts we may commit the user to, the identity acts they delegate, and a validity window past which no irreversible step can fire without re-authorisation. The planner proposes it with the plan; the user confirms it with one tap before any step executes, and can narrow or extend it from their own device at any time. Delegation is total inside it and impossible outside it. And every use of every alias is written into a plain-words access ledger the user can read:

  1. 14:32{{pan}} used to fill HDFC Bank's account-opening form, in our browser. Placed by the system. No person saw it.
  2. 14:40{{dob}} spoken to Max Healthcare's booking line, on our phone line. Placed by the system. The worker's audio was muted.
  3. 14:41A worker saw the form with {{pan}} shown as dots.

Those lines are the product. The trust story says it in a page a customer could read; the threat model in 03 names every path by which a person could see a real value and closes each one.

2Why now

Now, because the three things a consequence-bearing assistant needs have all arrived since the middle of 2025, and nobody who holds one of them holds the others: models that can plan and act; rails and rules that permit a delegate to act within an explicit, bounded grant; and a customer who has been taught to delegate at all. Table 0.1 carries the evidence.

2.1The labs have found the ceiling

The frontier labs spent 2025 shipping consumer agents and 2026 withdrawing them. Table 0.2 records the sequence: Operator, ChatGPT agent mode, Atlas, Instant Checkout and Project Mariner have each been closed or folded into products that hand the keyboard back at a login or a purchase, and the survivors refuse bank transfers, watch rather than act on financial sites, and advise against legal, medical and financial pages altogether. That is not a failure of the models but the labs discovering the wedge from the other side: a model alone inside a user's browser has no hands for the phone call or the counter, no surface it controls, and no way to bound what a hostile page can make it do. The failures the labs report and measure are injection and surface failures, not planning failures (Table 0.1); the harness around the model was missing, and the harness is what we build.

2.2The rules are moving toward bounded delegation

India and the UK are permitting delegation where it is explicit and bounded; the US is placing liability on whoever deploys the agent while its payments rule for a standing authorisation stays unwritten. In India, NPCI admits software profiles to delegated UPI payment within limits and only on an explicit user action, has piloted agentic payments with the labs on pre-authorised limits, and is reported to be preparing a protocol for agents on UPI; the RBI now requires a factor unique to every transaction and holds the bank liable when its authentication fails. In the UK, the competition regulator says a business is responsible for its agent whoever built it, and the Treasury has opened a payments consultation that covers agentic AI. In California, a deployer can no longer argue that the AI acted on its own, and Regulation E has no home yet for a standing authorisation given to an agent. Each of these rewards a service that can show, per task, what authority it held and what it did with it, and punishes one that runs on a customer's shared credentials. The envelope is that grant; the ledger is that record (Table 0.1).

2.3The customer has been taught to delegate

The flat-fee players are teaching our customer to delegate. Indian concierge start-ups have made a WhatsApp message to a human assistant a normal way to get a table booked or a document collected, and incumbents from Swiggy to Jio to Google are normalising delegation of the low-value task (Tables 0.1 and 0.2). A habit formed on errands is the habit the sensitive task needs, and none of them has a way to carry it past the line.

2.4The trust layer can be bought

One input we do not build. Tokenisation vaults with regional hosting are a product category, and buying that layer is fixed in DR-008; which vendor, and whether it can hold each market's values in region, is an open item in 07. Buying it puts the hardest part of the guarantee, keeping real values out of our own software, on a supplier whose whole business is that guarantee, and leaves us to build what nobody sells; the build-versus-buy table in 06 draws that line.

3Markets

India is where we build and where the floor runs; the US and the UK are where the commercial case is strongest; one core serves all three, and expansion is an adapter, a legal pack and a shift, fixed in DR-007. Which market the first cohort lives in is an open item in 07, with an owner and a trigger; the pilot in 06 opens whichever way it goes.

3.1India as the build base

Three properties make India the right place to build and to run the floor. The engineering depth is there: Anthropic opened an office in Bengaluru in February 2026, naming India Claude's second-largest market verified [17]. A large English-speaking workforce means an operations floor whose day shift covers India and whose two night shifts cover the west, in one office under one set of site controls; the shift design in 04 draws the wheel. And India Stack is the richest set of consented rails for the acts our verticals need: DigiLocker for documents, the Account Aggregator framework for financial data and UPI Autopay for payment mandates, the brief's fixed list as 02 draws it; a delegated UPI debit itself is the user's own act on their own device today, on UPI Circle or Reserve Pay (Table 0.1). Those rails are gated: DigiLocker releases a document only after the user authorises the requester in their own session verified [18], and only an entity regulated by a financial-sector regulator may pull data through an Account Aggregator verified [19]. That shapes the adapter, which reaches the rails through the user's own device or a regulated partner and never around them, and anything a rail returns terminates at the vault's own proxy in front of the adapter, so neither the adapter nor the planner ever holds a raw byte. The gating does not shrink the ceiling.

3.2The US and the UK as commercial ground

Willingness to pay for delegation is established in both countries: a hybrid assistant sector has existed for a decade and premium concierge is a listed business (Table 0.2). The institutions are numerous, slow and paper-bound, and increasingly reached through a single identity; the UK is consolidating government access on GOV.UK One Login and brought a digital-verification trust framework into force on 1 September 2026 to-verify. Liability is firming in our favour: a business answers for its agent, and payment regulation is being rewritten with agents in mind (Table 0.1). The adapters differ from India's, Open Banking and identity rails in the UK, bank-data aggregation and card tokenisation in the US, both to-verify in the passports section of 03. In all three markets, under today's rails, any act that needs an OTP, a biometric, a PIN or a bank's own login bounces to the user's device by design; an OTP is never ours, and the only login we ever hold is one to a non-financial portal the user has expressly delegated, kept in the vault as an alias and placed by the system where the flag permits. Full delegation is the designed ceiling, fixed in DR-003, and the per-jurisdiction flag table in 03 decides at runtime which acts are delegated, which bounce and which are prohibited pending a regulatory answer; that table is also the lobbying agenda, line by line.

3.3One core, three adapters

The planner, the classifier, the task cards, the alias vault and substitution, the envelopes, the state machine, verification, the worker console, the ledger, the intake gateway and the policy layer's mechanism are one build and country-agnostic, as 02 lists them; the flag table the mechanism reads is the per-market half. Per country we add a passport adapter, a legal pack and a shift, and the expansion playbook in 06 repeats that triplet for every market after the first. Residency is designed for: Bengaluru staff handling UK and EU personal data is a restricted cross-border transfer, one more reason the vault, with its regional hosting, is bought; the data-residency map in 03 sets it out.

4The competitive landscape

Every player in the field executes either through chat plus people who see everything, or through a model inside the user's own browser and accounts. None has an execution surface of its own, a published masking policy for its staff, or a bounded grant of authority, and the sensitive wedge is either refused or advertised with no safeguard behind it. Table 0.2 lists the players with each claim's status, and DR-013 records what "verified" means in this edition.

4.1India: Faff and its peers

The Indian field is a year or two old and already crowded, and read down Table 0.2 it has one shape: a human assistant on a chat thread, paid by the month or the hour, executing on the phone with whatever the customer has sent. The incumbents have moved too, Swiggy with Crew, Google with table bookings inside AI Mode, Jio with a call agent, NPCI with agentic UPI pilots, and all of it is low-value delegated commerce, not identity work.

Two facts define the field for us. First, the sensitive wedge is advertised without a trust architecture: Faff lists government and banking tasks, Crew lists Aadhaar and document updates, Hulp lists payments, healthcare and government documentation, and no vendor in the set publishes any limit on handling PAN, Aadhaar, bank credentials or one-time passcodes, nor any masking or need-to-know policy for its staff verified [20] [21] [22]. Second, the only published scope exclusion in the field is Faff's exclusion of business work verified [23]. The customer is being invited to hand over exactly the values the regulator tells them never to share.

4.2The US and the UK: a thinning field and a retreating one

Across the Atlantic the field is thinning rather than crowding: Table 0.2 records Panasonic closing Yohana, and Milo is reported to have shut in 2026 (to-verify in Table 0.2). Duckbill survives as the main consumer hybrid, executing with the customer's card stored in the app and a rule that it cannot impersonate the user; Fancy Hands is the one service in either field whose staff never see the card number, for capped payments the user approves one by one; Magic and Time etc work inside the client's own accounts, and Time etc's terms make the client entirely responsible for safeguarding their logins. In the UK we found no hybrid of AI and human operators launched in 2025 or 2026 to-verify: the entrants are WhatsApp assistants without human operators, human virtual-assistant firms, and the incumbents Ten Lifestyle Group and Quintessentially adding AI to human lifestyle managers.

The labs' consumer agents are a separate column and have retreated; section 2.1 and Table 0.2 record the closures. New entrants such as Instinct, Pally and Hark are raising very large rounds to act inside the user's email, screen and accounts, and Instinct's first weeks brought reports of plain-text email storage and a perpetual training licence over user data (Table 0.2). The surface is the user's own; the values pass through the vendor.

4.3What none of them can safely do

Set the two fields side by side and two postures appear. The first is refusal: the human services bar logins, account creation and medical, financial or legal work, or refer it out; the labs' agents refuse bank transfers, advise against financial, legal and medical sites, and hand logins and final purchases back to the user. The second is exposure: a card held in the app and spent up to a cap; logins handed to a named assistant through a password manager; a WhatsApp thread in which a pod of assistants reads whatever the customer sends, Aadhaar included. Only Fancy Hands keeps the payment value from its staff, and only for small purchases with per-payment approval. None describes a surface of its own, evidence redaction, a per-task grant or a ledger.

The regulators have drawn the line for them. In banking, the RBI's liability framework leaves the full loss with a customer whose own negligence, such as sharing payment credentials, caused it verified [63], and the RBI has told the public not to share logins, KYC documents, card details, PINs or OTPs with any unidentified person or agency verified [64].

In identity, UIDAI tells Aadhaar holders never to share an Aadhaar OTP, to use masked Aadhaar, and reminds organisations to display only the last four digits verified [65]; the Aadhaar Act makes unauthorised disclosure of identity information an offence punishable by imprisonment verified [66]. In the UK the business answers for its agent, and in the US a standing authorisation to an agent has no home yet in Regulation E (Table 0.1). A service whose model is "send us your details and we will sort it" is asking the customer to be negligent in the regulator's own terms, and accumulating the liability itself.

5The moat

The flat-fee players cannot follow us up the consequence axis because the property that makes their model work, a generalist who sees everything and can therefore do anything, is the property that makes the wedge unsafe. The trust architecture is not a feature they can add. It is a different company, with a different promise, floor and liability posture, visible to the user in every ledger line.

5.1Why the flat-fee model stops at the line

Unlimited tasks for a flat fee is only possible when any assistant can pick up any task with whatever the customer has sent, in a chat thread, on the assistant's own phone, using the customer's own logins. That is a leak path by design and a path to an unwanted act by design: there is no envelope because there is no boundary to draw one on. A vault bolted on afterwards changes nothing, because a person has read the value before it could be tokenised. Closing the paths means owning every execution surface, running the floor in an office under site controls, redacting every screenshot and recording before storage, and replacing "we do anything" with "we do these acts, inside this envelope, and we bounce the rest to your phone". Each cuts against the model that made them cheap, and the regulators' lines turn their exposure posture into a liability.

5.2Why the labs' agents stop there too

Models the labs have; hands they do not. Their agents act inside the user's own browser and accounts, so every sensitive value passes through the model and the vendor, and a hostile page can steer the agent; their own measurements of that are why they advise against sensitive sites (Table 0.1). They have no telephony, no counter, no human for the step that needs one, and no grant of authority beyond a confirmation prompt. Their consumer surfaces have churned within months, no foundation for a service answerable for a filing or a transfer. We buy their models and put them inside a harness they have not built.

5.3What the moat is made of

Eight structural properties, each closing a path a rival leaves open, each specified for an engineer in 02 and 03:

  1. Aliases everywhere: humans, the AI and almost all of our software see {{pan}}, never the value, which lives in a bought vault (the alias model).
  2. Substitution at the last boundary by one small isolated component, into a browser we run, a phone line we own or a mail relay we operate (the substitution component).
  3. Execution surfaces that are ours; a worker never uses their own phone, browser or email.
  4. Evidence redaction in the harness: no raw screenshot, recording or transcript is ever human-viewable, because the evidence system is itself a leak path (DR-009).
  5. An authority envelope per task and the point-of-no-return test on every step: irreversible and outside the envelope bounces to the user's device; irreversible and inside it runs under dual control (DR-004).
  6. A task state machine in which user abandonment is legitimate and system forgetting is forbidden as an architectural invariant, not a procedure (DR-005).
  7. An office-mandatory 24/7 floor whose site controls are part of the guarantee, with need-to-know task cards naming a pseudonymised client and only the fields the step needs (DR-006).
  8. A plain-words access ledger, per task, of every use of every alias and who saw what (the ledger specification).

Copying one is easy. Copying all eight is building us, and it cannot be done on a customer base and an operations culture assembled on the premise that the assistant sees everything.

5.4What the moat is not

It is not the model: we buy models, and the labs will keep improving them for us. It is not the channel: the front door is a per-market launch choice, amber in DR-001, above a channel-agnostic gateway. It is not price: economics is deferred by the founder's decision, and this thesis carries no number of ours because it needs none (05, DR-015). And it is not a claim that an incumbent could never build it; Swiggy, Jio or a bank could in principle, but every incumbent move in Table 0.2 runs the other way, toward low-value commerce inside their own apps, with no human floor under site controls and no liability posture for identity acts. If one publishes a masking architecture, surfaces of its own and a per-task envelope, this thesis narrows, and we will say so in 07.
