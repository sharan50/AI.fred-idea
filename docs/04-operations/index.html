1Task cards and pseudonymisation

A worker never receives a task. They receive a step, on a card the task service generated from the step record, carrying the least the step needs: a client number, the aliases the system will place, the facts in clear, the script, the expected outcome and a structured result. The console that renders it is specified in 02, worker console; this section says how a card comes to exist and what it may contain.

1.1How a card is generated

A card is the projection of one step whose class is human. The planner produces the plan, the classifier marks each step ai, human or user, and the card generator inside the task service assembles a card for every human step from the step record. A need-to-know filter runs first: it admits into the facts in clear only the non-sensitive task fields the step-type catalogue entry's script template names for the step type, an institution, a department, a window, a relationship word such as nominee or daughter, and only where the script references them. It admits no person's name: a name, a date of birth, an identifier or a contact detail is a personal-fact alias of Table 3.1 in 03 whoever it belongs to, the user's as {{name}} and a people node's qualified by the person reference ({{name.nominee}}, {{dob.nominee}}), vaulted at the proxy and placed by the substitutor like any other alias. It drops every other fact, drops any alias the policy layer does not permit on that surface in that jurisdiction (returning the step for re-classification), and replaces the user's identity with the task's client number. The hash service's screen runs last at generation, and the task service does not compute it: it holds no digest set and matches nothing itself, but submits the card payload to the hash service's screening call, as 03 specifies, which answers clean or found; a payload that comes back found is rejected before it leaves the task service and raises an incident. The screen is not the card's last line: the step sentence, the expected outcome and the facts in clear are free text the planner wrote from the tokenised request, and a value phrased outside the four detectors' reach could ride in them, so the console renders every free-text field of a card, the step sentence, the expected outcome, the facts in clear, a question and a variance note, through the same allowlist default paint the mail relay's renderer applies to an inbound message: every token outside the cleared vocabulary, an out-of-lexicon token, a proper noun that is not a cleared term, a digit run, a class-format match, is painted before the worker reads it, and a painted token the step needs is requested as a typed field on the task's thread or closes the step blocked-needs-user. Three components, one direction of travel: the planner writes the step record, its script drawn from the step-type catalogue entry in 02; the task service generates the card, and the filter and the screen run there, at generation, before a card exists anywhere; the console renders it, with its default paint as the second line behind the screen. Cards are generated when a step becomes ready, never in advance, because a card that exists is a card that can be read; when a plan is revised, the cards of superseded steps leave the console as the steps close cancelled with the outcome code superseded.

1.2Need-to-know and Client 4471

The client number is issued per task and is stable for every step of it, so it is the same on every card of the task and different on every task: two tasks by the same user carry two different numbers, and a worker cannot join them. The task service issues it when a task is accepted, from a random space large enough that a collision is rejected rather than resolved, never in sequence. It is stored in the task record beside the task id and the user reference the request format in 02 carries, and it is derived from neither, so nothing about a number says anything about a person, a task or another number. The user reference never reaches a card, a console view or a log the floor can read; the mapping from client number to task is read by the task service alone, when it generates a card, and is exposed to no support tool. "Client 4471" is the pattern: a word and a number, nothing that hints at a name, a city or a vertical.

Need-to-know applies at the field level. A worker booking a consultation learns the hospital, the department and the acceptable window; not the diagnosis, the referral letter, or the bank account the same client opened last week. "Not the diagnosis" is a mechanism, not a courtesy. A condition, a medication, a result or a legal matter is a value of the health and legal class of Table 3.1 in 03 ({{health.condition}}, {{legal.matter}}), tokenised at the vault's edge proxy whether the user typed it in a message or entered it in the device form's health fields, an adapter returned it, or a party said it on a call, where the pipeline's pass over the recording tokenises it and an incident is opened; the detector that catches a free-text mention is the dictionary-based health and legal term detector in the detector policy the proxy and the hash service's screen both run, one artefact shipped to both, whose reach is a to-verify that 03 records and the vault evaluation in 07 measures. Free text written on our side takes the second of those two routes: a worker's variance note or failed-final line, a question's free-line answer, an onboarding answer, a fact a closed step writes or a connector pull is submitted by the task service or the console to the hash service's screening call, which runs the same four detectors the proxy runs (identifier pattern, known-value digest, the user's declaration where a field is declared sensitive, and the health and legal dictionary), tokenises any match through its tokenise-only credential and returns the text with aliases in place; the task service and the console store only what the call returns. So a diagnosis is never in clear in the context store, in the step record's facts in clear, on a card or on a caption. An amount is a value of the same kind: a balance, a transaction, a loan, a salary, an overdue sum or a quoted fee is a financial-fact alias of Table 3.1 ({{balance.hdfc}}, {{amount.fee}}), a number with a currency word or symbol in written or spoken form, caught by the amount pattern the identifier-pattern detector carries at the proxy and in the hash service's screen, and by the pipeline's pass over a recording in its spoken form, so it reaches no card, caption, frame or stored result field as a number, and the envelope's cap is no exception: the card generator renders a cap line as presence and comparison only, "spend cap: no spend" for a cap of zero and otherwise "spend cap: set", the check card shows "remaining cap: sufficient for this act: yes", computed by the task service, and the amount-pattern screen therefore holds on every card payload. Where a step needs one amount for its outcome, the catalogue entry names that field, the surface controller compares the masked amount with the envelope's cap line, and the worker sees only the comparison, as the card in section 1.4 shows. What the booking needs is the speciality or the department, a non-sensitive task field the catalogue entry names, and that is what the filter admits. Underneath sits the alias rule from 03: everything the vault classes as sensitive is only ever an alias, so a card cannot carry a real value even by mistake, and the filter governs the non-sensitive remainder. The user's name, address, phone number and email are vault-classed, and so are a nominee's, a relative's or a contact's, aliases of the same class qualified by the person reference: a desk that asks for the patient's name gets it from the substitutor as {{name}}, placed like any other alias, never as a fact in clear. That is the console's alias-only rule seen from the card's side. One-time passcodes are never ours at all; {{otp}} is not an alias, and a step that needs one bounces to the user's device. The regulator asks the same of the customer: the Reserve Bank of India cautioned the public in August 2024 not to share login details, copies of KYC documents, card details, PINs, passwords or OTPs with unidentified persons or agencies verified [1]. Our floor is built so that a worker could not receive one even if a user tried to send it.

1.3The fields

The worker console owns the card's shape: six parts, in a fixed order, and nothing else, drawn in Figure 2.5. Table 4.1 says where each part's content comes from and the rule that bounds it. The surface panel sits beside the card, not on it. The card joins the step trace by the task reference and step position its header shows, which is how QA draws its sample and how the escalation desk receives a step; the client number is a second number with no relation to the first, and neither is derived from the user reference. Nobody on the floor looks a task up by the user: the desk receives a step through its outcome code, QA samples from the step trace, and a message from the user is joined to its task by the intake gateway, upstream of the floor.

1.4The script and the result

The script is numbered, in the second person, and marks the moments at which the system places a value: the worker cues placement; the tap goes from the console to the task service and on to the step's surface controller, which requests the placement, so the console never calls the substitutor; the substitutor places the value directly into the surface and answers the controller with a receipt, never a value; the worker's stream shows dots, and a call carries no placement at all; and the access ledger records the placement in plain words.

The result is structured: one code from the fixed vocabulary in the worker console, the fields it requires, free text bounded to the variance note. On a call the worker hears the far party in the ordinary way, because no value from the vault is ever on a call (DR-021): a reference the desk reads out is typed into the result form by the worker and tokenised into an alias on arrival, the ledger reading "reference captured as {{reference.max-healthcare}}", and a value the desk states unasked closes the step on the possible-exposure control, which opens an incident with the party named. The script's line declining read-backs is now part of the closure rather than a courtesy, because the channel has no mask behind it.

The script's fee line is an instruction to a person and one mechanism stands behind it: the worker records the amount the desk quotes in the result, the task service compares it with the envelope's cap line, and a step whose fee sits above the sub-cap closes blocked-outside-envelope whatever the worker says next. The agreement is bounded the same way, by the script rather than by a gate in the media path: a step holding no approval token carries no committing sentence, so the worker has nothing to say that could agree anything, and a desk that will not proceed without an agreement is closed blocked-outside-envelope. Behind that stands a second line: every result field, the variance note included, is submitted by the task service to the hash service's screening call, which runs the four detectors of section 1.2 and answers clean, or found with the text returned aliases in place; the task service holds no digest set and computes no match, stores only what the call returns, and raises a found answer as an incident that the shift lead holds. A question closes the step as blocked-needs-user, which the task service turns into a user step.

2The 24/7 shift design

One office runs the clock: a day shift for India and two night shifts for the west, the UK first and then the US, on a single 24-hour wheel in Indian Standard Time. The handover is the moment we design hardest for. No cost model is attached; pool sizing against the wheel is an input 05 defers. The two night shifts are night work in Karnataka, which the floor takes to need permits, transport and safeguards, including for women workers; what the law requires of each is to be verified from the primary source before the first night shift is rostered, and the item and its owner are in 07 to-verify.

2.1The wheel

The dial is Indian Standard Time, midnight at the top; the outer band holds the three shifts, the accent marks each handover where two bands overlap, and the arcs inside are each market's working day in Indian time, with the numbers in Table 4.2. Two of the three handovers, at 15:30 and 23:30, fall inside open markets. The third, at 07:30, sits on purpose in the quiet hour between the US west coast's close and India's open. All three are designed as events rather than a change of faces, because blocked steps hold response windows across them and a promised callback has to find a live desk.

2.2The offsets used

Offsets are facts, and Table 4.2 states the ones drawn. The wheel uses summer offsets; in winter every western arc moves an hour later on the dial and the shift windows absorb it without redrawing. The working day drawn is a plain nine to five-thirty for every market, India included, not a claim about any institution's hours.

2.3The three shifts and the handover

Each shift carries the same complement: an L1 pool, L2 pods staffed for the markets the shift holds, at least one L3 seat, a shift lead who holds QA for the shift, and escalation cover. A pod is defined by vertical, but its members on a shift are drawn for that shift's markets: the night-one banking pod knows UK institutions, the day-shift one Indian ones. What a pod learns about an institution is written back into that market's entries in the step-type catalogue of 02, which ship in the legal pack, so the knowledge outlives the person; the per-market complement is the shift in the playbook of DR-007, an adapter, a legal pack and a shift. The handover is the figure's accent because it is where a floor is most likely to drop something. Thirty minutes of overlap, illustrative, under one rule: before a shift ends, every item on the exceptions board has a named owner and a live timer, and nothing crosses the handover unowned. The outgoing shift lead walks the incoming one through the board, and an item that still needs work is re-owned by name as the first act of the handover. A card in running or checking is closed by its holder before they leave or re-assigned with its timer intact, and the surface decides which: a step on a live call is finished by its holder, whose shift ends when it closes; browser and mail steps re-assign; a call that cannot finish closes blocked-third-party with a callback window. The L3 seat passes with the list of steps in checking. The state machine makes this enforceable: a step whose holder left without closing it still carries its timer; at expiry the task service acts on the tick, as section 7 sets out, re-queuing the step to its pool with a fresh timer up to the step type's cap and closing it failed-final past the cap, which is what re-plans; and the watchdog's four checks catch the residue.

3What phone work covers

Phone work covers non-authenticating calls and nothing else: bookings, chasing, enquiries, availability and appointment setting. No value from the vault is placed on a call in either direction, and no script asks a worker to state one (DR-021), so a step that needs a value stated or verified by voice is placed on the managed browser or the relay, where the substitution component can place it, or it bounces to the user's own device. A worker asked for a value on a call says it is not available on the phone, offers the surface that can carry it, and closes the step blocked-needs-user if the desk will not proceed. A desk that states a value unasked is an exposure: the worker closes the step on the possible-exposure control, which opens an incident with the party named.

What a worker hears on such a call is the far party using the user's first name and the details of the thing being booked. That is accepted, and it is what the need-to-know card, the vetting of section 4, the site controls of section 5 and the access ledger are for; on this surface they are the mitigation rather than the closure, and the trust story says so in the user's own words.

3.1The workflow library and its tickets

The library the architecture specifies (DR-022) is an operational asset with an operational cost. A counterparty is modelled once, per step type, and from then on the worker is given a structured task card rather than a page; a counterparty that changes its process breaks that entry, and the break surfaces as a step that cannot reach its acceptance artefact. The floor's job is to catch it rather than to work around it: the exceptions board carries the failed step, the shift lead raises a library ticket naming the party, the step type and what changed, and the step is worked by hand or bounced until the ticket lands. Two floor metrics follow, reported with the exceptions board: open library tickets by party, and the share of steps worked by hand because the library was stale. 05 prices the maintenance.

4Tiers, escalation and dual control

Three tiers, and two functions outside them. QA and escalation are standalone functions, not tiers, reporting outside the tier line so that the people who check the work do not answer to the people who route it.

4.1The three tiers

L1, the generalist pool
Reversible steps in any vertical: a call to confirm an appointment, a booking form filled from aliases, never a KYC form, a status enquiry. L1 never fires an irreversible step. Every worker starts here, and its cards are the most fully scripted.
L2, the specialist pods
Six pods, one per vertical: banking and wealth; legal and administrative; identity and security; health and medical; family affairs; travel. A pod takes the step types that name it and the irreversible human steps in its vertical, executed under dual control with the AI checking. A pod knows how its institutions behave: which desk to ask for, which forms bounce, which fees are negotiable.
L3, super-trust
The smallest tier and the most vetted. L3 executes the six identity acts of the closed list in 01 that place an identity value or credential (present a document, state an identity number, answer security questions from the context store, complete a KYC form, use a delegated login to a non-financial portal, sign or e-sign), where the policy layer permits them in a jurisdiction, always with the value placed by the substitutor and never seen, and holds the human seat in dual control whenever the AI executes an irreversible step. The seventh act, attest a fact about the user, places nothing of its own: it rides beside a committable act on any step and takes that step's routing, which is why task 12 step 3, stating the client's name and date of birth to a booking desk, is an L1 card. L3 is drawn from L2 after a tenure and a further check, and a seat is staffed on every shift.

4.2Where a step goes

Routing is a rule, not a judgement, and it runs when the card is issued. The key is the step type, from the step-type catalogue in 02, and the rule is a routing table that operations owns, kept beside the catalogue and keyed by step type: L1 for a reversible step type with no institution-specific script; the vertical's pod for one that needs it; L3 for a step whose identity act places an identity value or credential, the six of section 4.1, and for the checking seat in dual control; attest-fact, which places nothing, routes with the step it rides on. The table's answer is stamped on the step record as the executor's tier when the card is issued, and a check card for an AI-executed step is offered only to the L3 checking pool. An irreversible step inside the envelope goes to L2 or L3, enters checking before it fires, and never goes to L1. Before any entry the table reads the scope block of the market's legal pack, as section 3.1 of 02 fixes, and for the pilot that block names L3 as the only pool for an irreversible human step, so every such step goes to L3, as 06 records; the rule is a line of the pack, not a setting of the table. A worker cannot pull a card from a pool above their own. Where a step type carries a specialist part and a reversible remainder, the plan carries them as two steps from the start, the pod's and then L1's: the split is the planner's at planning time and never a re-plan after the pod closes, and a pod that finds the split wrong closes failed-final with the reason, one of the re-planning triggers 02 fixes.

4.3Dual control on the floor

For an irreversible step inside the envelope, whichever party executes, the other checks before it fires: dual control, fixed in DR-004 and specified for the console in 02. On the floor it has two shapes. When the AI executes, an L3 worker checks: the console shows the step, the envelope lines it touches, the expected outcome and the exact act about to fire, with aliases in place of values, and the L3 confirms or refuses with a reason. When a worker executes, the AI performs the same check, and the console withholds the committing act until the result is recorded rather than trusting the worker to wait. The substitutor will not place a value for an irreversible step without the approval token the check issues; where the act places no value, the surface controller holds it. The committing actions are a closed list, the same on every page: a form submit, a committing click, a committing sentence on a call, a keypad press outside the navigation map, a relay release, a committing placement; every controller checks the envelope's validity window and version at each of them, as 02 fixes, and each surface holds the act by a default rule of its own. A committing placement is one whose alias is a payment instrument or a payment instruction, or whose catalogue entry marks it as consumed on placement: the substitutor requires the approval token for it like a submit and writes the step's fired marker before the first tone or character is delivered, so a not-confirmed receipt closes the step failed-final with the marker as its evidence, never failed-retryable, the reservation against the cap is not released, the re-plan issues a status enquiry before any repeat, and a narrowing that arrives inside the placement's window does not stop it, which the outcome report says. In the managed browser a click is a committing action unless the party directory's page map, a field beside the menu map, names the control as navigation: following a link inside the declared domains, scrolling, focusing a field and typing non-sensitive text are navigation by default; a button, a form submit or any scripted control is committing unless the page map names it; and any form submission or committing click on a party's domain is irreversible unless the catalogue entry names its undo route, the submit being fired by the browser controller on the task service's instruction, after the check. On a call there is no gate in the media path, so on a step holding no approval token the script is the bound: it carries only the lines the step needs and no committing sentence, the non-committing lexicon covers, greetings, questions and acknowledgements without agreement words; every other utterance is held, the caption reads "held; off script", and the worker re-phrases or sends the step for check; on a step holding a token the revealed committing sentence passes. Inside the lexicon the commitment dictionary remains the detector for what counts as agreement, agreement words, amounts, dates and account-changing verbs per language, and a commitment-form utterance that is not the revealed committing sentence for a step holding an approval token is cut before delivery, so the far party never hears it. On an irreversible step not yet checked, the caption shows "commitment held; send for check" and the step enters checking; on a reversible step the cut is final: the caption reads "commitment cut; this step commits nothing", the step does not enter checking and never holds a token, and if the party will not proceed without a commitment the worker closes the step blocked-outside-envelope. The committing sentence is not shown to the worker until the check is recorded; then it is released and the worker reads it as written. The step's fired marker is written at the release, as telephony in 02 specifies: a marker when the sentence reaches the worker's leg for the revealed sentence or begins the synthesised speech, an end marker when the sentence has been delivered. The start marker is the step's fired marker, and a step that carries one never returns to ready. A withdrawal or a narrowing that reaches the task service before the sentence is released stops it at once, the console withdrawing it from the card: on a withdrawal the step closes cancelled, cause withdrawn, and the outcome report cites the utterance record; on a narrowing it closes failed-final with the fired marker as its evidence, and the re-plan issues a status enquiry with the party before any repeat, as 02 fixes for every step with a fired marker. One that arrives after the release treats the act as fired, and the outcome report lists it with the step's fired marker as the evidence. The worker's structured result records what the party said in reply; it is never the evidence of whether the act fired. A key pressed on a party's voice menu is held by the same controller: the step's catalogue entry carries a menu map, the keys that navigate, per party, from the party register; the telephony controller sends a key the map names as navigation without a token, and any key the map names as committing, or any key the map does not name, is a committing action that needs the approval token and the window-and-version check like a submit. In the mail relay the rule is the template: the relay releases a message only if it instantiates the step's catalogue template, fixed sentences with named slots typed non-committing, or the step holds an approval token naming the hash of the message as checked; a message that matches no template is a committing action and needs the token, so a draft is not released until the check is on the record. On a reversible step, which holds no token, none of the three surfaces can commit anything: the browser controller refuses every click outside the page map and every submit, the telephony controller refuses every key outside the navigation map, and a script's line on fees is an instruction to a person, the gate being the closure. The executor is L2 or L3, never L1. Both identities live on the step record and the outcome record. The ledger records placements, views and mutes, the event types 03 fixes: the check itself writes no line of its own, and the checker's viewing of the masked stream does, as "A checker saw the masked stream for step n"; the user learns that the step was checked from the outcome report, which lists every irreversible act that fired and the party that checked it, as 01 sets out. A step in checking past its timer goes to the board.

4.4Escalation

A worker escalates by closing the step with the code that says why, and a card escalated is not a card failed. There is no escalate control: blocked-needs-user, blocked-outside-envelope, a blocked-third-party step past its window, failed-final and refused-policy are the escalations. The escalation desk is a standalone function staffed on every shift by people from L2 and L3 who are not, that shift, executing cards of their own. It does one of four things: chases a blocked third party and sets a new response window; re-routes a step that failed because its route was wrong, by adding the right route to the alternatives the failed-final step carries into re-planning, so the planner issues it again to a pod or to L3; confirms a refusal under policy, with the policy reference; or adds to the alternatives a failed-final step carries into re-planning. It never edits a card in place, because a card is a projection of the step record and the step record is the planner's. Escalations are counted per step type; a step type that keeps producing them is one QA proposes a change to, and its catalogue entry takes the change.

5Site controls as part of the trust guarantee

The room is part of the promise. The software makes "no person saw it" true on the screen; the site controls make it true around the screen. Office-mandatory, 24/7 operation is a fixed decision, DR-006, and the controls below are why it is a trust decision rather than a facilities preference. Three paths run through the room and are named in the threat model in 03 under the names Table 4.4 uses: the photograph or note, tampering with a station or a masking client, and a missed echo on a screen. On every one of them a real value is stopped by software before it reaches a screen: the masking of 03 is applied server-side, on the data a task card carries or before a frame is encoded, so no station holds a frame the masker has not cleared, and no value is ever on a call for a headset to carry (DR-021). The room is what makes that closure attestable rather than asserted: its controls are the record of a masking failure and the deterrent, never the closure, and they keep off the floor anything that could carry the task detail a card does show.

5.2What a visitor would see

A quiet floor. Identical workstations, each showing a client number, a numbered script, aliases in braces and, where a form is on screen, fields rendered as dots. Workers on headsets who fall silent while a panel reads "value placed". No paper. A wall display carrying the exceptions board, the shift lead's name at the top. A glass-walled zone for the L3 seats, two people in it at any time; the escalation desk beside it on the open floor. Lockers at the door, the visitor's phone inside one. Not a single real value on any screen, and that is the point of the tour.

5.3Why remote is not permitted now

Remote work is not permitted because a home cannot be attested. A home has a family, a camera in a laptop lid, a second screen, a phone on the desk and a network we do not run. The screen shows only aliases and dots wherever the worker sits; what a home cannot give us is the ability to say so to an auditor, a regulator or a user with evidence. Remote-safe operation is a designed-later programme, parked with an owner and a trigger in 07. It will need a remote station we own, a room we can attest by other means, and a legal pack per jurisdiction; until it exists, every card is executed on the floor.

6QA and the exceptions board

QA is a shift duty with a named owner, not a department that reads reports afterwards. The board is specified in 02, worker console; this section says whose it is and what they do with it.

6.1Who owns the board

The shift lead owns the board, and that ownership is the first line of the shift roster. The shift lead is the QA function's seat on the shift, the person the worker console names as accountable for the board at handover. The watchdog asks four questions of every record, as 02 fixes them, and puts a task on the board when any fails: no live timer; a step in checking past its check deadline or without a checker; a response window expired without resume, escalate-resume or extend-window following within a cycle; a nudge schedule exhausted without a lapse event within a cycle. The task service adds a task whose window expired with no route, and a task whose planning deadline expired twice, each under a holding timer (section 7). The shift lead gives every item a person and a timer before the shift ends, and the handover rule of section 2.3 applies: nothing crosses unowned, and an item that still needs work is re-owned by name first. The board is never emptied by fiat: an item leaves it only through a recorded transition, and the invariant in 02 is what the shift lead enforces by hand. The shift lead also holds the shift's escalation counts and the incidents the task service raised for a result the screening call found a value in, each a near-miss on the one thing the floor must never do.

6.2Sampling closed steps

Every shift the shift lead samples closed steps, stratified by tier, vertical and outcome code, weighted toward ok-with-variance, failed-final and refused-policy, and toward workers in their first weeks on a tier. Each is judged against its structured result first: the code and its fields, the expected outcome, the script, the ledger lines and the redaction manifest. The masked artefact, the only form that exists under 03's redaction rules and DR-009, is opened only where the record cannot settle the finding, and every opening is a named audit: a ledger event the user can read, as Table 3.3 in 03 admits under this duty. The picture is never the habit and never the measure. A finding is one of four kinds: the code was wrong for what happened; the script was followed and the outcome still wrong; the script was right and not followed; or the card was defective, by a need-to-know breach, a missing fact or the wrong surface. Findings become proposed changes to the entries of the step-type catalogue in 02, then to the routing table, then to the training queue, in that order, because a defective entry explains more failures than a careless worker.

6.3Verdict follow-up

The verdict is the user's and it is the ground truth of quality; the outcome codes are not, as 01 sets out. The shift lead reads every wrong-outcome and could-not-complete verdict against its step trace before the following shift ends, and a sample of done-rough. The reading is a search for the step, the template or the route that produced the verdict, not a grade for the worker. Whatever make-good policy the founder settles, an open item in 07, rests on it.

7Service levels, stated qualitatively

We state service levels in words on purpose. Response numbers are a pilot's output, not its input, and tying them to pool size now would smuggle in the economics that 05 defers. What can be fixed now is the shape of each promise and the mechanism that keeps it: every level below is carried by a timer in the state machine, so a missed level is acted on by the task service at expiry, and whatever that leaves is an exception on the board rather than a statistic discovered later.

We make no promise about how long a task takes end to end, because the longest part of most high-consequence tasks is an institution's own clock, which we can chase but not set; and none per worker, because the unit the user experiences is the task and the unit the floor manages is the step.
