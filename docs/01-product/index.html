1User journeys across the six verticals

Every journey has one shape, whatever the vertical. The user asks; the planner returns an ordered plan and proposes an authority envelope; the user confirms both with one tap; steps run, each classed ai (the AI executes), human (a worker executes from a task card) or user (the step bounces to the user's own device); an outcome report arrives with the access ledger attached; the user closes the task with a verdict. Nothing executes before the tap, and nothing irreversible fires outside the envelope. The six journeys below show where the envelope and the ledger appear and where a step must come back to the user. Institutions are named to make the examples concrete; ledger times are illustrative.

A step marked dual control is irreversible under the point-of-no-return test and inside the envelope: whichever party executes it, the other checks it against the envelope and the expected outcome before it fires. A user step is the user's own act (an approval in their bank app, an OTP, a signature, an appearance in person); the task waits in awaiting-user until they act, and nudges them if they go quiet.

1.1Banking and wealth: renew a maturing deposit (India)

The user forwards a maturity notice from HDFC Bank to our relay and asks us to renew the deposit for a longer tenor and add a nominee.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil; the principal stays at the bank. Parties: HDFC Bank. Acts: submit an application, accept terms. Identity acts: state {{pan}} and {{account.hdfc}} in the bank's form. Window: until the maturity date.

  1. ai Read the notice. The gateway tokenised the account number at the edge, so the planner sees {{account.hdfc}}, never the number.
  2. ai Compare the bank's published tenors in our managed browser.
  3. ai Fill the renewal and nomination form in our browser; the substitutor places {{pan}} and {{account.hdfc}} at the last boundary and the supervising worker sees dots.
  4. user The bank's app asks the account holder to approve; the task waits in awaiting-user and resumes on the tap.
  5. ai, dual control. Submit the renewal. Reversing it would need the bank's consent, so it is irreversible; a worker checks tenor, amount and nominee against the envelope before it fires. Closes ok with the bank's reference.

Had the bank demanded a fresh video KYC, that step would have been user from the start: the RBI reserves video customer identification to the regulated entity's own trained officials, with third parties assisting only at the customer's end verified [1]. The ledger reads:

  1. 11:04{{account.hdfc}} used to identify the deposit on HDFC Bank's renewal form, in our browser. Placed by the system. No person saw it.
  2. 11:05{{pan}} used to fill HDFC Bank's renewal form, in our browser. Placed by the system. No person saw it.
  3. 11:06A worker saw the form with {{pan}} shown as dots.

1.2Legal and administrative: contest a penalty charge notice (UK)

A parking penalty charge notice arrives with a deadline for representations. The user photographs it and the signage and asks us to contest it.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Parties: the issuing council. Acts: file a document. Identity acts: state the user's name and address as printed on the notice. Window: the deadline on the notice.

  1. ai Read the notice and the photos; extract the reference, the contravention code and the deadline.
  2. ai Check the council's published grounds and route in our browser.
  3. ai Draft the representation, showing each fact it relies on with its confidence from the context store.
  4. user Confirm the statement of facts; we never assert to an authority a fact the user has not confirmed. A question, so the task waits in awaiting-user.
  5. ai, dual control. File it. A filing cannot be withdrawn by our own action; a worker checks reference, deadline and attachments before it fires. Closes ok with the acknowledgement.
  6. The council's decision is a third party's act: awaiting-third-party, with the council's stated response window as the timer.

No vault alias was used, and the ledger says so in one line; the ledger exists for every task, including those where nothing sensitive was placed.

1.3Identity and security: a stolen phone (India)

The user's phone is stolen. They reach us from a friend's laptop through the web front door; the gateway binds the request to their account and the lost channel changes nothing.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Parties: the mobile operator, HDFC Bank. Acts: block a SIM, block a card, request reissue, schedule. Identity acts: state {{dob}} and answer the operator's security questions from the context store. Window: twenty-four hours (illustrative).

  1. ai Plan the loss response: the SIM, the cards, the sessions the user names.
  2. human, dual control. Call the operator's blocking line on our telephony. The worker reads the task card, which names the customer as Client 4471; the substitutor speaks {{dob}} while the worker's audio is muted. A SIM block is irreversible, since reissue needs the operator's consent, so the AI checks the request against the envelope before the worker asks for it. Closes ok.
  3. ai, dual control. Block {{card.primary}} on the bank's card-control page in our browser; a worker checks first. Closes ok.
  4. user A replacement SIM needs the user in person and a replacement card needs the bank's app: both bounce. Booking the store slot is reversible and ours.
  5. Resetting the UPI PIN is refused within the plan, and the report says so: a PIN is never ours.

Ledger lines: {{dob}} spoken to the operator's blocking line, on our phone line, the worker's audio muted; {{card.primary}} used on HDFC Bank's card-control page, in our browser, no person saw it.

1.4Health and medical: a specialist appointment and the records (India)

A clinic refers the user to a specialist at Max Healthcare. The user uploads the referral and asks us to book the earliest slot, pay the fee and have the records sent across.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: the consultation fee, at the amount the user sets. Parties: Max Healthcare, the referring clinic. Acts: book, schedule, make a payment under the cap. Identity acts: state {{dob}} on the booking line. Window: two weeks (illustrative).

  1. ai Read the referral; the gateway tokenised the patient identifiers.
  2. ai Check the specialist's slots on the hospital's portal in our browser.
  3. human Call the booking line. Booking is cancellable at no cost by our own action, so it is reversible and needs no dual control; the substitutor speaks {{dob}} with the worker muted. Closes ok-with-variance if the slot differs from the plan.
  4. ai, dual control. Pay the fee through the payment passport; a non-refundable prepayment fails the point-of-no-return test, so a worker checks amount and payee against the cap first. Where the rail demands the account holder's own approval, that is a user step on their device.
  5. user The clinic's records-release form needs the patient's signature. It bounces.
  6. human Chase the clinic for the transfer. Closes blocked-third-party with a response window.

The ledger carries the line the trust story uses: {{dob}} spoken to Max Healthcare's booking line, on our phone line, placed by the system, the worker's audio muted.

1.5Family affairs: a parent's repeat prescriptions (UK)

The user's mother wants her repeat prescriptions moved to a pharmacy that delivers, and the user asks us to arrange it.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Parties: the mother, her GP surgery, the pharmacy. Acts: submit an application, schedule. Identity acts: state the mother's {{nhs-number}} on the surgery's line, once she has delegated it herself. Window: one month (illustrative).

  1. ai Plan. The mother is a separate person, so her identity acts are hers to delegate: the plan opens with a one-tap delegation on her own device, and until she taps none of her identity acts is in the envelope.
  2. user (the mother) Delegate. The policy layer treats a family member as a separate delegator in every jurisdiction.
  3. ai Check the pharmacy's delivery area and sign-up route in our browser.
  4. human Call the surgery to change the nominated pharmacy: reversible by the same call at no cost, so no dual control. The substitutor speaks {{nhs-number}} with the worker muted.
  5. ai Create the delivery account with the address in the context store, and schedule the repeat-order reminders.

The context store gains two facts, the mother's pharmacy and the surgery's line, each sourced to this task and dated. The ledger shows the single use of {{nhs-number}} and that the mother's delegation preceded it.

1.6Travel: rebook a cancelled flight (US)

An airline cancels the user's flight the evening before. The cancellation is in the mailbox the user connected read-only; the user asks us to rebook within a budget they name and to move the hotel.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: the amount named. Parties: the airline, the hotel. Acts: book, cancel, make a payment under the cap. Identity acts: none; the booking reference identifies the trip. Window: until the new departure.

  1. ai Read the cancellation and the itinerary.
  2. ai Find alternatives within the cap in our browser. Two candidates differ in arrival time, so the plan asks which and waits in awaiting-user: a question, not a bounce.
  3. ai, dual control. Book the chosen flight. A non-refundable fare is irreversible, so a worker checks date, passenger name and fare against the envelope first; the substitutor places {{card.primary}} through the card passport and the worker sees dots.
  4. human Call the airline on our telephony for the refund of the cancelled ticket. Closes ok-with-variance if a credit is offered instead; accepting one commits the user, so the choice goes back to them.
  5. ai Email the hotel through our relay to move the check-in. Reversible.

Ledger lines: {{card.primary}} used to pay for the new ticket on the airline's checkout page, in our browser, no person saw it; a worker saw the checkout page with {{card.primary}} shown as dots.

2The task taxonomy

Task types are the unit at which the product is measured. Verdict rates are computed per task type; the nudge schedule is set per task type; the L2 pod that owns the specialist knowledge is chosen by vertical. A task type is therefore a vertical plus a shape of plan, and the shape is defined by three things: where the point of no return sits, which step classes the plan typically needs, and what the envelope must contain.

Reversibility belongs to a step, not to a task. Table 1.1 names the step that carries the point of no return in each type, because that is the step that will be dual-controlled or bounced. A step whose reversibility the classifier cannot decide is treated as irreversible. The classifier and the rule it applies are specified in the classifier in 02; the taxonomy here is the product's view of its output.

Three patterns in the table drive the design. The irreversible step is almost always a submission or a payment, which is why the envelope names committable acts and a spend cap rather than listing every action. The user steps cluster around factors that are the user's alone (approvals in their own bank app, biometrics, signatures, appearances), which is why the bounce is universal and per-country adapters only change what is delegated around it. The reversible work, most of the minutes in any task, needs neither check nor tap, so the plan's overhead sits exactly where the consequence sits.

3Intake and the channel-agnostic gateway

The front-door channel is not decided. Underneath it, one decision is fixed: every channel enters through a channel-agnostic intake gateway that normalises WhatsApp, the native app, SMS and iMessage, and the web into one internal request format for text, voice and attachments. The front door is a per-market launch choice, not an architecture commitment, and it stays open until a trigger fires: it is resolved when the first US or UK cohort is scoped amber. The record is DR-001; the item sits with an owner in the open items in 07.

From the user's side the gateway does four things, each the same on every channel.

  1. It identifies the user and binds the message to a task: a new request opens a task in received; a message referencing an existing task attaches to it; a message referencing a lapsed task revives it into planning. The stolen-phone journey in 1.3 depends on this.
  2. It tokenises at the edge. A PAN typed into a chat, a card photographed, an account number in a forwarded email: each is replaced by its alias before any model, worker or other component sees the message. The user sees the alias echoed back in plain words, which is the first line of the ledger.
  3. It normalises voice and attachments: a voice note becomes a transcript with the same masking; a photograph becomes a document reference plus extracted fields; the original is held only as redacted evidence.
  4. It acknowledges within the channel and says where the confirmation will arrive. A confirmation, an envelope edit and a verdict are taps on a surface that authenticates the user, whatever channel the conversation runs in; the gateway's job is to make the channel irrelevant to that.

The internal request format carries the channel and its message identifier; the resolved user; the task reference, if any; the text or transcript with aliases in place; attachment references; the market and locale; and the received timestamp that starts the planning timer. Its engineering is in the intake gateway in 02.

The amber matters to the product, not only to marketing: the channel decides how a user hands us a document, how a nudge reaches them and how quickly a confirmation is answered. The India cohort will most likely be met where Indian users already run their lives; the US and UK cohorts may not be, and the gateway lets the answer differ per market without a second build.

4The authority envelope from the user's point of view

The user grants authority once, in one tap, and can take it back at any moment. That is the whole promise of the envelope: delegation is total inside it and impossible outside it, and every irreversible act inside it is checked by a second party before it fires. The decision and the point-of-no-return test are recorded in DR-004; the capability ceiling it sits under is DR-003.

4.1Set-up at task start

The planner proposes the envelope with the plan, and the task waits in awaiting-authority until the user confirms. The proposal is least-authority by construction: what the plan needs and nothing more, drawn from the task type and the context store. The one-tap confirmation shows, in this order:

  1. The plan in plain words, step by step, each marked as ours to do, a call a person on our side will make, or something the user will do themselves.
  2. Spend cap: currency and amount for the task, and any per-step sub-cap the plan proposes.
  3. Contactable parties: the institutions, businesses or people we may contact as the user. Anyone else is outside.
  4. Committable acts: the act types we may commit the user to (book, cancel, submit an application, accept terms, make a payment under the cap, file a document, schedule).
  5. Delegated identity acts: the identity act types the user delegates in this jurisdiction (present a document, state an identity number, answer security questions from the context store, complete a KYC form). The policy layer filters this list before it is shown: an act the jurisdiction flags as bounce appears as "you will do this yourself", never as a box the user can tick. The per-jurisdiction table is the policy flags in 03.
  6. Validity window: start and expiry. A task past expiry cannot fire an irreversible step without re-authorisation.
  7. Which steps are irreversible and will be dual-controlled, and which will come back to the user's device.

Each line can be edited before confirming; an edit sends the task back to planning and a revised proposal returns. Confirming moves the task to active. A user who says nothing is nudged on the task type's schedule and the task lapses if the schedule runs out; lapsed is a system state, distinct from withdrawn, and a later message revives it.

4.2Edits mid-task: extend and narrow

The user can extend or narrow the envelope at any time from their device, and we can ask for an extension when a step would breach it. The effects are fixed.

Extension
Applies to steps not yet fired and re-runs classification for the affected steps. An extension request from us names the exact line it would change (a party, an act, an amount, a date) and arrives on the user's device as a one-tap approval; the user may approve, narrow instead, or withdraw the task.
Narrowing
Applies immediately to every step not yet fired. A step in checking is re-checked against the narrowed envelope before it fires. A step that has already fired is reported in the ledger and the outcome report; narrowing cannot unfire it, and we say so rather than imply otherwise.

The rule every step is classified against is the same everywhere in this publication. Irreversible and outside the envelope: bounce to the user's device. Irreversible and inside it: dual control. Reversible and inside it: execute, ai or human per the classifier. Anything outside the envelope that is reversible still cannot be executed by us; it becomes an extension request. Figure 1.1 draws the envelope as the user should picture it.

Where the ledger appears: after every step that used an alias, a plain-words line is written and is readable in the task view at once; the outcome report carries the full ledger; the trust story a customer reads shows the same lines. Its specification is the access ledger in 03, and its example is the ledger example in the trust story.

5The verdict system

Two loops, two jobs. The control loop decides whether a task can be shown as done; the improvement loop decides whether it was done well, and only the user can say. Keeping them apart is the decision recorded in DR-002: a step's outcome code is not ground truth of quality, and a user's verdict is not a gate on execution.

5.1The control loop, before "done" is shown

Every step closes with exactly one outcome code from a fixed vocabulary and the structured fields that code requires: ok, ok-with-variance, blocked-third-party, blocked-needs-user, blocked-outside-envelope, failed-retryable, failed-final, refused-policy, superseded. The codes drive the state machine: blocked-needs-user creates a user step; blocked-outside-envelope creates an extension request; failed-final triggers re-planning. Artefacts (redacted screenshots, transcripts, call metadata) attach for audit and dispute only. Dual control applies to irreversible steps and to nothing else. The vocabulary and its required fields are specified once, in the outcome codes in the worker console.

When every step is closed, the outcome report goes to the user and the task waits in awaiting-verdict. The report states what was done, each variance, what did not complete and why, every irreversible act that fired, and the ledger.

5.2The improvement loop, owned by the user

One tap closes every task. The user sees exactly five options, in this order and in these words:

  1. Done, satisfied
  2. Done, but rough
  3. Wrong outcome, needs redoing
  4. We couldn't complete it
  5. Withdrawn by me

lapsed is system-assigned and is never a button; the five-option ceiling holds. "Withdrawn by me" is available from the task view at any point, not only at the end: steps in flight are cancelled and an irreversible step that has already fired is reported. "Wrong outcome, needs redoing" closes the task with that verdict and opens a linked child task in planning; the parent is not reopened. "We couldn't complete it" is the user's word on a task whose report already says what blocked it.

The verdict joined to the step trace is the improvement measurement. Every product change, a new task type, a re-ordered plan, a changed script on a task card, a different nudge schedule, is judged against verdict rates per task type; nothing else is accepted as evidence that the product got better. Figure 1.2 shows both loops.

Assumptionassumption The incentive mechanics are assumed, not decided. A verdict is needed to close a task. A month in which the user gives a verdict on every task earns a small credit. "Wrong outcome, needs redoing" and "We couldn't complete it" trigger an automatic make-good. What the make-good is remains the founder's open decision, listed in 07; nothing in this publication prices it.

6Onboarding and context capture

We learn the user mostly by working for them. Onboarding exists to make the first tasks possible, not to build a profile in advance; the profile fills as tasks run, and every fact in it says where it came from, when, how sure we are, and whether the user has confirmed it. The store is a temporal knowledge graph, specified in the context store in 02 and decided in DR-016; sensitive values in it are aliases whose real values live in the vault.

6.1The interview

A short structured conversation on the user's channel, resumable, aimed at what tasks will need rather than at completeness: the people in the user's life and their relationship to the user; the institutions the user deals with; the documents that exist and where they are; and the preferences that shape every plan (the name to use with third parties, languages, the hours in which a nudge is welcome, a default spend cap the user is comfortable seeing proposed). A sensitive value given during the interview is tokenised at the edge like any other message; the interviewer sees {{pan}}, never the number. Every answer is recorded as a fact with source "interview", today's date and confirmed-by-user set, because the user said it themselves.

6.2Read-only connectors the user chooses

Connectors are optional, read-only by construction, scoped, and revocable from the user's device. They never carry a payment or an identity act; they are how documents and facts reach the context store without retyping. The set differs by market because the rails differ; the passports that carry them are in the passports in 03. The user sees each connector with its scope in plain words, the date consent was given and when it expires, and a switch to revoke it.

  • A mailbox and a calendar, read-only, so that notices, itineraries and appointments surface without forwarding.
  • India: issued documents from DigiLocker, where the user authorises us as a requester on DigiLocker's own consent screen, sees the purpose we state and may change it, and the consent carries its own expiry verified [2]. Financial information through the Account Aggregator framework, where only a regulated entity can be the information user, the aggregator holds no credentials and supports no transactions, and an assistant joins the journey only as a front end to a regulated partner verified [3]; the partner we use is recorded in 03.
  • UK: account information through an open banking provider, and identity credentials as the government's wallet rolls out; both are legal to-verifies, because whether an agent can hold a consent on a customer's behalf has not yet been researched to-verify.
  • US: bank-data aggregation and tokenised card credentials through a provider to-verify.

6.3Learning as a by-product of tasks

Every closed step writes what it learned back to the store: the pharmacy the mother now uses, the surgery's booking line, the bank's renewal reference, the fact that a hospital's booking line asks for a date of birth rather than a patient number. Each fact carries the task as its source, so a wrong fact can be traced to the step that produced it. Confidence is set when the fact is learned, falls as time passes, and rises when a later task uses the fact without contradiction. The plan confirmation shows the facts a plan relies on and a tap confirms them; a fact the user corrects is recorded as a new fact with the old one closed, never overwritten. A fact a step needs whose confidence is below the step's threshold becomes a question to the user rather than a guess, which is how data-quality problems become visible instead of silently wrong.

7What we refuse to do

The list is short and definite, and each item has a reason that follows from one of the two fatal failures or from the law of the market. A request that falls under it is refused from received or planning, before any step executes, with a reason code, and the user is told why. A single step that falls under it is not executed: the plan routes around it or the step bounces, and the report says which.

Anything outside the envelope
We cannot execute it, reversible or not: an irreversible step outside the envelope bounces to the user's device, and a reversible one becomes an extension request. The reason is fatal failure two, an irreversible act the user did not want, and the boundary is enforced by construction rather than by judgement.
Anything that requires a worker to hold a real value
A card number read aloud, a PAN typed by hand, an Aadhaar scan shown on a screen, a transcript with a number in it. The reason is fatal failure one: nobody on our side ever sees the sensitive data they are placing. Every such step runs on a surface of ours with the substitutor placing the value; where no surface of ours fits a third party's process, the step bounces to the user.
OTPs, PINs, passwords and biometrics
They are never ours; {{otp}} is not an alias and there is nothing to place. Each is a factor that belongs to the user alone, and they bounce. India's regulators draw the same line: the RBI has cautioned the public against sharing login details, KYC copies, card details, PINs, passwords or OTPs with any person or agency verified [4], and the UIDAI advises never to share an Aadhaar-linked OTP with anyone verified [5].
Tasks that need the user's credentials
We never hold a login to the user's own accounts. A shared credential would make the user liable for what follows: under the RBI's liability framework a loss caused by the customer's own negligence, such as sharing payment credentials, falls wholly on the customer verified [6]. Where an institution offers no consented access and no rail, the step is the user's.
Know-your-customer acts reserved to the institution
Where a jurisdiction reserves an act to the regulated entity's own staff, it is a policy flag, not a delegation: in India, video customer identification may be run only by the regulated entity's specially trained officials, with third parties assisting only at the customer's end verified [1]. Such steps are shown to the user as theirs at plan time, and the flag table records what we would ask a regulator to permit.
Anything the policy layer prohibits pending a regulatory answer
Some step types are prohibited in a jurisdiction until a regulator answers. Delegated spending on UPI runs under UPI Circle limits, and the NPCI's extension to software profiles admits them only in a closed group and only where each debit is initiated by explicit user action to-verify; in the US, whether a standing authorisation given to an agent counts as the consumer's authority under Regulation E is unsettled to-verify. A prohibited step closes refused-policy and the user is told the policy reference.
Illegal tasks, and impersonation without permitted delegation
We do not execute a task that is illegal in the market it runs in. We act as the user only within the identity acts they delegated and the policy layer permits, disclose where a jurisdiction requires disclosure that an agent is acting, and never act as a person who has not delegated to us, a family member included: the mother in 1.5 taps for herself. The UK's competition regulator has said a business remains responsible for what its agent does even where a third party built it verified [7]; that is the liability we accept inside the envelope and refuse to carry outside it.
Regulated advice
We do not diagnose, give legal advice or recommend investments; we find, book, prepare and follow up the professional who does, and put the facts in front of the user. The AI is the brain of the plan, not the user's doctor, lawyer or adviser, and a verdict system cannot measure advice.

A refused task is still a task: it has a state, a reason code and a record, and the refusal itself is reviewed when verdict rates for a task type suggest the line is drawn in the wrong place. Moving the line for a jurisdiction is a policy flag; moving it for the product is a decision record.
