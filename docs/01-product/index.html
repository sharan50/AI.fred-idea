1User journeys across the six verticals

Every journey has one shape. The user asks; the planner returns an ordered plan and proposes an authority envelope; the user confirms both with one tap on their bound device; steps run, each classed ai (the AI executes), human (a worker executes from a task card) or user (bounced to the user's own device); an outcome report arrives with the access ledger; the user closes the task with a verdict. Nothing executes before the tap. Ledger times are illustrative.

A step marked dual control is irreversible under the point-of-no-return test and inside the envelope: whichever party executes it, the other checks it against the envelope and the expected outcome before it fires, and on a browser step the checker sees the surface as the worker's stream shows it, every alias as dots. A user step is an act only the user can perform, or an approval without which an irreversible step cannot fire, and it goes to the bound device; a question seeks information the plan needs and is answered in the conversation; both hold the task in awaiting-user on the nudge schedule.

Two rules run through every journey. A third party that asks who is calling is answered in vault aliases: a name is not a secret, but it is the link between Client 4471 and a person, so the user's name and address are aliases, {{name}} and {{address}}, placed by the substitutor like {{dob}} or a security answer while the worker is muted; a task card carries Client 4471, the script and aliases, nothing else. And we hold no login to any account of the user's, so a step that acts on an institution runs by a passport adapter with a consented scope, by the institution's telephone line as a human step with aliases spoken, or by a bounce.

1.1Banking and wealth: renew a maturing deposit (India)

The user forwards a maturity notice from HDFC Bank to our relay and asks us to renew the deposit for a longer tenor and add a nominee.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Parties: HDFC Bank. Acts: submit an application, accept terms. Identity acts: state an identity number ({{account.hdfc}}), answer security questions. Window: until the maturity date.

  1. ai Read the notice; the gateway tokenised the account number at the edge, so the planner sees {{account.hdfc}}.
  2. ai Compare the bank's published tenors in our browser.
  3. human Call the bank's deposit line on our telephony, India having no consented rail for a renewal instruction. The substitutor speaks {{account.hdfc}}, {{dob}} and the security answers, {{security.hdfc}}, with the worker muted; the worker states the tenor and the nominee.
  4. user Approve the bank's own confirmation in its app; a bank's factor is always the user's.
  5. human, dual control. Confirm the instruction: reversing a renewal needs the bank's consent, so the AI checks tenor, amount and nominee against the envelope before the worker says yes. Closes ok with the bank's reference.

Ledger: {{account.hdfc}}, {{dob}} and {{security.hdfc}} spoken to HDFC Bank's deposit line, on our phone line; placed by the system; the worker's audio muted.

1.2Legal and administrative: contest a penalty charge notice (UK)

A parking penalty charge notice arrives with a deadline for representations; the user photographs it and the signage and asks us to contest it.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Parties: the issuing council. Acts: file a document. Identity acts: present a document, the notice, which carries {{name}} and {{address}}. Window: the deadline on the notice.

  1. ai Read the notice and the photos; extract the reference, the contravention code and the deadline.
  2. ai Check the council's grounds and its online form in our browser; draft the representation, showing each fact it relies on with its confidence.
  3. user Confirm the statement of facts; we never assert to an authority a fact the user has not confirmed.
  4. ai, dual control. File it on the council's form in our browser, the substitutor placing {{name}} and {{address}}. A filing cannot be withdrawn by our own action, so a worker views the filled form to check reference, deadline and attachments before it fires, seeing the two aliases as dots. Closes ok with the acknowledgement.
  5. ai Collect the council's decision. Closes blocked-third-party with the council's stated response window; the task waits in awaiting-third-party.
  1. 09:12{{name}} used to fill the council's representations form, in our browser. Placed by the system. No person saw it.
  2. 09:12{{address}} used to fill the council's representations form, in our browser. Placed by the system. No person saw it.
  3. 09:13A worker saw the form with {{name}} and {{address}} shown as dots.

1.3Identity and security: a stolen phone (India)

The user's phone is stolen. They reach us from a friend's laptop through the web front door with their login. The bound device, the surface that receives bounces and envelope prompts, is the stolen phone, so the login alone authorises nothing: the task cannot leave awaiting-authority until a new device is bound, and nothing irreversible fires before then. The acknowledgement says so and gives the operator's and the bank's own loss lines, so the user can block the SIM and the card at once without us.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Parties: the mobile operator, HDFC Bank. Acts: cancel (the SIM, the card), submit an application (reissue), schedule. Identity acts: answer security questions from the context store, {{dob}} among them. Window: twenty-four hours (illustrative).

  1. user Bind a replacement device. Binding is never a password alone: the user signs in on the new device through an identity rail the India passport adapter accepts, on the rail's own screen (the passports in 03), then confirms the envelope there.
  2. human, dual control. Call the operator's blocking line on our telephony, unless the user has already blocked the SIM. Every value the operator asks for, {{name}}, {{dob}}, {{address}}, the security answers, is placed by the substitutor with the worker muted. A block is a cancel only the operator can undo, so the AI checks the request against the envelope before the worker asks.
  3. human, dual control. Call the bank's card line to block {{card.primary}} the same way; we hold no login to the bank's card controls.
  4. user A replacement SIM needs the user at the operator's counter and a replacement card needs the bank's app: both bounce to the new device.
  5. user Reset the UPI PIN: a PIN is never ours, so the step bounces and the report records it.

Ledger: four aliases spoken on our phone line, the worker's audio muted each time; no person saw a value.

1.4Health and medical: a specialist appointment and the records (India)

A clinic refers the user to a specialist at Max Healthcare; the user uploads the referral and asks us to book the earliest slot, pay the fee and have the records sent across.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: the consultation fee, at the amount the user sets. Parties: Max Healthcare, the referring clinic. Acts: book, schedule, make a payment under the cap. Identity acts: answer security questions from the context store ({{dob}}). Window: two weeks (illustrative).

  1. ai Read the referral (the gateway tokenised the patient identifiers) and check the specialist's slots on the hospital's public portal in our browser.
  2. human Call the booking line. We can cancel the booking by our own action on the portal at no cost, so no dual control; the substitutor speaks {{name}} and {{dob}} with the worker muted. Closes ok-with-variance if the slot differs from the plan.
  3. user Pay the fee on their own device: the India flag for a delegated debit is bounce (section 7 gives the reason), so a UPI payment is the user's own act, under the cap. The ai dual-control form of this step is the capability ceiling, applied where a rail permits a delegated debit under the cap, as the card-tokenisation passport does in 1.6.
  4. user Sign the clinic's records-release form.
  5. human Chase the clinic for the transfer. Closes blocked-third-party with a response window.

Ledger: {{name}} and {{dob}} spoken to Max Healthcare's booking line, on our phone line, the worker's audio muted.

1.5Family affairs: a parent's repeat prescriptions (UK)

The user's mother wants her repeat prescriptions moved to a pharmacy that delivers.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Parties: the mother, her GP surgery, the pharmacy. Acts: submit an application, schedule. Identity acts: state an identity number, the mother's {{nhs-number}}, once she has delegated it. Window: one month (illustrative).

The mother is a second grantor. A dependant is onboarded as a delegator with a bound device and vault entries of their own, and their facts sit in a profile of their own, linked to the user's. The user's tap confirms the envelope without her identity acts; her tap is an extension by a second grantor, recorded with her identity, which re-runs classification for the steps it affects. The policy layer treats a family member as a separate delegator everywhere.

  1. user (the mother) Delegate {{nhs-number}} from her own bound device.
  2. human, dual control. Call the surgery to change the nominated pharmacy: undoing it needs the surgery to act, so the AI checks the pharmacy named against the envelope before the worker asks; the substitutor speaks her {{name}} and {{nhs-number}} with the worker muted.
  3. ai Create the delivery account with the pharmacy in our browser, the substitutor placing her {{name}} and {{address}}; no password is set.
  4. user (the mother) Set the password from the pharmacy's own link on her device, so we hold no credential.
  5. ai Schedule the repeat-order reminders.

The mother's profile gains two facts, her pharmacy and the surgery's line, sourced to this task; the ledger shows every use of her aliases, after her delegation.

1.6Travel: rebook a cancelled flight (US)

An airline cancels the user's flight the evening before; the cancellation is in the mailbox the user connected read-only. The user asks us to rebook within a budget they name and to move the hotel.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: the amount named. Parties: the airline, the hotel. Acts: book, cancel, make a payment under the cap. Identity acts: none; the booking reference identifies the trip and the passenger. Window: until the new departure.

  1. ai Read the cancellation and find alternatives within the cap in our browser. Two candidates differ in arrival time, so the plan asks which and waits in awaiting-user: a question, not a bounce.
  2. ai, dual control. Book the chosen flight in our browser; a non-refundable fare is irreversible, so a worker checks date, fare and booking reference against the envelope first. The payment runs over the card-tokenisation passport: a token bound to this task and its cap stands in for {{card.primary}}, so the airline never receives the card number and nothing is typed. A per-task token is not a standing authorisation, which is why the US flag for a delegated debit reads delegated on this rail and bounce off it (the policy flags in 03).
  3. human Call the airline on our telephony for the refund of the cancelled ticket. Closes ok with the refund reference; a credit offered instead closes blocked-outside-envelope, naming accept terms as the line it would breach, and an extension request goes to the user's device.
  4. ai Email the hotel through our relay to move the check-in.

Ledger: {{card.primary}} presented to the airline through the card-tokenisation passport as a token bound to this task; placed by the system; no person saw it.

2The task taxonomy

Task types are the unit at which the product is measured: verdict rates and the nudge schedule are set per task type. A task type is a vertical plus a shape of plan: where the point of no return sits, which step classes the plan needs, and what the envelope must contain.

Reversibility belongs to a step, not to a task, so Table 1.1 names the step that carries the point of no return in each type: the one dual-controlled or bounced. The test is applied as written: an act a third party must undo is irreversible even where it would undo it on request; only an undo by our own action at no cost counts, and a step the classifier cannot decide is treated as irreversible (the classifier in 02).

3Intake and the channel-agnostic gateway

The front-door channel is not decided. Underneath it, one decision is fixed: every channel enters through a channel-agnostic intake gateway that normalises WhatsApp, the native app, SMS and iMessage, and the web into one internal request format. WhatsApp is the leading candidate for India; nothing is fixed for the US or the UK; one trigger resolves both, and it fires when the first US or UK cohort is scoped amber. The record is DR-001; the item has an owner in the open items in 07.

From the user's side the gateway does four things on every channel.

  1. It identifies the user and binds the message to a task: a new request opens a task in received; a message referencing an existing task attaches to it; one referencing a lapsed task revives it into planning.
  2. It tokenises at the edge: a PAN typed, a card photographed, an account number in a forwarded email is replaced by its alias before any model, worker or component sees the message, and the ledger opens with the event: "{{pan}} received from you and stored in the vault. No person saw it."
  3. It normalises voice and attachments: a voice note becomes a masked transcript, a photograph a document reference plus extracted fields; the original is held only as redacted evidence.
  4. It acknowledges within the channel and says where the confirmation will arrive: a confirmation, an envelope edit, a bounce approval and a verdict are taps on the bound device, the authenticated device that receives bounces and envelope prompts, never the chat thread, which receives only the notification that something is waiting.

The internal request format carries the channel and its message identifier; the user as a pseudonymous reference; the task reference, if any; the text or transcript with aliases in place; attachment references; the aliases issued, for the ledger; the device binding; the consent references in force; the market and locale; and the received timestamp that starts the planning timer. Its engineering is in the intake gateway in 02.

4The authority envelope from the user's point of view

The user grants authority once, in one tap, and can take it back at any moment. Delegation is total inside the envelope and impossible outside it, and every irreversible act inside it is checked by a second party before it fires. The decision is DR-004; the capability ceiling above it is DR-003.

While a task is open the user sees the task view: its state; the timeline, with every transition and nudge recorded; the ledger lines as they land; the envelope, with its control for extending and narrowing; and the button "Withdrawn by me", present from the moment the task opens. The nudge schedule is per task type; the default is three nudges, then lapse (illustrative).

4.1Set-up at task start

The planner proposes the envelope with the plan, and the task waits in awaiting-authority until the user confirms. The proposal is least-authority by construction, drawn from the task type and the context store. The one-tap confirmation shows, in this order:

  1. The plan in plain words, each step marked as ours to do, a worker's call, or the user's own act.
  2. Spend cap: currency and amount for the task, and any per-step sub-cap the plan proposes.
  3. Contactable parties: the institutions, businesses or people we may contact as the user. Anyone else is outside.
  4. Committable acts: the act types we may commit the user to (book, cancel, submit an application, accept terms, make a payment under the cap, file a document, schedule).
  5. Delegated identity acts: the identity act types the user delegates in this jurisdiction (present a document, state an identity number, answer security questions from the context store, complete a KYC form), filtered by the policy layer before they are shown: an act the jurisdiction flags as bounce appears as "you will do this yourself", never as a box to tick (the policy flags in 03).
  6. Validity window: start and expiry. Expiry is an envelope line: an irreversible step past it closes blocked-outside-envelope and raises an extension request for a new window, with the task in awaiting-user.
  7. Which steps are dual-controlled and which come back to the user's device.

Both act lists are closed enumerations: the envelope validates every proposed act against them, the journeys use their words (a block is a cancel, a reissue an application, a name and address on a filing a document presented), and adding a type is a decision record. An edit before confirming sends the task back to planning; confirming moves it to active. A user who says nothing is nudged on the schedule and the task lapses if it runs out; lapsed is distinct from withdrawn, and a later message referencing the task revives it.

4.2Edits mid-task: extend and narrow

The user can extend or narrow the envelope at any time from their device, and we can ask for an extension when a step would breach it. The effects are fixed.

Extension
Applies to steps not yet fired and re-runs classification for them. A request from us names the exact line it would change (a party, an act, an amount, a date) and arrives on the user's device as a one-tap approval; the user may approve, narrow, or withdraw.
Narrowing
Applies immediately to every step not yet fired. A step in checking is re-checked against the narrowed envelope before it fires. A step that has already fired is reported in the ledger and the outcome report; narrowing cannot unfire it.

The rule every step is classified against is identical everywhere. Irreversible and outside the envelope: bounce to the user's device. Irreversible and inside it: dual control. Reversible and inside it: execute, ai or human per the classifier. Anything outside the envelope that is reversible still cannot be executed by us; it becomes an extension request. Figure 1.1 draws the envelope as the user should picture it.

The ledger's specification is the access ledger in 03; its example is the ledger example in the trust story.

5The verdict system

Two loops, two jobs. The control loop decides whether a task can be shown as done; the improvement loop decides whether it was done well, and only the user can say. Keeping them apart is DR-002: an outcome code is not ground truth of quality, and a verdict is not a gate on execution.

5.1The control loop, before "done" is shown

Every step closes with exactly one outcome code from a fixed vocabulary and the fields that code requires: ok, ok-with-variance, blocked-third-party, blocked-needs-user, blocked-outside-envelope, failed-retryable, failed-final, refused-policy, superseded, specified once in the outcome codes in the worker console. The codes drive the state machine: blocked-needs-user creates a user step, blocked-outside-envelope an extension request, failed-final a re-plan. Artefacts attach for audit and dispute only.

When every step is closed, the outcome report goes to the user and the task waits in awaiting-verdict. The report states what was done, each variance, what did not complete and why, every irreversible act that fired, and the ledger.

5.2The improvement loop, owned by the user

One tap closes every task. The user sees exactly five options, in this order and in these words:

  1. Done, satisfied
  2. Done, but rough
  3. Wrong outcome, needs redoing
  4. We couldn't complete it
  5. Withdrawn by me

lapsed is system-assigned and is never a button; the five-option ceiling holds. "Withdrawn by me" is available from the task view at any point: steps in flight are cancelled and an irreversible step already fired is reported. "Wrong outcome, needs redoing" closes the task with that verdict and opens a linked child task in planning; the parent is not reopened. "We couldn't complete it" is the user's word on a task whose report already says what blocked it.

The verdict joined to the step trace is the improvement measurement. Every product change, from a new task type to a changed script, is judged against verdict rates per task type; nothing else counts as evidence of improvement. Figure 1.2 shows both loops.

Assumptionassumption The incentive mechanics are assumed, not decided: a verdict is needed to close a task; a month with a verdict on every task earns a small credit; "Wrong outcome, needs redoing" and "We couldn't complete it" trigger an automatic make-good. What the make-good is remains the founder's open decision in 07; nothing here prices it.

6Onboarding and context capture

We learn the user mostly by working for them. Onboarding makes the first tasks possible rather than building a profile in advance; the profile fills as tasks run, and every fact in it says where it came from, when, how sure we are, and whether the user confirmed it. The store is a temporal knowledge graph (the context store in 02, DR-016); sensitive values in it are aliases.

6.1The interview

A short structured conversation on the user's channel, resumable, aimed at what tasks will need rather than at completeness: the people in the user's life; the institutions they deal with; the documents that exist and where they are; the preferences that shape every plan (the name to use with third parties, languages, nudge hours, a default spend cap). A sensitive value given in the interview is tokenised at the edge like any message. Every answer is a fact with source "interview", today's date and confirmed-by-user set.

6.2Read-only connectors the user chooses

Connectors are optional, read-only by construction, scoped, and revocable from the user's device; they never carry a payment or an identity act. The set differs by market (the passports in 03). The user sees each connector's scope in plain words, the consent date and expiry, and a switch to revoke it.

  • A mailbox and a calendar, read-only, so that notices, itineraries and appointments surface without forwarding.
  • India: issued documents from DigiLocker, where the user authorises us as a requester on DigiLocker's own consent screen, sees and may change the purpose we state, and the consent carries its own expiry verified [1]; financial information through the Account Aggregator framework, where only a regulated entity can be the information user and the aggregator holds no credentials and supports no transactions, so an assistant joins the journey only as a front end to a regulated partner verified [2], to be named in 03 to-verify.
  • UK: account information through an open banking provider, and identity credentials as the government's wallet rolls out; whether an agent can hold a consent for a customer is not yet researched to-verify.
  • US: bank-data aggregation and tokenised card credentials through a provider to-verify.

6.3Learning as a by-product of tasks

Every closed step writes what it learned back to the store: the pharmacy the mother now uses, the surgery's line, the bank's renewal reference, each fact carrying the task as its source so a wrong one can be traced to the step that produced it. Confidence is set at learning, falls with time, and rises when a later task uses the fact without contradiction. The plan confirmation shows the facts a plan relies on and a tap confirms them; a corrected fact is a new fact with the old one closed; a fact below a step's threshold becomes a question, not a guess.

7What we refuse to do

Each item follows from one of the two fatal failures or from the law of the market. A request under it is refused from received or planning, before any step executes, with the item's reason code, and the user is told why; a single step under it is routed around or bounced.

Anything outside the envelope refused-outside-envelope
An irreversible step outside the envelope bounces to the user's device; a reversible one becomes an extension request. The reason is fatal failure two, an irreversible act the user did not want.
Anything that requires a worker to hold a real value refused-real-value
A card number read aloud, a PAN typed by hand, an Aadhaar scan on a screen. The reason is fatal failure one: nobody on our side sees the data they are placing. Where no surface of ours fits a third party's process, the step bounces.
OTPs, PINs, passwords and biometrics refused-user-factor
They are never ours; {{otp}} is not an alias and there is nothing to place. Each is the user's own factor, and it bounces. India's regulators draw the same line: the RBI has cautioned the public against sharing login details, KYC copies, card details, PINs, passwords or OTPs with any person or agency verified [3], and the UIDAI advises never to share an Aadhaar-linked OTP with anyone verified [4].
Tasks that need the user's credentials refused-credential
We never hold a login to the user's own accounts; a shared credential would make the user liable, since under the RBI's liability framework a loss caused by the customer's own negligence, such as sharing payment credentials, falls wholly on the customer verified [5]. Where an institution offers no consented access and no rail, the step is the user's.
Know-your-customer acts reserved to the institution refused-reserved-kyc
Where a jurisdiction reserves an act to the regulated entity's own staff, it is a policy flag, not a delegation: in India, video customer identification may be run only by the regulated entity's specially trained officials, with third parties assisting only at the customer's end verified [6]. Such steps are shown as the user's at plan time.
Anything the policy layer prohibits pending a regulatory answer refused-policy-pending
Delegated spending on UPI runs under UPI Circle limits, and the NPCI admits software profiles only in a closed group and only where each debit is initiated by explicit user action to-verify; in the US, whether a standing authorisation given to an agent counts as the consumer's authority under Regulation E is unsettled to-verify. A prohibited step closes refused-policy and the user is told the policy reference.
Illegal tasks, and impersonation without permitted delegation refused-illegal
We do not execute a task that is illegal in the market it runs in. We act as the user only within the identity acts they delegated and the policy layer permits, disclose where a jurisdiction requires it, and never act as a person who has not delegated to us: the mother in 1.5 taps for herself. The UK's competition regulator has said a business remains responsible for what its agent does even where a third party built it verified [7]; that is the liability we accept inside the envelope and refuse outside it.
Regulated advice refused-advice
We do not diagnose, give legal advice or recommend investments; we find, book, prepare and follow up the professional who does.

A refused task is still a task, with a state, a reason code and a record. Refusals are measured by counts and reason codes per task type, since a refused task never reaches a verdict, and the line is reviewed when those counts suggest it is drawn in the wrong place; moving it for a jurisdiction is a policy flag, for the product a decision record.
