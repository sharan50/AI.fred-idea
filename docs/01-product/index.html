1User journeys across the six verticals

Every journey has one shape, whatever the vertical. The user asks; the planner returns an ordered plan and proposes an authority envelope; the user confirms both with one tap; steps run, each classed ai (the AI executes), human (a worker executes from a task card) or user (the step bounces to the user's own device); an outcome report arrives with the access ledger attached; the user closes the task with a verdict. Nothing executes before the tap, and nothing irreversible fires outside the envelope. The six journeys below are chosen to show where the envelope and the ledger appear, and where a step must come back to the user. Institutions are named to make the examples concrete; the times in the ledger lines are illustrative.

Two conventions run through the plans. A step marked dual control is irreversible under the point-of-no-return test and inside the envelope: whichever party executes it, the other checks it against the envelope and the expected outcome before it fires. A step marked user is the user's own act (an approval in their bank app, an OTP, a signature, an appearance in person); the task waits in awaiting-user until they act, and nudges them if they go quiet.

1.1Banking and wealth: renew a maturing deposit (India)

The user asks us to renew a fixed deposit that matures next week at HDFC Bank, for a longer tenor, and to add a nominee. The maturity notice arrived by email; they forward it to our relay.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil (the principal stays at the bank). Contactable parties: HDFC Bank. Committable acts: submit an application, accept terms. Delegated identity acts: state {{pan}} and {{account.hdfc}} in the bank's own form. Validity window: until the maturity date.

  1. ai Read the forwarded notice. The gateway tokenised the account number at the edge, so the planner sees {{account.hdfc}} and never the number. Closes ok.
  2. ai Compare the bank's published tenors and rates in our managed browser; confirm the tenor the user chose at confirmation. Closes ok.
  3. ai Fill the renewal and nomination form in our managed browser. The substitutor places {{pan}} and {{account.hdfc}} at the last boundary; the worker supervising the session sees dots. Closes ok.
  4. user The bank's app asks the account holder to approve. The step bounces; the task waits in awaiting-user; the user taps in their own app and the task resumes.
  5. ai, dual control. Submit the renewal. Reversing it would need the bank's consent, so the step is irreversible; it is inside the envelope, so a worker checks tenor, amount and nominee against the envelope before it fires. Closes ok with the bank's reference.
  6. Outcome report, ledger, verdict.

Had the bank demanded a fresh video KYC, that step would have been user from the start: the RBI's directions reserve video customer identification to the regulated entity's own trained officials, with third parties assisting only at the customer's end verified [1]. The ledger the user sees at the end reads like this:

  1. 11:04{{account.hdfc}} used to identify the deposit on HDFC Bank's renewal form, in our browser. Placed by the system. No person saw it.
  2. 11:05{{pan}} used to fill HDFC Bank's renewal form, in our browser. Placed by the system. No person saw it.
  3. 11:06A worker saw the form with {{pan}} shown as dots.

1.2Legal and administrative: contest a penalty charge notice (UK)

A parking penalty charge notice arrives with a deadline for representations. The user photographs it, sends it with two photos of the signage, and asks us to contest it.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Contactable parties: the issuing council. Committable acts: file a document. Delegated identity acts: state the user's name and address as printed on the notice. Validity window: the representation deadline on the notice.

  1. ai Read the notice and the photos; extract the reference, the contravention code and the deadline. Closes ok.
  2. ai Check the council's published grounds and route for representations in our managed browser. Closes ok.
  3. ai Draft the representation from the facts the user gave and the photos. Every fact it relies on is shown with its confidence from the context store.
  4. user Confirm the statement of facts. We never assert a fact to an authority on the user's behalf that the user has not confirmed. This is a question, so the task waits in awaiting-user.
  5. ai, dual control. File the representation. A filing cannot be withdrawn by our own action, so it is irreversible; a worker checks the reference, the deadline and the attachments before it fires. Closes ok with the acknowledgement.
  6. The council's decision is a third party's act. The task moves to awaiting-third-party with the council's stated response window as its timer, and escalates if the window expires.

No vault alias was used, and the ledger says so in one line. The ledger exists for every task, including the ones where it records that nothing sensitive was placed.

1.3Identity and security: a stolen phone (India)

The user's phone is stolen. They reach us from a friend's laptop through the web front door; the gateway binds the request to their account, and the loss of the usual channel changes nothing about the task.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Contactable parties: the mobile operator, HDFC Bank. Committable acts: block a SIM, block a card, request reissue, schedule. Delegated identity acts: state {{dob}} and answer the operator's security questions from the context store. Validity window: the next twenty-four hours (illustrative).

  1. ai Plan the loss response: block the SIM, block the cards, sign out of the sessions the user names.
  2. human, dual control. Call the operator's blocking line on our telephony. The worker reads the script from the task card, which names the customer as Client 4471; the substitutor speaks {{dob}} on the line while the worker's audio is muted. A SIM block is irreversible under the test, because reissue needs the operator's consent, so the AI checks the request against the envelope before the worker asks for it. Closes ok.
  3. ai, dual control. Block {{card.primary}} through the bank's card-control page in our managed browser; a worker checks before it fires. Closes ok.
  4. user A replacement SIM needs the user in person with their documents, and a replacement card needs the bank's app. Both bounce. We book the store slot, which is reversible, as an ai step.
  5. Resetting the UPI PIN is refused within the plan and the report says so: a PIN is never ours to hold or set.

Ledger lines: {{dob}} spoken to the operator's blocking line, on our phone line, placed by the system, the worker's audio muted; {{card.primary}} used to identify the card on HDFC Bank's card-control page, in our browser, placed by the system, no person saw it.

1.4Health and medical: a specialist appointment and the records (India)

A clinic has referred the user to a specialist at Max Healthcare. The user uploads the referral letter and asks us to book the earliest slot, pay the consultation fee and have their records sent across.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: the consultation fee, at the amount the user sets. Contactable parties: Max Healthcare, the referring clinic. Committable acts: book, schedule, make a payment under the cap. Delegated identity acts: state {{dob}} on the booking line. Validity window: two weeks (illustrative).

  1. ai Read the referral letter; the gateway tokenised the patient identifiers at the edge. Closes ok.
  2. ai Check the specialist's published slots on the hospital's portal in our managed browser. Closes ok.
  3. human Call the booking line. Booking is reversible (cancellable at no cost by our own action), so there is no dual control. The substitutor speaks {{dob}} with the worker's audio muted. Closes ok-with-variance if the slot offered differs from the one planned; the variance note goes to the user.
  4. ai, dual control. Pay the fee through the payment passport. A non-refundable prepayment fails the point-of-no-return test, so a worker checks amount and payee against the cap before it fires. Where the rail itself demands the account holder's approval, that approval is a user step on their own device; we never enter it for them.
  5. user The clinic's records-release form needs the patient's signature. It bounces.
  6. human Chase the clinic for the transfer. Closes blocked-third-party with a response window; the task waits in awaiting-third-party.

The ledger carries the line the trust story uses: {{dob}} spoken to Max Healthcare's booking line, on our phone line, placed by the system, the worker's audio muted.

1.5Family affairs: a parent's repeat prescriptions (UK)

The user's mother wants her repeat prescriptions moved to a pharmacy that delivers. The user asks us to arrange it.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: nil. Contactable parties: the mother, her GP surgery, the pharmacy. Committable acts: submit an application, schedule. Delegated identity acts: state the mother's {{nhs-number}} on the surgery's line, only once the mother has delegated it herself. Validity window: one month (illustrative).

  1. ai Plan. The mother is a separate person, so her identity acts are hers to delegate: the plan opens with a one-tap delegation on her own device. Until she taps, no identity act of hers is in the envelope.
  2. user (the mother) Delegate. The policy layer treats a family member as a separate delegator in every jurisdiction.
  3. ai Check the pharmacy's delivery area and sign-up route in our managed browser. Closes ok.
  4. human Call the surgery to change the nominated pharmacy. The change can be reversed by the same call at no cost, so it is reversible and needs no dual control. The substitutor speaks {{nhs-number}} with the worker's audio muted. Closes ok.
  5. ai Create the delivery account with the pharmacy using the address in the context store, and schedule the repeat-order reminders. Closes ok.

The context store gains two facts from this task, the mother's pharmacy and the surgery's line, each with this task as its source, today's date, and a confidence set by how it was learned. The ledger shows the single use of {{nhs-number}} and states that the mother's delegation preceded it.

1.6Travel: rebook a cancelled flight (US)

An airline cancels the user's flight the evening before. The cancellation email is in the mailbox the user connected read-only; the user asks us to rebook within a budget they name and to move the hotel.

Envelope proposed. Spend cap: the amount the user named. Contactable parties: the airline, the hotel. Committable acts: book, cancel, make a payment under the cap. Delegated identity acts: none; the booking reference identifies the trip. Validity window: until the new departure.

  1. ai Read the cancellation and the original itinerary. Closes ok.
  2. ai Find alternatives within the cap in our managed browser. Two candidates differ in arrival time; the plan asks the user which, and waits in awaiting-user. This is a question, not a bounce.
  3. ai, dual control. Book the chosen flight. A non-refundable fare is irreversible, so a worker checks the date, the passenger name and the fare against the envelope before it fires; the substitutor places {{card.primary}} through the card passport and the worker sees dots. Closes ok.
  4. human Call the airline on our telephony to claim the refund of the cancelled ticket. Closes ok-with-variance if a credit is offered instead of a refund; accepting a credit commits the user, so the choice goes back to them as a question.
  5. ai Email the hotel through our relay to move the check-in. Reversible. Closes ok.

Ledger lines: {{card.primary}} used to pay for the new ticket on the airline's checkout page, in our browser, placed by the system, no person saw it; a worker saw the checkout page with {{card.primary}} shown as dots.

2The task taxonomy

Task types are the unit at which the product is measured. Verdict rates are computed per task type; the nudge schedule is set per task type; the L2 pod that owns the specialist knowledge is chosen by vertical. A task type is therefore a vertical plus a shape of plan, and the shape is defined by three things: where the point of no return sits, which step classes the plan typically needs, and what the envelope must contain.

Reversibility belongs to a step, not to a task. Table 1.1 names the step that carries the point of no return in each type, because that is the step that will be dual-controlled or bounced. A step whose reversibility the classifier cannot decide is treated as irreversible. The classifier and the rule it applies are specified in the classifier in 02; the taxonomy here is the product's view of its output.

Three patterns in the table drive the design. First, the irreversible step in a high-consequence task is almost always a submission or a payment, which is why the envelope names committable acts and a spend cap rather than listing every action. Second, the user steps cluster around factors that are the user's alone (approvals in their own bank app, biometrics, signatures, appearances), which is why the bounce is universal and per-country adapters only change what is delegated around it. Third, the reversible work, which is most of the minutes in any task, needs neither check nor tap, so the plan's overhead sits exactly where the consequence sits.

3Intake and the channel-agnostic gateway

The front-door channel is not decided. Underneath it, one decision is fixed: every channel enters through a channel-agnostic intake gateway that normalises WhatsApp, the native app, SMS and iMessage, and the web into one internal request format for text, voice and attachments. The front door is a per-market launch choice, not an architecture commitment, and it stays open until a trigger fires: it is resolved when the first US or UK cohort is scoped amber. The record is DR-001; the item sits with an owner in the open items in 07.

From the user's side the gateway does four things, and every one of them is the same on every channel.

  1. It identifies the user and binds the message to a task: a new request opens a task in received; a message that references an existing task attaches to it, and a message referencing a lapsed task revives it into planning. The stolen-phone journey in 1.3 depends on this: the user reached us from a browser and the task did not care.
  2. It tokenises at the edge. A PAN typed into a chat, a card photographed, an account number in a forwarded email: each is replaced by its alias before any model, worker or other component sees the message. The user sees the alias echoed back in plain words, which is the first line of the ledger.
  3. It normalises voice and attachments: a voice note becomes a transcript with the same masking; a photograph becomes a document reference plus extracted fields; the original is held only as redacted evidence.
  4. It acknowledges within the channel and tells the user where the confirmation will arrive. A confirmation, an envelope edit and a verdict are taps on a surface that authenticates the user, whatever channel the conversation runs in; the gateway's job is to make the channel irrelevant to that.

The internal request format carries: the channel and its message identifier; the resolved user; the task reference, if any; the text or transcript with aliases in place; attachment references; the market and locale; and the received timestamp that starts the planning timer. The engineering of the gateway, including how the edge tokenisation is isolated from the rest of the pipeline, is in the intake gateway in 02.

Why the amber matters to the product rather than only to marketing: the channel decides how a user hands us a document, how a nudge reaches them, and how quickly a confirmation can be answered. The India cohort will most likely be met where Indian users already run their lives; the US and UK cohorts may not be. The gateway means the answer can differ per market without a second build.

4The authority envelope from the user's point of view

The user grants authority once, in one tap, and can take it back at any moment. That is the whole promise of the envelope: delegation is total inside it and impossible outside it, and every irreversible act inside it is checked by a second party before it fires. The decision and the point-of-no-return test are recorded in DR-004; the capability ceiling it sits under is DR-003.

4.1Set-up at task start

The planner proposes the envelope together with the plan, and the task waits in awaiting-authority until the user confirms. The proposal is least-authority by construction: it contains what the plan needs and nothing more, drawn from the task type and the context store. The one-tap confirmation shows, in this order:

  1. The plan in plain words, step by step, each marked as ours to do, a call a person on our side will make, or something the user will do themselves.
  2. Spend cap: currency and amount for the task, and any per-step sub-cap the plan proposes.
  3. Contactable parties: the institutions, businesses or people we may contact as the user. Anyone else is outside.
  4. Committable acts: the act types we may commit the user to (book, cancel, submit an application, accept terms, make a payment under the cap, file a document, schedule).
  5. Delegated identity acts: the identity act types the user delegates in this jurisdiction (present a document, state an identity number, answer security questions from the context store, complete a KYC form). The policy layer filters this list before it is shown: an act the jurisdiction flags as bounce appears as "you will do this yourself", never as a box the user can tick. The per-jurisdiction table is the policy flags in 03.
  6. Validity window: start and expiry. A task past expiry cannot fire an irreversible step without re-authorisation.
  7. Which steps are irreversible and will be dual-controlled, and which will come back to the user's device.

Each line can be edited before confirming; an edit sends the task back to planning and a revised proposal returns. Confirming moves the task to active. A user who says nothing is nudged on the task type's schedule and the task lapses if the schedule runs out; lapsed is a system state, distinct from withdrawn, and a later message revives it.

4.2Edits mid-task: extend and narrow

The user can extend or narrow the envelope at any time from their device, and we can ask for an extension when a step would breach it. The effects are fixed.

Extension
Applies to steps not yet fired and re-runs classification for the affected steps. An extension request from us names the exact line it would change (a party, an act, an amount, a date) and arrives on the user's device as a one-tap approval; the user may approve, narrow instead, or withdraw the task.
Narrowing
Applies immediately to every step not yet fired. A step in checking is re-checked against the narrowed envelope before it fires. A step that has already fired is reported in the ledger and the outcome report; narrowing cannot unfire it, and we say so rather than imply otherwise.

The rule that every step is classified against is the same everywhere in this publication. Irreversible and outside the envelope: bounce to the user's device. Irreversible and inside it: dual control. Reversible and inside it: execute, ai or human per the classifier. Anything outside the envelope that is reversible still cannot be executed by us; it becomes an extension request. Figure 1.1 draws the envelope as the user should picture it.

Where the ledger appears in all this: after every step that used an alias, a plain-words line is written and is readable in the task view at once; the outcome report carries the full ledger; and the trust story a customer reads shows the same lines. Its specification is the access ledger in 03, and its example is the ledger example in the trust story.

5The verdict system

Two loops, two jobs. The control loop decides whether a task can be shown as done; the improvement loop decides whether it was done well, and only the user can say. Keeping them apart is the decision recorded in DR-002: a step's outcome code is not ground truth of quality, and a user's verdict is not a gate on execution.

5.1The control loop, before "done" is shown

Every step closes with exactly one outcome code from a fixed vocabulary and the structured fields that code requires: ok, ok-with-variance, blocked-third-party, blocked-needs-user, blocked-outside-envelope, failed-retryable, failed-final, refused-policy, superseded. The codes drive the state machine: a blocked-needs-user creates a user step; a blocked-outside-envelope creates an extension request; a failed-final triggers re-planning. Artefacts (redacted screenshots, transcripts, call metadata) attach for audit and dispute only. Dual control applies to irreversible steps and to nothing else. The vocabulary and its required fields are specified once, in the outcome codes in the worker console.

When every step is closed, the outcome report goes to the user and the task waits in awaiting-verdict. The report states what was done, each variance, what did not complete and why, every irreversible act that fired, and the ledger.

5.2The improvement loop, owned by the user

One tap closes every task. The user sees exactly five options, in this order and in these words:

  1. Done, satisfied
  2. Done, but rough
  3. Wrong outcome, needs redoing
  4. We couldn't complete it
  5. Withdrawn by me

lapsed is system-assigned and is never a button; the five-option ceiling holds. "Withdrawn by me" is available from the task view at any point, not only at the end: steps in flight are cancelled and an irreversible step that has already fired is reported. "Wrong outcome, needs redoing" closes the task with that verdict and opens a linked child task in planning; the parent is not reopened. "We couldn't complete it" is the user's word on a task whose report already says what blocked it.

The verdict joined to the step trace is the improvement measurement. Every product change, a new task type, a re-ordered plan, a changed script on a task card, a different nudge schedule, is judged against verdict rates per task type. Nothing else is accepted as evidence that the product got better. Figure 1.2 shows both loops.

Assumptionassumption The incentive mechanics are assumed, not decided. A verdict is needed to close a task. A month in which the user gives a verdict on every task earns a small credit. "Wrong outcome, needs redoing" and "We couldn't complete it" trigger an automatic make-good. What the make-good is remains the founder's open decision, listed in 07; nothing in this publication prices it.

6Onboarding and context capture

We learn the user mostly by working for them. Onboarding exists to make the first tasks possible, not to build a profile in advance; the profile fills as tasks run, and every fact in it says where it came from, when, how sure we are, and whether the user has confirmed it. The store is a temporal knowledge graph, specified in the context store in 02 and decided in DR-016; sensitive values in it are aliases whose real values live in the vault.

6.1The interview

A short structured conversation on the user's channel, resumable, aimed at what tasks will need rather than at completeness: the people in the user's life and their relationship to the user; the institutions the user deals with (bank, insurer, clinic, employer, landlord, school); the documents that exist and where they are; preferences that shape every plan (name to use with third parties, languages, hours in which a nudge is welcome, a default spend cap the user is comfortable proposing). A sensitive value the user gives during the interview is tokenised at the edge like any other message; the interviewer sees {{pan}}, never the number. Every answer is recorded as a fact with source "interview", today's date, and confirmed-by-user set, because the user said it themselves.

6.2Read-only connectors the user chooses

Connectors are optional, read-only by construction, scoped, and revocable from the user's device. They never carry a payment or an identity act; they are how documents and facts reach the context store without the user retyping them. The set differs by market because the rails differ; the passports that carry them are specified in the passports in 03. What the user sees is the same in every market: a list of connectors, each with its scope in plain words, the date consent was given and when it expires, and a switch to revoke it.

  • A mailbox and a calendar, read-only, so that notices, itineraries and appointments surface without forwarding.
  • India: issued documents from DigiLocker, where the user authorises us as a requester on DigiLocker's own consent screen, sees the purpose we state and may change it, and the consent carries its own expiry verified [2]. Financial information through the Account Aggregator framework, where only a regulated entity can be the information user, the aggregator holds no credentials and supports no transactions, and an assistant joins the journey only as a front end to a regulated partner verified [3]; the partner we use is recorded in 03.
  • UK: account information through an open banking provider, and identity credentials as the government's wallet rolls out; both are recorded as legal to-verifies, because the question of whether a consent can be held on a customer's behalf by an agent has not yet been researched to-verify.
  • US: bank-data aggregation and tokenised card credentials through a provider to-verify.

6.3Learning as a by-product of tasks

Every closed step writes what it learned back to the store: the pharmacy the mother now uses, the surgery's booking line, the bank's renewal reference, the fact that a hospital's booking line asks for a date of birth rather than a patient number. Each fact carries the task as its source, so a wrong fact can be traced to the step that produced it. Facts decay: a confidence set when the fact was learned falls as time passes and rises when a later task uses it without contradiction. The plan confirmation shows the user the facts a plan relies on, and a tap confirms them; a fact the user corrects is recorded as a new fact with the old one closed, never overwritten. A fact needed by a step whose confidence is below the step's threshold becomes a question to the user rather than a guess, which is how data-quality problems become visible instead of silently wrong.

7What we refuse to do

The list is short and definite, and each item has a reason that follows from one of the two fatal failures or from the law of the market. A request that falls under the list is refused from received or planning, before any step executes, with a reason code, and the user is told why. A single step that falls under it is not executed: the plan routes around it or the step bounces, and the report says which.

Anything outside the envelope
We cannot execute it, reversible or not. An irreversible step outside the envelope bounces to the user's device; a reversible one becomes an extension request. The reason is fatal failure two: an irreversible act the user did not want is the failure the envelope exists to make impossible, so the boundary is enforced by construction rather than by judgement.
Anything that requires a worker to hold a real value
A card number read aloud, a PAN typed by hand, an Aadhaar scan shown on a screen, a transcript with a number in it. The reason is fatal failure one: nobody on our side ever sees the sensitive data they are placing. Every such step runs on a surface of ours with the substitutor placing the value; where no such surface exists for a third party's process, the step bounces to the user.
OTPs, PINs, passwords and biometrics
They are never ours; {{otp}} is not an alias and there is nothing to place. Each is a factor that belongs to the user alone, and they bounce. India's regulators draw the same line: the RBI has cautioned the public against sharing login details, KYC copies, card details, PINs, passwords or OTPs with any person or agency verified [4], and the UIDAI advises never to share an Aadhaar-linked OTP with anyone verified [5].
Tasks that need the user's credentials
We never hold a login to the user's own accounts. A shared credential would make the user liable for what follows: under the RBI's liability framework a loss caused by the customer's own negligence, such as sharing payment credentials, falls wholly on the customer verified [6]. Where an institution offers no consented access and no rail, the step is the user's.
Know-your-customer acts reserved to the institution
Where a jurisdiction reserves an act to the regulated entity's own staff, it is a policy flag, not a delegation. In India, video customer identification may be run only by the regulated entity's specially trained officials, with third parties assisting only at the customer's end verified [1]. Such steps are shown to the user as theirs at plan time, and the flag table records what we would ask a regulator to permit.
Anything the policy layer prohibits pending a regulatory answer
Some step types are neither delegated nor bounced but prohibited in a jurisdiction until a regulator answers. Delegated spending on UPI, for instance, runs under UPI Circle limits, and the NPCI's extension to software profiles admits them only in a closed group and only where each debit is initiated by explicit user action to-verify; in the US, whether a standing authorisation given to an agent counts as the consumer's authority under Regulation E is unsettled to-verify. A prohibited step closes refused-policy and the user is told the policy reference.
Illegal tasks, and impersonation without permitted delegation
We do not execute a task that is illegal in the market it runs in. We act as the user only within the identity acts they delegated and the policy layer permits, and where a jurisdiction requires disclosure that an agent is acting, we disclose. We never act as a person who has not delegated to us, a family member included: the mother in 1.5 taps for herself. The UK's competition regulator has said a business remains responsible for what its agent does even where a third party built it verified [7], which is the liability we accept inside the envelope and refuse to take on outside it.
Regulated advice
We do not diagnose, give legal advice or recommend investments. We find, book, prepare and follow up the professional who does, and we put the facts in front of the user. The reason is scope: the AI is the brain of the plan, not the user's doctor, lawyer or adviser, and a verdict system cannot measure advice.
A worker's own tools
Not a refusal the user will see, but the one that makes the others hold: no step runs on a worker's own phone, browser or mailbox. The execution surfaces are ours, and a task that could only be done otherwise is not done.

A refused task is still a task: it has a state, a reason code and a record, and the refusal itself is reviewed when verdict rates for a task type suggest the line is drawn in the wrong place. Moving the line for a jurisdiction is a policy flag; moving it for the product is a decision record.
