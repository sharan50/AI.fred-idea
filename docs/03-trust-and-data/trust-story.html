On this page

1The promise

How it is built is in 03 Trust and data; this page is what we would say to you. You hand us a task that matters: opening a bank account, chasing a hospital appointment, renewing a passport, answering a tax notice. We get it done. An AI plans the work and does what it can. Where a person is needed, one of our staff follows a short, exact instruction. And one thing makes this safe to trust: nobody who works for us ever sees your numbers.

Your PAN, your Aadhaar, your card and your date of birth live in a locked vault run by a specialist company, not on our desks. Our staff and our AI work with labels instead, like {{pan}}. When a form needs the real number, a machine with no screen types it in, and the person watching sees dots. On a phone call, the machine speaks or keys the number while the person's line is muted both ways. Every time that happens, a line is written in your ledger, in words you can read.

Before we start, you set the limits: how much we may spend, who we may contact, what we may commit you to. Inside them we act. Outside them, nothing happens without your tap and passcode on your own phone, and a lost phone is suspended by one login. Anything that cannot be undone, a payment or a submitted application, gets a second check before it fires.

2What your ledger looks like

Every use of your numbers is one line, written by the machine that used it, not by a person. A bank-account task might read:

  1. 14:02{{pan}} received from your message and stored in the vault. No person saw it.
  2. 14:32{{pan}} used to fill HDFC Bank's account-opening form, in our browser. Placed by the system. No person saw it.
  3. 14:32A worker saw the form with {{pan}} shown as dots.
  4. 14:40{{dob}} spoken to Max Healthcare's booking line, on our phone line. Placed by the system. The worker's audio was muted.

Three things are promised. Every use is written down; the machine cannot use a number without writing the line first. "No person saw it" is not a rule staff follow; it is what their screen could show. And you can read your ledger at any time. The label in brackets names your number without holding it; the times are illustrative.

3What we never do

  • We never ask you for a one-time code, a PIN or a bank password; those come to your own phone and you enter them yourself, and where one must go into a form on our screen, the worker sees dots. A login to a non-bank website you choose to give us goes into a form that posts straight to the vault, and we only ever see a placeholder.
  • No person who works for us, at any level, sees your numbers, documents or card: every screen and every line they work with is built so that only placeholders can appear on it, and if a failure ever put a value where a person could see it, your ledger tells you.
  • Nothing that cannot be undone happens outside the limits you set. If a task needs more, we ask, and it waits for your tap.
  • If you go quiet, we remind you, then mark the task lapsed and say so; one message revives it. We never quietly forget it.
  • You close every task with one tap: done, satisfied; done, but rough; wrong outcome, needs redoing; we couldn't complete it; withdrawn by me. That answer is how we improve.
