On this page

1The promise

You hand us a task that matters: opening a bank account, chasing a hospital appointment, renewing a passport, answering a tax notice, moving a parent's pension. We get it done. An AI plans the work and does what it can. Where a person is needed, one of our staff follows a short, exact instruction. And one thing makes this safe to trust: nobody who works for us ever sees your numbers.

Your PAN, your Aadhaar, your card, your account number, your date of birth live in a locked vault run by a specialist company, not on our desks. Our staff and our AI work with labels instead, like {{pan}}. When a form needs the real number, a machine with no screen types it in, and the person watching sees dots. On a phone call, the machine speaks or keys the number while the person's line is muted. Every time that happens, a line is written in your ledger, in words you can read.

Before we start a task, you set its limits: how much we may spend, who we may contact, what we may commit you to. Inside those limits we act. Outside them, nothing happens without a tap from you on your own phone. Anything that cannot be undone, a payment, a submitted application, a signed form, gets a second check before it fires, whoever is doing it.

2What your ledger looks like

Every task has a ledger. Every use of one of your numbers is one line in it, written by the machine that used it, not by a person. This is what a bank-account task might look like:

  1. 14:02{{pan}} received from your message and stored in the vault. No person saw it.
  2. 14:32{{pan}} used to fill HDFC Bank's account-opening form, in our browser. Placed by the system. No person saw it.
  3. 14:32A worker saw the form with {{pan}} shown as dots.
  4. 14:33HDFC Bank's confirmation page displayed {{pan}}. Masked before any person saw it.
  5. 14:40{{dob}} spoken to Max Healthcare's booking line, on our phone line. Placed by the system. The worker's audio was muted.
  6. 15:22{{account.new-hdfc}} received in HDFC Bank's reply and stored in the vault. No person saw it.

The ledger promises three things. Every use of a number is written down; there is no way for the machine to use one without writing the line first. "No person saw it" is not a rule we ask staff to follow; it is what the screen they were looking at was able to show. And you can read your ledger at any time, for any task, for as long as you are with us.

The label in curly brackets is how we refer to your number without holding it. The same label means something only for you; on a colleague's screen it is a different label for a different person. The names of the institutions are real, the times are illustrative.

3What we never do

  • We never ask you for a one-time code, a PIN or a password, and we never enter one. Those steps come to your own phone for you to do.
  • No person who works for us sees your numbers, your documents or your card. Not a junior, not a manager, not an engineer. There is no screen on which they could.
  • Nothing that cannot be undone happens outside the limits you set. If a task needs more, we ask, and it waits for your tap.
  • If you go quiet, we remind you, and then we pause the task and tell you so. We never quietly forget it.
  • You close every task yourself, with one tap: done and satisfied; done but rough; wrong outcome, needs redoing; we couldn't complete it; or withdrawn by you. Your answer is how we get better.

How all of this is built is in the engineering pages of this publication, starting with 03 Trust and data. This page is the part we would say to you.
