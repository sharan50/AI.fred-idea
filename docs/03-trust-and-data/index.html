1The alias model

A real value crosses five boundaries between the user's hands and an institution's, and no person on our side stands inside any of them. This section follows a value along that path, boundary by boundary, and states what may exist on each side. Figure 3.1 draws the zones; section 2 follows one placement; sections 3 and 4 specify the two boundaries most likely to leak. The named paths by which a person could see a value, and their closure, are gathered in the threat model. The customer's one-page version is the trust story.

1.1Alias types, classes and their policy

An alias is a typed name, never a disguised value. It is written {{type}} or {{type.qualifier}}, with an optional partial suffix, .last4 or .masked, that only the vault appends: lower-case letters, digits and hyphens in each segment; the type names the kind of value, the qualifier tells instances apart ({{card.primary}}, {{account.hdfc}}, {{address.home}}) and the suffix names a vault-issued partial ({{card.primary.last4}}, {{aadhaar.last4}}, {{doc.aadhaar.masked}}). An alias is unique to one user and stable across tasks, so the same string on two users' task cards names two values and carries no character of either. The vault maps it to the current version of the value: a reissued card becomes a new version behind the same alias, and the ledger records that as custody, not as a use. The vault is a bought tokenisation service of the Skyflow, VGS and Basis Theory class, hosted in the region the user's market requires; DR-008 fixes buy over build and the vendor is open in 07, so nothing here asserts what any provider offers to-verify. Every alias belongs to one class, and the class is its whole policy: which surfaces may receive it, which partial forms the maskers must expect and cover, and whether an echo from the third party is normal. The flag is a separate lookup: the policy layer is keyed by jurisdiction and act type and answers delegated, bounce or prohibited pending with a condition, and a missing row evaluates to bounce (section 6); the class and the surface are checked by the substitutor after the flag, and an alias whose class does not admit the surface is refused, the step closing refused-policy with nothing placed. The credential class has one boundary. A login to a bank, a payment app or any account that can move money is never an alias: it sits in the never-ours row of Table 3.1 and the step bounces. A login to a non-financial portal, a utility, a telecom, an insurer's claims desk, a records service, that the user has expressly delegated in the envelope is the credential class, admitted to the managed browser only and only where the jurisdiction's line "Use a delegated login to a non-financial portal" in Table 3.5 reads delegated; it is placed by the substitutor like any other value, never echoed and never displayed.

Partial forms are masked as strictly as full ones, because a last-four beside a card that names "Client 4471" is enough to join a value to a person. Where a step must tell two instruments apart, the vault issues a partial under its own alias, {{card.primary.last4}}, and nothing on our side ever cuts a value. Aadhaar is the strict case and has one rule everywhere on this page: {{aadhaar}} may exist in the vault as received, as a number or as a document, and is never placed in full on any surface; the only placeable form is the vault-issued partial {{aadhaar.last4}}, which is what UIDAI's requirement that an organisation processing Aadhaar display no more than its last four digits allows verified [1], and an Aadhaar document is placeable only where the vault can issue it masked to the same rule, otherwise the presentation bounces to the user's device. The never-ours class is a refusal, not a gap: the RBI cautions the public against sharing logins, KYC copies, card details, PINs, passwords or OTPs with any person or agency verified [2], which is the line the credential class stops at, and those acts bounce to the user's device as the passports section specifies. Beside the value, the vault record carries the alias's policy fields: type, class, permitted surfaces, the identity-act type its use implies, the residency region, how and when it entered, its version, and whether the user confirmed it. The policy layer and the envelope decide a use at runtime; the substitutor enforces that decision at the moment of use. The health and legal class is the one class whose values are words rather than numbers, and it follows the same rule: a condition, a medication, a result or a legal matter enters by the device form's health fields, by a health-record rail or by the dictionary detector the proxy and the hash service's screen both run (1.2, 1.3), is vaulted at the proxy, or by the hash service where the text was written on our side (1.3), and is never in clear on a card, in the context store, in a request's facts_in_clear or on a call caption; a booking carries the speciality or the department, a non-sensitive task field the catalogue entry names, never the diagnosis. The personal-fact class covers every natural person in the context store, not only the user: a people node's name, date of birth, identifiers, contact details and address are aliases of the same class qualified by the person reference ({{name.nominee}}, {{dob.nominee}}, {{phone.mother}}), their values are vaulted at the proxy like the user's, and the hash service holds their digests in both digest sets, so digest masks, the transcriber's match and the redactor catch them anywhere on any surface; the substitutor speaks or types them with the worker muted, the need-to-know filter in 04 admits no person's name, and the relationship word, daughter or nominee, is the non-sensitive task field the catalogue entry may name (Table 3.6, path 26).

The harness codes against one vault interface of its own, so the vendor sits behind it (DR-008). Five operations: tokenise (class, value, region, policy fields), returning an alias, held by the vault's edge proxy and, for the third door of 1.2, by the hash service; detokenise (alias, placement id), returning the current version, held by the substitutor alone; digest, returning the two keyed digest sets of 1.2 to the hash service; issue-partial (alias, form), returning .last4 or .masked under the parent alias, the masked form of a document being the vault's own rendering; and an event feed of custody events (alias, version, event kind, time, never a value), which the hash service subscribes to and turns into the ledger's custody lines. Credentials are issued per operation, per class and per region, to a workload identity and never to a person. The type of an alias is the declared schema field at the adapter door or the detector's class at the other two; the qualifier is chosen at issue from a non-sensitive attribute by a rule per class (the issuer's short name for an account, primary then secondary in order of entry for a card, home or work for an address, the document type for a document), a collision takes a numeric suffix ({{account.hdfc-2}}), and the user sees the label on their device and may rename it, which is a custody event. Nothing here asserts that a named provider offers these operations under these names to-verify.

1.2Boundary one: the three doors a real value enters by

Every real value enters by one of three doors, and at each door the vault, not a process of ours, is what receives the bytes. The first is the user's own input. The channel webhook and the device form terminate at the vault's edge proxy, a tokenising proxy the provider runs in the market's region under our detector policy, so the message body, every attachment and the audio of a voice note are the proxy's bytes and never the intake gateway's. The proxy tokenises under the four detectors 02 specifies: identifier patterns, the amount pattern of the financial-fact class among them, so a balance or a fee in a rail response or an inbound message is tokenised as it arrives, known-value digests, the user's own declaration, and a dictionary-based health and legal term detector, per market and language, that tokenises a free-text mention of a condition, a medication, a result or a legal matter in a message or a transcript into the health and legal class of Table 3.1. The same four run in the hash service's screening call (1.3), and the dictionary detector's policy is one artefact shipped to both; how completely a dictionary catches free text is to-verify, and the canary test in section 7 plants such mentions to measure it. The proxy transcribes a voice note inside the same boundary so that only the alias transcript, a document alias for the audio and the time spans of the detected values leave it (the evidence pipeline receives the transcript and the spans, never the audio), and hands the gateway the message with aliases in place of values and document aliases such as {{doc.pan-card}} in place of uploads. The gateway parses a tokenised message and holds no raw byte at any moment. That the chosen vault can run this proxy in region with our detectors loaded is a requirement in the vendor evaluation open in 07, and nothing here asserts that a named provider does to-verify. For values we know we need we do not ask in chat: the user's device shows a form whose fields post straight to the vault, and we receive the alias. Closure: no raw body reaches a log or a queue of ours, because none reaches a process of ours; the model runtime carries a second, separately deployed detector that refuses to plan on a candidate value and returns the message for re-tokenisation; the task card is generated from the tokenised request and screened again before it reaches a console; and the user's channel history stays on their device and the channel provider's servers, which are not ours.

The second door is a passport adapter. A rail returns real values by design, so the rail's response terminates at the same vault edge proxy, deployed in front of the adapter, which tokenises it against a declared schema in which sensitivity is stated per field and never inferred; an undeclared field is sensitive until it is classified. The adapter reaches the rail only through the proxy, so no response path exists that the proxy does not terminate, and the rail token the adapter presents on each call lives in the vault and is injected into the call by the proxy. What the adapter receives is the tokenised payload, aliases plus non-sensitive attributes (document type, issuer, dates, whether the name matched), and never a raw byte. Closure: no payload log, because the adapter never has a payload to log; network egress from the adapter to the proxy alone; tokenise rights at the proxy and never detokenise rights anywhere on this path; and a schema reviewed with the market's legal pack so a new rail cannot leak by omission.

The third door is a third-party echo: a confirmation page repeating a card number, a voice system reading an account number back, a reply quoting it. It opens on the surfaces and is closed there, by maskers that run before any frame, audio segment or message reaches a person; the masks themselves are specified in 3.1 and 3.2. A value we already hold is recognised by keyed digest and replaced with its alias; a new one, such as an account number the institution issues, is tokenised into a new alias by the hash service, which holds the tokenise credential for this door, with a ledger line. Recognition never compares against the value itself, and no masker holds anything a value could be recovered from: the vault emits, through the hash service, a keyed digest of each value in every normalised form Table 3.1 names; the key is held in the hash service alone, is never issued to a masker and rotates with the digests; a masker holds the digest set and obtains the digest of each candidate window it scans from the hash service over a batched digest call, windows in and digests out and never a match or a value, rate-limited to a frame's or a segment's volume, and matches locally. Short values such as a date of birth are weak against guessing, which is why the key stays where it is, why the maskers also mask by coordinate and by field type, why the digest set never leaves the real-value zone, and why the hash service holds no detokenise right.

1.3What exists on each side

Boundary two is the edge of the alias zone, the widest boundary in the design: the intake gateway and the passport adapters behind the vault's edge proxy, the planner and its model runtime, the classifier, the policy flags, the worker console, the task cards, the context store, the logs, the access ledger, the QA and support tools and the evidence store all live inside it and hold aliases and vault-issued partials only. The vault's client library exists in exactly two places of ours, the substitutor and the hash service; it is not installed in any of these, none holds a credential the vault would accept, and network policy denies them the vault's endpoints and the substitutor's inbound queue. A component in the alias zone that must screen text it holds, the task service over result fields and card payloads, the model runtime's second detector over its input and output, the logging pipeline over a line before it is written, never holds a digest set and never computes a match: it submits the text to the hash service's screening call, which runs the same four detectors the proxy runs (identifier pattern, known-value digest, the user's declaration where a field is declared sensitive, and the dictionary-based health and legal term detector of 1.2, from the one policy artefact shipped to both), tokenises any match through its tokenise-only credential, and answers clean, or found with the text returned with aliases in place, so the caller receives the alias and never the run. Every free-text field written on the operator side or through the device surface goes through that call before it is stored: a worker's variance note or failed-final line, a question's free-line answer, an onboarding answer, a fact a closed step writes, a connector pull, each submitted by the task service, on its own behalf or for a field the console sends it, and the task service and the console store only what the call returns, so a condition a worker types or a user answers reaches the context store, the step record and the outcome report as an alias (02, 9.3). The call is rate-limited per caller, and a found answer on text a person on our side wrote, a card payload or a log line opens an incident, so the service cannot be worked as an oracle (Table 3.6, path 24); a found answer on the user's own answer or a connector pull writes a "received" line, as the first door does. Across that boundary lies the small region where a value may exist: the vault, at rest and regionally hosted, and its edge proxy, for the bytes of one message or payload until they are tokenised; the substitutor, in memory for one placement; a surface, transiently, in a field, on a call leg or in the relay's memory-only outbound store; the evidence pipeline, in memory until redaction is verified; and the hash service, in memory while it screens a text. The accent outline in Figure 3.1 is that region's edge, and the entry boundary at the top of the figure is the vault's own proxy: between them they are everywhere a real value exists on our side. Only dots, tones, aliases and redacted evidence cross the outline outward, and one more thing: the ledger line the substitutor writes for every use, which the user reads on their own device.

The topology that carries these boundaries is two accounts per market region and one floor. The alias-zone account holds the intake gateway and the adapters behind their proxies, the planner runtime, the task service with its step runner, the policy layer, the context store, the ledger, the logs and the evidence store; the real-value-zone account holds the substitutor, the three surface controllers deployed with their surfaces (02, 4.4), the surfaces, the evidence pipeline and the hash service, with the vault's regional instance and its edge proxy beside it on the provider's side. Between the two, network policy admits four flows and no other: the step runner's instructions to the three controllers (a step's signed context, a placement tap, the worker's remote input) and the screening call to the hash service inward; the controllers' returns (the compositor's encoded stream, the panel and composer state, the receipts and the surface's signal) and the ledger writer's lines with the masked artefacts outward. No host in the real-value zone allows interactive access in production (2.3). The vault's endpoints are reachable from the real-value-zone account only; the internet, the carrier and the sandbox are reachable from the surfaces only. The floor in Bengaluru is a third network with no account of its own: a console is a client of each region's alias zone over a private link, and the stream it renders arrives from each region's stream gateway, an egress point that emits the compositor's output and nothing else, so the floor reaches three real-value zones only as a viewer of dots (section 9).

1.4What each ledger line has to be able to say

"Placed by the system; no person saw it" is not a promise about culture. It is a property of the component that placed the value, of the stream the worker watched and of the pipeline that kept the evidence, and every claim that recurs in the ledger maps to the property that makes it true. "Placed by the system" holds because exactly one non-human component can detokenise, and every placement it makes is bound to a step, a card and a policy check, so a line is never written for a placement without a step. "No person saw it" holds because a worker reaches every surface through a rendered stream that the masker clears before delivery, raw frames exist only inside the accent boundary of Figure 3.1, and the console has no channel that carries a raw frame. "The worker's audio was muted" holds because the mute is applied by our media server on the worker's leg in both directions, never by the worker's client, and the line is written from the server's own mute record and never from a worker's attestation. "In our browser" and "on our phone line" hold because the site controls in 04 keep a worker's own tools out of the room. "Masked before any person saw it" holds because the store accepts an artefact only with the verifier's signature over its hash (section 4). A line the system cannot make true by construction is a line the system is not allowed to write.

2The substitution component

One component holds the right to turn an alias back into a value, and it does one thing with that right: it places the value into a surface we run, writes the line, and tells nobody, its caller included, what the value was. Boundary three is that component's edge. Aliases, target descriptors and a signed step context go in; receipts and ledger lines come out.

2.1Boundary three: the interface and the checks

The substitutor accepts one message, a placement request, over one authenticated queue that only the three surface controllers can write to: the browser controller, the telephony controller and the mail relay, each acting for a step and never for a person. The step runner inside the task service instructs a controller for an ai step; for a human step the worker's tap on "place {{pan}}" goes from the console to the task service, which hands it to the step's surface controller; the console itself never calls the substitutor, and the controllers, deployed with their surfaces in the real-value zone and instructed by the task service's step runner, are its only writers. A worker can trigger a placement and can never receive one. The request carries the task id, the step id, the alias, the surface and a target descriptor typed per surface: for the browser, the session, the frame, the field's accessibility node and declared kind (text, password, upload), and the page origin; for telephony, the call, the far leg, the mode (synthesised speech or keypad tones), the spoken format and the cue (now, or after the next prompt ends); for mail, the message id and the placeholder occurrences to rewrite. A delegated debit under the cap is a placement like any other: the alias is the payment instrument, the substitutor obtains from the passport adapter a token bound to this task, its cap and the named merchant, or a payment instruction under a mandate the user set up, and places it on the surface, so the checks below apply to it; and it is a committing placement, because the institution may execute it on receipt with no submit, click, sentence or key to follow. A placement whose alias is a payment instrument or a payment instruction, or whose catalogue entry marks it as consumed on placement, is a committing action in its own right: it needs the approval token like a submit, and the substitutor writes the step's fired marker before the first tone or character of it is delivered, so the controller's committing-action check has nothing left to guard after it; the rail's own binding of the token to the task and its cap is a second layer, chipped in Table 3.5, never the closure (section 5). With it travels a signed step context issued by the task service when the step entered running. Its fields are fixed: the task id and step id; the step record's executor, surface, party, act type and field list, the aliases the step carries, which is what a task card renders; the envelope version with its lines; the policy lookup, naming the act type and both jurisdictions it used, the delegator's (the user's, the market on the request, for the user's own aliases; the one her delegation record carries for a dependant's) and, for an act toward an institution, the institution's market, with the flag and the condition's result under each, the stricter governing (section 6); for a dependant's alias her delegation record and its validity end; where the act moves money, its amount and the remaining cap, the task's cap less the committed-spend total the task service keeps, fired acts plus the amounts outstanding approval tokens reserve; for an irreversible step the dual-control approval token that the worker console page specifies, bound to the check id and the envelope version, otherwise null; issued-at and expires-at; a nonce; and the id of the signing key. The encoding is canonical JSON and the signature is Ed25519 under the task service's key for the market region, one key per region held in the alias zone's secret store; the substitutor holds the public keys, the current and the previous, in its signed build configuration, changed under the two-person rule of 2.3, so distribution needs no channel to the task service, and the key rotates on a fixed schedule (illustrative: quarterly) and on any suspicion, with a two-key overlap. The substitutor verifies the signature and checks the context itself; it has no route to the task service, the envelope store or the policy layer and needs none. The context is short-lived by construction: its validity is no longer than the placement window (illustrative: two minutes for a browser field or a message, three for a call, where a placement may wait for a prompt; a legal pack may shorten either and never lengthen it), it names the envelope version it was issued against, for a step naming a dependant's aliases it expires no later than her delegation's validity end, and it dies with any edit to the envelope. An extension or a narrowing voids every context and every approval token the task has issued, and so does the revocation or expiry of a dependant's delegation for every step that names her aliases: the task service returns every running step to ready with its context voided, so no step stays running across an edit; a step that held a token re-enters checking and needs a fresh token before it can fire; a step already in checking is re-checked against the new version; and a fresh context is issued only against the new version, so a controller must obtain one before it can request a placement at all (dual control in 02; the console shows the same sequence). A step that names a dependant's aliases is valid for the earlier of the envelope's window and her delegation's validity, an extension of the window does not extend past her delegation, and a step left without her authority re-classifies as a user step for her (01, 1.5). The substitutor for its part refuses a context whose envelope version differs from the version named in the dual-control token and, for a reversible step, one whose version differs from the envelope version stamp, a second signed statement of the task id, the envelope version and its time under the same key, which the controller fetches from the task service at request time, dated inside the window, and sends with the request. A placement is therefore never checked against a superseded envelope.

Every check must pass, in order, and the first failure returns a receipt of refused naming the check and nothing else, with a "refused" line written; the controller closes the step and the task service records refused-policy with the check as the policy reference, whichever check failed, and a worker chooses nothing. The context signature is valid and inside its short validity. The step is running and the caller is the controller of its executor's surface. The alias is in the step record's field list carried by the context, the list a task card renders for a human step and an ai step carries with no card, so the list is the allow-list. Every delegation record the alias's owner references is live: for a dependant's alias the context names her record and its validity end, and her revocation voids the context as an envelope edit does. The alias's class admits the surface and the flag for the act, looked up under each jurisdiction the context names, reads delegated under both, the stricter result governing (section 6). The envelope admits the act, its validity window is open, an act that moves money is within the remaining cap the context carries, so two acts each under the cap cannot together exceed it, and the target belongs to a contactable party: the browser session's origin is on that party's declared domains and presents its certificate; the far leg is a number we dialled from the party's entry in the market's party register (section 3), so an inbound call never receives a placement; the recipient is on the party's mail domain in the same entry. The step is reversible or the approval token is present, current, names this envelope version and is unconsumed, so dual control is enforced at the substitutor and not only at the console: a token is single-use, the controller consumes it before it issues the committing action, a consumed token is refused, and the token reserves its act's amount against the cap from issue until the step closes without a fired marker; a committing placement is refused without the token whatever the step's classification, and for it the substitutor itself consumes the token and writes the fired marker before the first tone or character leaves it, so a step whose placement reached the surface holds the marker whatever the surface then reports. The target is plausible for the type: a card number goes only into a field whose declared kind or label matches. And the placement is the first for this step, alias and target, or carries the retry token the runner issues for any re-placement of the same step, alias and target, whatever the first receipt was, so a second format after a rejected submit and a second attempt after a failure pass the same gate. A placement window bounds the whole exchange (illustrative: two minutes), after which the request expires unplaced and the line closes as "not confirmed".

Only then does it detokenise, with a credential scoped to the alias's class and the task's market region, render the format the descriptor asks for, inject directly through the surface's privileged driver, zero its memory and return a receipt: a placement id and one of placed, not-confirmed, refused or failed, with the failed check or the reason class. The receipt returns to the controller, which closes or continues the step while the task service records the outcome, and the mapping is one way: refused closes the step refused-policy; not-confirmed and failed close it failed-retryable with a retry token, then failed-final at the step's cap, except on a committing placement, whose fired marker already stands: there not-confirmed closes the step failed-final with the marker as its evidence, no retry token is issued, the reservation is not released, and the re-plan issues a status enquiry before any repeat (Table 3.2); placed leaves the outcome to the surface's signal and the runner. Every controller writes a fired marker before it issues a committing action, and the substitutor writes it before a committing placement's first tone or character; the closed list of committing actions reads the same on every page: a form submit, a committing click, a committing sentence on a call, a keypad press outside the navigation map (3.2), a relay release, a committing placement. A step with a fired marker never closes failed-retryable: an unconfirmed fire, a submit that times out, a call that drops after the committing sentence or a committing placement the surface's signal did not confirm, closes failed-final with the marker as its evidence, and the re-plan issues a verification step, a status enquiry, before any repeat, so an irreversible act fires at most once (02). A narrowing that arrives inside a committing placement's window does not stop it, because the marker is already written, and the outcome report says so. It never returns the value, its length, a partial, a digest or a rendering of it, and it has no operation that reads a field, a leg or a message back. A caller that wants to know whether the field is filled learns it from the surface's own signal.

2.2The placement and the line are one transaction

The line is written ahead of the injection, as "placing", and confirmed as "placed" by the surface's own signal: the browser driver's input-event sequence and the field's validity state, with the driver's read-back of field values disabled; the media server's playback record; the relay's delivery record. The "no person saw it" clause of a placed line is scoped to that confirmation: it covers the frames and segments up to the surface's signal, from the compositor's mask record and the stream record, and a later unmasked exposure of the same field or leg writes its own incident line rather than rewriting this one (8.2). If confirmation does not arrive inside the window, the line closes as "not confirmed", the surface clears what it can (the field is cleared, the message is dropped from the relay's outbound store) and the step closes failed-retryable with a retry token, or, where the placement was a committing one and its first tone or character had left the substitutor, failed-final with the fired marker the substitutor wrote (2.1). The ledger is append-only: a line is never edited, only followed. A substitutor that cannot write the ledger cannot place, because the "placing" write precedes the vault call; that ordering is what makes the ledger complete rather than usually complete, and it closes the gap a crash between injection and write would otherwise leave.

2.3Isolation, and the two halves

The substitutor runs in its own account and its own hardened runtime per market region: a separate network segment, memory never swapped, no persistent volume, no core dumps, no interactive access in production. Its network position is the design: one inbound queue from the three controllers, and outbound paths only to the vault's detokenise endpoint for its region, the browser driver's control socket, the telephony media gateway, the relay's injection endpoint into its outbound store, and the ledger writer. No route exists between it and the model runtime, the console, the context store or the logging pipeline. Its logs are structured and alias-only; the value is never a log argument, and the shared logging library used elsewhere is not linked, so a careless call cannot reach a logger that does not exist. The one vault credential that permits detokenisation is issued to its workload identity, short-lived and scoped per class and region; the only other vault credentials of ours are the hash service's, a digest credential and a tokenise-only credential for the third door and never detokenise, and the gateway, the adapters, the model runtime, the console and the pipeline hold none. Deployment is by signed build under a two-person rule, one approver from outside the owning team, recorded as a change; its operators see counts and reason codes. The rule of no interactive access is the whole zone's, not the substitutor's alone: no host in the real-value zone, the proxy client, the substitutor, the surface controllers, the media server, the browser sandbox, the relay, the evidence pipeline and the hash service, offers a shell, a debugger, a packet capture or a driver socket to a person in production, and each is deployed by the same signed build under the same two-person rule. Break-glass access to any of them needs two named approvers, first drains the host of live sessions and zeroes its memory, is time-boxed, and writes an incident and a break-glass ledger event on every task that had a session on the host, so whatever was in that memory is gone before a person arrives and the user reads that someone was there (Table 3.6, path 25).

Two halves make the design fail safely. The substitutor cannot open a browser session, dial a number or address a message; it only fills a target that a surface controller created under a step, so a compromised substitutor has nowhere to send a value except where the plan already sends it. The surfaces cannot detokenise; a compromised surface holds dots, the digest set without its key, and aliases, and can match what is on its own screen and nothing beyond it (1.2). The model runtime and the console cannot reach the vault, the substitutor's queue, the managed browser's document (they receive a rendered stream), the far leg's audio during a window, or the relay's outbound body after the swap. Unauthorised disclosure of Aadhaar identity information is an offence under section 37 of the Aadhaar Act 2016 verified [3]; the isolation above is what lets us say that no employee is ever in a position to commit it.

3Execution surfaces

Boundary four is the surface, the one boundary a value must cross, because the institution on the far side needs the value itself. Ownership makes it safe: every surface is ours, a worker never uses their own browser, phone or mailbox, and each surface renders the value as dots, tones or an alias on its human side while its institution side receives it. The component view of the same three surfaces is in 02; this section is the specification of the human side. Two configuration sets it reads are this page's and not the step-type catalogue's, whose columns 02 fixes: the surface settings per market (the browser stream's delay line, the telephony delay buffer, the maskers' format set from Table 3.1) and the party register per market (for each contactable party, its declared web domains and their certificates, the numbers we dial, its mail domain, the keys of its voice menu that navigate and the keys that commit, from which a step's catalogue entry takes its menu map (02, Table 2.6), and a mask map for each page a step may reach, naming the regions and fields the masker may deliver as text and, for a page marked as an authenticated portal, the whitelist of fields a step may read). Both are read by the controllers and the maskers at run time, versioned and signed with the market's legal pack, and changed only as a recorded change; a page with no mask map clears nothing (3.1).

3.1The managed browser

The browser runs in our sandbox in the market's region, one session per task, and the worker never runs it: the console shows a pixel stream of the session and forwards clicks, scrolling and keystrokes into ordinary fields. The worker holds no document, no cookie, no download and no clipboard. When the task card reaches a field that needs a value, it offers one action, "place {{pan}}", and nothing to type. The tap goes from the console to the task service, which hands it to the browser controller, the controller to the substitutor, and the substitutor's privileged driver session, attached to the same browser over a channel the worker's client does not have, focuses the field the descriptor names, types the value with synthesised input events so the site's own validation runs, and reports the field's rectangle to the stream compositor, which paints it as dots from focus until the field is cleared or the page navigates, following it through scrolling because the driver re-reports on every frame. Autofill, form history, extensions and the driver's read-back of field values are disabled in the session.

Every frame passes a masker before the worker sees it, and the stream runs behind the live page by a fixed delay line set so the masker always finishes first (a few seconds; illustrative, an entry per market in the surface settings). The masker works on the document's text and accessibility tree, never on pixels, and applies five masks in a fixed order. Coordinate masks: the rectangle of every placement record, painted from focus until the field is cleared or the page navigates. Field-type masks: every field whose declared kind is password, and every field whose label or accessibility name matches a class name in Table 3.1, painted from the first frame whatever it holds. Digest masks: every candidate window of the text layer, digested through the hash service against the keyed digest set (1.2), with each match's layout box painted. Format masks: every text run on every page, not only after a submit, is matched against the class formats of Table 3.1, the identifier patterns, dates in every order, card and account shapes, currency amounts in the written form of the financial-fact class, a name, the user's or any people node's in the context store, as whole tokens in any field or region, and any name-shaped run in a field the party register marks as an identity field (1.1), the reference shapes the register records for the party, and the health and legal term dictionary of 1.2, and a run that matches is painted, so a value in no known form is covered by its form. Region masks: image and canvas regions painted whole unless the mask map clears them. The same replacement runs over the document object at every capture point, so a DOM capture never holds a value. When a confirmation page repeats the value the region is painted, the panel reads "the page repeats {{pan}} (masked)", the model runtime receives the masked text layer, and the ledger writes the echo and its masking. On any page reached by a submit that used a placement the masker also covers, by shape, every digit run longer than a threshold (illustrative: six) until the page changes, so an echo the matcher cannot read is covered by shape rather than by recognition. What may be delivered as text is decided by the step's mask map, the party register's list of the regions and fields cleared for delivery on that page: a party's default map clears headings, labels, navigation and button text by their accessibility roles and nothing else, a page's map adds the fields the step needs to read, and on a page the register marks as an authenticated portal there is no default: every text run and every figure is painted except the fields the map whitelists as needed for the step's outcome, so balances, history and other people's identifiers that were never vaulted are dots to everyone, and a portal page with no whitelist is ai with no stream to a person. A frame is delivered only when every text run and every image region on it is accounted for: matched by digest or by format, painted by coordinate or field type, covered by shape, or cleared by the step's mask map. A run the masker can neither match nor clear is painted by default and the frame is held for re-masking: it is not delivered, the stream shows the hold screen with a reason, the frame is re-masked with the default paint and wider shape masks and re-checked by the verifier of 4.1 before it can leave, and the step pauses, still running with the surface held and its timer live. There is no residual of plain text below a threshold: a run that matches nothing and that no map clears is painted, never delivered. That is the fail-closed default: over-masking costs a worker a moment; a frame the masker cannot clear does not leave the pipeline, so the camera and the two-person zone in 04 record a failure of this mechanism and are never its closure. If the masker cannot confirm a frame is clean for any other reason, because the hash service is unreachable or the frame missed the delay, the same hold applies. A hold changes no step's class: an ai step has no stream to a person and continues on the driver unaffected; a human step waits, and if the hold outlasts a bounded wait (illustrative: one minute) the task service closes it failed-retryable on the tick and re-queues it on the same route, so another worker takes the card once the masker is clear; a human step that reaches a page with no mask map holds the same way, and the re-plan runs it as ai or the party register gains a map as a recorded change. Every held frame writes the line "A frame was held until it could be masked; no person saw it", an echo-masked event in its held form (8.1). The capture at submit and response is the same masked frame and a document snapshot with the same masks and the same whitelist applied, so QA, a checker and the worker see the same fields and nothing else (4.1). Where a step needs an amount for its outcome, a quoted fee against the sub-cap or a slot's price, the catalogue entry names that one amount field, the controller compares the amount the masker painted with the envelope's cap line, and the panel shows only the comparison, "fee inside the sub-cap: yes", never the amount; a step whose script cannot decide from the comparison closes blocked-needs-user with the amount held as an alias for the user's device. Where a check needs a party-issued reference confirmed, the council's reference against the filled form in the legal journey of 01, the browser controller's field-match check asks the hash service whether the field's digest equals the digest behind {{reference.council}} and the check panel reads "reference matches the notice: yes", so the checker confirms the reference while it stays dots.

3.2Telephony

We place the call from our numbers on our carrier; the worker's headset is on a leg we control, the institution is on another, and our media server mixes them. A script step reading "state {{dob}}" carries the same single action, and on the tap the substitutor's media function puts the value onto the far leg as synthesised speech or keypad tones, in the format the descriptor names. For the placement window, from the tap until the last tone or syllable is delivered plus a guard interval, the media server mutes the worker's leg in both directions, the far leg to the worker's headset and the worker's microphone to the far leg, and the panel shows "value placed" with a timer, then "line open"; the conversation resumes when the window ends, and the mute cannot be lifted from the worker's client. The worker never hears the far party's raw audio. The far leg reaches only the media server and the evidence pipeline's streaming transcriber; what the media server delivers to the worker's headset is a re-voiced rendering of the masked transcript, synthetic speech with the caption beside it, behind a fixed delay buffer at all times, from the first ring to the last and not only after a placement, its length in the market's surface settings, long enough for the transcriber and the digest matcher to clear each segment. The transcriber's default is an allowlist, the counterpart of the browser's default paint: a span is delivered only if it is cleared, that is, made of the language's common lexicon, the party register's terms for that party, the catalogue entry's expected phrases, or numbers below the digit-run threshold; every other span, an out-of-lexicon token, a proper noun that is not a cleared term, a digit run, a match to any class format or digest, is replaced by a tone and the caption reads "span masked", so a street address, a relative's name or a condition phrased outside the dictionary is not delivered either, whether or not any detector could name it. Every span is also matched against the known digests and against the class formats of Table 3.1 in their spoken forms, digit runs, sequences spelled letter by letter, dates with month names or numbers, amounts spoken with a currency word, rupees, pounds, dollars, thousand, lakh or crore, the reference shapes the party register records for the party, and the health and legal term dictionary of 1.2, and a match is a tone whatever the allowlist would have cleared. A span matching a known value in spoken or keyed form, which is what a voice system reading a number back produces, is a tone and the panel shows "value read back, masked"; a span matching a class format or a reference shape is a tone before its value is known, and the panel shows "reference masked", so a new account, policy or application number an agent reads out never reaches the worker's ear, and the transcriber tokenises it into a new alias by the hash service with a "received" line, so the task holds it as an alias that nobody heard. A worker who needs a masked span to proceed asks the party to send it to the task address, where it is tokenised (3.3), or closes the step blocked-needs-user. A balance, a fee or any other amount an agent or a voice system speaks is a financial fact of Table 3.1 and is a tone the same way; where the script needs the amount, a quoted fee against the sub-cap, the catalogue entry names that one amount field, the controller compares the masked amount with the envelope's cap line, and the caption shows only the comparison, "fee inside the sub-cap: yes"; a script that cannot decide from the comparison closes the step blocked-needs-user with the amount held as an alias for the user's device. The rendering fails closed on its own account too: when the matcher cannot keep pace with the buffer or cannot reach the hash service nothing is re-voiced, the panel shows the reason until the matcher catches up, and the mute is recorded. The worker's outbound leg runs behind the same delay line, through the media server's outbound gate, which matches the worker's speech against a commitment-form detector per language, agreement words, amounts, dates and account-changing verbs from the catalogue's commitment dictionary: a commitment-form utterance that is not the revealed committing sentence for a step holding an approval token is cut before delivery, the far party never hears it, the caption shows "commitment held; send for check" and the step enters checking; on a reversible step, which holds no token, the gate cuts every such utterance, so a worker's own words cannot commit the user on this surface. A key pressed on the party's voice menu is a committing action unless the step's catalogue entry names it as navigation: the entry carries a menu map, the keys that navigate, per party, from the party register, the telephony controller sends a key the map names as navigation through to the far leg without a token, and a key the map names as committing, or any key the map does not name, needs the approval token and the window-and-version check like a submit; on a reversible step the controller refuses every key outside the navigation map. The closed list of committing actions every controller checks reads the same on every page: a form submit, a committing click, a committing sentence on a call, a keypad press outside the navigation map, a relay release, a committing placement (02, 4.4). The script's line not to agree a fee above the sub-cap stays an instruction to a person; the gate is the closure (02, 4.2; 04). The script also tells the worker to decline read-backs and to ask for a new identifier by mail or to the user, which is a courtesy to the far party and not a closure; the closure is the re-voiced rendering and its allowlist. Live captions come from the same delayed, masked transcript. The transcriber runs on raw audio inside the evidence pipeline and emits a transcript in which every placement window and every matched segment is the alias; the stored recording carries the same windows as silence; and the pipeline's second pass over the stored transcript, which flags any span the live allowlist delivered and should have held and opens an incident, is the record of a failure of this mechanism, never its closure. A live video identification is not ours to do: a bank's V-CIP is run by the bank's own trained officials with the customer present verified [4], so the classifier marks it user and it bounces.

3.3The mail relay

Outbound mail is composed with aliases, by the worker in the console or by the AI, from a per-task address at our domain. The relay releases a message only if it instantiates the step's catalogue template, fixed sentences with named slots typed non-committing, or the step holds an approval token naming the hash of the message as checked; a message that matches no template is a committing action and needs the token, and on a reversible step, which holds none, the relay releases template instances only, so free-composed text cannot commit the user through the relay (02, 4.3). On send the relay, as the surface controller, sends the substitutor a placement request naming the message and the occurrences, with the signed step context; the substitutor rewrites them in the relay's outbound store through the relay's injection endpoint, writes the line, and the relay delivers. That outbound store is the one place a value rests outside a field or a call leg, and it is built not to rest: memory only, inside the real-value zone, on an instance with no persistent volume and no swap, encrypted under a key held by the relay's workload identity and released to nothing else, and bounded by the placement window. A receiving server that has not accepted the message inside the window, because it defers or greylists, gets nothing further: the body is dropped, the line closes "not confirmed", the step closes failed-retryable with a retry token, and the next attempt is a fresh composition and a fresh placement. The relay keeps no sent copy. The evidence copy is the alias draft and the delivery receipt, and the console keeps no history a worker can open, so nothing anyone can re-open holds a value. A worker's own mailbox is never a route. Inbound mail to a task address terminates at the vault's edge proxy like any other channel, with the digest set added: known values, our own value quoted back included, become their aliases, new values become new aliases with a "received" line, an amount, a balance or a fee in the body becomes a financial-fact alias by the amount pattern of 1.2, attachments become document aliases, and only then does the tokenised message reach the relay's renderer or the planner. The console never shows the tokenised message as it stands: the relay's renderer applies the same allowlist the telephony transcriber applies (3.2) and paints by default every token outside the cleared vocabulary, the language's common lexicon, the party register's terms for that party, the catalogue entry's expected phrases and numbers below the digit-run threshold, so an out-of-lexicon token, a proper noun that is not a cleared term, a digit run or a match to any class format is painted whether or not the four detectors named it, and the worker reads the painted rendering; a painted token the worker needs is requested from the party as a typed field to the task address, where it is tokenised, or the step closes blocked-needs-user. The planner receives the tokenised message, screened again by the model runtime's detector. Behind the proxy's detectors, the renderer's default paint is the second line for what a person reads, as the browser's default paint is for a frame (3.1) and the transcriber's allowlist for a call.

4Artefact redaction

The evidence system is the fifth boundary and the one most easily forgotten: a screenshot, a recording or a transcript is a real value written down. So the pipeline lives inside the real-value zone, nothing raw leaves it, and it is designed as a leak path first and a record second. DR-009 fixes this; 02 places the pipeline among the components and draws it; this section is the redaction contract.

4.1Boundary five: capture, redact, verify, store

Capture takes browser frames and document snapshots before each submit and after each response, the raw audio of both call legs, the transcript, call metadata and the alias version of mail into the pipeline's memory only: nothing raw is written to disk, queued or logged, memory is not swapped, and an instance that crashes loses its material rather than dumping it. Each artefact carries the placement registry from the substitutor's receipts, which alias went into which field rectangle, which audio window, which message occurrences; the registry is the map the redactor works from, and an artefact without it is not accepted. Redaction applies five masks in a fixed order: coordinate masks from every registry entry, with a margin, from the frame of placement to the frame of navigation; known-value masks by keyed digest over text layers, document captures and transcripts with spoken digits normalised; audio windows, every placement and every matched read-back replaced with silence and a transcript marker naming the alias; format masks on every frame, capture and transcript, any run or span matching a class format, the party's recorded reference shapes or the health and legal dictionary, with the shape rule's digit runs beyond the threshold after a submit that used a placement; and mask-map masks, every run and figure the step's mask map does not clear, which on an authenticated portal page is everything outside the whitelist. Aliases replace matches in transcripts and document captures, so a transcript reads {{dob}} where the value was. The output is the masked artefact and a redaction manifest stating, in aliases, what was masked by which rule.

Verification is a second pass by a separately built service with its own recogniser over the pixels, its own speech recogniser, its own normaliser and its own digest set: the hash service issues two digest sets under two keys, one to the maskers and one to the verifier, so the verifier derives nothing from the vault and holds no vault credential of any kind; it shares no code with the redactor, is owned by a different team and changes on a different schedule. It checks the mask map as well as known values and formats: a run or figure outside the step's whitelist on a portal page, or outside the cleared regions on any page, is a hit. It fails closed. Any hit quarantines the artefact in memory: nothing is stored, an incident opens naming only the alias and the rule that missed, redaction re-runs from memory with wider masks, and after a small fixed number of attempts, or when the incident closes if that is sooner, the raw material is dropped and the step's evidence becomes a structured note, "evidence withheld: redaction could not be verified". The step keeps its outcome code, because artefacts attach for audit and dispute only and never for the outcome; the user's ledger records that the artefact was destroyed rather than kept. The store accepts an artefact only with the verifier's signature over its hash, so nothing can be written around the second pass, and the incident handler receives the manifest and the matched digest's id, never the artefact. Only a verified artefact is stored, encrypted, under the task and in the market's region, for the retention the market's legal pack sets to-verify; the ledger outlives it.

4.2Who may open what, and when

There is no raw store for anyone to be granted access to, so Table 3.3 is the whole access list, and every opening is itself a ledger event the user can read. 5The passports

A passport is how a value or an authority the user already holds reaches a task without passing through a person: a universal device bounce-back, and per market an adapter to the rails that release documents, financial data and payment authority under the user's own consent. Every rail below is gated to the named user and to an approved counterparty, so the adapter reaches a rail through the user's own session or a regulated partner and never around them, and whatever a rail returns terminates at the vault's edge proxy in front of the adapter (1.2). The interface every adapter implements is in 02; what each rail permits a delegate to do is recorded here as it stood in the research notes of this edition, with the design intent stated where the notes did not reach. Nothing in this section moves the capability ceiling; it records the current state the flags in section 6 respond to.

5.1The universal device bounce-back

Every market shares one passport that needs no rail: the user's bound device. A user step bounces there in one of the four forms the classifier in 02 fixes, approval, user act, one-time code and do-this-yourself, and the device's signed response closes it. The first is a one-tap approval, which is always an envelope confirmation or a device-signed extension. Every envelope line carries a scope, task-wide or one step by its id: a one-tap approval of a step's request produces a new version carrying a step-scoped line; a one-tap approval of a task-level request the task service raises, the validity-window renewal, produces a task-wide line; and a user's own edit produces a task-wide line. Nothing we perform runs on the tap alone; classification re-runs for every step on the new version, and a step-scoped line admits only its step, so that step is re-classified as inside the envelope and fires under dual control while no other step gains from the line, whereas a task-wide line is read by every step the re-plan re-issues (01, 4.2; 02). The other three are acts the user performs on their own device. A user act is done in the institution's own app or session: a biometric or face check, a UPI PIN, a bank-app approval, an e-signature ceremony, a consent screen (DigiLocker, Account Aggregator, Open Banking, a government login), with our script on the device and a callback to the task. A one-time code is entered by the user on their own device and by nobody on our side, and where the institution's flow allows it the user enters it in the institution's own app or page on that device, so nothing passes through us at all. Where the code must land in a field on our surface, it travels on a one-shot channel: the browser controller or the telephony controller opens the channel for exactly one step, encrypted end to end to the surface driver's key so every process between the device and the driver carries ciphertext, and closes it on first use or when the code expires, whichever is first (the institution's own validity; illustrative: a few minutes). The driver places the code under a placement record like any other placement, so the compositor paints the field by coordinate before the first character lands and the driver's read-back stays disabled; on a call the code goes out as keypad tones inside a muted window. The code touches no vault, no model, no console and no log, and the substitutor is not involved because there is nothing to detokenise. The ledger line is "You entered a one-time code yourself, on your device; the worker saw the field as dots", and Table 3.6 carries the path as 23. {{otp}} is never an alias because the code is never ours to hold, and a code read aloud to a call in progress is a read-back the buffer mutes (3.2). And a "do this yourself" step hands the user a precise instruction when a jurisdiction reserves the act to them, shown at plan time as "you will do this yourself", never as a box to tick (the envelope in 01). The ledger writes each as "you did this yourself, on your device", and no worker is present for any of them.

6The policy flag table and the lobbying agenda

Full delegation, identity acts included, is the designed ceiling; the flag table is what the policy layer reads at runtime to decide, per jurisdiction and act type, whether an act is delegated, bounces to the user's device, or is prohibited pending a regulatory answer (DR-003). Detracting is a flag; adding would be a rebuild. A bounce line wins even where the user ticked the identity act in the envelope; a prohibited pending line closes the step refused-policy before anyone attempts it and carries a legal item in 07. The table ships in each market's legal pack and is signed before a pilot opens (06). It doubles as the lobbying agenda: for every line that is not delegated, the last two columns say what to ask the regulator and what the user gains if the answer is yes. A flag is one of three values, delegated, bounce or prohibited pending, and the lookup has one interface: the policy layer is keyed by jurisdiction and act type, the rows below, and every step type in the catalogue 02 owns carries exactly one act type and no flag of its own, the flag living in the policy layer alone, so the classifier, the envelope filter and the substitutor all ask the same question with the same key; a row that does not exist evaluates to bounce. The alias class and the surface are not part of the key: the substitutor checks them after the flag (1.1). Every flag is a legal item to verify, not a design constraint, and is chipped so. A one-time code has no line in the table: it is the user's factor by design, {{otp}} is never an alias and no flag is consulted for it (the classifier in 02; 5.1). The jurisdiction in the key is the delegator's: for the user's own aliases the user's jurisdiction, the market on the request; for a dependant's aliases the jurisdiction her delegation record carries, so an identity act on her aliases is flagged under it and never the user's. 8The access ledger

The ledger is the product of the boundaries above, and it is how the trust story becomes visible to the customer: one plain-words line for every use of every alias, readable by the user on their own device. It is written by the component that performed the event, from that component's own record, and never by a person. Its record form is in 02; this section fixes what it holds, how it is worded, how long it lives and what it can never contain.

8.1Fields and event types

Every line carries: the task id and step id; the time, shown to the user in their own time zone; the alias; the event type; the use, in words; the surface; the party; who placed it (the system, the user on their device, or a named component for custody events); who saw it, as a category and never a name; the outcome of the event (placing, placed, not confirmed, refused, masked, withheld); the policy reference and the envelope version; and, for evidence events, the artefact reference and, for openings, the viewer's role. The event types are fixed: received (a value entered the vault by any of the three doors); custody (a new version behind an alias, a partial issued, a value destroyed at the end of retention); placed in a browser field, on a phone line or through the relay, with its placing and not-confirmed forms; refused; shown-as-dots, with its checker form for the masked stream or leg a dual-control checker watched; echo-masked, with its held form for a frame the masker held rather than delivered; read-back-muted; bounced (the user performed the act on their device), with its code-relayed form for a one-time code the driver placed from the user's device under a placement record; evidence-stored, evidence-withheld and evidence-opened; reconciled (the provider's access log matched the ledger); released (a legal request); and break-glass (a person was given emergency access to a real-value host that had handled the task, after it was drained and zeroed; 2.3). A line without a step is possible only for custody, reconciliation and release events, and each of those names the component that wrote it. A dual-control check is not an event here, because the check itself uses nothing; the checker's viewing is, written as "A checker saw the masked stream for step 2" in the shown-as-dots form; both identities live on the step record, and the check is shown on the task's timeline and in the outcome report (verification in 02).

8.2Wording rules

Past tense, one sentence per event, and the user is "you". The alias appears as written, {{pan}}, never expanded, never as a partial, a length, a digest or a description that would narrow it ("your 10-character number"). The party is named as the user knows it, "HDFC Bank's account-opening form", not by an internal id. "Placed by the system" is written only for a substitutor placement confirmed by the surface's signal. "No person saw it" on a placed line covers the frames and segments up to the surface's confirmation, and is written only when the compositor's mask record and the stream record agree that none of them reached a person unmasked; a later unmasked exposure of the same field or leg writes its own incident line, so a line is never rewritten and never claims more than its window (2.2). "The worker's audio was muted" is written only from the media server's mute record. A worker is "a worker" and a reviewer "a QA reviewer", never a name or a tier, because the ledger is the user's record of their data and not a personnel file. A refusal names what was refused and that nobody answered it. Times are the surface's, not the console's, so a placement on a call is timed by the media server. Nothing in a line is free text a person typed; every line is assembled from fields, so the wording cannot drift and cannot carry a value by accident.

8.3Retention, reading, and what it never contains

The ledger outlives every artefact it refers to: an artefact is destroyed at the end of its retention and the ledger records that it was, so "no raw copy was kept" can still be checked after the copy is gone. Its own retention is the task's life plus the period the market's legal pack sets, and the user may export it at any time; the periods are the legal pack's to settle to-verify. The user reads it per task, alongside the outcome report, on their bound device, and may subscribe to it as it is written; a line is never shown in the chat thread itself, which receives only the notification that something is waiting. It is append-only, replicated within the market's region, and readable by the user, by the task service that assembles the outcome report, by QA for a task under review, and by nobody else without a legal request that itself writes a line. It never contains: a real value; a partial, a length, a digest or a rendering of one; a screenshot or a transcript; a worker's name; the user's name (the user is "you"); an internal identifier the user could not act on; or a line a person wrote by hand.

9The data-residency map

Sensitive values stay in the market's region and only aliases travel to the floor. That is the whole map, and it is why the vault is bought: a provider's regional hosting gives each market a vault instance in its own region, and everything of ours that holds a value transiently, the substitutor, the surfaces and the evidence pipeline, is deployed in that region too. The floor in Bengaluru, running 24/7 under the shift design in 04, receives the rendered stream, the task cards, the ledger writes and the masked artefacts a review needs: dots, aliases and masked material, never a value. Figure 3.4 draws the three markets, and the accent is the one crossing that needs contractual safeguards: personal data of UK and EU residents, even in alias form and pseudonymised, processed by staff in Bengaluru is a restricted transfer under UK GDPR that needs a transfer mechanism (the international data transfer agreement or addendum) and a transfer risk assessment, with the processor terms to match to-verify. The US crossing is governed by contract and by the applicable state law rather than by a transfer regime, which the legal pack confirms per cohort to-verify. The India crossing is domestic. Model inference for a market runs in that market's region where the model vendor offers it, otherwise in India under the same safeguards as the floor, and for the pilot that choice follows the first market; in either case it sees aliases to-verify. A region is never overridden for availability: a vault or substitutor outage in one region delays a step; it never routes a value through another region (Table 3.2). Routing is by one key: the market the gateway sets on the request from the user record selects the region, India, the UK or the EU for a UK user, or the US, and that region names the vault instance, the substitutor deployment, the surfaces, the evidence pipeline and the hash service the task will use for its whole life; a step never chooses a region. The rule in DR-007 follows: the user's market fixes where values rest and are placed and the institution's market fixes only the rail, so a UK user acting on an Indian institution keeps their vault, substitutor and surfaces in the UK region, the UK-region browser or line reaches the Indian institution as it reaches any other party, and the India adapter's proxy tokenises the rail's response into the user's UK vault instance. Each market's entry in the map lists, per component, the region and the safeguard, and ships in the legal pack (02); the expansion playbook in 06 adds a row per market.
