On this page

1The promise

AI.fred is a high-trust, high-reliability, human-in-the-gap, AI-first personal assistant. You give it a task with money, identity or an institution in it. Every request becomes an ordered plan. The AI executes what it can; where it cannot, it hands one of our people a precise, low-judgement instruction. The reliability is on tasks that carry real consequence, and it is made safe by one property: nobody on the operator side ever sees the sensitive data they are placing. Your numbers live in a vault; our people and our AI work with labels; a machine with no screen places the real value at the last moment; and every use is written into a ledger you can read, in plain words.

The flat-fee errand services cannot safely touch these tasks, because their staff see everything. We can, because ours cannot. That is the wedge and the moat, and the thesis carries the argument without a single number of economics in it.

2Human in the gap

The AI plans; the humans are hands. A request enters through one gateway whatever channel it came by, and the gateway's edge is the vault's own tokenising proxy, so nothing of ours, the gateway included, ever holds a real value. The planner turns it into steps and proposes an authority envelope, the limits the user confirms with one tap: spend cap, contactable parties, committable acts, delegated identity acts, validity window. A classifier sorts each step into one of three classes. An ai step runs in our managed browser, on our phone line or through our mail relay. A human step goes to a worker as a task card that names the customer only as a pseudonym and the values only as labels. A user step, an OTP, a biometric, a PIN, an act the jurisdiction reserves to the customer, bounces to the user's own device. Every step closes with a code from a fixed vocabulary; every irreversible step inside the envelope is checked by a second party before it fires; every task that reaches a report closes with the user's one-tap verdict, and a task refused at intake or lapsed unjudged ends without one, the ledger saying which. 3The two fatal failures, made structurally impossible

3.1A worker sees something they should not

Sensitive values live in a bought tokenisation vault, regionally hosted. Humans, the AI and almost all of our software handle aliases only, like {{pan}}. One small isolated component swaps the real value in at the last boundary: into a form field in a browser we run, rendered as dots in what the worker sees; onto a phone line as synthesised speech or keypad tones while the worker's audio is muted in both directions; into an email through our relay. Every execution surface is ours; a worker never uses their own tools. Evidence is redacted before it is stored and verified by an independent second pass, so no raw screenshot, recording or transcript is ever human-viewable. 03 names every path by which a person could see a value, the evidence system included, and closes each one.

3.2An irreversible act the user did not want

Every task carries an authority envelope: spend cap, contactable parties, committable acts, delegated identity acts, validity window. Delegation is total inside it and impossible outside it. Every step is classified against a point-of-no-return test. Irreversible and outside the envelope: it bounces to the user's device. Irreversible and inside it: dual control, so whichever party executes, the other checks before it fires. 01 shows the envelope from the user's side; 02 specifies the classifier and the state machine that never lets a task be forgotten.

4Three markets, one build

India is the home base for build economics: high technical ability, a low-cost geography, a large English-speaking population. The US and the UK are where commercial viability lies. We build for all three from one universal core, as 02 lists it: planner, classifier, task cards, alias vault and substitution, authority envelopes, state machine, verification, worker console, access ledger, intake gateway, and the policy layer's mechanism, whose flag table is the per-market half. Each country adds a passport adapter to its identity and payment rails, a legal pack and a shift on the 24/7 wheel. Expansion is that unit repeated. 02 draws the seam; 06 is the playbook.

5What exists today, and what this publication is

What exists is this publication: a design-and-planning record produced in September 2026 by the founder, Dhruv Sharan, with Claude Code as writer, designer, researcher and verifier under a written brief. There is no code, no customer and no pilot yet. The founder's decisions are DR-001 to DR-011, DR-015 and DR-016, DR-001 fixed as a gateway with its front door amber; DR-012 to DR-014 were taken during the build against the founder's bars and are marked so in the decision ledger; every record carries what it rejected and what it closes off. Every open question is listed with an owner and a trigger in 07. The site is served as plain HTML from a repository with no build step; every page prints to a clean PDF; and the address is unguessable and kept out of search indexes until the access question in 07 is settled.

A sceptical reader should be able to get from this page and the thesis why this wins where the flat-fee players cannot follow. An engineer who has never met the founder should be able to start building the harness from 01, 02 and 03 without asking a question the publication does not answer. Those are the bars the publication was built against.

6How to read this publication

Five status words run through every page, at two levels. On a claim, the chip says how much to trust the sentence it follows. On a page, the same word in the stamp and the control block says how settled the document is. Verified is never a page status; when several apply, the precedence is amber, then to-verify, then assumption, then decision, so a page's status is a function of its own claims and a harness checks it. A verified chip is always followed by a bracketed reference to a numbered source at the foot of the page. In this edition, verified means, in the words of DR-013, that the claim's text was found in a search-engine excerpt attributed to the named source, or in a named and reputable third party's report of it, or, the stronger case, in the official text read in full from a faithful mirror on a code-hosting site; page fetches were refused for almost every host, code-hosting domains excepted. DR-013 is the authority for that standard, and 07 carries the re-read as an open item.
