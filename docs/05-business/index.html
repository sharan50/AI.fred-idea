On this page

1Why this page exists now

The previous edition deferred economics until after a Series A or B. That position was wrong and has been withdrawn. A seed process asks for the shape of the unit economics before it asks for anything else, and a plan whose cost per task might exceed its price per task cannot be tested by waiting. The engine is still unchosen, but an engine is a set of parameters, and a model built on parameters tells you which capability differences are worth paying for. That is the order this page now works in.

Everything below rests on informed priors, not on customers. The company has none yet.

Assumptionassumption Every number on this page is modelled rather than measured. The simulation, its scenarios and its limits are described on the model page, which names the run, the seed and the date the figures are quoted from; the instrument that replaces the priors is the measurement protocol, which is thirty tasks and about a week of work.

2The task model

Every task resolves into one of five outcomes, and the mix is what sets the cost base.

Bounced is deliberately not folded into human-led. It costs almost no labour and therefore barely moves headcount, but it is the outcome that spends the product promise, because the user is handed the task back. It is a product metric, not a cost metric.

3The one ratio that decides everything

Delivery cost scales with usage, not with users. The governing number is therefore human minutes per user per month, which is the product of promised task volume and blended handling time, set against average revenue per user.

At 158 minutes a month against a $37 average there is no scale at which the arithmetic recovers, because a generalist minute fully loaded costs roughly Rs 7 to Rs 16 depending on seniority mix and year, and the subscription buys only a few of them. At 61 minutes against a $70 average the business is a normal, capital-light services-plus-software company. assumption

Four parameters move the ratio, and they are the four the plan commits to. The run that shows what they are worth moves six others as well, which is set out in section 5, because the gap between the two configurations should not be read as though all of it were planned.

4The cost split

At 1,000 users on the earlier and now superseded promise of two tasks a day, the split of delivery cost ran labour 73 per cent, tokens 24 per cent, everything else 3 per cent. Figure 5.1 is the wider picture, the four blocks of the cost base month by month, in which delivery labour is one line among four; payment fees and make-goods are charged on revenue and sit outside it. assumption Two things follow and both are durable.

First, the intuition that tokens are the dominant cost is wrong at current prices. Token cost per task falls with model prices but rises with agent loop length and context size, and in the modelling those two roughly cancel for around two years. Tokens are never the largest line in any scenario.

Second, fixed overhead is trivial. Space and connectivity are about two per cent of cost, which is why the cheap-rent argument never moved the answer. The step costs that do move it are the foreign entities in section 8, and the floor that carries them, which is staged in DR-006 and priced as a step in 04.

Conservative and optimistic lines are drawn from coherent families of paths, ranked by outcome, rather than by blending unrelated percentiles. The planning line weights them 65 to 35, conservative to optimistic.

One result is worth stating because it is easy to miss. In the previously specified configuration, the paths that end with the best cash position are the ones that grew slowly. A model in which growth is the thing that hurts you is the formal statement of negative unit economics.

Three qualifications belong with that table, and a reader who finds them for themselves will trust the rest of the page less. First, the planning line is an average taken within a family of paths, month by month, so it is not a path and its statistics are not a path's. The trough of an average is shallower than the average of troughs: the $1.58m above sits at the forty-fifth percentile of what a path in this model actually needs, where the median path needs 15.0 crore, about $1.69m, the seventy-fifth percentile 21.5 crore and the ninetieth 30.0 crore, about $3.37m. assumption The round sizes in section 7 are set above the ninetieth percentile, so the plan is not under-raised; the trough is simply a smaller number than the one it is read as. The same arithmetic applies to the crossing in Figure 5.2: averaged lines cross at month 23, the median path at month 25.

Second, the configuration behind the right-hand column moves six parameters besides the four levers: a lower churn floor, a lower engineering headcount exponent, a lower tail handling floor, a faster approach to the automation ceiling, and the two foreign prices. Two of those carry real weight. Putting the churn floor back where the previous configuration had it costs 1.9 crore of peak cash and 4.6 points of the chance of ever turning profitable; putting the engineering exponent back costs 6.6 crore and 5.6 points, which is more than either automation lever is worth. assumption They are assumptions in the run and commitments nowhere, which is why both appear among the five measurements in section 10.

Third, the model prices a user and not a task: revenue is users times price, and the task allowance enters only as cost. The metered allowance of DR-024 therefore earns nothing in these numbers, and the tasks-per-user lever is modelled as a cost cap alone. That understates the lever and it means the commercial decision of the revision has not yet been tested by the instrument that is supposed to test it. Rebuilding the revenue side to price the tail is an open item in 07.

6How users arrive

This section exists because the model was interrogated and gave an uncomfortable answer. On the organic-only run the record used to publish, the rate at which users arrive in month 1 owned thirty-four per cent of the variance of the cash need, forty per cent of whether the business ever turned profitable at all, and sixty per cent of the month it crossed over, while the thirty-one drivers on the delivery side owned under seven per cent of the first of those between them. A plan whose largest variable has no mechanism behind it cannot be planned against, which is why growth is now two mechanisms rather than a rate, as DR-025 fixes, and why the published line carries them. assumption

Putting media in changes the sensitivity as much as it changes the trajectory. On the published line the arrival rate owns ten per cent of the variance of the cash need rather than thirty-four, and the largest single driver is now G&A at 100 users, at twenty-eight per cent, followed by the India price at twelve. Whether the business ever turns profitable has almost no variance left to explain, because every path does. What growth still decides is when: it owns thirty-five per cent of the variance of the crossover month. assumption The cliff of the organic-only run is a cliff you fall off by not being found, and media is what stops you standing on it.

6.1The two mechanisms

Organic is word of mouth and referral, and it is the engine. A share of the installed base arrives each month, most likely eighteen per cent and between ten and thirty, decaying by a little under one per cent of itself a month, against a reachable Indian market between twenty-five thousand and two hundred and fifty thousand users at the tenth and ninetieth percentiles, starting from two hundred users in month 1. Its cost is a referral incentive per arrival, not a media rate. The mechanism is conditional rather than automatic: word of mouth at that rate holds only while the product is worth recommending, and the condition is a net promoter score of 60 or above on the paying cohort, which the founder sets as the floor. Below it the organic half of this section is not assumed to hold, and media is not the remedy: a score under 60 is a product finding and the verdict system of 01, 5 is where it is answered. assumption

Marketing is a monthly budget, fifteen per cent of revenue once there is revenue and a floor of three lakh a month before there is, converted into arrivals at a cost that rises as the budget grows, because the cheap audience is bought first. Three channels carry it: short video on Instagram bought on performance at three fifths of the budget, AI-influencer placements at three tenths, and coverage in the technology and business papers at the remaining tenth, which is earned rather than bought and therefore both the cheapest arrival in the mix and the one that cannot be scaled with money. assumption

The two compound into each other. Every bought arrival joins the installed base that word of mouth then works on, so a rupee of media buys a user and the users that user brings. That is what Figure 5.5 shows and it is the reason to spend early rather than later: the bought share of arrivals peaks in the first months and falls to about a seventh by month 60 even as the absolute spend rises throughout. Media is the primer. Word of mouth is the engine.

6.2What a user is worth, and what one may cost

On the model's own figures a user is worth about $1,172 after the cost of serving them: $64 a month at month 36, against a 4.2 per cent monthly churn, which is a mean life of twenty-four months, at a seventy-eight per cent delivery margin. At $120 a paid arrival the payback is under three months; at $250 it is five. assumption

Against the organic-only floor of section 6.5, which needs 24.5 crore of peak cash on the median path and turns profitable in eighty-nine paths out of a hundred, spending fifteen per cent of revenue at $120 a paid arrival needs 15.0 crore, crosses over at month 25 rather than month 40, and reaches ninety thousand users rather than thirty thousand by month 60. The paid cost at which that advantage disappears is about $1,124 an arrival at fifteen per cent of revenue and about $671 at twenty-five; at five per cent of revenue the cash need is never worse than organic alone, even at $2,100. assumption The reading is that the budget should be modest and the spend should start early: a small budget is robust to being wrong about the cost, and a large one is not.

6.3The number nobody has

What a paid arrival actually costs. Nobody has run a reel, and this is the one prior in the whole model that two weeks and a small media budget can replace, which is why it is the first of the five measurements in section 10 rather than the last. The range it could sit in is wide and the publication already contains both ends of it. The geography model assumes a median cost to acquire of Rs 1,150 in India, Rs 8,500 in the United Kingdom and Rs 11,200 in the United States, which at 89 rupees are about $13, $95 and $126; those are referral costs rather than media costs. The partner review at 08 records a consumer fintech benchmark of US$1,340 to $2,140, marked to-verify there, struck on paid acquisition at a $149 monthly price of its own estimating. assumption The break-even above sits inside that range and towards its upper end, which is the useful thing to know before any money is spent: paid acquisition has to be very expensive indeed before it stops paying, and the cheap end of the range would change the shape of the company.

6.4Growth changes sign with the unit economics

In the previously specified configuration, moving the arrival rate from its bottom tenth to its top takes the peak cash need from 96 crore to 199: growth is the thing that hurts you, which is what negative contribution per user means. With the four levers the same move takes it from 52 crore to 13. assumption So the order matters: the ratio of section 3 is established by measurement first, and only then is the budget worth raising, because a plan that spends on acquisition before the ratio holds is spending to lose money faster.

6.5What the page above still carries

Every number in sections 5 and 7 is now the run with both mechanisms in it, at $120 a paid arrival and fifteen per cent of revenue in media: models/sim_growth.csv, twenty thousand paths at seed 20260913. The organic-only run is kept as models/sim_viable.csv and is the floor, the case where nothing is spent on being found: it needs 17.3 crore rather than 14.1, crosses over at month 29 rather than 23, and reaches thirty-seven thousand users by month 60 rather than ninety-five thousand. The model that produces the published line rebuilds the floor character for character when marketing is switched off, so the two are comparable path by path and the gap between them is what media is worth on this plan rather than an artefact of two different runs.

7Funding

Sized as window burn times a 1.3 buffer. Raise against the conservative line, plan against the planning line, and never against the optimistic one. The arithmetic behind them has moved with the published line and is now small: 16.0 crore through month 18 on the planning line and 17.6 on the conservative one, which at 89 rupees are $1.79m and $1.98m, and after month 18 the planning line asks for 2.3 crore more and the conservative one 9.0, with nothing needed after month 36. assumption The table below is deliberately well above that, and these round sizes are guidance and not a plan. They are what the arithmetic of a modelled line asks for, sized against the conservative one and buffered; the moment the build starts, the usage, the retention and the cost of acquiring a user will all move, and the round that gets raised will be sized against what a lead investor will price and what the next eighteen months actually need rather than against this table. Read the shape, not the figures: a seed that buys measurement, an A that buys a proven unit economics and a second market, and a B that is optional if the first two land.

8Geography

Purchasing power is the argument for expansion and it holds: a US user paying $105 costs the same to serve as an Indian user paying $52, because the delivery floor is in Bengaluru either way. assumption

Three things qualify it. Acquisition cost tracks competition for attention rather than purchasing power, running ten times the Indian figure against a two times price difference. Staff serving those hours carry a night and language premium. And automation share is worse abroad at entry, because the workflow library is built against Indian institutions.

The decisive finding is about timing rather than principle.

9The commerce layer

The company already executes purchases the user asked for. Routing them through channels that pay a take rate is a margin on the existing business rather than a new one, worth roughly 1.2 per cent of routed spend once the share of transactions with a payable channel is allowed for. It reaches around twelve per cent of revenue by month 60, but because it is close to pure margin it more than doubles contribution at month 36. assumption It needs merchant leverage rather than users, which arrives somewhere around four to seven thousand subscribers.

It is constrained by one rule that is not negotiable: a merchant may never pay for placement in a recommendation. The moment they can, the assistant stops being the user's agent, which is the property the subscription is bought for. The rule is recorded in DR-024 and the withdrawal of data sold as an asset is in DR-019.

10What replaces this page

Nothing here is evidence. Five things must be measured, and the order below is the model's own rather than this record's intuition, which had the first of them missing entirely. They are: what a paid arrival costs, which decides whether the media half of the plan is worth spending at all and which two weeks of spend settles; G&A at a hundred users, which on the published line is the largest single driver of the cash need; twelve-month churn on a paying cohort, with the net promoter score beside it, because the organic mechanism rests on both; minutes per user per month; and whether the engineering headcount exponent falls as the workflow library generalises. Willingness to pay above $50 sits with the second of those, being the India price the sensitivity now ranks second. assumption The first is the subject of section 6 and has no instrument yet beyond a budget, which is the largest hole in this page. The second is answered by the measurement protocol, which is thirty tasks and about a week of work. The third and fourth need a pilot cohort and a priced waiting list. The fifth is visible only over time, in whether the tenth institution costs less to add than the second. All five are open items with owners in 07.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title. This publication is a design record, not an offer.
