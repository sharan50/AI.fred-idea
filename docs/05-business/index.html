On this page

1The deferral

Task economics, pricing and unit costs are deferred. The founder has ruled them a post-Series A or B problem, and the ruling is recorded as DR-015. This publication therefore carries no price point, no cost per task, no margin, no headcount model and no market-size claim of our own. The thesis in 00 carries its argument without them: the wedge is high-consequence tasks, and the trust architecture is the moat, whatever the price turns out to be.

Two things stay in the publication that a reader might mistake for economics. The 24/7 shift design and the site controls in 04 are operational design: they describe how the floor runs and why office-mandatory operation is part of the trust guarantee. No cost model is attached to them. The build-versus-buy table in 06 records what we build and what we buy and why; it carries no vendor prices.

2The trigger for reopening

The question reopens when a Series A or Series B closes, whichever the founder names as the point at which the venture must price and cost itself against its pilot's verdict data. Until that trigger fires, an economics section is not written, and any number that appears in a design page is labelled illustrative and carries no pricing meaning.

The trigger is the founder's to fire, and it is stated here with its owner; 07 does not repeat it.

3What will be needed when it reopens

Three inputs, none of which exists honestly before a pilot has run:

  1. Price points per market and per task type, set against what the verdict rates in each vertical show customers value.
  2. Minutes per task, measured from the step trace: minutes of worker time per step class, per vertical, per market, including dual-control checks and exceptions handling.
  3. Pool sizing against the 24/7 floor: the smallest L1, L2 and L3 complement that keeps every shift on the wheel staffed, and the utilisation that floor implies at pilot volumes.

The step trace and the verdict record that the harness produces are designed so that the second and third inputs fall out of operation rather than out of estimation. That is the only economic design choice this publication makes. The data story that DR-019 names and leaves open, what the context store could be worth beyond the user's own tasks, reopens with the economics and by a decision record, as 07 records.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, to do in your personal life what the general assistants do, and that can be trusted with the high-value, low-volume tasks you dread a few times a year: the ones that carry real consequence. AI.fred is a working title. This publication is a design record, not an offer.
