1Why this page exists now

The previous edition deferred economics until after a Series A or B. That position was wrong and has been withdrawn. A seed process asks for the shape of the unit economics before it asks for anything else, and a plan whose cost per task might exceed its price per task cannot be tested by waiting. The engine is still unchosen, but an engine is a set of parameters, and a model built on parameters tells you which capability differences are worth paying for. That is the order this page now works in.

Everything below rests on informed priors, not on customers. The company has none yet.

Assumptionassumption Every number on this page is modelled rather than measured. The simulation, its scenarios and its limits are described on the model page, which names the run, the seed and the date the figures are quoted from; the instrument that replaces the priors is the measurement protocol, which is thirty tasks and about a week of work.

3The one ratio that decides everything

Delivery cost scales with usage, not with users. The governing number is therefore human minutes per user per month, which is the product of promised task volume and blended handling time, set against average revenue per user.

At 158 minutes a month against a $37 average there is no scale at which the arithmetic recovers, because a generalist minute fully loaded costs roughly Rs 7 to Rs 16 depending on seniority mix and year, and the subscription buys only a few of them. At 61 minutes against a $70 average the business is a normal, capital-light services-plus-software company. assumption

Four parameters move the ratio, and they are the four the plan commits to. The run that shows what they are worth moves six others as well, which is set out in section 5, because the gap between the two configurations should not be read as though all of it were planned.

4The cost split

At 1,000 users on the earlier and now superseded promise of two tasks a day, the split of delivery cost ran labour 73 per cent, tokens 24 per cent, everything else 3 per cent. Figure 5.1 is the wider picture, the four blocks of the cost base month by month, in which delivery labour is one line among four; payment fees and make-goods are charged on revenue and sit outside it. assumption Two things follow and both are durable.

First, the intuition that tokens are the dominant cost is wrong at current prices. Token cost per task falls with model prices but rises with agent loop length and context size, and in the modelling those two roughly cancel for around two years. Tokens are never the largest line in any scenario.

Second, fixed overhead is trivial. Space and connectivity are about two per cent of cost, which is why the cheap-rent argument never moved the answer. The step costs that do move it are the foreign entities in section 8, and the floor that carries them, which is staged in DR-006. Neither model prices that floor as a step. Both carry a per-head seat cost rising from about Rs 3,200 a month to at most 1.9 times that as the floor grows, and no term at all for the jump a shift or a market adds, so at the cash trough the whole attested site of 04, 2, badge-controlled zones, camera coverage, a two-person glass-walled zone and locked-down workstations across three shifts, is carried at about Rs 5.2 lakh a month, 1.3 per cent of cost. assumption That is a co-working desk and not an attested floor, and the step arrives in the same months as the trough the seed is sized against. 04 pointed here for the price and this page pointed there; neither had it, and it is an open item in 07.

Third, and larger than either: no tax. Neither model carries a consumption tax term of any kind, on either side. If the $52 in India and the $85 in the United Kingdom are gross of the tax the customer pays rather than net of it, about a seventh of Indian revenue and a sixth of British revenue in every figure on this page does not exist. Taking both out, which is models/sim_growth_net_of_tax.csv, moves the cash trough from 14.1 crore to 18.7 and the conservative line from 20.5 to 28.5, takes revenue per user at month 36 from $70 to $59, pushes the crossing from month 23 to month 26, and takes the share of paths profitable from month 36 onward from 84 to 71 per cent. assumption Which of the two readings is right is a question about the price, not about the model, and it decides whether the priced waiting list of section 10 has to test $52 or $61. It is an open item in 07 and, until it is answered, the safe reading of every figure on this page is the net-of-tax one.

One result is worth stating because it is easy to miss. In the previously specified configuration, the paths that end with the best cash position are the ones that grew slowly. A model in which growth is the thing that hurts you is the formal statement of negative unit economics.

Three qualifications belong with that table, and a reader who finds them for themselves will trust the rest of the page less. First, the planning line is an average taken within a family of paths, month by month, so it is not a path and its statistics are not a path's. The trough of an average is shallower than the average of troughs: the $1.58m above sits at the forty-fifth percentile of what a path in this model actually needs, where the median path needs 15.0 crore, about $1.69m, the seventy-fifth percentile 21.5 crore and the ninetieth 30.0 crore, about $3.37m. assumption The round sizes in section 7 are set above the ninetieth percentile, so the plan is not under-raised; the trough is simply a smaller number than the one it is read as. The same arithmetic applies to the crossing in Figure 5.2: averaged lines cross at month 23, the median path at month 25.

Second, the configuration behind the right-hand column moves six parameters besides the four levers: a lower churn floor, a lower engineering headcount exponent, a lower tail handling floor, a faster approach to the automation ceiling, and the two foreign prices. Two of those carry real weight. Putting the churn floor back where the previous configuration had it costs 1.9 crore of peak cash and 4.6 points of the chance of ever turning profitable; putting the engineering exponent back costs 6.6 crore and 5.6 points, which is more than either automation lever is worth. assumption Those two deltas are measured on the organic-only floor, because the reversion run exists only for it; on the published line the chance of ever turning profitable has almost no variance left to move, so the cash figures are the half of each delta that still reads across. They are assumptions in the run and commitments nowhere, which is why both appear among the five measurements in section 10.

Third, the model prices a user and not a task: revenue is users times price, and the task allowance enters only as cost. The metered allowance of DR-024 therefore earns nothing in these numbers. This page has said the lever is modelled as a cost cap alone, and that was wrong in a way worth correcting: there is no cap. Tasks a user a month is a demand prior drawn between 15 and 40, whose mode is the 26 of Table 5.2, and 56 per cent of the published paths average above that figure, by 2.6 tasks a month. assumption So the model neither bills the overage the allowance exists to bill nor declines to serve it: it is missing on both sides, and the net sign of the omission is unknown rather than conservative. The commercial decision of the revision has not been tested by the instrument that is supposed to test it, and rebuilding the revenue side to price the tail is an open item in 07.

6How users arrive

This section exists because the model was interrogated and gave an uncomfortable answer. On the organic-only run the record used to publish, the rate at which users arrive in month 1 owned thirty-four per cent of the variance of the cash need, forty per cent of whether the business ever turned profitable at all, and sixty per cent of the month it crossed over, while the thirty-one drivers on the delivery side owned under seven per cent of the first of those between them. A plan whose largest variable has no mechanism behind it cannot be planned against, which is why growth is now two mechanisms rather than a rate, as DR-025 fixes, and why the published line carries them. assumption

Putting media in changes the sensitivity as much as it changes the trajectory. On the published line the arrival rate owns ten per cent of the variance of the cash need rather than thirty-four, and the largest single driver is now G&A at 100 users, at twenty-eight per cent, followed by the India price at twelve. Whether the business ever turns profitable has almost no variance left to explain, because every path does. What growth still decides is when: it owns thirty-five per cent of the variance of the crossover month. assumption The cliff of the organic-only run is a cliff you fall off by not being found, and media is what stops you standing on it.

6.1The two mechanisms

Organic is word of mouth and referral, and it is the engine. A share of the installed base arrives each month, most likely eighteen per cent and between ten and thirty, decaying by a little under one per cent of itself a month, against a reachable Indian market between twenty-five thousand and two hundred and fifty thousand users at the tenth and ninetieth percentiles, starting from two hundred users in month 1. Its cost is a referral incentive per arrival, not a media rate. The mechanism is conditional rather than automatic: word of mouth at that rate holds only while the product is worth recommending, and the condition is a net promoter score of 60 or above on the paying cohort, which the founder sets as the floor. Below it the organic half of this section is not assumed to hold, and media is not the remedy: a score under 60 is a product finding and the verdict system of 01, 5 is where it is answered. assumption

Marketing is a monthly budget, fifteen per cent of revenue once there is revenue and a floor of three lakh a month before there is, converted into arrivals at a cost that rises as the budget grows, because the cheap audience is bought first. Three channels carry it: short video on Instagram bought on performance at three fifths of the budget, AI-influencer placements at three tenths, and coverage in the technology and business papers at the remaining tenth, which is earned rather than bought and therefore both the cheapest arrival in the mix and the one that cannot be scaled with money. assumption

The two compound into each other. Every bought arrival joins the installed base that word of mouth then works on, so a rupee of media buys a user and the users that user brings. That is what Figure 5.5 shows and it is the reason to spend early rather than later: the bought share of arrivals peaks in the first months and falls to about a sixth by month 60 even as the absolute spend rises throughout. Media is the primer. Word of mouth is the engine.

6.2What a user is worth, and what one may cost

On the published line a user is worth about $1,291 on delivery margin alone: $70 a month at month 36, against a 4.3 per cent monthly churn, which is a mean life of twenty-three months, at an eighty per cent margin after labour and tokens. That is the conventional figure and it is not a figure after the cost of serving a user, because the eighty per cent excludes engineering, compliance, general and administrative cost, one-time costs and payment fees, which Figure 5.1 shows are together the larger half of the cost base. All in, the same month contributes $27 a user, or $37 before any acquisition spend, so the same arithmetic gives $637 to $865 rather than $1,291. assumption The higher figure is the one an investor will compare against a cost per arrival and the lower is the one that pays for the company; both belong on the page. On the organic-only floor the delivery-margin figure is $1,172, at $64 a month and a seventy-eight per cent margin, and that is what this page carried before the promotion. Against the $122 an arrival that media costs in month 1, or the $130 it averages over the first year, the payback is under three months. Against the $1,368 it costs by month 60, below, there is no payback: at $56 of margin a month it would take about twenty-five months to recover, and the mean life is twenty-three. assumption So the marginal bought user stops paying for itself somewhere before month 60 on the model's own figures, which is the argument for capping the budget rather than holding it at a share of revenue, and nothing in the plan caps it yet. It is an open item in 07.

Against the organic-only floor of section 6.5, which needs 24.5 crore of peak cash on its median path, not on its planning line and turns profitable in eighty-nine paths out of a hundred, spending fifteen per cent of revenue at $120 a paid arrival needs 15.0 crore, crosses over at month 25 rather than month 40, and reaches ninety thousand users rather than thirty thousand by month 60. The paid cost at which that advantage disappears is about $1,124 an arrival at fifteen per cent of revenue and about $671 at twenty-five; at five per cent of revenue the cash need is never worse than organic alone, even at $2,100. assumption The reading is that the budget should be modest and the spend should start early: a small budget is robust to being wrong about the cost, and a large one is not.

6.3The number nobody has

What a paid arrival actually costs. Nobody has run a reel, and this is the one prior in the whole model that two weeks and a small media budget can replace, which is why it is the first of the five measurements in section 10 rather than the last. The range it could sit in is wide and the publication already contains both ends of it. The geography model assumes a median cost to acquire of Rs 1,150 in India, Rs 8,500 in the United Kingdom and Rs 11,200 in the United States, which at 89 rupees are about $13, $95 and $126. On the published line those figures no longer price an arrival: they set the ratio between markets, and what a market actually pays is a media budget plus a referral incentive scaled by that same ratio. The partner review at 08 records a consumer fintech benchmark of US$1,340 to $2,140, marked to-verify there, struck on paid acquisition at a $149 monthly price of its own estimating. assumption The break-even above sits inside that range and towards its upper end, which is the useful thing to know before any money is spent: paid acquisition has to be very expensive indeed before it stops paying, and the cheap end of the range would change the shape of the company.

One thing about the $120 needs saying plainly, because it is easy to read as a cost and it is an input. It is the low-volume price of short video, the channel that carries three fifths of the budget, and not of the cheapest channel, which is earned press at $42 and cannot be scaled with money. Weighted by the modelled mix it is $148 at zero volume, and because a channel's cost rises as its budget does, the cost the run actually pays climbs with the budget: about $122 an arrival in month 1, $140 by month 12, $471 by month 36 and $1,368 by month 60, all on the planning line the rest of this page uses. assumption So the published line does not assume cheap acquisition at scale. It assumes a cheap start and a saturating channel, and by month 60 it is paying the middle of the benchmark band above. The break-even figures do not test it: they are stated against the same input anchor, so they say how high the anchor may go and not how high the effective cost may go. Testing a budget cap needs a sweep over the budget rule rather than over the anchor, and the model does not carry one.

6.4Growth changes sign with the unit economics

In the previously specified configuration, moving the arrival rate from its bottom tenth to its top takes the peak cash need from 96 crore to 199: growth is the thing that hurts you, which is what negative contribution per user means. With the four levers the same move takes it from 52 crore to 13. assumption So the order matters: the ratio of section 3 is established by measurement first, and only then is the budget worth raising, because a plan that spends on acquisition before the ratio holds is spending to lose money faster.

6.5What the page above still carries

Every number in sections 5 and 7 is now the run with both mechanisms in it, at $120 a paid arrival and fifteen per cent of revenue in media: models/sim_growth.csv, twenty thousand paths at seed 20260913. The organic-only run is kept as models/sim_viable.csv and is the floor, the case where nothing is spent on being found: on its planning line it needs 17.3 crore rather than 14.1, crosses over at month 29 rather than 23, and reaches thirty-seven thousand users by month 60 rather than ninety-five thousand; the 24.5 crore of section 6.2 is the same floor on its median path, which is the comparison the break-even is struck against. On its conservative line, which is the line section 7 says to raise against, the floor is a different proposition: it needs 49.6 crore, about $5.57m, troughs at month 56, shows its first positive month at month 57 and ends month 60 still 48.7 crore behind, with nine thousand users. assumption So the downside of the media bet is $2.30m against $5.57m and not the three crore the planning lines show. The reason is the band construction: the conservative family is the fifteen per cent of paths with the worst terminal cash, and in a world with no media those are the companies nobody found. The model that produces the published line rebuilds the floor character for character when marketing is switched off, so the two are comparable path by path and the gap between them is what media is worth on this plan rather than an artefact of two different runs.

One property of that run needs stating plainly, because it is not obvious from the page and it cuts across section 8. The run opens the United Kingdom around month 20 and the United States around month 36, which is the late sequence section 8 finds worse than not expanding, and it charges each market's entry cost and its monthly counsel but not the standing cost of a foreign entity, and no night premium. The geography model charges that standing cost at Rs 0.95 crore a month by month 36 and Rs 2.60 by month 60, which is Rs 7.6 crore cumulatively to month 36 and Rs 55.5 to month 60. The same run also buys foreign paid arrivals at the Indian channel price rather than at the ratio section 8 applies, and pays the Indian referral incentive on foreign word of mouth. assumption

So the published line is neither the India-only case nor any of the geography cases. Suppressing both launches in the same run, which is models/sim_growth_india_only.csv, takes revenue per user at month 36 from $70 to $59, users at month 60 from ninety-five thousand to sixty thousand, and the cash need from 14.1 crore to 12.8: roughly a third of the users and eleven dollars of the revenue per user are foreign, and the cost of being in those markets is not in the number. Every figure in sections 5 and 7 reads that way, the funding plan included. Which run is the plan of record is a decision rather than a modelling question, and until it is taken the safe reading of sections 5 and 7 is that they understate the cost of the geography they contain. It is an open item in 07.

7Funding

Sized as window burn times a 1.3 buffer. Raise against the conservative line, plan against the planning line, and never against the optimistic one. The arithmetic behind them has moved with the published line and is now small: 16.0 crore through month 18 on the planning line and 17.6 on the conservative one, which at 89 rupees are $1.79m and $1.98m, and after month 18 the planning line asks for 2.3 crore more and the conservative one 9.0, with nothing needed after month 36. assumption Those figures are the published line's, and the published line is not the sequence this record commits to. On the foreign-led sequence of DR-023, compared without its commerce layer, the same arithmetic asks for 22.3 crore through month 18 and 22.3 crore again after it, which is $2.51m and $2.50m: a seed 27 per cent larger than stated and a Series A two and a half times it. assumption So the seed band below is not well above the arithmetic on the sequence of record; its floor is exactly equal to it. The table below is deliberately above the published line's figures, and these round sizes are guidance and not a plan. They are what the arithmetic of a modelled line asks for, sized against the conservative one and buffered; the moment the build starts, the usage, the retention and the cost of acquiring a user will all move, and the round that gets raised will be sized against what a lead investor will price and what the next eighteen months actually need rather than against this table. Read the shape, not the figures: a seed that buys measurement, an A that buys a proven unit economics and a second market, and a B that is optional if the first two land.

8Geography

Purchasing power is the argument for expansion and it holds: a US user paying $105 costs the same to serve as an Indian user paying $52, because the delivery floor is in Bengaluru either way. assumption

Three things qualify it. Acquisition cost tracks competition for attention rather than purchasing power: the geography model's own per-market figures put a user at 7.4 times the Indian cost in the United Kingdom and 9.7 times in the United States, against a two times price difference. That ratio is what prices both halves of acquisition abroad on the published line, the media and the referral incentive alike, so a single wrong ratio is wrong twice. Staff serving those hours carry a night and language premium. And automation share is worse abroad at entry, because the workflow library is built against Indian institutions.

What the ratio means in money, at the budgets the run spends: a paid arrival costs about $885 in the United Kingdom and $1,165 in the United States for short video at low volume, $1,093 and $1,439 weighted by the modelled channel mix, and, by the time the budget has grown, far more: about $1,518 and $1,419 an arrival at month 36 in the late sequence, and, in the foreign-led sequence this record actually adopts, about $2,938 and $1,839 at month 36 and $8,423 and $5,352 at month 60. assumption The consumer fintech figure of $1,340 to $2,140 in the review at 08 is the only outside benchmark the record holds, and the sequence of record runs clean through it: cheaper than the benchmark only while the budget is small, at the benchmark by month 36 in the United States and above it in the United Kingdom, and multiples of it by month 60. That is a budget rule with no cap in it meeting a channel that saturates, and it is the same finding as the one in section 6.2. A referral incentive abroad is scaled by the same ratio, from Rs 250 at home to about Rs 1,844 and Rs 2,428, which is $21 and $27.

The scenarios below carry the two acquisition mechanisms of section 6, so they sit on the same basis as the rest of this page, and with media switched off each of the four rebuilds the organic-only run it replaces exactly, which is what makes the two bases comparable path by path. The decisive finding is about timing rather than principle, and it survives the promotion.

What the promotion changes is the size of the penalty and the case for the alternative. The conservative cash requirement of the late sequence is now roughly double the India-only case rather than nearly triple it, and the foreign-led case, compared like for like without its commerce layer, costs almost as much cash as the late sequence it is preferred over, $3.85m against $3.98m, for about two and a half times the contribution and fourteen more points of the chance of being profitable by month 36. The sequencing conclusion holds; the cash argument for it has largely gone, and what is left is a contribution argument.

The comparison is also flattered by how the conservative line is built. It is an average within the family of paths ranked fifth to twentieth on terminal cash, so it lands at a different percentile of what a path actually needs in each scenario: the seventy-second for India only, the eighty-third for the late sequence, the seventy-sixth for foreign led. One finding is new, and it cuts against the sequence rather than for it. Media makes the mix a function of where the money is spent, and the model splits the budget evenly across live markets, which buys about seven times as many Indian arrivals per rupee. Against the organic-only basis that lifts the foreign share of the late sequence from twelve to twenty-four per cent of users by month 60 and thins it in the foreign-led case from sixty-three to fifty-two, taking revenue per user there from $107 to $92 at month 36 on the same commerce-carrying basis, and to $77 once the commerce layer is taken out of both. Against the allocation rules the record might instead have chosen, the same finding reverses: put the whole budget into India and the late sequence's foreign share at month 60 is seven per cent rather than twenty-four, and its conservative cash need falls from 35.5 crore to 29.9. assumption So the foreign share is a property of the allocation rule and not of expansion. The even split is conservative on cash, which is why it is the one published, and it is not neutral on the mix; nothing in this record decides the rule, and the gap is an open item in 07.

One bound is worth stating, because it is the pessimistic reading of the change above, and it is where the sequence is most exposed. If a word-of-mouth arrival abroad in fact costs what the geography model's own per-market figure says rather than a scaled referral incentive, the India-only case needs 19.7 crore of conservative cash rather than 18.2, the late sequence 43.2 rather than 35.5, and the foreign-led case, on the same commerce-free basis as the table, 52.0 rather than 34.3. assumption In that reading the foreign-led case is the most expensive of the three in cash and is still the better of the two expansions on contribution, Rs 6.76 crore a month at month 36 against 3.42, and on the odds of profitability, 81 per cent against 73. So the cash case for going early does not survive the pessimistic reading of the foreign acquisition cost; the contribution case does. That is the reading a sceptical reader should hold until the cost of an arrival is measured in a foreign market rather than inferred from an Indian one.

The thresholds are therefore gates rather than forecasts: 400 users in the United Kingdom and 600 in the United States to carry the local cost base. Below them a market is a drag; above them each user is the arbitrage. The sequence and the gates are DR-023, which is amber, and the roadmap carries the dates in 06. The gates themselves do not reconcile with the cost model behind this page, which scales a foreign entity's standing cost with its user count: at both gates met the standing cost is about $122,000 a month against about $97,000 of foreign subscription revenue, so on those medians a market at its gate does not even cover the standing cost, let alone the delivery, the engineering or the acquisition behind it. The gates look as though they were struck against the unscaled cost, which is about $57,500 and is covered; with the scaling in, they are about a quarter too low. That is a contradiction between an amber decision and the instrument, it is the founder's to resolve rather than the model's, and it is recorded in 07.

9The commerce layer

The company already executes purchases the user asked for. Routing them through channels that pay a take rate is a margin on the existing business rather than a new one, worth roughly 1.2 per cent of routed spend once the share of transactions with a payable channel is allowed for. It reaches around fourteen per cent of revenue by month 60. Those figures are from the late-expansion scenario, which is the sequence DR-023 rejects, because that is the only commerce run the model carries and there is no India-only one. On the foreign-led sequence the same layer is worth 5.54 crore a month at month 36 and 16.9 per cent of revenue, on Rs 133,700 of routed spend a user, because a foreign basket is four times the Indian one. assumption Because it is close to pure margin it adds Rs 1.68 crore a month of contribution at month 36, which is more than twice the absolute figure of the organic-only run but about two fifths of a subscription base that is no longer near break-even at that month. assumption It needs merchant leverage rather than users, and the threshold behind that is routed spend rather than headcount: meaningful terms at Rs 25 to 50 crore a year. It is constrained by one rule that is not negotiable: a merchant may never pay for placement in a recommendation. Nothing here is evidence. Five things must be measured, and the order below is the model's own where the model has an opinion, which is for four of the five: what a paid arrival costs is not a sampled driver in the sensitivity at all, and the four that are are listed in its order. They are: what a paid arrival costs, which decides whether the media half of the plan is worth spending at all and which two weeks of spend settles; G&A at a hundred users, which on the published line is the largest single driver of the cash need; minutes per user per month; whether the engineering headcount exponent falls as the workflow library generalises; and twelve-month churn on a paying cohort, with the net promoter score beside it, because the organic mechanism rests on both. Churn is last because the sensitivity puts it last, at a fiftieth of the variance G&A owns, which is worth knowing given how much of this page rests on it. Willingness to pay above $50 sits behind all five, as the trigger of the pricing decision rather than of the model, though the India price is the second-ranked driver in the sensitivity overall. assumption The first is the subject of section 6 and has no instrument yet beyond a budget, which is the largest hole in this page. Minutes per user per month is minutes a task times tasks a user, and the protocol measures the first: how many tasks a user actually runs is not something thirty tasks chosen by the founder can establish, and it owns two fifths of the variance of the ratio the whole page turns on. The third is visible only over time, in whether the tenth institution costs less to add than the second. All five are open items with owners in 07.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title.
