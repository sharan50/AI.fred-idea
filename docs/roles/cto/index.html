1Your brief

You own the harness: the planner, the classifier and its step catalogue, the execution surfaces and their controllers, the state machine and its invariant, verification and the evidence pipeline, the context store, the intake gateway, the market adapters and the worker console. You also own the first half of the trust design, the alias model, the substitution component, the surfaces, artefact redaction and the passports, because those are components before they are policy. The two fatal failures are yours to make structurally impossible: nobody on our side sees a real value, and no irreversible act happens outside an envelope. Every closed list the pages restate, the committing actions, the identity acts, the outcome codes, the step classes, is checked by the dependency map, so a change to one is a change to its whole closure. Read the product's envelope and verdict sections and the roadmap's sequence before you build; they say what the harness is for and in what order it earns its keep.

5Key blocks

Architecture, 1 Overview

The harness is a control system wrapped around a planner, not a chat model with tools attached. A request enters through one gateway, becomes a plan of classified steps, and each step executes on a surface we own, closes with a code from a fixed vocabulary and leaves behind a redacted artefact. Every component of ours outside the real-value zone handles aliases only, and the zone holds four of ours: the substitution component, the three surface controllers with their surfaces, the evidence pipeline, and the hash service while it screens a text. A real value exists on our side only inside the real-value zone of Figure 2.1: in the vault and its edge proxy, which are bought and hold the bytes of one message or one rail response until they are tokenised; in the substitution component, in memory for one placement; on the surface field, call leg or outbound store it has just placed into, for as long as the third party takes to consume it; in the evidence pipeline, in memory until redaction is verified; and in the hash service, in memory while it screens a text written on our side (section 8.3). Every task sits in one named state with a live timer until a recorded transition ends it. Those sentences are the architecture; the rest of this page is their consequences.

Architecture, 2 The planner

A plan and an envelope proposal are the planner's output, and its contract is that neither contains a real value. Its inputs are the normalised request, the context-store facts that bear on it (each with confidence and validity), the step templates, which are the catalogue entries of section 3.3 for the step types the task type in the taxonomy in 01 names (the planner's template library that 04 refers to is that part of the catalogue), the flag table and scope block of the user's market and, when re-planning, the task's envelope and closed steps. The planner is a model with a schema-validated output, and the harness rejects a plan that fails validation: each rejection is a recorded plan-rejected event, the planner is re-run with the validator's errors, up to three attempts (illustrative) inside the planning deadline, and at the cap, or when the deadline expires first, the task service acts as section 5.3 specifies for a planning deadline. The model runtime has no vault SDK and the request reaches it already tokenised, so the planner cannot leak what it never held.

Architecture, 3 The classifier

Every step gets exactly one class, one reversibility and one route, by a rule that is the same in every document of this publication. The classes are ai (executed by the harness on a surface, without a person), human (executed by a worker from a task card) and user (bounced to the user's own device). The class says who executes; the reversibility and the envelope say how. An undo route the catalogue names counts under the test below only where the envelope admits the undo's act, because the undo is an act of ours like any other and nothing of ours runs outside the envelope: where the envelope does not admit it, the step is irreversible for that task, whatever the catalogue's default. Task 12's envelope keeps cancel beside schedule, so its booking step stays reversible (sections 3.2 and 7.3). The mail relay is open to a step only where the user has granted their mailbox's send scope, which the classifier reads as it reads the scope block; a mail step for a user without it is the user's, with a draft the harness wrote (DR-018).

Architecture, 4 Execution surfaces

We own every surface, and a worker never touches a task with their own tools. Because the browser, the phone line and the mail relay are ours, the substitution component (isolated as specified in the substitution component in 03) sits at the edge of each, what a worker sees of a value is masked, no value is placed on a call at all (DR-021), and the evidence is redacted before it leaves. A real value exists on a surface only between placement and the third party's consumption of it. Each surface has a controller, and only two of the three controllers may write a placement request, the browser controller and the relay, a call carrying no value to place (4.2): the console never calls the substitution component, and the worker's tap on Place goes from the console to the task service, which hands it to the step's surface controller, which requests the placement (section 4.5). The site controls that keep a worker's own devices out of the room are the site controls in 04; the human side of each surface, the masks and the failure modes, are the surfaces in 03.

Architecture, 5 The state machine

A task cannot be forgotten, because forgetting has no transition. The task service holds every task in exactly one named state, gives every non-terminal state a live timer, records every change of state as an explicit event and hands anything that has lost its timer to the watchdog. The invariant below is the contract; it appears identically on the worker console page, where a person is most tempted to leave something for later.

Architecture, 6 The market adapter model

One core is built once and knows no country; each market is added as a repeatable unit of three parts. The core is the planner, the classifier, the task cards, the alias vault and substitution, the authority envelopes, the state machine, verification, the worker console, the access ledger, the intake gateway and the policy layer's mechanism; the flag table that mechanism reads is the per-market half. The unit per country is a passport adapter, a legal pack and a shift. The harness that keeps a worker in Bengaluru from seeing a value is the same harness everywhere, so a new market never re-argues the trust design. The decision is DR-007; the playbook is the expansion playbook in 06.

Architecture, 7 Verification

Verification is the control loop, and it runs before "done" is ever shown to the user: every step closes with exactly one outcome code from a fixed vocabulary and the structured fields that code requires. The test is the expected outcome written on the step record before execution, so the executor never decides after the fact what success meant. Artefacts attach for audit and dispute only. Outcome codes are not ground truth of quality; the user's verdict is, the improvement loop in the verdict system in 01, split from the control loop by DR-002. The vocabulary is the table on the worker console page; this section specifies the mechanics around it.

Architecture, 8 The evidence pipeline

No raw artefact is ever human-viewable, and the pipeline that guarantees it is part of the harness, not a later clean-up. Every surface emits screenshots, DOM captures, recordings, transcripts and call metadata into four stages: capture, redact, verify, store. Raw artefacts exist only in the pipeline's memory; a person may view an artefact only after verify has passed it, only for audit, dispute or a dual-control check (section 7.2), with the viewing logged. The evidence system is a leak path in its own right, and the threat model in 03 treats it as one; the decision is DR-009, the redaction contract is artefact redaction in 03.

Architecture, 9 The context store

The context store lets the planner act without asking the user things they have already told us, and its design rule is that every fact says where it came from and how much to trust it. It is a temporal knowledge graph: nodes are the user, institutions, documents, people and places; edges are facts with validity intervals. A people node is a natural person other than the user, a nominee, a relative, a contact, and it carries aliases, never values: its name, date of birth, identifiers, contact details and address are aliases of the personal-fact class of Table 3.1 in 03 qualified by the person reference, {{name.nominee}}, {{dob.nominee}}, {{phone.mother}}, vaulted at the proxy like the user's own and carried in both of the hash service's digest sets, so the digest masks, the transcriber's match and the redactor catch them on any surface exactly as they catch the user's; the relationship word, daughter or nominee, is a non-sensitive task field the catalogue entry may name, and no person's name is ever a fact in clear. The brief fixes a Graphiti-style temporal graph; the library choice is recorded in DR-016. Sensitive fields live in the vault: the store holds the alias, never the value, and a fact about a PAN is a fact about {{pan}}. A health or legal matter is a class of its own in Table 3.1 in 03, {{health.condition}}, {{health.medication}}, {{health.result}} and {{legal.matter}}: it enters through the device form's health fields or a rail, is vaulted at the proxy, and is never in clear in the store, on a card, in a step's facts_in_clear or on a call caption; what a booking needs is the speciality or department, a non-sensitive task field the catalogue entry names, never the diagnosis.

Architecture, 10 The intake gateway

Every channel arrives at one door and leaves it as one format, already tokenised. The gateway accepts WhatsApp, the native app, SMS and iMessage, and the web, in text, voice and attachments, and produces the internal request record every other component understands; inbound mail, a reply on the task's thread fetched by the mailbox connector or forwarded by the user to the task's relay address, enters by the same door as a reply and is never a front door. Which channel is the front door in a market is a launch choice, held amber with its trigger in intake in 01 and recorded in DR-001; the gateway is the fixed decision underneath, and nothing downstream knows which channel a request came from.

Worker console, 1 The task card

A task card is the whole of a worker's world for the duration of one step, and it is generated, not written. When a human step reaches ready, the task service's card generator projects the step record from the step record in 02 into a card, with the script the planner wrote from the step template, and offers it to the pool the step type's catalogue entry names in its pool field: L1 generalists, an L2 specialist pod for the vertical, or L3 super-trust, per the tiers in 04. Cards are offered in order of earliest step deadline, then plan order, to every signed-in worker whose tier and pod on the shift roster match the pool; a worker's identity is the console sign-in bound to their badge, and their id, w_..., is on every event they cause. A worker claims a card, the step moves to running, and the card is live until the step closes. A claim survives a dropped connection for a grace interval (illustrative: two minutes), after which the card returns to the pool as a recorded step-state event with the surface released; at shift end a live card is handed over by name under the handover rule in 04 or returned the same way, and a call in progress is finished by the worker on it first. Then it is gone: the console keeps no history a worker can open once a step closes, and a worker never holds two live cards at once.

Worker console, 2 The alias-only rule

Aliases are all a worker ever handles, and the console enforces this by what it is built from rather than by what workers are told. The rule has three layers: what a worker can see, what a worker cannot see, and what the console technically cannot render even if someone tried.

Worker console, 3 Structured outcome codes

Exactly one code from the fixed vocabulary below closes a step, together with the structured fields that code requires, and the console will not submit a result until those fields are present. The vocabulary is the control loop specified in verification in 02: it decides what the task service does next, it is not a judgement of quality, and the user's verdict remains the only measure of that.

Worker console, 4 Dual control from the worker's side

For an irreversible step inside the envelope, whichever party executes, the other checks before it fires, and a worker meets this in two roles. Only an irreversible step enters checking: a reversible step never holds a token, and there is no gate to cut a commitment on one (DR-021), so the card carries no committing sentence and the caption says what the step may not agree, "this step commits nothing", and never brings it here (section 1). As executor, and only as an L2 or L3 worker (L3 alone in the pilot; L1 never fires an irreversible step, per the tiers in 04), the worker prepares the act from the card as usual, but the control that would fire it reads "Send for check" rather than "Fire"; pressing it moves the step to checking, freezes the prepared act, starts the check deadline and shows the worker a waiting state with the timer. On a call the prepared act is the committing sentence: it stays hidden on the card while the telephony controller holds the line, and it is released to the worker only when the check is on the record. The checker for a worker-executed step is the AI, in a separate model call that receives the act in one sentence, the envelope lines it touches, the expected outcome and, as the evidence, the masked text layer of the live surface for the step or an artefact the store holds after verify, and no conversation history. It answers approve or decline with a reason in a schema the harness validates; a call that errors, times out or fails validation is a decline with the reason "checker unavailable". On approve the step returns to running with an approval token, a record signed by the task service that names the check, the envelope version it was checked against, the checker and its expiry, the step deadline or the envelope's validity window, whichever is sooner; the Fire control is enabled only while a valid token exists and the window is open, the surface and the substitutor refuse an irreversible act without one, the token is single-use and reserves the act's amount against the task's remaining cap, the controller consumes it and writes the step's fired marker before the act leaves the surface, and where the act is a submission the surface fires it for the worker; on a call the card releases the sentence and the worker reads it as written, and the step's fired marker is written at the release, so a withdrawal or a narrowing that arrives before it stops the sentence at once, and one that arrives after it means the act fired, which the outcome report states from the fired marker and never from the worker's result. The outcome record carries both identities. On decline the card shows the reason and the step returns to ready, or re-plans if the reason is that the route is wrong.

Worker console, 5 The exceptions board

The exceptions board is where the invariant is enforced by people, and it is a duty, not a dashboard. The watchdog specified in timers, nudges and the watchdog in 02 runs on a fixed cycle and places on the board every task that fails one of its four checks; the task service adds a task whose response window or planning deadline expired without a route. The board has four columns, and a card leaves a column only by an action that restores what was missing or by a recorded transition; there is no dismiss.

Worker console, 6 The invariant

Nowhere is a person more tempted to leave a task for later than at the console, so the rule that forbids it is stated here in exactly the words used in the state machine in 02. The harness checks that the two are character for character the same.

03 Trust and data, 1 The alias model

A real value crosses five boundaries between the user's hands and an institution's, and no person on our side stands inside any of them. This section follows a value along that path, boundary by boundary, and states what may exist on each side. Figure 3.1 draws the zones; section 2 follows one placement; sections 3 and 4 specify the two boundaries most likely to leak. The named paths by which a person could see a value, and their closure, are gathered in the threat model. The customer's one-page version is the trust story.

03 Trust and data, 2 The substitution component

One component holds the right to turn an alias back into a value, and it does one thing with that right: it places the value into a surface we run, writes the line, and tells nobody, its caller included, what the value was. Boundary three is that component's edge. Aliases, target descriptors and a signed step context go in; receipts and ledger lines come out.

03 Trust and data, 3 Execution surfaces

Boundary four is the surface, the one boundary a value must cross, because the institution on the far side needs the value itself. Ownership makes it safe: every surface is ours, a worker never uses their own browser, phone or mailbox, and each surface renders the value as dots or an alias on its human side while its institution side receives it, and the call is not one of them: a value never reaches a call at all (DR-021, 3.2). The component view of the same three surfaces is in 02; this section is the specification of the human side. Two configuration sets it reads are this page's and not the step-type catalogue's, whose columns 02 fixes: the surface settings per market (the browser stream's delay line and the maskers' format set from Table 3.1) and the party register per market (for each contactable party, its declared web domains and their certificates, the numbers we dial, its mail domain, the keys of its voice menu that navigate and the keys that commit, from which a step's catalogue entry takes its menu map (02, Table 2.6), beside it a page map per page naming the controls that navigate, every other control being committing (3.1), and a mask map for each page a step may reach, naming the regions and fields the masker may deliver as text and, for a page marked as an authenticated portal, the whitelist of fields a step may read). Both are read by the controllers and the maskers at run time, versioned and signed with the market's legal pack, and changed only as a recorded change; a page with no mask map clears nothing (3.1).

03 Trust and data, 4 Artefact redaction

The evidence system is the fifth boundary and the one most easily forgotten: a screenshot, a recording or a transcript is a real value written down. So the pipeline lives inside the real-value zone, nothing raw leaves it, and it is designed as a leak path first and a record second. DR-009 fixes this; 02 places the pipeline among the components and draws it; this section is the redaction contract.

03 Trust and data, 5 The passports

A passport is how a value or an authority the user already holds reaches a task without passing through a person: a universal device bounce-back, and per market an adapter to the rails that release documents, financial data and payment authority under the user's own consent. Every rail below is gated to the named user and to an approved counterparty, so the adapter reaches a rail through the user's own session or a regulated partner and never around them, and whatever a rail returns terminates at the vault's edge proxy in front of the adapter (1.2). The interface every adapter implements is in 02; what each rail permits a delegate to do is recorded here as it stood in the research notes of this edition, with the design intent stated where the notes did not reach. DR-004 The authority envelope and the point-of-no-return test, 1 Decision

Every task carries exactly one authority envelope, confirmed by the user with one tap before any step executes, and every step is classified against a point-of-no-return test. The planner proposes the envelope with the plan, least-authority by construction from the task type and the context store; the user confirms it, which takes the task from awaiting-authority to active, or revises it, which returns the task to planning. Inside the envelope we act without asking again; outside it we cannot act at all, and the test in 1.2 sorts every step into execute, dual control, bounce or extension request.

DR-005 An explicit task state machine, with lapsed distinct from withdrawn, 1 Decision

Every task is in exactly one named state, every non-terminal state carries a live timer, and no task reaches a terminal state without an explicit, recorded transition. A user who goes quiet is legitimate: the task moves through visible nudges into lapsed, a terminal state distinct from withdrawn, and a later message from the user revives it. A system that goes quiet is forbidden, as an architectural invariant rather than a procedure: a task with no live timer is an exception, and the watchdog places it on the exceptions board until a person gives it one.

DR-007 A universal core with per-country adapters, legal pack and shift, 1 Decision

We build one universal core and add a thin, named layer per country. The core is country-agnostic, and 02 lists its eleven components: the planner, the classifier, task cards, the alias vault and the substitution component, authority envelopes, the state machine, verification, the worker console, the access ledger, the intake gateway and the policy layer's mechanism. Per country we add passport adapters to its identity and financial rails, a legal pack and a data-residency map; operationally, a shift. India is the build base, the US and the UK are the commercial ground, and we build for all three from the first line.

DR-008 Tokenisation vault bought, not built, 1 Decision

We buy the tokenisation vault. Every sensitive value, an identity number, a card, an account, a date of birth, a passport, enters a bought vault of the Skyflow, VGS and Basis Theory class and leaves it in only two ways: through the substitution component, into a surface, or as the address of an outbound message, resolved inside the vault's own edge proxy and handed to the channel provider, never to us. Humans, the AI model and almost all of our software handle aliases: {{pan}}, {{card.primary}}, {{nhs-number}}. The vault is hosted regionally per market. It is one of the four bought components in the build-versus-buy table in 06. Its edge is a tokenising proxy the provider runs in the market's region under our detector policy: the channel webhook, the device form and every rail response terminate there, so the intake gateway and the passport adapters receive aliases and never hold a raw byte. Its SDK is present in exactly two places of ours, the substitutor and the hash service; the gateway, the adapters, the model runtime, the worker console and the logging pipeline hold none and have no network route to the vault, which is the gate 06 checks at stage 1.

DR-016 The context store as a temporal knowledge graph, 1 Decision

The context store is a temporal knowledge graph. Every fact in it is a statement about a subject, the user, a person in their life, an institution or a document, and carries the fields Table 1.2 in 01 lists, in the record shape 02 gives: subject, predicate, value or alias, source, the date observed, the interval it is believed to hold, a confidence, whether and when the user confirmed it, and where the real value lives. A fact is never overwritten: a correction closes the old interval and opens a new fact. A user's correction closes the old fact at once; a contradiction a task observes leaves both facts open and flagged, unusable by any step, until the user resolves it. Confidence is set by the source and decays with time, rising again when a later task uses the fact without contradiction. A fact below the threshold a step needs becomes a question to the user rather than a guess, which is how data-quality problems become visible rather than silently wrong. The store is filled mostly as a by-product of doing tasks, topped up by a short onboarding interview and by read-only connectors the user chooses. Sensitive fields hold aliases, {{nhs-number}} and never the number; the real value lives in the vault and the vault SDK is absent from the store's runtime. A fact a step writes, like every free-text field written on our side, a worker's variance note, a failed-final line, a question's free-line answer, an onboarding answer, a connector pull, is submitted by the task service to the hash service's screening call, and the screen runs the same four detectors the vault's proxy runs, identifier pattern, known-value digest, the user's declaration where a field is declared sensitive, and the dictionary-based health and legal term detector, whose policy is one artefact shipped to both; a match is tokenised through the hash service's tokenise-only credential and returned as an alias in place, the task service stores only what the call returns, and the store rejects any value that matches a pattern. The Graphiti style is named in the ledger as the pattern, not as a dependency: what we take from it is stated here in our own terms, and the library question is answered in 1.3.

DR-018 Mail sent from the user's own address, 1 Decision

Every email a third party receives for a user comes from the user's own address. The relay we run composes it with aliases, the substitutor swaps the values in, and the message leaves either through the user's own mail provider, under a send scope the user granted on their own device, or by the user's own hand, from a draft the harness wrote. Which route a task takes is decided by what the user has connected, and the harness helps to the extent the user lets it: with a send scope we send, with a read scope we see the reply, with neither the user pastes the draft and forwards the reply. No message to a third party leaves from an address of ours; the per-task address at our domain that the previous edition sent from becomes inbound-only, an address the user forwards to. Nobody migrates their mail to us, and the recipient never sees a stranger's domain. The relay stays ours, as the ledger's line that every execution surface is ours requires: what changes is whose address the message carries, not who composes, checks and places.

DR-021 Telephony masking dropped; no value is ever spoken, 1 Decision

We do not build synthesised-speech substitution, muted worker audio or a re-voiced transcript. Our people place calls in the ordinary way, and they hear the far party in the ordinary way. No value held in the vault is ever spoken on a call, in either direction. A task that cannot proceed without one either moves to an online channel where the substitution component can place it, or bounces to the user's own device.

DR-022 Structured task cards replace pixel masking, 1 Decision

For workflows we have modelled, the worker is given a structured task card derived from the page rather than a stream of its pixels: the fields the step needs, with their labels, their validity state and their aliases. The substitution component places the real values at submit, as it does on every surface. Masking then happens at the data layer, where it is deterministic, rather than at the pixel layer, where it is probabilistic.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title.
