5Key blocks

05 Business, 1 Why this page exists now

The previous edition deferred economics until after a Series A or B. That position was wrong and has been withdrawn. A seed process asks for the shape of the unit economics before it asks for anything else, and a plan whose cost per task might exceed its price per task cannot be tested by waiting. The engine is still unchosen, but an engine is a set of parameters, and a model built on parameters tells you which capability differences are worth paying for. That is the order this page now works in.

05 Business, 2 The task model

Every task resolves into one of five outcomes, and the mix is what sets the cost base.

05 Business, 3 The one ratio that decides everything

Delivery cost scales with usage, not with users. The governing number is therefore human minutes per user per month, which is the product of promised task volume and blended handling time, set against average revenue per user.

05 Business, 4 The cost split

At 1,000 users on the earlier and now superseded promise of two tasks a day, the split of delivery cost ran labour 73 per cent, tokens 24 per cent, everything else 3 per cent. Figure 5.1 is the wider picture, the four blocks of the cost base month by month, in which delivery labour is one line among four; payment fees and make-goods are charged on revenue and sit outside it. assumption Two things follow and both are durable.

05 Business, 5 The trajectory

Twenty thousand simulated paths, sixty months, every driver sampled rather than assumed. Figure 5.2 is the planning line itself, revenue against the whole cost base; Figure 5.3 is the ratio the levers move; Figure 5.4 is the cash the trough asks for. Conservative and optimistic lines are drawn from coherent families of paths, ranked by outcome, rather than by blending unrelated percentiles. The planning line weights them 65 to 35, conservative to optimistic.

05 Business, 6 How users arrive

This section exists because the model was interrogated and gave an uncomfortable answer. On the organic-only run the record used to publish, the rate at which users arrive in month 1 owned thirty-four per cent of the variance of the cash need, forty per cent of whether the business ever turned profitable at all, and sixty per cent of the month it crossed over, while the thirty-one drivers on the delivery side owned under seven per cent of the first of those between them. A plan whose largest variable has no mechanism behind it cannot be planned against, which is why growth is now two mechanisms rather than a rate, as DR-025 fixes, and why the published line carries them. assumption

05 Business, 8 Geography

Purchasing power is the argument for expansion and it holds: a US user paying $105 costs the same to serve as an Indian user paying $52, because the delivery floor is in Bengaluru either way. assumption

05 Business, 9 The commerce layer

The company already executes purchases the user asked for. Routing them through channels that pay a take rate is a margin on the existing business rather than a new one, worth roughly 1.2 per cent of routed spend once the share of transactions with a payable channel is allowed for. It reaches around fourteen per cent of revenue by month 60. Those figures are from the late-expansion scenario, which is the sequence DR-023 rejects, because that is the only commerce run the model carries and there is no India-only one. On the foreign-led sequence the same layer is worth 5.54 crore a month at month 36 and 16.9 per cent of revenue, on Rs 133,700 of routed spend a user, because a foreign basket is four times the Indian one. assumption Because it is close to pure margin it adds Rs 1.68 crore a month of contribution at month 36, which is more than twice the absolute figure of the organic-only run but about two fifths of a subscription base that is no longer near break-even at that month. assumption It needs merchant leverage rather than users, and the threshold behind that is routed spend rather than headcount: meaningful terms at Rs 25 to 50 crore a year. The working note behind this page converted that to four to seven and a half thousand subscribers, and that conversion was wrong by about nine times. On the model's own central spend per user it is about 430 to 870 subscribers, and the base passes it at months 4 and 9. assumption So merchant volume was never the binding constraint; the time it takes to sign merchants is, which is the twelve to twenty-two months the model allows and which no growth rate shortens.

05 Business, 10 What replaces this page

Nothing here is evidence. Five things must be measured, and the order below is the model's own where the model has an opinion, which is for four of the five: what a paid arrival costs is not a sampled driver in the sensitivity at all, and the four that are are listed in its order. They are: what a paid arrival costs, which decides whether the media half of the plan is worth spending at all and which two weeks of spend settles; G&A at a hundred users, which on the published line is the largest single driver of the cash need; minutes per user per month; whether the engineering headcount exponent falls as the workflow library generalises; and twelve-month churn on a paying cohort, with the net promoter score beside it, because the organic mechanism rests on both. Churn is last because the sensitivity puts it last, at a fiftieth of the variance G&A owns, which is worth knowing given how much of this page rests on it. Willingness to pay above $50 sits behind all five, as the trigger of the pricing decision rather than of the model, though the India price is the second-ranked driver in the sensitivity overall. assumption The first is the subject of section 6 and has no instrument yet beyond a budget, which is the largest hole in this page. The second needs a quarter of actual overhead and the fifth a paying cohort twelve months old. The fourth is half answered by the measurement protocol, which is thirty tasks and about a week of work. Minutes per user per month is minutes a task times tasks a user, and the protocol measures the first: how many tasks a user actually runs is not something thirty tasks chosen by the founder can establish, and it owns two fifths of the variance of the ratio the whole page turns on. Nothing in the record measures it yet, which is the second largest hole here after the cost of an arrival. The third is visible only over time, in whether the tenth institution costs less to add than the second. All five are open items with owners in 07.

The model, 1 What the model is

Two instruments sit behind every number in 05. The first is a deterministic unit model: set the assumptions, fill the capture sheet from the measurement protocol, and headcount, cost per task and margin fall out, with a thirty-six-month trajectory sheet that applies improving automation, a learning curve and user growth. The second is a Monte Carlo simulation over sixty months, which samples every driver rather than assuming it and reports the share of paths that reach profitability, the cash trough and the peak cash requirement.

The model, 2 The scenarios

Two configurations of the unit economics, one promotion of the second, and four of the geography, each run as twenty thousand paths. Five of the seven carry the two acquisition mechanisms: the promotion and the four geography scenarios. The other two are organic only by construction, and are the floor rather than the line the publication quotes.

The model, 3 The run the publication quotes

Every figure on 05 comes from sim_growth.csv, except the India-only reading of it that section 6.5 quotes, which is sim_growth_india_only.csv: a run of twenty thousand paths at seed 20260913 dated 14 September 2026, which is the viable configuration with both acquisition mechanisms in it at $120 a paid arrival and fifteen per cent of revenue in media. The organic-only run it was promoted over is kept as sim_viable.csv, and 05 quotes it only where it names it as the floor. The geography figures of 05, 8 and the commerce figures of 05, 9 come from geo_growth_india_only.csv, geo_growth_expansion.csv, geo_growth_expansion_commerce.csv and, for the sequencing table's third column, geo_growth_foreign_led_nocommerce.csv, with geo_growth_foreign_led.csv beside it for the same scenario carrying its commerce layer, at the same paths, seed and date, with the four organic-only runs kept beside them as geo_*.csv; each of the four is rebuilt character for character with media switched off, which is the self test the geography script runs before it writes anything. The bands move slightly between seeds, so the seed and the date are quoted wherever a number appears, and a figure re-derived from a different run is a different number. assumption

DR-024 Pricing is metered, and the tail is priced, 1 Decision

Subscriptions carry a stated monthly task allowance rather than an unlimited promise, and access to the consequential tier is priced explicitly: a stated number of complex tasks a year, metered beyond it. Tiers are differentiated by what they give access to, not by the status of the user holding them.

DR-025 Growth is organic and marketing, and the plan names both, 1 Decision

Growth is two mechanisms and the plan names both. Organic is word of mouth and referral: a share of the installed base each month, decaying as the easy audience is used up, against a market ceiling, and costing a referral incentive per arrival rather than a media rate. It is not a rate that arrives on its own: word of mouth at that level is conditional on the product being worth recommending, and the condition the founder sets is a net promoter score of 60 or above, measured on the paying cohort. Below it the organic mechanism is not assumed to hold and the media budget is not the remedy: a score under 60 is a product finding, and the answer to it is in the verdict system rather than in a reel. Marketing is a monthly budget converted into arrivals at a cost that rises as the budget does, across three channels: short video on Instagram bought on performance, AI-influencer placements bought per campaign, and coverage in the technology and business papers, which is earned rather than bought and therefore cheap and capped.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title.
