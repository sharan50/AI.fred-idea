1Your brief

You hold the thesis and the scope. The publication argues that a personal assistant can be trusted with the tasks that carry real consequence because the design makes the two fatal failures structurally impossible, and that the flat-fee players cannot follow because the trust architecture, not price or channel, is the moat. You own that argument, the product as the user meets it, the roadmap that sequences the build, the open items with their triggers, the ledger of decisions and the independent review. Every decision in the ledger is fixed unless its record says amber; an objection goes to the open items and the build complies. Your reading path starts with the thesis and ends with the review, which is the record read by someone who did not write it. What you are asked most often is what is not known, so read the open items with their triggers before anything else on the path.

5Key blocks

Contents, 1 The promise

AI.fred is a high-trust, high-reliability, human-in-the-gap, AI-first personal assistant. You give it a task with money, identity or an institution in it. Every request becomes an ordered plan. The AI executes what it can; where it cannot, it hands one of our people a precise, low-judgement instruction. The reliability is on tasks that carry real consequence, and it is made safe by one property: no person on our side ever sees a value from the vault, and no task is handled in a channel where a value would appear. Your numbers live in a vault; our people and our AI work with labels; a machine with no screen places the real value at the last moment; and every use is written into a ledger you can read, in plain words.

Contents, 2 Human in the gap

The AI plans; the humans are hands. A request enters through one gateway whatever channel it came by, and the gateway's edge is the vault's own tokenising proxy, so nothing of ours, the gateway included, ever holds a real value. The planner turns it into steps and proposes an authority envelope, the limits the user confirms with one tap: spend cap, contactable parties, committable acts, delegated identity acts, validity window. A classifier sorts each step into one of three classes. An ai step runs in our managed browser, on our phone line or through our mail relay. A human step goes to a worker as a task card that names the customer only as a pseudonym and the values only as labels. A user step, an OTP, a biometric, a PIN, an act the jurisdiction reserves to the customer, bounces to the user's own device. Every step closes with a code from a fixed vocabulary; every irreversible step inside the envelope is checked by a second party before it fires; every task that reaches a report closes with the user's one-tap verdict, and a task refused at intake or lapsed unjudged ends without one, the ledger saying which. Figure i.1 draws the loop; 02 specifies it.

Contents, 3 The two fatal failures, made structurally impossible

Sensitive values live in a bought tokenisation vault, regionally hosted. Humans, the AI and almost all of our software handle aliases only, like {{pan}}. One small isolated component swaps the real value in at the last boundary: into a form field in a browser we run, rendered as dots in what the worker sees; into an email through our relay. On a call our own person speaks in the ordinary way, and no value from the vault is ever spoken, in either direction (DR-021): a step that cannot proceed without one moves to a surface where the component can place it, or it bounces to the user's own device. Every execution surface is ours; a worker never uses their own tools. Evidence is redacted before it is stored and verified by an independent second pass, so no raw screenshot, recording or transcript is ever human-viewable. 03 names every path by which a person could see a value, the evidence system included, and closes all but the two it leaves open and 07 declares.

Contents, 4 Three markets, one build

India is the home base for build economics: high technical ability, a low-cost geography, a large English-speaking population. The US and the UK are where commercial viability lies. 00 Thesis, 1 Customer and scope

The customer is not defined by a task, they are defined by a relationship. They are a household with more obligations than hours and enough income that an hour is worth more than a few hundred rupees: dual-income professionals, founders and senior operators, and non-resident families carrying admin in two countries at once. What they buy is not a set of tasks. It is an assistant that already knows the answer to the questions a new assistant would have to ask.

00 Thesis, 2 The context profile

The general assistants start every conversation from nothing. The user restates their preferences, their constraints and their circumstances each time, and the quality of the answer is capped by how much they can be bothered to type. A personal assistant who has worked for someone for two years does not have that problem, and neither does a butler or a consigliere: when they are told to find somewhere for dinner tonight, they already know the part of town, the diary, what the last four dinners were, who is coming and what the person will not eat.

00 Thesis, 3 Why now

Now, because the three things a consequence-bearing assistant needs have all arrived since the middle of 2025, and nobody who holds one of them holds the others: models that can plan and act; rails and rules that permit a delegate to act within an explicit, bounded grant; and a customer who has been taught to delegate at all. Table 0.1 carries the evidence.

00 Thesis, 4 Markets

India is where we build and where the floor runs; the US and the UK are where the commercial case is strongest; one core serves all three, and expansion is an adapter, a legal pack and a shift, fixed in DR-007. Which market the first cohort lives in is an open item in 07, with an owner and a trigger; the pilot in 06 opens whichever way it goes.

00 Thesis, 5 The competitive landscape

Every player in the field executes either through chat plus people who see everything, or through a model inside the user's own browser and accounts. None has an execution surface of its own, a published masking policy for its staff, or a bounded grant of authority, and the sensitive wedge is either refused or advertised with no safeguard behind it. Table 0.2 lists the players with each claim's status, and DR-013 records what "verified" means in this edition.

00 Thesis, 6 The moat

The flat-fee players cannot follow us up the consequence axis because the property that makes their model work, a generalist who sees everything and can therefore do anything, is the property that makes the wedge unsafe. The trust architecture is not a feature they can add. It is a different company, with a different promise, floor and liability posture, visible to the user in every ledger line.

00 Thesis, 7 The scope, and what it gives up

What the previous edition called the endgame is the starting scope, not a later phase. AI.fred acts across the whole of a user's personal economic life from the first day and builds the profile of section 2 as it does so; the consequential tasks are the acquisition hook and the proof of trustworthiness, not the boundary of what the product does. DR-019 and DR-020 fix it, and this section records what the claim costs.

01 Product, 1 User journeys across the six verticals

Every journey has one shape. The user asks; the planner returns an ordered plan and proposes an authority envelope; the user confirms both with one tap on their bound device; steps run, each classed ai (the AI executes), human (a worker executes from a task card) or user (bounced to the user's own device); an outcome report arrives with the access ledger; the user closes the task with a verdict. Nothing executes before the tap. Ledger times are illustrative.

01 Product, 2 The task taxonomy

Task types are the unit at which the product is measured: verdict rates and the nudge schedule are set per task type. A task type is a vertical plus a shape of plan: where the point of no return sits, which step classes the plan needs, and what the envelope must contain.

01 Product, 3 Intake and the channel-agnostic gateway

The front-door channel is not decided. Underneath it, one decision is fixed: every channel enters through a channel-agnostic intake gateway that normalises WhatsApp, the native app, SMS and iMessage, the web, and mail the user forwards to a task's own relay address, inbound only, into one internal request format; mail is a channel, never a front door, because it cannot carry a confirmation. WhatsApp is the leading candidate for India; nothing is fixed for the US or the UK; one trigger resolves both, and it fires when the first US or UK cohort is scoped amber. The record is DR-001; the item has an owner in the open items in 07.

01 Product, 4 The authority envelope from the user's point of view

The user grants authority once, in one tap, and can take it back at any moment. Delegation is total inside the envelope and impossible outside it, and every irreversible act inside it is checked by a second party before it fires. The decision is DR-004; the capability ceiling above it is DR-003.

01 Product, 5 The verdict system

Two loops, two jobs. The control loop decides whether a task can be shown as done; the improvement loop decides whether it was done well, and only the user can say. Keeping them apart is DR-002: an outcome code is not ground truth of quality, and a verdict is not a gate on execution.

01 Product, 6 Onboarding and context capture

We learn the user mostly by working for them. Onboarding makes the first tasks possible rather than building a profile in advance; the profile fills as tasks run, and every fact in it says where it came from, when, how sure we are, and whether the user confirmed it. The store is a temporal knowledge graph (the context store in 02, DR-016); sensitive values in it are aliases.

01 Product, 7 What we refuse to do

Each item follows from one of the two fatal failures or from the law of the market. A request under it is refused from received or planning, before any step executes, with the item's reason code, and the user is told why; a single step under it is routed around or bounced. Two components refuse and one list is the enumeration. From received, the task service refuses by rule, before the planner runs, against the market's refusal list in the legal pack: a request naming an act or a party the list prohibits outright. From planning, the planner refuses: its output is a plan or a refusal, and its schema admits no third thing. In both cases the refuse transition carries one of the nine reason codes below, which are the canonical enumeration; a single step under an item is never a refusal of the task but is routed around, bounced or closed refused-policy by the classifier's lookup.

Full Review 11/09, 2 The idea in one page

The record describes a service for the tasks that carry real consequence and arrive a few times a year: a bank's KYC refresh, a passport renewal, a tax notice, a hospital admission for a parent, an insurance claim, an estate or property act. Full Review 11/09, 10 Founder, team shape and execution risk

The record describes a company that needs, before its first customer, the disciplines of a payments-security vendor, a browser-automation lab, a real-time media company, a three-jurisdiction compliance function and an operations floor, and it describes them well. Full Review 11/09, 11 Security and trust: a red-team read of the design

The trust design is unusually well thought through for a pre-product venture, and its central mechanism holds up: one detokenising component, a device-signed envelope, aliases everywhere else, and a ledger written from component records rather than from people. Read as an attacker, though, the record has a scoping flaw that shapes everything after it. Table 3.6 in 03 is, by its own title, the list of paths by which a human could see a real value, meaning a human on the operator's side. There is no adversary-centric threat model at all: no compromised party website, no third-party script on the bank's page reading the field the substitutor just typed into, no attacker who is the user (a scam victim tapping the envelope, or a synthetic identity using the harness to run KYC at scale), no lawful-access request to the vault vendor, no supply-chain compromise of the signed build. Twenty-six paths, seven adversarial passes and 123 findings were spent almost entirely on insider visibility, which is the one problem the architecture already solves by construction, while the problems the market has actually been losing on since 2025 (indirect prompt injection into browser agents, mail-borne zero-click injection, bribed offshore support staff, agents that fire irreversible acts against explicit instruction) are bounded in a single paragraph or absent.

DR-001 Channel-agnostic intake gateway; WhatsApp front door amber, 1 Decision

We build one intake gateway. It accepts WhatsApp, a native app, SMS and iMessage, and the web, and normalises each into one internal request format carrying text, voice and attachments; every component behind it, the planner first, sees that format and nothing else. The front door, the channel a market launches with, is a per-market choice, not an architecture commitment; today it is amber.

DR-002 Verdict system split into a control loop and an improvement loop, 1 Decision

We run two loops that never borrow each other's job. The control loop runs before "done" is shown: every step closes with one outcome code from a fixed vocabulary, artefacts attach for audit and dispute only, and dual control applies only to irreversible steps. It is not ground truth of quality. The improvement loop belongs to the user: a one-tap verdict closes every task that reaches a report, chosen from exactly five options, while a task refused at intake or lapsed unjudged closes without one and the ledger says which; the verdict joined to the step trace is the measurement against which every product change is judged.

The dependencies inside this publication are mapped, and the map is a tool. DR-028 Pages assembled from fragments, held fresh by the harness, 1 Decision

Every page under the docs directory is assembled from HTML fragments kept under src/pages/ by tools/build.mjs, a script with no dependency that runs on the same Node the harness runs on. A page under three thousand words of prose is one file, copied byte for byte. DR-029 A generated front door and one generated page per owner, 1 Decision

The publication has a generated front door and one generated page per owner. Ownership is set at section level in roles.json, the one source of it: a role owns pages and sections, a page's owner owns every section another role does not list, a decision follows its layer unless the file names its owner, and a decision record's page follows its decision. Decisions, 2 How a record works

A record has four parts, in a fixed order, and a fifth when it is amber: the decision, stated in one paragraph and then in its substance; the rejected alternative or alternatives and what each would have cost; what the decision closes off, as a list a reader can hold the publication to; its status, with the date and who took it; and, for an amber record, the trigger that resolves it. A record with no rejected alternative is not a decision but a description, and the harness rejects it.
