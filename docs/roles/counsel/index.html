5Key blocks

03 Trust and data, 6 The policy flag table and the lobbying agenda

Full delegation, identity acts included, is the designed ceiling; the flag table is what the policy layer reads at runtime to decide, per jurisdiction and act type, whether an act is delegated, bounces to the user's device, or is prohibited pending a regulatory answer (DR-003). Table 3.5 records the designed flag, the ceiling, and every cell is a legal item; the pilot's runtime setting is narrower, as 07 records: for identity acts outside India the loaded flag is bounce until counsel's answer sets it, so the UK and US identity rows below are the ceiling and not the pilot's value, and for the act make a payment under the cap the pilot's loaded flag is bounce in every market, so the debit is the user's own act on their device until a rail admits a registered agent, the Unified Agent Protocol row of Table 3.4 being the first candidate. Detracting is a flag; adding would be a rebuild. A bounce line wins even where the user ticked the identity act in the envelope; a prohibited pending line closes the step refused-policy before anyone attempts it and carries a legal item in 07. The table ships in each market's legal pack and is signed before a pilot opens (06). It doubles as the lobbying agenda: for every line that is not delegated, the last two columns say what to ask the regulator and what the user gains if the answer is yes. A flag is one of three values, delegated, bounce or prohibited pending, and the lookup has one interface: the policy layer is keyed by jurisdiction and act type, the act-type rows below (the table's last row is the basis those acts rest on, not a key), and every step type in the catalogue 02 owns carries exactly one act type and no flag of its own, the flag living in the policy layer alone, so the classifier, the envelope filter and the substitutor all ask the same question with the same key; a row that does not exist evaluates to bounce. Where a line carries a condition, it is a conjunction of named predicates from a fixed list (the portal is non-financial; the login, the consent or the fact is in the envelope; a mandate the user set up names the payee; a network token bound to this task and its cap exists for the merchant), evaluated by the policy layer from the envelope record and the adapter's facts at classification time and again from the substitutor's context at placement, never a free expression; a delegated whose condition does not hold reads bounce. The alias class and the surface are not part of the key: the substitutor checks them after the flag (1.1). Every flag is a legal item to verify, not a design constraint, and is chipped so. A one-time code has no line in the table: it is the user's factor by design, {{otp}} is never an alias and no flag is consulted for it (the classifier in 02; 5.1). The jurisdiction in the key is the delegator's: for the user's own aliases the user's jurisdiction, the market on the request; for a dependant's aliases the jurisdiction her delegation record carries, so an identity act on her aliases is flagged under it and never the user's. For an act toward an institution the same lookup is made twice, once under the delegator's jurisdiction and once under the institution's market, and the stricter result governs, prohibited pending over bounce over delegated, a row missing in either evaluating to bounce, so an India-market user's identity act on a UK institution reads the UK row and bounces until counsel's answer sets that flag; the rail is the institution's market's, and the signed step context names both jurisdictions the lookup used, so the substitutor checks the flag under the same keys (2.1; 01, 1.5; DR-003).

03 Trust and data, 7 The threat model: paths by which a human could see a real value

The first fatal failure is a worker on our side seeing something they should not, and the threat model is organised as the list of paths by which that could happen, each named, and each closed except the two this edition leaves open and 07 declares. A path is closed when a component property, not a procedure, stops the value before it reaches a person, and when the ledger records what happened. The evidence system is on the list, because it is the store people most want to open; so are the ledger itself, the room, the staff of every provider we buy from, the vault, the carrier and the sandbox, and the user's own habits. Figure 3.3 maps the twenty-six paths; Table 3.6 gives each its mechanism and its ledger line. The three paths that run through the room, 5, 6 and 7, all close in software: the browser stream's masking happens server-side before encoding, so nothing on a screen or in a headset is a real value, whoever photographs, tampers or looks, and the format masks, the default paint and the hold of 3.1 cover what no map predicted. The three control rows of Table 4.4 in 04, Clean floor and locker, Device policy and Monitored substitution stations, are each path's record and its deterrent, never its closure. No row of either table claims that a value reaches a screen as a residual, and the site controls and this table can be audited against each other. The proof that the closures hold is the canary test the build sequence in 06 requires at each surface's gate and before the pilot, and its procedure is fixed here. A planted value of every class in Table 3.1, in every normalised form the class lists, enters by each door in turn: typed and pasted into the channel, spoken in a voice note, uploaded, returned by a rail stub through the adapter's proxy, and echoed by a stub institution on each surface. A scripted task then places each on each surface, and a scanner searches for every form of every planted value in the evidence store, every database, cache, queue, backup and replica we run, every log, trace and metric index, the ledger, the console's stream as recorded at a test station, the transcript and the captions, and the relay's outbound store after the window. The gate asserts three things: zero matches; one "placing" and one "placed" or "not confirmed" line per placement; and that the verifier fails closed on an artefact deliberately under-masked for the run and the hash service's screening call refuses a planted value handed to it from the alias zone. Two planted values are never vaulted: a reference in a stub party's recorded shape and a free-text condition from the dictionary, echoed on a stub page and typed into a stub form, which must reach no test station as text; a call carries no planted value, because it carries no value at all. A gate that cannot plant a class has not tested it, and says so in the run's record.

03 Trust and data, 8 The access ledger

The ledger is the product of the boundaries above, and it is how the trust story becomes visible to the customer: one plain-words line for every use of every alias, readable by the user on their own device. It is written by the component that performed the event, from that component's own record, and never by a person. 03 Trust and data, 9 The data-residency map

Sensitive values stay in the market's region and only aliases travel to the floor. That is the whole map, and it is why the vault is bought: a provider's regional hosting gives each market a vault instance in its own region, and everything of ours that holds a value transiently, the substitutor, the surfaces and the evidence pipeline, is deployed in that region too. The floor in Bengaluru, running 24/7 under the shift design in 04, receives the rendered stream, the task cards, the ledger writes and the masked artefacts a review needs: dots, aliases and masked material, never a value. Figure 3.4 draws the three markets, and the accent is the one crossing that needs contractual safeguards: personal data of UK and EU residents, even in alias form and pseudonymised, processed by staff in Bengaluru is a restricted transfer under UK GDPR that needs a transfer mechanism (the international data transfer agreement or addendum) and a transfer risk assessment, with the processor terms to match to-verify. The US crossing is governed by contract and by the applicable state law rather than by a transfer regime, which the legal pack confirms per cohort to-verify. The India crossing is domestic. Model inference for a market runs in that market's region where the model vendor offers it, otherwise in India under the same safeguards as the floor, and for the pilot that choice follows the first market; in either case it sees aliases to-verify. A region is never overridden for availability: a vault or substitutor outage in one region delays a step; it never routes a value through another region (Table 3.2). Routing is by one key: the market the gateway sets on the request from the user record selects the region, India, the UK or the EU for a UK user, or the US, and that region names the vault instance, the substitutor deployment, the surfaces, the evidence pipeline and the hash service the task will use for its whole life; a step never chooses a region. The rule in DR-007 follows: the user's market fixes where values rest and are placed and the institution's market fixes only the rail, so a UK user acting on an Indian institution keeps their vault, substitutor and surfaces in the UK region, the UK-region browser or line reaches the Indian institution as it reaches any other party, and the India adapter's proxy tokenises the rail's response into the user's UK vault instance. Each market's entry in the map lists, per component, the region and the safeguard, and ships in the legal pack (02); the expansion playbook in 06 adds a row per market.

The trust story, 4 What we never do

  • We never ask you for a one-time code, a PIN or a bank password; those come to your own phone and you enter them yourself, and where one must go into a form on our screen, the worker sees dots. For a payment we do everything up to the tap that moves the money, and that tap is yours, on your own phone. A login to a non-bank website you choose to give us goes into a form that posts straight to the vault, and we only ever see a placeholder.
  • No person who works for us, at any level, sees your numbers, documents or card: every screen and every line they work with is built to show only placeholders, and if a failure ever put a value where a person could see it, your ledger tells you. Two things are still a promise rather than a machine, and we would rather you heard them from us: a health or legal matter said in ordinary words on a call, which no detector can reliably catch, and the name and number of a person you ask us to contact, which someone here has to read in order to put it in the list we dial from. Both are written down as open, with an owner, in the open items.
  • Nothing that cannot be undone happens outside the limits you set. If a task needs more, we ask, and it waits for your tap.
  • If you go quiet, we remind you, then mark the task lapsed and say so; one message revives it. We never quietly forget it.
  • You close every task with one tap: done, satisfied; done, but rough; wrong outcome, needs redoing; we couldn't complete it; withdrawn by me. That answer is how we improve.

DR-003 Full delegation as the capability ceiling, with per-jurisdiction policy flags, 1 Decision

Full delegation, identity acts included, is the designed capability ceiling of the harness. What a country permits is a runtime matter: a policy layer holds a per-jurisdiction flag table that decides, act type by act type, whether a step is delegated, bounced to the user's own device, or prohibited pending a regulatory answer. Detracting from the ceiling is a flag; adding to it would be a rebuild, so we build the ceiling now and set the flags conservatively. Conservative has one meaning: a lookup that finds no row evaluates to bounce, never to delegated, an unknown step type counts as irreversible and outside the envelope and bounces too, and every such bounce is a request to add a row, which is the catalogue rule in 02. This record supersedes the earlier A/B/C capability-tier framing.

DR-009 Evidence redaction as part of the harness, 1 Decision

Evidence is redacted inside the harness before it is stored, and no raw artefact is ever human-viewable. Every screenshot, recording, transcript and DOM capture, and the call metadata beside them, passes through one pipeline in a fixed order: capture, redact, verify, store. Redaction applies five artefact masks in the fixed order 03 specifies: coordinate masks at the points where the substitutor placed a value, with a margin, from the frame of placement to the frame of navigation; known-value masks by keyed digest, the digests issued by the hash service and never the values, over text layers, document captures and transcripts; audio windows, the allowlist of 03, 3.3 run inside the pipeline over its own batch transcript of the recording, there being no live transcriber to run it (DR-021), so that every placement, every matched read-back and every span the allowlist withheld is replaced with silence, the transcript naming the alias for a placement or a match and reading "span masked" for a withheld span; format masks, every run or span that matches a class format of Table 3.1, the party's recorded reference shapes or the health and legal dictionary; and mask-map masks, every run and figure the step's mask map does not clear. Aliases replace matches in transcripts and document captures, so a transcript reads {{dob}} where the value was, and a stored recording or transcript holds no known value, no class-format match and no span a worker flagged as an exposure. Verification is a second, independent pass by a separately built service with its own digest set under a second key; it checks the allowlist's windows as it checks known values, formats and the mask map, and it fails closed. An artefact that fails is never written: it is quarantined in memory, an incident opens naming only the alias and the rule that missed, redaction re-runs from memory with wider masks, and after a small fixed number of attempts the raw material is dropped and the step's evidence becomes a structured note, "evidence withheld: redaction could not be verified". Nobody views the quarantined artefact, the incident handler included; the handler receives the redaction manifest and the id of the matched digest and nothing else. The step keeps its outcome code, and the user's ledger records that the artefact was destroyed rather than kept. Raw artefacts exist only in the pipeline's memory. The pipeline ships inside the managed-browser stage of the build sequence, because that is the first moment a real value exists outside the vault.

DR-026 A call is for account mapping, never for authentication, 1 Decision

A call is used only where the institution asks for identity at the start of it in order to find the account, and not in order to open it. The test is the institution's own workflow, not our judgement of the risk: if the identifier a desk asks for is a key it maps to a record, the party is eligible for phone work; if it is a secret that grants access to what the record holds, the party is not, and the task runs on the browser or bounces to the user's own device.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title.
