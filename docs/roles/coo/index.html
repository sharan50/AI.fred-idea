1Your brief

You own the floor: the need-to-know task cards and their pseudonymisation, the three worker tiers with dual control, the shift design in its staged form, site controls as part of the trust guarantee, QA and the exceptions board, and the service levels stated qualitatively. You also own the measurement protocol, the thirty-task instrument that replaces the priors in the economics, because it is run on the floor and scored by the people who work it. The trust story makes promises about what a worker can and cannot see; the floor is where those promises are kept or broken, and the site controls are the second line behind the harness, never the first. Read the worker console before the operations page, because the console is the surface a worker actually holds. The office is mandatory and the shift wheel arrives in stages; the staging is an amber decision with a trigger the founder has yet to set.

5Key blocks

04 Operations, 1 Task cards and pseudonymisation

A worker never receives a task. They receive a step, on a card the task service generated from the step record, carrying the least the step needs: a client number, the aliases the system will place, the facts in clear, the script, the expected outcome and a structured result. The console that renders it is specified in 02, worker console; this section says how a card comes to exist and what it may contain.

04 Operations, 2 The shift design, staged

The wheel below is the shape at scale. The pilot runs one shift in one controlled room, because at pilot volume the 24-hour wheel is neither affordable nor required by law, and the attested site with three shifts arrives when the first foreign market goes live or the floor passes a headcount threshold the founder has yet to set (DR-006, amber). Two costs follow and both belong to the room rather than to a task: the floor is a step cost, so each shift and each market added is a jump rather than a slope, and seat cost per head rises with headcount, by something between 1.15 and 1.9 times by the time a floor passes three hundred, because a company that size has to become a place people will join. assumption 05 prices both.

04 Operations, 3 What phone work covers

Phone work covers non-authenticating calls and nothing else: bookings, chasing, enquiries, availability and appointment setting. No value from the vault is placed on a call in either direction, and no script asks a worker to state one (DR-021), and a party is eligible for phone work at all only where its desk asks for identity at the start of a call to find the account rather than to open it, which DR-026 fixes as a field in the party register rather than a judgement a worker makes on the call. So a step that needs a value stated or verified by voice is placed on the managed browser or the relay, where the substitution component can place it, or it bounces to the user's own device. A worker asked for a value on a call says it is not available on the phone, offers the surface that can carry it, and closes the step blocked-needs-user if the desk will not proceed. A desk that states a value unasked is an exposure: the worker closes the step on the possible-exposure control, which opens an incident with the party named.

04 Operations, 4 Tiers, escalation and dual control

Three tiers, and two functions outside them. QA and escalation are standalone functions, not tiers, reporting outside the tier line so that the people who check the work do not answer to the people who route it.

04 Operations, 5 Site controls as part of the trust guarantee

The room is part of the promise. The software makes "no person saw it" true on the screen; the site controls make it true around the screen. Office-mandatory, 24/7 operation is a fixed decision, DR-006, and the controls below are why it is a trust decision rather than a facilities preference. Three paths run through the room and are named in the threat model in 03 under the names Table 4.4 uses: the photograph or note, tampering with a station or a masking client, and a missed echo on a screen. On every one of them a real value is stopped by software before it reaches a screen: the masking of 03 is applied server-side, on the data a task card carries or before a frame is encoded, so no station holds a frame the masker has not cleared, and no value is ever on a call for a headset to carry (DR-021). The room is what makes that closure attestable rather than asserted: its controls are the record of a masking failure and the deterrent, never the closure, and they keep off the floor anything that could carry the task detail a card does show.

04 Operations, 6 QA and the exceptions board

QA is a shift duty with a named owner, not a department that reads reports afterwards. The board is specified in 02, worker console; this section says whose it is and what they do with it.

04 Operations, 7 Service levels, stated qualitatively

We state service levels in words on purpose. Response numbers are a pilot's output, not its input, and tying them to pool size now would smuggle in a measured economics that 05 does not have, because its numbers are modelled priors. What can be fixed now is the shape of each promise and the mechanism that keeps it: every level below is carried by a timer in the state machine, so a missed level is acted on by the task service at expiry, and whatever that leaves is an exception on the board rather than a statistic discovered later.

Measurement protocol, 1 What it measures

Three estimated numbers become three measured ones, at a cost of about a week of one person's time and a few hundred dollars of interface spend, before any floor is leased or any engine is chosen.

Measurement protocol, 2 Why the rubric is built this way

The failure mode this protocol exists to avoid is classification by plausibility. A task described as "book a restaurant" looks automatable, so it gets counted as automatable, and the AI-executable share comes out inflated. The correction is positive evidence: a task counts as AI-only when an agent actually completed it and the output passed a stated acceptance test, and not otherwise.

Measurement protocol, 3 Tiers

Every run resolves to exactly one of five outcomes, the same five the task model in 05, 2 costs.

Measurement protocol, 4 Timing definitions

These definitions are the whole of the measurement, so they are applied identically on every run.

Measurement protocol, 5 The task set

Thirty tasks, stratified across the mix the product intends to serve, ten in each expected tier. Expected tier is a prediction recorded before the run; observed tier is the result. The gap between the two columns is itself a finding.

Measurement protocol, 6 Run protocol

Three runs per task, on three different counterparties where the task allows it. One run tells you nothing about variance, and variance across counterparties is the thing that decides whether a workflow generalises. Record the average and note the spread where it is wide.

Measurement protocol, 7 The learning curve sub-test

The claim that human handling time falls with practice is central to the operating model and is testable cheaply. Take the four highest-volume assisted task types. One operator runs each twenty times against different counterparties, recording handling time on every run, and the mean of runs one to five is compared with the mean of runs sixteen to twenty. Two outcomes matter: handling time roughly halves and is still falling at run twenty, in which case the practice argument holds and the operating model can lean on it; or it flattens at sixty to seventy per cent of the first attempt by run ten, in which case practice is real but bounded and the model must assume the floor rather than the slope. assumption

Measurement protocol, 8 Decision thresholds

Set before the data arrives, so the result is read rather than negotiated.

Measurement protocol, 9 Practical and legal cautions

Run the measurement set against your own accounts and your own identity only. Speaking to an institution as another person, even a consenting friend, is the exposure the legal rows of 03 flag in India, the United Kingdom and the United States, and it is not worth incurring for a measurement exercise. The consequence is that this run does not test the delegation boundary: an operator acting on their own account sees every value and receives their own one-time codes, so the bounce rate cannot rise the way it will on a floor where DR-021 and DR-027 forbid a worker both, and the assisted-minutes floor is measured under a looser operating model than the one it has to hold in. Both readings are therefore optimistic, and the bounce threshold below cannot trip by construction.

Measurement protocol, 10 What comes out of it

A filled capture sheet gives, per task type and in aggregate: automation share on positive evidence, human minutes split into active, wrap and hold, token cost per task, and bounce rate. The model turns those into required headcount, users per generalist, cost per task and gross margin, and the sensitivity grid shows how far the answer moves when the two dominant variables move.

DR-006 Office-mandatory 24/7 operation with site controls, 1 Decision

Operations are office-mandatory: a worker executes from premises of ours, on our devices, never from home and never on their own machine. What is staged is the size of the premises and the hours they cover. For the pilot the floor is a small controlled room on one shift, with the same device policy, the same locker at the door, the same monitored stations and the same badge log; the attested 24-hour site with a day shift in Bengaluru and two night shifts covering the west amber arrives when the first foreign market goes live under DR-023's gates, or when the floor passes a headcount threshold the founder has not yet set. At pilot volume the 24-hour wheel is neither affordable nor required by law, and asserting it as a precondition priced the company for a scale it does not have. Site controls, the device policy, a clean floor, monitored substitution stations and an attestable room, remain part of the trust guarantee we make to the user rather than a workplace preference, and the room is attestable from the first day even when it is one room. Remote-safe operation is a designed-later programme, parked in 07 with an owner and a trigger.

DR-027 No name ever reaches a worker, 1 Decision

No name reaches a worker. Not the user's, not a dependant's, not a third party's who is a natural person: not on a card, not in a caption, not in a script line, not in a result field, and not out of a worker's own mouth on a call. There is no first-name exception, no initials, and no "as it appears on the card". The rule is absolute because the thing it protects is not the name.

AI.fred is a personal AI that builds your context, task by task, until it can act in your personal life the way someone who has known you for years would. It handles the ordinary things, a table, a return, a rental search, a trip, and it is built so that it can also be trusted with the ones you dread: the bank, the visa, the claim, the admission. The dreaded tasks are why people come. The ordinary ones are why they stay, and why the context compounds fast enough to be worth anything. AI.fred is a working title.
